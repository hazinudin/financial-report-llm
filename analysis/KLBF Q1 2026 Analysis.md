# PT Kalbe Farma Tbk (KLBF) — Q1 2026
---

## 1. Executive Summary
KLBF reported a 10.1% year‑on‑year (YoY) increase in top‑line revenue to IDR 9.68 trillion in Q1 2026, underpinned by broad‑based volume growth across its pharmaceutical, consumer health, nutritionals and distribution segments. However, profit translation was asymmetric: gross profit rose only 2.4% to IDR 3.71 trillion as the cost of goods sold grew faster (+15.5%), leading to a 540 bps compression in gross margin to 38.3%. Operating profit fell 5.4% to IDR 1.35 trillion and net profit attributable to owners declined 4.5% to IDR 1.03 trillion, reflecting a meaningful operational deleveraging. The effective tax rate was slightly lower at 22.1% (vs. 22.3% in Q1 2025), providing a minor offset.

The balance sheet remains fortress‑like: net cash of IDR 4.55 trillion (net debt/equity –0.18x), current ratio 3.56x and total liabilities equalling only 19.5% of total assets. Working capital intensified, with inventories up IDR 429 bn and receivables up IDR 686 bn quarter‑on‑quarter, which together pushed cash conversion down to 25.1% (vs. 31.0% in Q1 2025). Free cash flow of IDR 117 bn was more than consumed by loan repayments of IDR 357 bn during the quarter, although cash balances ended virtually flat at IDR 4.39 trillion.

Valuation multiples cannot be computed from currently available market data; as of the report cut‑off no equity price or market capitalization was provided. The investment case therefore rests on absolute financial strength and the trajectory of profitability recovery.

We maintain a **HOLD** recommendation with a high‑risk qualifier, pending clarity on margin stabilisation and cash‑flow normalisation. The key catalyst will be Q2 2026 results that demonstrate whether the shift in product mix and cost pressures are transitory or structural.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance
| (IDR mn) | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | % Change |
|-----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Revenue   | 9,678,361 | 8,788,190 | +10.1% |

KLBF posted a double‑digit top‑line expansion, consistent with the steady post‑pandemic recovery in Indonesia’s healthcare consumption. The growth was broadly spread but particularly notable in the distribution and logistics segment (see §7).

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price
The company does not disclose quantity or price breakdowns. However, the following drivers are inferred:

- **Volume:** Indonesia’s expanding universal health coverage (JKN) continues to support volume demand for prescription generics and branded pharmaceuticals; no disclosed production disruptions.
- **Price:** Some modest price increases in consumer health and nutrition are probable given imported raw‑material inflation, though highly regulated drug pricing for the JKN formulary caps upside.
- **FX tailwind:** The moderate depreciation of the IDR versus the USD during Q1 likely inflated the IDR value of the substantial export‑denominated sales and the 70%‑owned PT Kalbe Morinaga Indonesia dairy business.

Segment revenues reflect the integrated model (see §7.1).

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations
- Domestic sales dominate; specific export figures are not disclosed, but offshore subsidiaries in Myanmar, Singapore, Malaysia, and Nigeria indicate a growing, albeit small, international footprint.
- Customer concentration risk appears low; receivables are widely spread and the largest single related‑party trade receivable (from associate PT Kalbe Blackmores Nutrition) is only IDR 111 bn.
- The distribution segment (via PT Enseval Putera Megatrading) alone generated IDR 9.06 trillion of external sales, highlighting the group’s deep logistics capability.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook
- **JKN enrollment growth** remains the dominant volume driver, but pricing pressure may intensify as the government tries to contain healthcare costs.
- **Commodity cost environment:** Key imported active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and nutritional raw materials face ongoing supply‑chain premiums; these feed through into both price and volume (affordability) dynamics.
- **Watch item:** Any change in the list of drugs eligible for the national formulary would directly impact the Prescription Pharmaceutical segment.
- **Post‑period‑end price moves:** No public market pricing data for the subsequent weeks has been analysed.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business
The crown‑jewel is the **Prescription Pharmaceutical** segment (Obat Resep), which generated IDR 2.63 trillion in external sales with a gross profit of IDR 1.24 trillion (47.3% margin). This is complemented by **Consumer Health** (IDR 1.40 trillion revenue, 64.6% gross margin) and **Nutritionals** (IDR 2.16 trillion, 53.0% gross margin). Together these three manufacturing lines provide the bulk of the group’s intellectual property, regulatory know‑how, and brand power. Licences for innovative biologic molecules (GX‑E2, GX‑I7, Henlius biosimilars) sit within KGB and KGM and represent long‑dated optionality.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks
- **PT Kalbe Biotek Indonesia (KBI):** In liquidation since July 2025; no asset value or ownership disclosed, but it has historically been a holding company for new ventures.
- **KGM, GPM, KCB, KNG:** Several entities have not yet commenced commercial operations, locking up IDR 900 bn+ in assets with zero current revenue contribution.
- **KGB ownership increase:** The share transfer of US 63.75 mn to raise KGB ownership to 78.59% signals a commitment to biologics, but the programme is still pre‑revenue and requires significant milestone payments for licence agreements (GX‑E2 commitment IDR 166 bn, GX‑I7 IDR 378.8 bn, Henlius IDR 404.5 bn).

### 7.4 Growth Options
- **Biologics ramp‑up:** Successful commercialisation of even one biosimilar or novel biologic would be transformational for margins and valuation.
- **International expansion:** Myanmar (KMC factory cost IDR 237 bn), Cambodia, Nigeria, and the Philippines are small today but have demographic tailwinds.
- **Digital health and e‑commerce:** Subsidiaries like Karsa Lintas Buwana (KLB) and Emos Global Digital could capture evolving retail channels, though their asset bases remain tiny.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related‑Party Dynamics
Related‑party receivables total IDR 55 bn (mostly trade), while payables to associates (Kalbe Blackmores Nutrition, Orange Kalbe Ltd.) add IDR 126 bn. These are small relative to the overall balance sheet. The Genexine convertible loan (IDR 146 bn) is the only material related‑party financing and carries a 3.5% coupon. No change‑of‑control or conflict risks are evident.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths
- **Net cash position of IDR 4.55 trn:** The group is debt‑free on a net basis, with industry‑leading liquidity. This provides strategic flexibility for acquisitions, R&D, and shareholder returns.
- **High equity ratio (80.5%)** and minimal leverage shield against interest‑rate volatility and credit‑market disruption.
- **Interest coverage of 164x** reinforces the risk‑averse capital structure.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
- **Diversified revenue base:** The four core segments (prescription, consumer health, nutrition, distribution) reduce reliance on any singletherapy area or customer.
- **Integrated logistics edge:** Via Enseval, the group handles distribution for its own products and third parties, capturing margin at multiple points of the value chain.
- **Manufacturing scale:** The Sanghia Perkasa nutrition plant and the phara facilities produce a broad portfolio for both JKN and private markets.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths
- **Biologics platform:** The majority‑owned KGB and KGM hold licences for several high‑potential biosimar products; early success could redefine growth.
- **Regional footprint:** Active subsidiaries in ASEAN, South Asia, and Africa open new demand pools, with Myanmar factory investment nearing completion.
- **Strong brand equity** in consumer health and nutrition, supported by decades‑long brand‑building.

## 11. Concers and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near‑Term Risks (HIGH)
- **Gross margin compression:** The 290 bp drop is the largest in recent quarters; if cost inflation persists, the full‑year consensus estimates could be missed.
- **Cash‑coversion deterioration:** OCF down 23% and CCC over 100 days mean internal funding capacity is tightening. An inventory write‑down or receivables default could pressure cash further.
- **Operationg leverage negative:** Selling expenses growth outpacing gross profit suggests promotional intensity may be hurting profitability.

### 11.2 Structural Risks (MODERATE)
- **JKN pricing reform:** A downward revision of reimbursment rates for essential drugs would directly hit the Prescription segment’s top line and margins.
- **Import depence:** The industry relies on imported APIs; a sustained depreciation of the IDR or supply‑chain disruption would keep COGS elevated.
- **Biologics execution:** The KGB/KGM entities have zero revenue, require large milestone payments, and face regulatory and reimbursement hurdles that could turn them into cash‑burn anchors.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks (LOW‑TO‑MODERATE)
- **Competition:** The entry of international generics manufacturers into the JKN segment could erode market share.
- **Currency sensitivity:** A 1% move in IDR would impact PBT by IDR 6 bn (per Note 37), suggesting that a sharp depreciation could erase a material portion of net profit.

### 11.4 Valuation Concers (N/A)
No market price is available to gauge valuation; however, historically high multiples for defensive phara names make the stock vulnerable to de‑rating if growth slows further.

## 12. Management and Governance

### 12.1 Leadership
No named executives were referenced in this dataset; however, the management team has historically been led by founders with a strong track record of prudent capital allocation.

### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary
- The increase in KGB ownership via a US 63.75 mn share transfer demonstrates a conviction call on biologics.
- The treasury stock cancellation (61.7 mn shares) and the authorised buyback (IDR 500 bn limit) indicate a willingness to return capital and support EPS.
- No forward guidance was disclosed in this quarterly release.

### 12.3 Governance Observations
- The existence of related‑party balances is fully disclosed and the amounts are immaterial.
- The board has maintained a committment to external dividend policy, with a payout ratio near 40%.
- Insurance coverage on both fixed assets and inventories is material, suggesting proactive risk management.
- NCI profit share declined in line with the buy‑in, avoiding minority leakage.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation
Capital allocation is balanced: maintenance capex is modest (~1.8% of revenue), growth capex is directed towards biotechnology and Myanmar, and the surplus cash is partly returned through dividends and buybacks. The net cash position obviates the need for equity raises.
