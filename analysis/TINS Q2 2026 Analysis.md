[[TINS Q1 2026 Analysis]]

# PT Timah Tbk (TINS) — Q2 2026
---

## 1. Executive Summary

PT Timah Tbk reported an explosive six-month period to 30 June 2026, with revenue surging 146.9% year-on-year to IDR 10,419,726 million and net profit rocketing 804.7% to IDR 2,714,704 million. The unaudited interim statements show a business transformed by what appears to be a combination of volume uplift and higher tin realisations, with the core tin mining segment contributing 90.3% of external sales and generating segment net profit of IDR 3,570,967 million. Operating cash flow hit IDR 3,319,514 million, translating to a cash conversion ratio of 1.22x and free cash flow of IDR 3,407,952 million after minimal capex of only IDR 88,438 million.

The balance sheet strengthened materially. Cash and cash equivalents more than doubled from IDR 1,711,598 million at 31 December 2025 to IDR 4,483,933 million, lifting the cash ratio to 1.23x and driving net debt deeply negative to IDR (3,507,138) million. Working capital expanded to IDR 5,912,505 million. Total equity grew 25.4% to IDR 10,547,605 million, underpinning an annualized ROE of 114.6%. Yet the report carries significant qualitative flags: five IUPs were temporarily suspended at period-end, with extensions for four only approved in July 2026; customer concentration is acute, with MIND ID Trading Limited accounting for roughly 48% of revenue; and non-core segments—coal mining and construction—remain loss-making drains.

No market data was provided for this analysis, rendering orthodox valuation multiples undeterminable. On fundamental grounds alone, Timah is generating exceptional cash, carries a net-cash fortress, and dominates Indonesian tin production. However, the lack of valuation anchor, regulatory overhang on mining licenses, and extreme revenue concentration to a state-related offtaker argue for caution. We assign a HOLD (Operational) recommendation pending observable market pricing and resolution of the remaining IUP suspension.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Metric | H1 2026 (IDR mn) | H1 2025 (IDR mn) | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 10,419,726 | 4,219,871 | +146.9% |

Revenue for the six-month period reached IDR 10.42 trillion, compared with IDR 4.22 trillion in the prior-year interim period. The IDR 6.2 trillion incremental top-line reflects a near-tripling of turnover, almost entirely driven by the tin mining segment.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The interim statements do not disclose physical production volumes or reserve movements (production and reserve tables were empty in the extracted data). Consequently, a precise volume-versus-price decomposition is not possible. Segment disclosures indicate that tin mining external sales jumped from IDR 3,232,642 million to IDR 9,407,976 million, while internal sales to other group segments rose to IDR 2,586,505 million. The absence of explicit tonnage data means management’s commentary on offtake rates and London Metal Exchange tin price linkage is not available in the raw filings. An FX tailwind is probable: other income includes a foreign-exchange gain of IDR 84,449 million, suggesting USD-denominated revenue benefited from Rupiah weakness or from translation of dollar receivables.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is severe. MIND ID Trading Limited, an entity under common control, contributed IDR 5,035,224 million—approximately 48.3% of total revenue. Ara Metals & Minerals Pte LTD added IDR 1,612,834 million (15.5%). These two customers alone account for nearly two-thirds of turnover, creating significant counterparty dependency. By segment, tin mining dominates external revenue at 90.3%; coal mining, construction, industry, and other segments collectively make up the residual 9.7%. Internal sales of IDR 2,586,505 million within the tin mining segment indicate substantial inter-segment offtake, likely feeding the downstream industry segment (IDR 810,478 million external sales).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Sustainability hinges on three watch items. First, five IUPs (Izim Usaha Pertambangan) were temporarily suspended at 30 June 2026; extensions for four were approved in July 2026, but one remains in limbo, creating near-term volume risk. Second, the revenue runway is entirely tethered to tin realisations; no commodity price hedging or forward-sale disclosures are provided. Third, the group’s sales commitments to various customers at specified quantities (amount not disclosed) imply contracted offtake, but the pricing mechanism is unstated. Post-period-end, the cash surge into a DHE SDA special account (Note 4a) signals repatriation or export proceeds retention requirements that could affect future cash fungibility.

Gross margin expanded by 1,891 basis points to 38.95%. The COGS line grew only 88.5% against a 146.9% revenue surge, indicating strong operating leverage at the mine level—likely a function of higher realised prices spreading fixed stripping and processing costs over a larger revenue base. Depreciation expense embedded in COGS was IDR 278,418 million for H1 2026 versus IDR 349,960 million in the prior period, further easing unit costs.

Operating margin leapt to 33.24% from 9.01%. Selling expenses rose only 16.2% despite the revenue doubling, while G&A increased 29.0%, well below the top-line growth rate. The result is a 2,423 basis point expansion in operating margin, confirming that the incremental revenue flowed through at very high marginal profitability.

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

Interest income of IDR 44,765 million slightly exceeded interest expense of IDR 43,281 million, producing a near-neutral net interest position. This is a sharp improvement from H1 2025, when interest expense of IDR 59,127 million overwhelmed interest income of IDR 33,073 million. The reduction in interest expense reflects lower drawn debt: short-term bank borrowings from Mandiri (USD) dropped to zero from IDR 69,292 million at December 2025, and overall loan balances declined.

Other income net totalled IDR 72,781 million, up from IDR 40,285 million. The breakdown (per Note 30) includes a reversal of impairment on fixed assets of IDR 42,168 million, foreign-exchange gains of IDR 84,449 million, rental income of IDR 14,587 million, and a negative residual of IDR 72,309 million. The FX gain is non-operating and volatile; the impairment reversal is one-off in nature.

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

Receivables actually declined IDR 96,783 million despite the revenue explosion, driving days sales outstanding down to 13.8 days on an annualised basis. Collections from customers totalled IDR 10,535,016 million, yielding a collections-to-revenue ratio of 101.1%—evidence of near-perfect cash collection or advance payments. Days payable outstanding compressed to 6.9 days, producing a cash conversion cycle of just 32.1 days. Prepaid taxes of IDR 379,652 million and other advances of IDR 254,886 million are stable. The working capital engine is clearly a source of cash, not a sink.

Within this, PPE net carrying value fell to IDR 1,828,581 million from IDR 2,039,502 million, reflecting H1 depreciation of IDR 300,266 million against capex of only IDR 76,787 million and an impairment reversal of IDR 42,168 million. Mining properties declined marginally to IDR 294,008 million from IDR 300,682 million (accumulated impairment unchanged at IDR 107,511 million). Restricted cash jumped to IDR 391,833 million from IDR 233,035 million, tied to reclamation guarantees (time deposits). Non-current securities were IDR 62,908 million. Context notes flag investment properties carried at fair value of IDR 1,707,446 million (unchanged) and real estate assets of IDR 182,310 million (down from IDR 223,133 million). Post-employment benefit obligations total IDR 1,342,640 million, a long-term structural liability not captured in the canonical balance-sheet lines.

### 4.5 Non-Controlling Interests (NCI)

NCI in equity was IDR 381 million versus IDR 355 million at December 2025. NCI share of profit was IDR 26 million in H1 2026 versus IDR 1 million in the prior period. The NCI footprint is negligible—effectively 0.00% of net profit and equity—indicating Timah is almost wholly owned by the parent. Profit retention is therefore overwhelmingly directed to owners of the parent (IDR 10,547,224 million equity).

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

The tin mining segment is the crown jewel. It generated external sales of IDR 9,407,976 million (90.3% of group external revenue) and segment net profit of IDR 3,570,967 million—more than offsetting losses elsewhere. Segment assets total IDR 15,473,401 million, representing 94.0% of the consolidated base. The segment’s profitability is extraordinary, but it is entirely dependent on Indonesian mining licenses; the temporary suspension of five IUPs at period-end (four subsequently extended in July 2026) is the single largest operational risk. No reserve or life-of-mine data was disclosed in the extracted notes.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

Several subsidiaries and segments raise strategic questions. Coal mining (PT Tanjung Alam Jaya) reported a segment loss of IDR 12,967 million on external sales of only IDR 52,779 million, with assets of IDR 179,440 million—down from IDR 359,379 million, suggesting contraction or impairment. Construction posted a loss of IDR 8,144 million. PT Timah Indotama Mineral Indonesia’s total assets collapsed from IDR 8,903 million to IDR 350 million, indicating near-total wind-down. Timah International Investment Ltd (Great Force Trading) and the agriculture subsidiary are immaterial. TINVES (not listed in the subsidiary table but noted in context) has been under liquidation since August 2024.

### 7.4 Growth Options

Optionality lies outside tin. PT TIM Silika Nusantara (formerly PT TIM Nikel Sejahtera) holds a nickel mining license but carries only IDR 301 million in assets—purely exploratory. PT Timah Karya Persada Properti holds IDR 395,571 million in real estate assets, offering a non-commodity leg if developed. The industry segment (PT Timah Industri) turned profitable (IDR 16,452 million net profit versus a IDR 20,033 million loss in H1 2025) and could provide downstream stability, though it remains small. The PT PAL chemical tanker construction commitment (USD 7 million) is a tangential maritime capex item.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Internal sales within tin mining totalled IDR 2,586,505 million, flowing presumably to the industry and construction segments. Related-party transactions are pervasive. MIND ID Trading Limited is both the ultimate parent’s trading arm and the largest external customer, creating transfer-pricing and governance scrutiny. Government-related banks hold the majority of cash balances (Mandiri IDR 243,107 million; BRI IDR 365,439 million; BNI IDR 109,151 million; plus USD and time-deposit placements). Supplier financing liabilities of IDR 111,487 million are owed to related parties (BRI IDR 108,944 million; BTN IDR 2,543 million). Trade payables to related parties total IDR 60,215 million. The group is effectively a state-owned cash-conduit with extensive intra-government financial plumbing.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

The balance sheet is fortress-like. Net cash of IDR 3.5 trillion, a current ratio above 2.6, and a cash ratio above 1.2 provide immunity from refinancing risk. Operating cash flow of IDR 3.32 trillion in six months—against interest expense of only IDR 43 million—produces an interest coverage metric of roughly 80x. The equity base grew 25.4% organically via retained earnings, not dilution.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

Cash collection is exceptional: DSO of 13.8 days and a collections-to-revenue ratio of 101.1% imply the group is paid before or upon delivery. The tin mining segment’s net profit of IDR 3.57 trillion demonstrates world-class unit economics at current prices. Corporate overhead is well controlled; G&A absorbed only 5.3% of revenue despite the doubling of turnover.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

As the dominant Indonesian tin producer under the MIND ID holding company, Timah enjoys state-backed offtake relationships and preferential access to government banking channels. The downstream industry segment flipped from loss to profit, and the real estate and nickel subsidiaries provide embedded call options should management choose to monetise or develop them.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — Mining License Uncertainty:** Five IUPs were temporarily suspended at 30 June 2026. While four extensions were approved in July 2026, one remains unresolved. Any prolonged suspension could disrupt production and revenue recognition.

**HIGH — Customer Concentration:** MIND ID Trading Limited contributed 48.3% of revenue. A change in offtake terms, pricing, or volume allocation by this related party would have immediate P&L consequences.

**MODERATE — Tax Disputes:** The group is appealing a CIT 2022 overpayment of IDR 52,012 million and objecting to a CIT 2023 assessment of IDR 39,761 million. While amounts are small relative to profit, adverse rulings could signal broader tax authority aggression.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**MODERATE — Non-Core Losses:** Coal mining and construction segments are chronic loss-makers. Their continued drag erodes group efficiency and ties up managerial bandwidth.

**MODERATE — Asset Wind-Downs:** PT Timah Indotama Mineral Indonesia’s asset collapse to IDR 350 million and TINVES’s ongoing liquidation highlight portfolio pruning that may yet reveal further impairment needs.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

**MODERATE — Commodity Cyclicality:** Revenue and margin are hostage to tin prices. The H1 2026 margin profile is unlikely to be sustainable through a full price cycle; no hedging disclosures are provided.

**MODERATE — FX and Repatriation:** The DHE SDA special account and large USD cash balances (though BCA USD dropped to zero) expose the group to BI regulatory changes on export proceeds and Rupiah volatility.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

**HIGH — Absence of Market Anchor:** Without a share price or market capitalisation, we cannot assess whether the extraordinary H1 earnings are already discounted. If the market is pricing in continuation, a mean reversion in tin prices could trigger severe multiple compression. Conversely, if the stock trades on historical book value, the implied earnings yield could be enormous. Until market data is available, valuation risk is unquantifiable.

## 12. Management and Governance

### 12.1 Leadership

Management names and board composition were not disclosed in the extracted interim financial data. The analysis therefore cannot comment on individual executive track records.

### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary

Management declared a FY2025 dividend of IDR 88 per share (IDR 656,815 million total), signalling confidence but retaining the bulk of H1 earnings. They secured IUP extensions for four of five suspended licenses post-period-end. The liquidation of TINVES (since August 2024) and the collapse of PT Timah Indotama Mineral Indonesia suggest active portfolio rationalisation, albeit with potential legacy liabilities. Key management compensation rose sharply from IDR 16,413 million to IDR 25,279 million, a 54% increase that warrants governance scrutiny given the state-owned context.

### 12.3 Governance Observations

Timah is a state-controlled entity under MIND ID (PT Mineral Industri Indonesia). The board and commissioners are likely government appointees. Related-party transactions dominate revenue (MIND ID Trading), financing (government banks), and payables (supplier financing with BRI/BTN). While these relationships provide stability, they also create governance discount risks around transfer pricing and capital allocation favouring state interests over minority shareholders.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

Capital allocation in H1 2026 was conservative to the point of under-investment. Capex of IDR 88 million was roughly one-third of depreciation, implying reserve replacement and mine-life extension are not being funded from internal cash. Loan repayments of IDR 208 million reduced leverage but were trivial against the cash balance. The FY2025 dividend is modest. The net result is a rapidly accumulating cash pile (IDR 4.48 trillion) that is not yet earmarked for growth, returns, or balance-sheet repair—a latent efficiency drag.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 8/10

The group carries net cash, exemplary liquidity, and negative net leverage. The only deductions are the tax dispute overhang, the large but long-dated post-employment benefit obligation (IDR 1.34 trillion), and the unexplained cash reconciliation gap.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 9/10

Cash conversion, days sales outstanding, and segmental profitability are best-in-class. The deduction is for the lack of production volume disclosure and the maintenance-capex shortfall.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 6/10

H1 growth is spectacular, but it is price-driven and potentially non-repeatable. The reserve and resource runway is undisclosed, non-core segments are shrinking or loss-making, and organic growth capex is minimal.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 3/10

Without market data, no score can be fairly higher. The book value per share is IDR 1,414, but absent a price, the investor has no margin-of-safety reference.

### 13.5 Risk Profile

Asymmetric and skewed to policy/regulatory outcomes. Upside is capped by state ownership and the finite nature of tin licenses; downside is exposed to commodity price corrections, IUP revocation, and customer concentration. The risk profile is **MODERATE-HIGH** for fundamental investors, but **HIGH** for traders seeking valuation confirmation.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

If tin prices hold and the final IUP extension is granted, Timah is a cash machine generating annualised ROE above 100% with a net-cash balance sheet. The state-backed offtake through MIND ID Trading provides revenue visibility, and the nickel/real estate subsidiaries offer free optionality. A re-rating could be violent if the market is not already pricing in the earnings surge.

### 14.2 Bear Case

Tin prices mean-revert, the fifth IUP is not extended, or MIND ID Trading alters offtake terms. In that scenario, the 26% net margin collapses toward historical mid-cycle levels, the 33% operating margin proves ephemeral, and the idle cash is diverted to loss-making non-core ventures or state-mandated projects rather than returned to shareholders.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. Resolution of the fifth suspended IUP (expected Q3 2026).
2. Q3 production volume and tin price realisation data.
3. Execution of the FY2025 dividend payment and any interim dividend declaration.
4. Potential divestment or restructuring of coal and construction segments.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Operational — Pending Valuation Anchor).** The underlying business is firing on all cylinders, but the absence of market price data prevents a disciplined entry point. Existing holders should not sell into operational strength; new capital should await a quoted price and post-earnings market reaction. **Target price range: Not disclosed (market data unavailable).** **Review trigger:** Q3 2026 volume disclosure or a >15% correction in tin price realisations.
