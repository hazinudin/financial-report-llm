# PT Timah Tbk (TINS) — Q2 2026 (Six-Month Interim, unaudited)
---

**Reporting basis:** All company figures are in millions of Rupiah (IDR mn) from the unaudited interim consolidated statements for the six months ended 30 June 2026 (P&L and cash flow compared against H1 2025; balance sheet compared against 31 December 2025). Non-controlling interests, EPS, and ratios follow the computed-ratios block. External market/operational data (tin prices, volumes, royalty) are cited with their sources and are not canonical company figures. No market-data stub was provided, so market-based valuation multiples are **not disclosed** in this note.

## 1. Executive Summary

PT Timah delivered a historically extraordinary H1 2026: revenue of IDR 10,419,726 mn (+146.9% YoY vs IDR 4,219,871 mn), gross profit of IDR 4,058,945 mn (+379.9%), operating profit of IDR 3,463,178 mn (+810.9%), and net profit of IDR 2,714,704 mn (+804.7%), with EPS of IDR 364 vs IDR 40. Gross margin printed at 38.95% (vs 20.04% in H1 2025, +1,891 bps) and net margin at 26.05%. Management itself has stated that H1 net profit already exceeds the full-year 2026 plan, prompting a submitted RKAP revision (Timah corporate release, 2026-07-28). The growth optics are dominated by an extremely weak prior-year base (H1 2025 net margin of only ~7.1%); the run-rate, not the growth rate, is the meaningful metric.

The driver decomposition is roughly half price, half volume. External research (Public Expose Live, 2026-09-10; Timah press release, 2026-04-30; LME monthly averages via westmetall) indicates H1 2026 LME tin cash averaged ~USD 50,319/t (+56.7% YoY), with a 3 June 2026 all-time high of ~USD 57,960/t (3M) on Indonesian export-licensing frictions (Perdagangan rules effective 1 April 2026) and a persistent global refined-tin deficit (~173.5kt output vs ~179.3kt consumption in H1 2026). TINS's realised ASP rose ~52% YoY to ~USD 49,794/t while unit cash cost rose only ~11% YoY to ~USD 22,435/t — a cash spread of ~USD 27.3k/t that did the margin work, with H1 tin sales volume of ~10,984t (+85% YoY per Public Expose) providing the volume leg. Crucially, the sequential picture is less euphoric than the YoY print: derived Q2 2026 standalone revenue was ~IDR 4.96trn, ~ -9.3% QoQ on export-shipment delays and a smelter compressor failure (BRI Danareksa 1H26 note, 2026-08-18).

The balance sheet is now a fortress with a caveat. Cash more than doubled to IDR 4,483,933 mn (from IDR 1,711,598 mn at YE2025), driven by OCF of IDR 3,319,514 mn (+608.1%) against minimal capex of only IDR 88,438 mn (Rp 76,787 mn PP&E + Rp 11,651 mn other) versus six-month depreciation of IDR 300,266 mn (Note 14). Loans-to-equity is 0.10x, net debt is **negative** IDR 3,507,138 mn (net cash), and the current ratio is 2.62x. The caveat: FCF of IDR 3,407,952 mn is flattered by underinvestment — H1 capex covered barely 30% of depreciation, implying asset-base erosion and a future catch-up capex burden. The Rp 656,815 mn FY2025 dividend (declared 12 June 2026 AGMS) sat in accrued liabilities at 30 June and was paid 10 July 2026 (Note 32; Timah filing, 2026-06-15).

Effective tax was 23.45% of PBT (IDR 831,719 mn on IDR 3,546,423 mn) — the negative sign in the computed block is a storage convention on the expense line, not a tax benefit; the rate is essentially at the 22% statutory level plus non-deductibles (research brief, medium confidence). The NCI profit "spike" (IDR 26 mn vs IDR 1 mn) is a base effect on an immaterial denominator — subsidiaries are 99.75–100% owned — and is not a subsidiary earnings story.

With no market-data stub provided, P/E, P/B, EV and EV/EBITDA cannot be computed and are stated as not disclosed. Book value per share is IDR 1,414 (vs FY2025 DPS of IDR 88). Key swing factors for H2 2026: (1) the proposed PP 19/2025 royalty revision toward a 20% top bracket above USD 50,000/t HMA (BRI Danareksa estimated up to ~Rp 2.3trn extra FY2026 royalty, -42% net profit sensitivity; reported as delayed, medium confidence); (2) post-period Perpres 79/2026 (31 August 2026) permitting above-quota Belitung production after the 2026 RKAB was exhausted within ~2.5 months; (3) tin price persistence (BMI raised its 2026 forecast to USD 51,000/t, 2026-09-17). Rating: **HOLD (unanchored — pending market data)** on operational strength offset by tin-price cyclicality, single-offtaker concentration, royalty-policy risk, and an over-harvested asset base.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Metric | H1 2026 (IDR mn) | H1 2025 (IDR mn) | % Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 10,419,726 | 4,219,871 | +146.9% |
| Tin mining external sales (segment) | 9,407,976 | 3,232,642 | +191.0% |
| Industry segment external sales | 810,478 | 644,288 | +25.8% |
| Other segment external sales | 129,967 | 180,887 | -28.1% |
| Coal segment external sales | 52,779 | 122,901 | -57.1% |
| Construction segment external sales | 18,526 | 39,153 | -52.7% |

Consolidated revenue nearly 2.5x'd YoY, but the composition concentrated further into tin: the tin mining segment rose from 76.6% to 90.3% of external sales, while coal, construction and "other" all shrank in absolute terms. Per external research, the Q2 2026 standalone quarter was ~IDR 4.96trn — **-9.3% QoQ** versus Q1 2026's ~IDR 5.47trn — because April–May export shipments were delayed by the new export-licensing regime effective 1 April 2026 and RKAB permit resets, plus an off-schedule compressor failure that briefly disrupted smelting (BRI Danareksa 1H26 note, 2026-08-18; wowbabel, 2026-04-08 / 2026-06-01). So the H1 number embeds a licensing-constrained Q2, not a sequential acceleration.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

TINS does not disclose production/volume in the interim statements themselves (production: [] in the fact sheet), so the decomposition rests on external disclosures, clearly labelled:

- **Price:** H1 2026 LME tin cash averaged ~USD 50,319/t (+56.7% YoY); derived Q2 average ~USD 51,900–52,000/t (April ~48,942; June ~53,358 monthly averages). Realised ASP ~USD 49,794/t, +52% YoY (Public Expose Live, 2026-09-10; Timah press release, 2026-04-30; westmetall LME averages).
- **Volume:** H1 2026 tin sales ~10,984t (+85% YoY vs ~5,933t); refined tin production 10,865t (+58%); ore output 12,232t Sn (+75%). Q2 2026 standalone sales ~4,975t vs ~3,109t in Q2 2025 (+60% YoY, -17% QoQ) (Public Expose Live; BRI Danareksa 1H26 note).
- **Spread:** unit cash cost ~USD 22,435/t, +11% YoY — the ASP/cost spread (~USD 27.3k/t, +55% YoY) is the single largest explanation of the margin expansion.
- **FX:** IDR/USD around ~17,900 (research brief, medium confidence) supported USD-denominated realisation in rupiah terms.

Net read: roughly half price, half volume, against a sub-scale H1 2025 base.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

| Customer | H1 2026 Revenue (IDR mn) | Share of Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| MIND ID Trading Limited | 5,035,224 | ~48.3% |
| Ara Metals & Minerals Pte LTD | 1,612,834 | ~15.5% |
| All others | 3,771,668 | ~36.2% |

Concentration remains the defining feature. MIND ID Trading — a related party under common MIND ID control — accounted for ~48% of revenue (Note 25), consistent with the ~48% flagged at Q1 2026 ([[TINS Q1 2026 Analysis]]). Ara Metals & Minerals appears as a new >10% customer with no prior-period revenue. Trade receivables from MIND ID Trading were IDR 882,015 mn at 30 June 2026 (Note 34f). 97% of tin sales were exports, with China 35–36% of volume (Public Expose Live, external). The segment mix (tin 90.3% of external sales; loss-making coal and construction segments both shrinking) means the equity story is effectively a pure tin price/volume story with small drag from non-core segments.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The H1 print already exceeds the full-year plan (net profit = 169% of the ~Rp 1.61trn FY2026 target per management/Timah release), and TINS submitted a revised RKAP in September 2026 (raising the target toward 30,000t Sn / Rp 15.4trn revenue; investortrust, 2026-09-10). Sustainability drivers and watch items:

- **Price:** LME cash eased from the 3 June 2026 all-time high (~USD 57,525/t spot) to ~USD 51,100/t by 30 June; post-period it held ~USD 50,000–56,000/t through Oct 2026, and BMI raised its 2026 forecast to USD 51,000/t on AI/solder demand against constrained supply (thehindubusinessline, 2026-09-08; theoregongroup, 2026-09-17). The market was in deficit in H1 (global refined output ~173.5kt vs consumption ~179.3kt, Public Expose).
- **Volume:** Perpres 79/2026 (31 August 2026, post-period) authorises above-RKAB Belitung production for one year with independent surveyor verification — this matters because TINS had exhausted its 2026 RKAB quota within ~2.5 months and Belitung ore output had risen ~10x (cnbcindonesia, 2026-09-16; Bloomberg Technoz, 2026-09-17). The government also allowed TINS to buy ore from local Bangka Belitung miners (ITA, 21 August 2026, external).
- **Royalty:** PP 19/2025's progressive 3–10% of HMA schedule put TINS in the 10% bracket for the whole quarter (HMA > USD 40,000/t); a proposed revision to a 20% top bracket above USD 50,000/t was flagged in Q2 but reported delayed — the largest single regulatory risk to H2 margins (investortrust, 2026-05-11; ussindonesia, 2026-05-11; medium confidence).
- **Reserves/runway:** not disclosed in the interim statements — cannot be assessed here.

COGS rose 88.7% in absolute terms (the "decrease" flag in some pipelines is a sign-convention artifact) — but on 60% higher tonnage, with the 10% HMA royalty now embedded (royalty payments of Rp 567.99bn in Q1 2026 alone vs Rp 64.23bn in Q1 2025, +784% YoY per asatunews, 2026-05-12). Gross margin nonetheless expanded because realised ASP (+52% YoY) outran unit cash cost (+11% YoY) — price leverage, not cost cuts. Note the prior-year margin was depressed by sub-scale output (H1 2025 ore output ~7kt Sn vs 12.2kt now), inflating unit costs.

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

- **Receivables / DSO:** 13.8 days annualized (computed), down sharply; collections from customers of IDR 10,535,016 mn vs revenue IDR 10,419,726 mn — a collections ratio of 101.1%, i.e., prior-period receivables were being collected as fast as current sales were booked. Trade receivables from MIND ID Trading of IDR 882,015 mn (Note 34f) remain the largest single exposure. Receivables **fell** 5.8% vs YE2025 despite revenue 2.5x-ing — excellent cash discipline.
- **Inventories / DIO:** 25.2 days annualized (computed), broadly stable value (IDR 2,877,480 mn vs 2,801,315 mn). External context: Q2 inventory change was ~+Rp 220bn vs ~-Rp 182bn in Q1 (mediakampung, 2026-07-29, medium confidence) — tin metal drawn down and partly rebuilt.
- **Prepaid taxes / other advances:** prepaid taxes flat at ~Rp 380bn; other advances Rp 254,886 mn (vs 252,881) — unremarkable.
- Cash conversion cycle: DSO 13.8 + DIO 25.2 - DPO 6.9 = **32.1 days** — exceptionally short for a miner, reflecting the export-offtake model's fast settlement.

### 4.4 Non-Current Assets Analysis

- **PP&E net:** IDR 1,828,581 mn, down 10.4% from IDR 2,039,502 mn — depreciation (Rp 300,266 mn in six months) against capex of only Rp 76,787 mn. Accumulated impairment IDR 76,133 mn (Note 14). This is the asset-erosion signal: the fleet and smelting assets are being run harder than they are being replaced.
- **Mining properties:** IDR 294,008 mn (vs 300,682), net book value down Rp 6,674 mn with a standing impairment of Rp 107,511 mn (Note 17).
- **Investment properties:** fair value IDR 1,707,446 mn, unchanged from YE2025 (Note 16) — a large, passive, non-earning-use block (~10% of total assets).
- **Restricted cash:** IDR 391,833 mn (vs 233,035, +68%), largely time deposits under reclamation guarantees (Note 37: time deposits Rp 391,833; bank guarantees fell to Rp 71,660 from Rp 222,164 as collateral was replaced by deposits) and, externally, DHE-SDA placement requirements under PP 21/2026 (effective 1 June 2026, ≥30% of export proceeds for ≥3 months; medium confidence).
- **Associates/JVs:** carrying value IDR 357,527 mn (cost 449,140, provision 91,613) vs IDR 360,924 at YE2025 (Note 13) — including a Rp 91,690 mn stake in PT Pertamina Bina Medika (IHC).
- **Securities non-current:** IDR 62,908 mn (vs 71,135); current government bonds IDR 37,905 mn, new vs zero at YE2025 (Note 11).

### 4.5 Non-Controlling Interests (NCI)

NCI is immaterial by construction: equity NCI of IDR 381 mn (vs 355) and NCI profit of IDR 26 mn (vs 1) on subsidiaries held at 99.75–100% (except 65%-owned PT Timah Indotama Mineral Indonesia, whose total assets collapsed from Rp 8,903 mn to Rp 350 mn, and 50%-owned PT Timah Nigeria Limited). The +2,500% NCI profit "growth" is arithmetic on a single-digit-million base (Q1 2026: Rp 10mn vs Rp 2mn per the Q1 accounts) — not a subsidiary earnings event. TINS is effectively a wholly-owned structure: profit retention at the parent is total, which is also why ROE to owners (114.6% annualized) and total ROE are identical.

Total capex of IDR 88,438 mn equals only **29% of six-month depreciation** (IDR 300,266 mn, Note 14). Even adjusting for the fact that the same depreciation line fell 20% YoY (H1 2025 depreciation Rp 374,610 mn vs H1 2025 capex of only Rp 104,845 mn — underinvestment is a multi-year pattern, not just a 2026 choice), the current gap is extreme. Read: this is not growth capex, and it is barely maintenance capex. The FCF surge is partly a deferred-investment artefact; PP&E net is shrinking ~Rp 211k mn per half-year. The unresolved item is TINS's stated FY2026 capex plan (the Q1 2026 deck referenced "selective investment") — not disclosed in this filing.

### 5.4 Free Cash Flow Analysis

| Item | H1 2026 (IDR mn) |
|---|---|
| OCF | 3,319,514 |
| Capex | (88,438) |
| **FCF** | **3,407,952** |

FCF margin = 32.7% of revenue. Core FCF stripping one-offs: the OCF already embeds cash one-offs on both sides (FX-linked settlements, prior receivable collections); the dominant non-recurring tailwind is not in OCF but in what is **absent** — capex. Normalising capex to depreciation alone (~Rp 300k mn) would still leave ~Rp 3.1trn of FCF, so the conclusion survives: this is genuinely cash-generative at an unprecedented scale. But the gap between Rp 88bn capex and Rp 300bn depreciation is a real economic cost being deferred, and a royalty hike to 20% (if enacted) would hit this FCF directly at the top line.

## 10. Strengths

- Net cash of IDR 3,507,138 mn — 33% of equity in net cash; loans/equity 0.10x; no refinancing risk (maturities 2027–2030).
- OCF IDR 3,319,514 mn with 122.3% cash conversion and 101.1% collections ratio — earnings are cash, not accruals.
- Current ratio 2.62x, cash ratio 1.23x, CCC of only 32 days.
- Equity +25.4% in six months purely from retained profit.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- Unit economics: ASP +52% YoY vs cash cost +11% YoY — the spread widened to ~USD 27.3k/t (external).
- Production recovery: H1 ore output +75%, refined tin +58% YoY; four of five suspended IUPs extended in July 2026 (Note 1d).
- Fixed-cost absorption: G&A +29% against revenue +147%.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- Vertically integrated SOE position with export infrastructure and the Belitung above-quota licence (post-period Perpres 79/2026) giving volume runway into H2.
- Deep net cash gives optionality: catch-up capex, non-core divestments, or larger dividends.
- MIND ID group alignment guarantees offtake, even if it concentrates the book.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

- **HIGH — Royalty escalation:** the proposed PP 19/2025 revision toward a 20% top bracket above USD 50,000/t HMA would, per BRI Danareksa (external, medium confidence, reported delayed), add ~Rp 2.3trn of FY2026 royalty and cut net profit up to 42% (NPM toward ~9%). At current prices TINS sits squarely in the exposed bracket.
- **HIGH — Tin price reversion:** the margin structure is a price artefact. LME cash already eased from ~USD 57.5k (3 June) to ~USD 51.1k (30 June); every ~10% tin price decline roughly maps to a far larger proportionate hit to operating profit given fixed costs and the 10% royalty floor tied to price.
- **HIGH — Fifth suspended IUP** remains unresolved (Note 1d); the July 2026 extension covered four only.
- **MODERATE — Q2 sequential softness:** export-licensing/RKAB frictions cut Q2 shipments ~17% QoQ; if permitting normalises, competitors' supply returns too, pressuring price.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

- **HIGH — Asset-base erosion:** capex (Rp 88bn) at 29% of six-month depreciation (Rp 300bn); PP&E down 10.4% in six months; H1 2025 showed the same pattern (capex Rp 105bn vs depreciation Rp 375bn). This is a multi-year underinvestment in the franchise generating today's cash — the cash flow is partly borrowed from the asset base.
- **HIGH — Offtaker concentration:** MIND ID Trading ~48% of revenue and Rp 882bn of receivables; terms are intra-group and reallocation risk sits with the parent, not the market.
- **MODERATE — Non-core drag:** coal, construction and other segments remain collectively loss-making.
- **MODERATE — NCI/subsidiary cleanups** (TINVES liquidation, Nigeria JV) still absorbing management attention.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

- **HIGH — Regulatory stack:** progressive royalty (PP 19/2025), DHE-SDA 30% onshore placement for ≥3 months (PP 21/2026, effective 1 June 2026 — a working-capital/parking constraint on Rp 4trn+ of export proceeds), export-licensing regime (1 April 2026), and RKAB quota administration. Each has already bitten within the last two quarters.
- **MODERATE — Tax disputes:** DGT appeals on CIT 2022/2023 (Rp 52,012 mn / Rp 39,761 mn) open.
- **MODERATE — Reclamation provisions** rising (Rp 431,889 mn, +15.3%) with restricted cash/ time-deposit collateral locked at Rp 391,833 mn.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

- Market data not provided; the structural concern is that a 114.6% annualized ROE and 26% net margin are cycle-peak numbers being annualised off six months. Indonesian SOE miners are typically priced at de-rated cycle multiples (coal SOE peers at 4–6x P/E per pipeline DB). Buying TINS on 2026 run-rate EPS risks paying peak earnings at a non-peak multiple if the royalty revision or tin-price mean-reversion lands in H2 2026.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

- Tin remains in structural deficit (H1 2026 output 173.5kt vs consumption 179.3kt) with AI/solder demand support and BMI's raised USD 51,000/t 2026 forecast; elevated-for-longer prices sustain H2 margins.
- Perpres 79/2026 unlocks above-quota Belitung production for a year — volume growth without new licence risk; RKAP revision signals management confidence.
- The Rp 3.5trn net cash plus FCF machine enables catch-up capex, a special dividend, or opportunistic M&A — any of which re-rates a stock trading against a fortress balance sheet.
- Four of five IUPs extended; the fifth is a free option.

### 14.2 Bear Case

- Tin price mean-reversion: a move back toward USD 35–40k/t collapses the ASP/cost spread, and the 26% net margin reverts toward single digits; the equity de-rates on peak-trough earnings compression.
- The 20% royalty bracket is enacted: ~Rp 2.3trn FY2026 burden, up to -42% net profit (broker estimate) — a direct top-line-to-bottom-line haircut with no operational offset.
- Capex at 29% of depreciation means today's cash is borrowed from tomorrow's production; by 2027 the volume base itself is at risk.
- Q2 already showed the fragility: -9.3% QoQ revenue on permitting friction; MIND ID reallocation of offtake is a single-decision risk to ~48% of revenue.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. Enactment or final shelving of the PP 19/2025 royalty revision (the H2 2026 margin binary).
2. Resolution of the fifth suspended IUP and RKAB/permit normalisation for H2 export volumes.
3. Revised RKAP confirmation and any FY2026 interim/special dividend given net profit already exceeding the annual target.
4. Capex re-acceleration announcement (or continued absence) — the asset-base signal.
5. Divestment/restructuring of coal and construction segments.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (valuation unanchored — no market data).** Operationally this was an exceptional half-year with best-in-class cash generation and a fortress balance sheet, but the earnings engine is a tin-price cycle at its historical peak, the regulatory overlay (royalty revision, DHE-SDA, export licensing) can remove a third to a half of net profit by administrative decision, and the company is consuming its asset base while it harvests. Without a market price to anchor multiples, no target price range is assigned; the review trigger is the royalty revision outcome and LME tin holding above USD 45,000/t, at which point the deep net cash and above-quota volume runway would argue for an upgrade — while a fall below USD 40,000/t with the 20% bracket enacted would argue for capitulation on the growth thesis.
