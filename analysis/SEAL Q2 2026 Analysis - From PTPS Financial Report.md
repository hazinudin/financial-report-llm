## Key Observations

### Positive Indicators
- **Revenue Acceleration in Q2 2026:** Q2 revenue of Rp 860.6M is +56.1% higher than Q1 2026 (Rp 551.2M), indicating improving top-line momentum
- **Revenue On Track to Exceed 2025:** H1 2026 revenue (Rp 1.41B) already represents ~55% of full year 2025 (Rp 2.56B); at the current pace, FY 2026 revenue could reach ~Rp 2.8-3.1B
- **Asset Base Expansion:** Total assets grew +9.4% (Rp 3.47B) in six months, from Rp 37.05B to Rp 40.52B, suggesting ongoing investment (possibly plantation development or fixed assets)
- **H1 2026 Revenue Growth vs H1 2025:** Estimated +10.4% revenue growth year-over-year for the first half

### Concerns
- **Liabilities Surge:** Total liabilities jumped +196.7% in six months (Rp 1.85B → Rp 5.48B), with the liability-to-asset ratio rising from 5.0% to 13.5% — a significant deterioration in leverage. The breakdown of these new liabilities is **not disclosed** in the PTPS report
- **Losses Worsened Year-over-Year:** H1 2026 net loss (Rp 655.6M) is nearly **triple** the derived H1 2025 net loss (Rp 230.6M), a +184.4% deterioration — reversing the improvement trend noted in the Q1 2026 analysis
- **Q2 Losses Worse Than Q1:** Q2 2026 net loss (Rp 408.0M) exceeded Q1 2026 (Rp 247.5M) by +64.8%, despite higher revenue, suggesting rising cost pressures or one-off charges
- **2025 Losses Were Back-Loaded:** Derived H1 2025 loss (Rp 230.6M) vs full year 2025 (Rp 2.63B) shows ~Rp 2.4B of losses concentrated in H2 2025 — a seasonal or cyclical pattern that may repeat in 2026
- **Net Assets Essentially Flat:** Despite asset growth of Rp 3.47B, net assets declined marginally (-0.5%), as losses were funded by increased liabilities rather than equity
- **Continued Losses:** Company remains loss-making across all reported periods (2024, 2025, H1 2026)
- **Unpaid Investment:** PTPS still owes Rp 1.68B for share investment, unchanged since Dec 2025

## Conclusion

SEAL continues to operate as a palm oil company with PT Pulau Subur as a significant minority shareholder (30%). The H1 2026 results present a **mixed but deteriorating** picture compared to the more optimistic Q1 2026 analysis:

**Top-line improvement:** Revenue accelerated sharply in Q2 2026 (+56.1% vs Q1), and H1 2026 revenue is tracking ahead of the 2025 run rate, suggesting full-year 2026 revenue will likely exceed 2025. The company's revenue trajectory since 2024 (Rp 0.44B → Rp 2.56B → ~Rp 2.8-3.1B estimated 2026) shows continued growth.

**Bottom-line deterioration:** However, losses have worsened significantly year-over-year — H1 2026 net loss (Rp 655.6M) is nearly triple the derived H1 2025 loss (Rp 230.6M). This reverses the positive "loss reduction" narrative from the Q1 2026 analysis, which had compared Q1 2026 against a full-year-averaged 2025 baseline. When compared apples-to-apples (H1 vs H1), SEAL's losses have materially deteriorated in 2026.

**Balance sheet warning:** The most significant concern is the **196.7% surge in total liabilities** (Rp 1.85B → Rp 5.48B) in just six months, pushing the liability-to-asset ratio from 5.0% to 13.5%. While assets grew 9.4% (indicating ongoing investment), the funding source shifted toward debt. The composition of these new liabilities is not disclosed, making it difficult to assess whether this represents productive financing (e.g., plantation development borrowings) or deteriorating trade/operating payables. The flat net assets (-0.5%) confirm that losses are being absorbed by increased leverage rather than equity.

The investment carrying value has gradually declined from Rp 9.39B (Dec 2024) to Rp 8.81B (Dec 2025) to Rp 8.68B (June 2026), a cumulative Rp 711M erosion over 18 months due to accumulated losses.

Looking ahead, the key watch item is whether 2025's pattern of back-loaded losses (H2 2025 contributed ~Rp 2.4B of the full-year Rp 2.63B loss) repeats in 2026. If so, H2 2026 could see a further significant deterioration despite the H1 revenue improvement. Direct access to SEAL's own financial statements would be necessary to understand the liability composition, cost structure, and plantation operational metrics driving these results.
