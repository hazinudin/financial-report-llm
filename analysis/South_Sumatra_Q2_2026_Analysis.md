# South Sumatra Palm Oil Plantations: Q2 2026 Weather & Yield Outlook

> Companion to [[South_Sumatra_Q1_2026_Analysis]]. Q2 financial reports (H1 2026) are not yet released at the time of writing (Jul 16, 2026); this analysis is built from **BMKG seasonal forecasts, ENSO monitoring (BMKG/NOAA/JMA), and GAPKI monthly production prints** rather than company-level Q2 disclosures.

## Summary

Q2 2026 (Apr–Jun) marks the **onset — not the peak —** of a forecast strong-El-Niño dry season across South Sumatra. BMKG flagged the dry season beginning in **May 2026**, peaking **July–August**, with **PTPS's Palembang territory (ZOM 125) reaching peak dry one month earlier than normal**. National CPO production already slipped **−7.01% MoM in May 2026** (GAPKI), the first monthly decline since the Oct-2025→Mar-2026 strong-output cycle. Critically, palm oil yields respond to drought with a **9–12 month lag** (MPOC), so the real yield cliff for South Sumatra operators arrives in **Q1–Q2 2027**, not Q2 2026. PTPS — the only pure-play, single-region South Sumatra name (1,090 ha, Palembang-only) — is the **most exposed** in the cohort.

## 1. Weather Outlook — South Sumatra (BMKG Forecast, issued Apr–May 2026)

BMKG's South Sumatra Climatology Station (Wandayantolis, Palembang) released the seasonal forecast ahead of the dry season:

| Element | Forecast |
|---|---|
| **Dry season onset** | May 2026 |
| **Peak dry season** | July – August 2026 (national peak = August) |
| **Duration** | 7–15 decades (≈ 3–5 months) |
| **Central Sumsel** | Longest dry period: **13–15 decades** (~4–5 months) |

### Seasonal Zone (ZOM) Breakdown

| ZOM | Coverage | Peak Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **125** | **Palembang, Musi Banyuasin, Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir, Muara Enim, Ogan Ilir** ← *PTPS territory* | **One month EARLIER than normal** |
| **135** | Ogan Komering Ilir (OKI), East Ogan Komering Ulu (OKU) | One month earlier than normal |
| Others (12 ZOMs) | Most of South Sumatra | August 2026 |
| **137** | Pagar Alam, southern/western Lahat, parts of Musi Rawas & Empat Lawang | One month LATER than normal |

**Implication for PTPS**: PTPS's plantations sit squarely in **ZOM 125**, the zone flagged for an **earlier-than-normal peak dry** — meaning dry stress was building through late Q2 itself (June), not just Q3. This compresses the window between the Q1 biological slowdown (see §5) and the El Niño dry stress — effectively back-to-back yield drags with no recovery buffer.

### Risks Flagged by BMKG
- **Forest & land fires (karhutla)** — hotspot control priority
- **Drought / limited clean water supply**
- **Pest & disease threats** persisting despite rainfall variations
- Water resource management, reservoir optimization, weather modification operations on standby

## 4. The Lag Effect Framework — Why Q2 2026 Is Not the Cliff

> *"The [El Niño] impact typically becomes visible **nine to twelve months after the onset of weather stress**."* — Malaysian Palm Oil Council (MPOC), Jun 22, 2026

This is the single most important framing for PTPS / South Sumatra yield analysis:

| Stress Window | Visible Yield Impact |
|---|---|
| El Niño dry stress **begins May–Jun 2026** | FFB yield damage surfaces **Feb–May 2027** |
| Peak dry **Jul–Sep 2026** (worst soil moisture) | Max yield damage surfaces **Apr–Jul 2027** |
| El Niño dissipates **early 2027** | Recovery begins **late 2027 → 2028** |

**Implication**:
- **Q2 2026 itself** carries only a modest *direct* yield drag (early water stress, fruit abortion beginning). The bigger Q2 risk is via **price/mix** and biological-slowdown dynamics.
- **Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep)** will be the worst quarter for *in-season* yield drag — peak dry, fire risk, acute water stress.
- **Q1–Q2 2027** is the **lagged yield cliff** — when the 2026 dry season's damage actually flows through to FFB tonnage. This is the window where concentrated South Sumatra exposure becomes most painful.

From [[South_Sumatra_Q1_2026_Analysis]]:

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | Change |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| TBS/FFB Harvested | 6,026 t | 3,040 t | **−49.6%** |
| Revenue | Rp 19.6B | Rp 10.1B | **−48.3%** |

**Important clarification**: The Q1 drop (Jan–Mar 2026) occurred **before** the BMKG-forecast May dry-season onset. It is therefore **not** an El Niño effect — most likely causes are:
1. **Biological post-peak slowdown** after the strong Oct-2025→Mar-2026 output cycle (the same dynamic MPOC cited for Malaysia's −6.9% MoM May print)
2. Lingering 2025 weather/operational effects
3. Company-specific factors at PT Pulau Subur's 1,090 ha estate

**Q2 2026 is the first quarter where El Niño stress is actually in the soil** for South Sumatra — making it a transition quarter: the tail of the biological slowdown meets the onset of dry stress, with no recovery window in between (especially given ZOM 125's earlier-than-normal peak).

## 9. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Did Q2 2026 weather stress hit South Sumatra?** | Yes — dry season began May per BMKG forecast, building through June. ZOM 125 (PTPS) flagged for earlier-than-normal peak. |
| **Was Q2 the worst quarter for PTPS yield?** | **No.** Q2 was the onset. The in-season worst is **Q3 2026** (peak dry); the lagged yield cliff is **Q1–Q2 2027**. |
| **Is PTPS particularly exposed?** | **Yes — most exposed in the cohort.** Single-region (Palembang, 1,090 ha), no diversification cushion. The shield that protected LSIP/SIMP/STAA in Q1 does not exist for PTPS. |
| **Was the Q1 −49.6% drop El Niño?** | **No** — Q1 predates the May dry-season onset. Most likely biological post-peak slowdown + company-specific factors. |
| **Should the El Niño yield hit be in PTPS thesis models?** | **Yes — for H1 2027**, not for Q2/Q3 2026 reported numbers. Position sizing should discount a material H1 2027 FFB decline on top of the already-weak Q1 2026 base. |

**Net**: Q2 2026 is a **transition quarter** — the tail of the biological slowdown meets the onset of El Niño dry stress, with ZOM 125's earlier peak compressing any recovery window for PTPS. The real yield cliff is a 2027 story. Pair this with the B50/DSI export-channel margin compression flagged in [[CPO]] (§5, §7) and PTPS sits at the intersection of two structural headwinds: lagged yield damage (H1 2027) and state capture of the export margin (from Jan 1, 2027). The weak-Rupiah USD-revenue tailwind partially offsets, but does not neutralize, the concentration risk.
