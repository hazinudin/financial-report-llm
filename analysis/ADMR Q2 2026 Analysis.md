# PT Alamtri Minerals Indonesia Tbk (ADMR) — Q2 2026
---

*All figures are in thousands of US Dollars unless otherwise stated. This analysis is based on the unaudited interim consolidated financial statements for the six months ended 30 June 2026, with comparative prior-year income statement and cash flow data for the six months ended 30 June 2025 and balance sheet data as at 31 December 2025.*

## 1. Executive Summary

ADMR’s H1 2026 results capture a company at a capital-allocation inflection point: the legacy coal mining engine is generating record gross profitability, while the 65%-owned KAI aluminium smelter pivot is structurally altering the balance sheet, cost base, and cash flow profile. Revenue rose 31.5% year-over-year to $583.9m, gross profit surged 54.2% to $277.8m, and gross margin expanded 705 bps to 47.6%. However, net profit growth of only 17.0%—and net margin compression to 27.8%—reveals that the aluminium ramp-up is already diluting bottom-line quality. Interest expense exploded to $27.1m from $0.6m in H1 2025, with internal notes attributing 96.9% of total finance cost ($26.3m) to a newly recognised $690.7m lease liability with related-party power provider PT Kaltara Power Indonesia (KPI) for KAI’s smelter power supply. This is not a temporary accounting timing shift; it is a persistent, related-party structural cost embedded in the aluminium strategy.

The balance sheet ballooned 43% to $4.13bn. Non-current assets reached $2.92bn, driven by KAI construction-in-progress and a massive $664.4m non-cash fixed-asset addition via lease liabilities. Inventories nearly quintupled to $478.2m, reflecting inaugural KAI raw-material positions ($319.9m aluminium inventory plus $33.8m raw materials) alongside coal stockpiles. Operating cash flow turned deeply negative at -$173.7m (from +$130.3m in H1 2025) as payments to suppliers ($633.4m) outstripped customer collections ($613.1m) and inventory absorbed cash. Free cash flow was -$197.6m. Leverage effectively doubled: total liabilities/equity reached 1.31x and net debt/equity hit 0.75x, up from 0.23x at the end of Q1 2026. With no market data provided, valuation multiples cannot be computed.

We maintain a **HOLD (Development Risk)** rating. The investment case rests on KAI’s commissioning trajectory, but the smelter is consuming the majority of group assets (63%) and liabilities, generating pre-commissioning losses (-$45.5m in the metal-processing segment), and concentrating counterparty risk through related-party power and offtake arrangements. Coal earnings provide a near-term buffer, but regulatory uncertainty around the post-December 2026 export-sovereignity rule (PP 24/2026) and DHE SDA forex controls adds policy overhang.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet does not disclose production volumes or sales tonnage. Consequently, a volume-versus-price decomposition cannot be performed. The company’s revenue recognition is concentrated in coal sales; the metal-processing segment reported zero external sales in H1 2026 despite KAI’s first aluminium exports of 35,063 MT to the US and South Korea in June 2026, suggesting revenue recognition was either post-period or classified as trial/tolling. The price environment for coal is not externally verified—no benchmark Newcastle or HBA realisations are disclosed. FX tailwinds/headwinds are indeterminate; however, the group discloses material CNY-denominated payables and a net monetary liability position of $(38.1)m at 30 June 2026 (vs $(1.8)m at 31 December 2025), indicating increased FX sensitivity.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration remains acute. AIS (related party) accounted for $208.7m (35.7% of revenue), and PT Kinxiang New Energy contributed $77.4m (13.3%). The top two customers therefore represented 49.0% of consolidated revenue. Supplier concentration is equally concentrated in related parties: PT Sapta Indra Sejati (SIS) and PT Maritim Barito Perkasa (MBP) accounted for $64.6m and $63.4m of cost-of-revenue purchases, respectively.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Sales commitments (offtake contracts) more than doubled to $3,566m for 2026 deliveries versus $1,600m at the prior year-end, providing headline coverage. However, the composition—coal versus aluminium—is not split, and the metal-processing segment has yet to record external revenue. Forward sustainability depends on:
- **Coal benchmark realisations**: No ASP or volume guidance is disclosed.
- **Regulatory overhang**: PP 24/2026 and Permendag 15/2026 mandate that after 31 December 2026 coal exports must be channelled through a state-owned export entity (BUMN Ekspor). Management states it cannot yet determine the impact on operations or contracts extending beyond that date.
- **DHE SDA**: Export-proceed placement rules (GR 21/2026 effective 1 June 2026) require a certain percentage to be placed in the domestic financial system for a minimum period. The group holds DHE SDA in a Special Account presented as cash and cash equivalents.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

The mining segment generated $581.8m in external sales and $209.5m in net profit on $1,487m of assets—an annualised segment ROA of roughly 28%. MC and LC are the producing mines; fair value of their fixed assets was appraised at $543.0m versus carrying value $516.5m as at 31 December 2025 (Level 3, KJPP reports). Royalties payable to the government stood at $33.3m, subject to audit by the Directorate General of Mineral and Coal. The segment remains the sole cash and profit generator.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

The metal-processing segment holds $2.62bn of group assets but reported a net loss of $45.5m (versus $4.6m in H1 2025) and zero external revenue. KAI has not declared a commercial operations date (COD). Note 13 indicates certain KAI fixed assets are pledged for bank loans, and the group capitalised $32.5m of borrowing costs in H1 2026. Whether interest capitalisation continues into H2 depends on IAS 23 criteria and commissioning milestones that are not disclosed.

### 7.4 Growth Options

KAI Phase 1 targets 500,000 tonnes/year of aluminium capacity, ramping to 1.5m tonnes. First exports of 35,063 MT to the US and Korea shipped in June 2026. The segment’s revenue recognition, cost curve, and EBITDA breakeven are the critical unknowns for FY2027-2028. No reserves or resources are disclosed for the aluminium business.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Internal sales between segments were $6.8m (other services). Related-party transactions dominate the working capital and cost structure:
- **Revenue:** AIS ($208.7m sales, $56.5m receivables).
- **Cost of Revenue:** SIS ($64.6m), MBP ($63.4m).
- **Accrued Expenses:** MBP ($17.2m), SIS ($14.7m).
- **Lease Liabilities:** KPI ($690.7m, 29% of total group liabilities).
- **Finance Cost:** KPI ($26.3m, 97% of total).

The KPI lease is the most material related-party item: it is non-arm’s-length, long-dated (7 years), and underpins KAI’s entire power supply. Any disruption or renegotiation would directly impair smelter viability.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Structural interest/finance cost surge:** The KPI lease added $26.3m in semi-annual lease interest. This is recurring and will escalate as KAI ramps.
- **HIGH — Negative operating free cash flow:** OCF of -$173.7m and FCF of -$197.6m imply continued dependence on external financing.
- **MODERATE — Tax volatility:** The $56.2m tax charge and negative ETR convention mask underlying tax-planning uncertainty; tax losses carried forward ($23.9m) have no deferred tax asset recognition.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — KAI execution risk:** No COD disclosed; the segment lost $45.5m in H1 with zero external revenue. The $2.59bn asset base is 63% of group assets.
- **HIGH — Related-party concentration:** AIS (36.6% of revenue), SIS/MBP (~42% of disclosed COGS purchases), and KPI (97% of finance cost) create governance and pricing-risk concentrations.
- **MODERATE — NCI leakage:** KAI’s 35% NCI will absorb a material share of future aluminium profits, reducing attributable EPS.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **MODERATE — Export channel uncertainty:** PP 24/2026 mandates BUMN Ekspor intermediation for coal exports after 31 December 2026; ADMR cannot quantify the impact.
- **MODERATE — DHE SDA forex controls:** GR 21/2026 and prior amendments lock export proceeds into domestic financial institutions, potentially constraining offshore liquidity.
- **LOW — Pillar Two global minimum tax:** Group ETR is above 15%, so no top-up tax is required for H1 2026, but future aluminium losses could alter this.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- **Not assessable:** Absent market data, no P/E, EV/EBITDA, or dividend yield benchmark can be calculated. The stock’s implicit valuation rests entirely on KAI commissioning narratives that are not yet reflected in segment revenue.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 6/10

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 7/10
Coal mining is performing well—revenue up 31.5%, collections strong, and segment net profit up 45.4%. However, the absence of volume disclosures, the lack of KAI revenue recognition, and the reliance on related-party logistics and power supply cap the score.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 7/10
KAI offers genuine strategic optionality (500kt→1.5Mt aluminium capacity, inaugural exports to OECD markets). The reserve/runway is long if execution succeeds. But the project is late-stage, capital-intensive, and pre-revenue, with no COD disclosed.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 5/10

### 13.5 Risk Profile
**Moderate-to-High.** The risk vector is asymmetrical: downside is protected somewhat by coal cash flows, but KAI execution, related-party concentration, and Indonesian regulatory shifts (export SOE rule, DHE SDA) present material tail risks. Interest coverage is not disclosed, but the KPI lease interest alone consumes 12% of H1 coal gross profit.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
Coal gross margins remain elevated, funding KAI’s Phase 1 ramp without dilutive equity. Aluminium exports to the US and Korea command premium pricing amid geopolitical supply concerns ([[Strait_of_Hormuz_Crisis]], [[Aluminium faces 'black swan' supply shock, Mercuria says]]). KAI reaches COD in late 2026, flipping the metal-processing segment from a $45m semi-annual drag to a profit contributor by 2027. Attributable EPS re-rates on vertical-integration scarcity value.

### 14.2 Bear Case
KAI commissioning slips into 2027, extending the pre-revenue cash burn. The KPI lease and syndicated debt service consume all coal free cash flow, forcing dividend cuts or additional borrowing. Regulatory implementation of BUMN Ekspor after December 2026 disrupts coal contract economics and receivables timelines. Related-party governance discounts widen, compressing the multiple even if aluminium production starts.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. KAI commercial operations date (COD) announcement and first revenue recognition.
2. KAI Phase 1 ramp to 500kt run-rate and unit-cost disclosure.
3. Clarity on PP 24/2026 BUMN Ekspor implementation rules for 2027 coal exports.
4. Quarterly OCF inflection back to positive as KAI inventory converts to sales.
5. Any restructuring of KPI lease terms or interest-rate hedging.

### 14.4 Recommendation
**HOLD (Development Risk).** ADMR is uninvestable on a purely quantitative valuation basis because market data is not disclosed. Fundamentally, the stock is a coal-cash cow funding a high-risk aluminium start-up. Existing shareholders should await KAI COD and a sustained return to positive operating free cash flow before adding exposure.
