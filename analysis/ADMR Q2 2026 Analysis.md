# PT Alamtri Minerals Indonesia Tbk (ADMR) — Q2 2026
---

> **Reporting basis.** All figures in **thousands of US Dollars (US$'000)** unless otherwise stated, from the **unaudited/unreviewed consolidated interim financial statements for the six months ended 30 June 2026** (the "Q2 2026" pipeline label denotes the 1H2026 reporting period; income-statement and cash-flow comparatives are 1H2025, balance-sheet comparatives are 31 December 2025). Statements authorised by Board resolution 26 August 2026. Valuation figures are **not provided** — no market data stub was supplied for this report, so market-based multiples are marked "not available" rather than estimated. Peer multiples could not be pulled from the pipeline DB (read lock held by another process at drafting time) and are therefore omitted rather than guessed.

## 1. Executive Summary

ADMR's 1H2026 is a tale of one business printing cash and another consuming it. Revenue rose **31.5% YoY to US$583.9m** (from US$443.9m in 1H2025), essentially all of it metallurgical coal (mining segment external sales US$581.8m), on a firm hard-coking-coal price tape — HCC averaged ~US$243/t in June 2026, ~+39% YoY, while ADMR's 1Q26 realised ASP was US$183/t (+17% YoY) against a ~US$232/t SGX Australian benchmark (per company update, 2026-05-21). Gross profit surged **54.2% to US$277.8m** as costs lagged prices, lifting gross margin **~696 bps to 47.6%** from 40.6%. Net profit grew a more modest **+17.0% to US$162.6m** (EPS US$0.0043, +26.5% on the per-share line), because everything below gross profit deteriorated: interest expense exploded from US$0.6m to **US$27.1m**, the metal-processing segment (KAI) lost **US$45.5m** with zero revenue, and the tax charge rose **64% to US$56.2m** (effective rate stepping up from ~19.8% to ~25.7% — note the fact-sheet's −25.7% is a sign-convention artifact, see §3.4). Net margin therefore **fell ~345 bps to 27.8%** from 31.3% — a pipeline flag to the contrary is a data error, corrected here.

The balance sheet has been transformed in six months by the Kalimantan aluminium smelter. Total assets grew **+42.9% to US$4.13bn**; inventories rose **+436% to US$478.2m** (≈US$320m aluminium finished/WIP goods plus ~US$34m aluminium raw materials awaiting monetisation in 2H2026); gross bank debt roughly doubled to **US$1.85bn** on KAI syndicated-facility drawdowns (Tranche A now fully drawn at US$981.4m) and a new US$100m OCBC working-capital line; and a **US$691.6m lease liability** appeared, dominated by a US$690.7m related-party lease with PT Kaltara Power Indonesia (KPI) tied to the smelter's power arrangements. Interest-bearing-liabilities-to-equity now stands at **1.03x** and total D/E at **1.31x** (vs 0.68x loans/equity at FY25-end). Operating cash flow swung to **−US$173.7m** (from +US$130.3m), wholly explained by the inventory build, and free cash flow was **−US$197.6m**. Cash nonetheless rose to US$491.5m — because the build is **debt- and lease-funded**, not internally generated.

The investment case is now a race: does KAI's aluminium revenue arrive before the coal cycle and the carry cost of ~US$1.85bn of gross debt erode the earnings base? First cargoes shipped June 2026 (35,063 MT to the US and South Korea per [[forecast/ADMR_Q2_2026_Earnings_Forecast]]; per external reporting, 31,494t to the US and 3,569t to Korea), ~60,675t is hedged for July–September 2026 via a DBS commodity forward (fair value US$27.0m in OCI), and the FY2026 sales target is 350kt against a 500ktpa Phase-1 nameplate. Post-period coal prices remain supportive (Queensland PLV ~US$265/t by end-August 2026, a two-year high; ICI-3 +37% YTD at US$85.7/t — Wood Mackenzie fortnight report, 2026-08-31; Metal.com, 2026-09-01). With no market data provided, we cannot anchor a valuation multiple; on fundamentals — annualised ROE of 37.1%, a clean-up coal engine funding a giant unproven second leg — the risk/reward is balanced but asymmetric to the downside if KAI's ramp slips. **HOLD (Development Risk).**

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The statements disclose **no production or sales volumes for the half** (production and reserves tables in the fact sheet are empty), so the volume/price split cannot be computed from the filings. Externally, the +31.5% is characterised as **price-led**: the coking-coal benchmark backdrop (HCC ~US$243/t in June 2026, +~39% YoY; brokers raised assumptions to ~US$230/220/200 per tonne for 2026/27/28) accounts for the bulk of the uplift, with volumes broadly stable (per Indo Premier and Ajaib research summaries, 2026-05). The geographic revenue table (below) shows the mix shifting: Japan re-entered at US$30.8m (nil in 1H2025), India collapsed to US$3.5m from US$23.2m, China went to zero from US$8.5m, and Singapore remained the largest external destination at US$278.7m. These geographic labels are likely trader/transhipment destinations rather than end-use; Singapore has historically been the routing hub for the Enviromet brand's Asian customers.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is the headline structural feature. Per the customer note: **AIS (a related party) purchased US$208.7m — 35.7% of consolidated revenue** (Note 33 cites 36.58% of mining revenue) — and **PT Kinxiang New Energy US$77.4m (13.3%)**. Two customers therefore account for ~49% of the topline. AIS is also the group's marketing agent for Maruwai Coal and Lahai Coal coal sales, sits on the receivable ledger at US$56.5m (down from US$95.7m at FY25-end), and booked a US$3.8m selling-expense/commission relationship — a single related party spanning sales, receivables and commissions. On the supply side, related parties **SIS (US$64.6m) and MBP (US$63.4m)** together represent ~42% of disclosed cost of revenue.

Segment-wise, mining carried essentially all revenue (99.6%); the metal-processing (KAI) segment recognised **zero revenue** despite first cargoes shipping in June — revenue recognition lagged shipment; and other services contributed a de minimis US$2.1m externally (plus US$6.8m intersegment).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The forward picture is two-legged. **Coal:** the post-period tape is firm — Queensland PLV FOB reached a two-year high ~US$265/t by end-August 2026 on Chinese restocking, and Indonesian ICI-3 rose 37% YTD to US$85.7/t as of 28 August 2026 (Wood Mackenzie Fortnight Flash, 2026-08-31; Metal.com, 2026-09-01). Offsetting risks: El Niño-driven low Barito River water levels threatening ~3.9mt/month of Kalimantan barging capacity with force majeure already declared by some shippers, and MoEMR Decree 26.K/MB.01/MEM.B/2025 tightening HBA benchmark discipline (benchmark price as a lower sales limit for royalty/tax purposes). **Aluminium:** 350kt FY2026 sales target (raised from 300kt), 60,675t hedged for Jul–Sep 2026, ~72 potlines running, full 500ktpa ramp targeted by end-2026 (Bloomberg Technoz, 2026-06-06; Bisnis, 2026-07-13 — external). **Regulatory:** PP 24/2026 (effective 1 June 2026) mandates that from 1 January 2027 coal exports flow only via the state Export SOE; the company states it **cannot yet determine the impact** on export contracts beyond that date — a direct threat to the Singapore/Singapore-hubbed export mix that produced ~58% of 1H2026 revenue. DHE SDA rules (GR 36/2023 as amended by GR 8/2025 and GR 21/2026) continue to trap export proceeds in special accounts (presented as cash but with placement constraints). Reserve life/runway is **not disclosed** in the processed statements — mining properties of US$176.6m (mines in production US$150.7m) carry no reserve table in this filing.

The margin expansion is a price/cost scissor: revenue +31.5% against COGS +16.0%. Costs include sharply higher royalties (externally reported ~+50% to US$39.5m — external context) and, notably, **US$26.1m of depreciation newly allocated to inventories** (vs nil in 1H2025; total depreciation US$45.4m vs US$14.3m) as KAI's asset base entered service and smelter costs began flowing into inventory rather than directly to COGS. That accounting routing means KAI's cost absorption is currently **hidden in the balance sheet** (inventory) rather than the P&L — gross margin overstates the steady-state economics of a ramping smelter.

The interest-expense explosion has two drivers: (i) **capitalisation stop** — as KAI moved from construction to commissioning, borrowing costs previously capitalised (US$32.5m capitalised in 1H2026 vs US$38.5m in 1H2025, still partially capitalised on qualifying assets) began hitting the P&L; and (ii) the **KPI lease**: lease interest expense jumped to US$26.3m from US$0.06m, and KPI alone accounts for US$26.3m or **96.9% of total finance cost** — the 1GW power arrangement is now the single biggest below-the-line item in the group. **Inventories/DIO 74.7 days** — this is the story. Composition per the notes: aluminium finished/WIP **US$319.9m**, aluminium raw materials **US$33.8m**, coal stockpile ~US$86m, fuel/lubes ~US$38.9m. **Prepaid/other:** prepaid taxes US$35.4m (+115%, attribution between income tax/VAT not disclosed — open item); advances and prepayments fell to US$9.6m; prepaid loan transaction costs went to zero. Other receivables from related parties rose to US$26.7m from US$7.6m (KPI US$23.6m). DPO is only 9.95 days — the group pays suppliers (heavily related-party: SIS/MBP) faster than it collects, contributing to a cash conversion cycle of **84.5 days**.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Cash-flow inversion.** OCF −US$173.7m, FCF −US$197.6m; cash growth is 100% financing-driven. If aluminium monetisation slips a quarter, the funding gap compounds.
- **HIGH — El Niño barge constraint.** Low Barito River levels threaten Kalimantan coal shipments (~3.9mt/month industry barging at risk; force majeure already declared by some shippers — external, Wood Mackenzie 2026-08-31), potentially hitting 3Q2026 volumes and logistics cost precisely when the coal tape is strong.
- **MODERATE — 2H aluminium margin uncertainty.** KAI's realised premium to LME is unknown; the ~US$26m/half KPI lease interest becomes a fixed cost against unproven ingot margins.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — KAI execution.** A first-time aluminium producer with zero recognised revenue, US$45.5m half-yearly losses, no disclosed COD date, and ramp-to-500ktpa still ahead. Any commissioning or potline issue is unhedged beyond 60,675t.
- **HIGH — Leverage step-change.** Net debt/equity 0.18x → 0.75x in six months; lease maturities of US$620.6m fall due within 1–5 years; Tranche A fully drawn leaves limited syndicated headroom.
- **MODERATE — Inventory quality.** US$478.2m (DIO 74.7 days) is 39% of current assets in unsold metal and materials; a 10% aluminium price fall on US$320m of inventory is a ~US$32m hit, more than twice the entire derivative gain.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **HIGH — PP 24/2026 Export SOE mandate.** From 1 January 2027, coal exports only via BUMN Ekspor; company "cannot yet determine" the impact on contracts beyond that date. Singapore-destined exports (~48% of 1H2026 revenue) are the exposure.
- **MODERATE — Related-party ecosystem.** AIS 35.7% of revenue; SIS/MBP ~42% of cost of revenue; KPI 29% of total liabilities and 96.9% of finance cost; parent/affiliate facilities and guarantees. Pricing on every side of the P&L is intra-group.
- **MODERATE — DHE SDA / FX.** Export proceeds trapped in special accounts (GR 21/2026); net monetary liabilities grew to US$38.1m; a 5% IDR move swings post-tax profit ±~US$1.4–1.6m.
- **LOW — Pillar Two / HBA discipline.** ETR above 15% GloBE floor for now; MoEMR Decree 26/2025 tightens benchmark-price floors for royalty/tax.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- No market data provided — we cannot verify whether the market has already priced the smelter's 2H2026 contribution. The [[forecast/ADMR_Q2_2026_Earnings_Forecast]] missed Q2 cash conversion badly; consensus models carrying positive Q2 OCF will be revised down even as headline EPS beats — a recipe for multiple compression on "low-quality beat" optics.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
2H2026 delivers the double catalyst: 60,675t+ of hedged aluminium converts the US$320m inventory into revenue at ~US$3,000/t-plus realisation (external assumptions), coal holds at PLV ~US$265/t, and KAI flips from a US$45m drag to a positive segment, restoring net margin to the low-30s with a structural second leg. The US$3.57bn delivery commitment book de-risks volume, the DBS hedge de-risks price, and ADMR rerates from coal-cyclical to integrated energy-metals compounder — the exact re-rating path sketched in [[ADMR Q1 2026 Analysis]]'s bull case.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Aluminium ramp slips (no disclosed COD, first-time producer), the inventory devalues in a falling LME tape, El Niño chokes Q3 coal barge shipments, and PP 24/2026 forces Singapore-hubbed exports through a state SOE channel from 2027 at unquantified margin cost. Interest expense runs to US$50–55m annualised against shrinking cash generation, the dividend is cut, and net debt/equity breaches 1.0x — the equity becomes a leveraged call option on two commodities with a fixed related-party power bill.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. **First aluminium revenue recognition** in 2H2026 statements — the single most important disclosure event; watch whether June cargoes were recognised in Q3 and at what realised ASP/premium.
2. **2H2026 OCF sign flip** as the US$320m inventory monetises (350kt FY target).
3. **Coking-coal tape durability** (Queensland PLV ~US$265/t as of end-August 2026; broker deck at US$230/220/220 for 26/27/28).
4. **PP 24/2026 implementation detail** — export-SOE channel mechanics and margin impact before 1 January 2027.
5. **KPI lease terms disclosure** (CALK/notes) — indexation, tenor, purchase options on the US$690.7m arrangement.
6. Barito River water levels through Q4 2026 (El Niño logistics).

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Development Risk).** No target price is given because no market data was provided for this report and peer multiples were unavailable — an explicit limitation, not evasion. The fundamental setup is a strong coal engine at cyclical highs funding a genuinely on-time, genuinely large smelter build that has not yet produced a dollar of revenue or a day of positive group cash flow. The margin structure has permanently changed (interest + lease carry, higher ETR), and the balance sheet has doubled its leverage in six months. Hold the position; add only on evidence of aluminium revenue recognition with disclosed realised premiums and a positive 2H OCF print — and reduce if Q3 2026 shows KAI still pre-revenue with OCF still negative. **Review trigger: the 3Q2026 report (expected ~November 2026), specifically KAI segment revenue and the operating cash flow line.**
