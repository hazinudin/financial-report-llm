# PT Timah Tbk (TINS) — Q1 2026
---

*Reporting currency: Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Units: millions, except per-share figures which are stated in full Rupiah. The interim financial statements are unaudited for the period ended 31 March 2026. The income statement and cash flow comparative refer to the three months ended 31 March 2025; the balance sheet comparative refers to 31 December 2025.*

## 1. Executive Summary
TINS delivered an explosive start to 2026, posting revenue of IDR 5,465,058 million against IDR 2,097,892 million in the prior-year quarter, a pre-computed expansion of 160.5%. The tin mining segment, which recorded external sales of IDR 4,980,674 million, drove the entire uplift. Net profit surged to IDR 1,500,750 million from IDR 116,861 million, reflecting pre-computed net profit growth of 1,184.2%, while basic EPS jumped from IDR 16 to IDR 202. The result is not merely a revenue story: operating profit scaled to IDR 1,877,973 million from IDR 147,686 million—operating profit growth of 1,171.6%—as selling and general and administrative expenses were held near prior-year absolute levels despite the top-line explosion, pushing the pre-computed operating margin to 34.36% and net margin to 27.46%.

The balance sheet strengthened materially over the three months to March 2026. Cash increased to IDR 3,164,311 million from IDR 1,711,598 million at the prior fiscal year-end. The current ratio stands at 2.76x, the quick ratio at 1.88x, and the cash ratio at 1.05x, indicating exceptional short-term liquidity. Total equity grew by a pre-computed 18.4% to IDR 9,957,829 million, while total assets expanded 11.6% to IDR 15,229,203 million. Noncurrent loans dropped to IDR 522,916 million from IDR 805,255 million, and with cash far exceeding reported loans, the group is in a net-negative debt position on a cash-versus-loans basis, although the canonical net debt ratio is not disclosed because the securities line is null in the schema.

Operating cash flow generation was equally dramatic at IDR 1,949,037 million versus IDR 261,409 million in Q1 2025, a pre-computed increase of 645.6%, and the cash conversion ratio of 1.30x indicates that reported earnings are well backed by collections. Yet free cash flow is negative at IDR 517,531 million, a figure that diverges from the simple OCF-less-capex proxy because of unmapped financing and investing movements in the canonical definition. Capital expenditure collapsed to IDR 9,055 million for property, plant and equipment against full-year 2025 additions of IDR 296,864 million, signaling a maintenance phase or project completion.

The concentration profile is extreme and warrants caution. A single related party, MIND ID Trading Limited, contributed IDR 2,260,107 million of revenue and carried IDR 999,514 million in trade receivables. Context notes indicate related-party revenue surged from IDR 702,681 million previously, underscoring deepening dependence on a government-linked offtaker. Meanwhile, PP 19/2025 introduced progressive tin royalty rates of 3%–10% based on HMA per ton, effective 26 April 2025; the financial impact is not quantified in these statements and represents a margin wildcard from Q2 onward. With no market data stub provided, valuation multiples are unavailable, and the shares cannot be benchmarked against a target price. We therefore assign a preliminary HOLD pending disclosure of tradable valuation metrics.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Customer/offtaker concentration**: MIND ID Trading Limited is the only >10% revenue customer disclosed, with absolute revenue of IDR 2,260,107 million and receivables of IDR 999,514 million. Any disruption to this related-party offtake would immediately impair revenue and collections.
- **HIGH — Royalty escalation**: PP 19/2025 progressive tin royalty rates (3%–10% based on HMA per ton) took effect on 26 April 2025. The Q1 2026 financials do not reflect this regime; Q2 margins will face pressure, and the impact is unquantified.
- **MODERATE — Receivables credit risk**: A material trade receivable is concentrated with a single related party. While historical collection appears strong, the lack of arm’s-length diversification elevates credit risk.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **MODERATE — Segment imbalance**: Tin mining is the only profitable segment. Coal, construction, and other segments are loss-making. The group lacks a second earnings leg.
- **MODERATE — Related-party governance**: Revenue from related parties surged materially, and the majority of cash, payables, and receivables are with government-linked entities. Transfer-pricing scrutiny and independence concerns are elevated.
- **MODERATE — Asset impairment**: Subsidiary DAK recognized IDR 67,443 million in fixed-asset impairments. TINVES is in liquidation. These items signal non-core asset quality issues.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **MODERATE — Commodity exposure**: No tin price hedging or forward-sale disclosures are provided. Revenue is fully exposed to spot and contract price volatility.
- **MODERATE — Environmental provisions**: Reclamation provision (noncurrent) is IDR 297,227 million, and context notes indicate a current portion of IDR 144,494 million. Rising environmental liabilities could pressure future cash flows.
- **LOW — Tax reconciliation**: Context notes flag a discrepancy between prior-period income tax expense tables (IDR 45,185 million) and reconciliation (IDR 41,140 million), suggesting minor tax-line volatility.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- **LOW — Unrateable valuation**: With no market data, P/E, P/B, and EV/EBITDA are undefined. The earnings surge may already be priced in, or the market may be discounting the related-party concentration; without a price, neither can be tested.
