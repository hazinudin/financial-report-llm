[[MAPA Q1 2026 Analysis]]

# PT MAP Aktif Adiperkasa Tbk (MAPA) — Q2 2026
---

## 1. Executive Summary

MAPA delivered a robust top-line and bottom-line performance for the six months ended June 30, 2026, with revenue reaching Rp 10,122.8 billion (+15.1% YoY) and net profit climbing to Rp 864.5 billion (+32.0% YoY). Gross margin expanded to 47.25% per computed ratios, while net margin improved to 8.54% and pre-tax margin reached 11.07%. The growth was broad-based across the retail segment, which contributed 93.3% of external sales, and was supported by store network expansion and brand-mix effects. However, the headline H1 figures mask a sequential deceleration into the second quarter: implied Q2 revenue grew only 4.3% quarter-on-quarter, with gross profit margin compressing from the Q1 2026 level of 48.0% to an implied Q2 level near 46.5%, and net profit dropping from Rp 472.7 billion in Q1 to an implied Rp 391.8 billion in Q2. This front-loading suggests H1 strength was partly driven by carry-over demand and early-year clearance dynamics, raising questions about H2 momentum amid a deteriorating Indonesian retail macro environment.

The balance sheet grew 11.0% versus December 2025, driven by a 12.8% build in inventories to Rp 6,378.0 billion and an 8.7% increase in PPE. Liquidity remains adequate on the surface—current ratio at 2.12x and a net cash position per Note 37C (cash of Rp 1,160.4 billion exceeds total bank loans of Rp 538.4 billion)—but the 68.3% surge in current loans to Rp 1,355.9 billion signals a Q2 drawdown of revolving credit facilities after a Q1 position of zero bank-loan interest expense. The cash ratio compressed to 0.27x from 0.39x at March 2026, and time deposits collapsed from Rp 375.0 billion to Rp 103.3 billion, indicating cash deployment into working capital or prepayments. Inventory now represents 40.5% of total assets, and with DIO at 57.5 days, markdown risk is elevated if consumer discretionary spending continues to soften.

Valuation multiples cannot be assessed because no market data stub was provided for the period. On a book-value basis, BVPS stands at Rp 332.09 per computed ratios, but without a share price, P/E, P/B, and EV/EBITDA are not disclosed. The effective tax rate was an anomalous negative 22.84%, producing a tax credit of Rp 255.9 billion that flattered net profit; this is not expected to recur and is flagged as an internal must-explain item. NCI profit swung from a Rp 7.5 billion loss in H1 2025 to a Rp 136 million profit in H1 2026, with an implied Q2 spike to Rp 17.9 billion, introducing volatility at the minority level.

We maintain a **HOLD (Moderate Risk)** view. The business is operationally healthy, FCF-positive (Rp 530.9 billion), and backed by a deep undrawn credit facility pool (including DBS USD 40 million). However, the combination of sequential Q2 margin compression, macro retail contraction in Indonesia (-3.7% to -3.9% YoY in April–May 2026 per external data), inventory intensity, and the non-recurring tax credit argues for caution until H2 sell-through and operating profit disclosures confirm sustainability.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

Revenue growth of 15.1% was underpinned by retail segment expansion and new store contributions. Collections from customers totaled Rp 10,167.8 billion, yielding a collections ratio of 1.004x, indicating clean cash conversion of sales.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet does not disclose unit volume, same-store sales, or pricing splits. Segment data indicates that retail external sales (Penjualan eceran) contributed Rp 9,440,073 million (93.3% of external revenue), while non-retail external sales contributed Rp 682,683 million (6.7%). Internal sales within the non-retail segment were Rp 342,746 million. No production or procurement volume data is disclosed.

External research indicates that MAP Group (the parent ecosystem) targeted 550–600 new stores in 2026 and pursued global brand acquisitions, supporting volume-driven revenue growth. Conversely, macro data from Indonesia Investments and Trading Economics shows Indonesian retail sales contracted by 3.7% in April 2026 and 3.9% in May 2026 YoY—the steepest slump since May 2023—suggesting MAPA outperformed a weak pricing and demand environment through brand strength and premium positioning rather than broad pricing power.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

No single customer exceeded 10% of net revenues in H1 2026 or H1 2025 (Note 24). No single merchandise supplier exceeded 10% of total revenue (Note 25). Geographically, Indonesia accounted for Rp 7,711,678 million (76.2% of net revenue) and held Rp 4,608,871 million of non-current assets (excluding deferred tax) out of a group total of Rp 6,509,438 million (Note 34). The remainder is spread across Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines, and Cambodia.

Segment composition is heavily retail-centric, with non-retail (wholesale/distribution) acting as a small adjunct. The customer list in the canonical fact sheet is empty, so granular customer-mix or concentration metrics beyond the >10% threshold are not disclosed.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The H1 revenue run-rate implies full-year revenue above Rp 20 trillion if momentum holds, but the implied Q2 sequential growth of only 4.3% (versus Q1 2026 base of Rp 4,954.7 billion) signals deceleration. External drivers to monitor include:
- **Indonesian retail demand**: BI rate hikes to 5.75% by June 2026 and fuel-price inflation are pressuring household discretionary spending (Bank Indonesia, June 2026).
- **Inventory sell-through**: With DIO at 57.5 days and inventories at 40.5% of assets, H2 clearance pricing could compress gross margin if demand softens further.
- **New store ramp**: The 550–600 store expansion plan (Katadata, IDNFinancials) will add fixed costs; revenue per new store and cannibalization rates are not disclosed.

Reserve or license runway data is not disclosed.

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

Trade receivables declined 13.5% to Rp 329.7 billion, with DSO at 3.0 days (annualized), reflecting strong cash collections. The loss allowance on trade receivables fell marginally to Rp 8,457 million from Rp 8,700 million, while the ECL rate on >120 days past due dropped sharply from 92% to 59% (Note 6). Inventories surged 12.8% to Rp 6,378.0 billion, representing 40.5% of total assets. DIO is 57.5 days (annualized), but this is an H1 average; the Q1 2026 profile cited DIO of 109.8 days, suggesting Q2 inventory turnover may not have improved proportionally. Inventories are insured at an equivalent of Rp 6,359.4 billion (Note 8). Prepaid taxes jumped 55.0% to Rp 218.1 billion, with the bulk added in Q1 (Rp 181.9 billion) and only Rp 36.2 billion in Q2, indicating front-loaded estimated tax payments.

Trade payables eased 4.4% to Rp 1,455.9 billion; DPO is 13.1 days. Accrued current liabilities fell 9.3% to Rp 655.5 billion.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

The retail segment (Penjualan eceran) is the crown jewel, generating Rp 9,440.1 billion in external sales (93.3% of total) and holding Rp 13,721.5 billion in segment assets (87.2% of total). The brand portfolio spans performance (New Balance, Adidas, Hoka, Asics), lifestyle (Birkenstock, Dr. Martens, Steve Madden, Converse), and mass (Skechers, Aldo). License and distribution rights are concentrated in indirect subsidiary PSIPH (Philippines) for Hoka, Nike, and Adidas, and in AAA for Dr. Martens. No impairment was indicated on these intangibles as of June 2026. Customer concentration is low, and no quantifiable reserve or license expiration schedule is disclosed.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

- **ASR (50% owned):** A new retail JV established in 2026 with paid-in capital of Rp 87,500 million; carrying amount Rp 87,769 million. Strategic intent is unclear.
- **MAIN (India):** Assets of Rp 238.6 billion but no disclosed activity; established in 2025 as part of AIH expansion.
- **Idle / no-activity entities:** PAR, MGR, SDWA, SDIA, MGP carry minimal or no disclosed operations but hold combined assets of Rp 159.7 billion.
- **Payless (PAL):** Assets of Rp 353.1 billion; ownership not disclosed. Status of the Payless brand post-acquisition is not quantified.

### 7.4 Growth Options

Geographic expansion outside Indonesia is the primary growth vector. Vietnam (MMA) and Thailand (MAA (T)) are the largest international asset bases. The July 1, 2026 acquisition of Sports Direct Malaysia Sdn Bhd by AIH for USD 148.9 million (paid advance USD 29.6 million on June 30) adds a new Southeast Asia retail platform. The MAP Group’s 550–600 new store target for 2026 (Katadata; IDNFinancials) supports volume growth, but per-store economics and cannibalization are undisclosed.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Non-retail internal sales were Rp 342,746 million. Related-party trade receivables totaled Rp 3,542 million (down from Rp 4,892 million at December 2025), and other receivables from related parties were Rp 5,443 million (down from Rp 9,596 million). Related-party other payables fell sharply to Rp 19,154 million from Rp 44,644 million, driven by lower payables to MAP (Rp 17,690 million vs Rp 36,908 million). Purchases from MAP were Rp 4,177 million (0.04% of revenue). Non-retail sales to related parties (primarily PT Panen Lestari Indonesia) were Rp 22,528 million (3.46% of non-retail sales). Intercompany eliminations are not detailed beyond the segment internal sales line.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths
- **Net cash position**: Cash of Rp 1,160.4 billion exceeds drawn bank loans of Rp 538.4 billion (Note 37C).
- **Strong liquidity metrics**: Current ratio of 2.12x and working capital of Rp 4,848.7 billion provide short-term resilience.
- **Positive FCF**: H1 FCF of Rp 530.9 billion supports capex and dividends without forced external funding.
- **Low leverage**: Loans-to-equity of 0.26x and D/E of 0.62x are conservative for a retailer.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
- **Multi-brand, multi-geography portfolio**: Exposure to Indonesia (76% of revenue), Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, and the Philippines reduces single-market dependency.
- **Premium brand mix**: Hoka, Birkenstock, Dr. Martens, and New Balance command higher price points and customer loyalty, supporting gross margin.
- **Efficient collections**: DSO of 3.0 days and collections ratio of 1.004x indicate near-cash sales or rapid receivables turnover.
- **Scale economies**: Revenue growth of 15.1% with selling expense growth of only 13.3% and G&A growth of 7.4% demonstrates operating leverage.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths
- **Parent-group ecosystem**: MAP Group provides shared infrastructure, brand relationships, and management services.
- **Deep undrawn credit pool**: Over 20 facilities from global and local banks (DBS, HSBC, Standard Chartered, MUFG, BNI, etc.) provide contingent liquidity; only HSBC Singapore is drawn.
- **M&A optionality**: The Sports Direct Malaysia acquisition and India entity (MAIN) provide call options on regional consolidation.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Inventory markdown risk**: Inventories at Rp 6,378.0 billion (40.5% of assets) and DIO of 57.5 days leave the group exposed to obsolescence and discounting if H2 demand weakens. The Q2 Indonesian retail slump (-3.7% to -3.9% YoY) heightens this risk.
- **HIGH — Sequential margin compression**: Implied Q2 gross margin and net margin both compressed versus Q1, suggesting H1 profit was front-loaded. If the trend continues, H2 earnings will miss run-rate expectations.
- **MODERATE — Tax credit non-recurrence**: The negative 22.84% ETR added Rp 255.9 billion to net profit. Normalization to a statutory rate would reduce reported earnings by roughly 15–20%.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **MODERATE — License concentration**: PSIPH (Philippines) carries goodwill and license rights for Hoka, Nike, and Adidas; AAA carries Dr. Martens. Loss or non-renewal of any major license would impair segment value.
- **MODERATE — NCI volatility**: The swing in NCI profit from a Rp 7.5 billion loss to an implied Q2 profit of Rp 17.9 billion introduces unpredictability in profit attribution to owners.
- **MODERATE — Lease intensity**: ROU assets of Rp 2,403.8 billion and lease liabilities of Rp 2,007.7 billion represent a fixed-cost backbone that is difficult to flex in a downturn.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **MODERATE — Macro consumer weakness**: Indonesian retail sales contracted sharply in Q2 2026 amid BI rate hikes and fuel-price inflation (Bank Indonesia, May–June 2026). MAPA outperformed in H1 but may face mean reversion.
- **LOW — Interest rate and FX**: The drawn HSBC facility is SGD-based at 2.08%; BI rate hikes do not directly affect this cost. FX net liabilities in USD equivalent are disclosed but not quantified as a material risk (Note 37).
- **LOW — Tax dispute**: The FY2021 SKPKB of Rp 49.6 billion is under objection; exposure is small relative to H1 profit.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
No market data was provided, so directional valuation concerns cannot be assessed. On a book-value basis, the shares trade at an unknown multiple of Rp 332.09 BVPS. Until market data is available, valuation risk is unquantifiable.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
MAPA continues to outpace Indonesian retail through premium brand mix (Hoka, Birkenstock, Dr. Martens) and store expansion. Gross margin sustains above 47%, FCF remains positive, and the Sports Direct Malaysia acquisition opens a new revenue leg. The net cash balance sheet and undrawn DBS USD 40 million facility provide dry powder for further consolidation.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Indonesian consumer spending contracts further, forcing inventory clearance at depressed margins. The Q2 sequential compression is the start of a sustained downtrend, not a one-off. Tax credits reverse, operating profit is weaker than implied by gross margin, and NCI volatility erodes owner earnings. License non-renewal or brand fatigue amplifies downside.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
- Q3 2026 sell-through and inventory aging disclosure.
- Operating profit and segment margin disclosure in full-year reporting.
- Integration update on Sports Direct Malaysia (post-July 2026).
- New store opening cadence and same-store sales data.

### 14.4 Recommendation
**HOLD (Moderate Risk)**. MAPA is a well-capitalized, growing retailer with strong brands, but the H1 results were front-loaded, the tax credit is non-recurring, and the macro backdrop is deteriorating. Without market data, a target price range cannot be established. **Review trigger**: Q3 2026 interim results or disclosure of operating profit and inventory turnover by brand.
