# PT Bank Central Asia Tbk (BBCA) — Q1 2026
---

**Basis of preparation.** All company figures in this note are from the unaudited consolidated interim financial statements of PT Bank Central Asia Tbk for the three months ended 31 March 2026 (approved for issuance 23 April 2026), expressed in millions of Rupiah unless otherwise stated. Balance-sheet comparatives are the audited 31 December 2025 figures. Income-statement comparatives are the unaudited three months ended 31 March 2025. Computed ratios are used verbatim from the deterministic ratios block; where a ratio is null (banks do not disclose COGS, inventories, or current/non-current splits in the industrial convention), we state "not disclosed/not applicable" rather than estimating. No market-data stub was provided for this run, so no market-based valuation multiples (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, dividend yield) are computed in this note; where external research cites prices or broker targets, they are labelled as external context with their sources and dates, not as our own valuation.

**A critical convention note (read before using any margin in this note).** The canonical fact sheet models BBCA on an industrial template. Its "revenue" of IDR 30,335,632m is *total interest income plus other operating income* — i.e., a gross revenue concept that includes interest expense *above* the operating line rather than netting it out. This does **not** reconcile to BCA's own presentation of net revenue of IDR 27,981,990m (net interest income IDR 21,108,433m plus net non-interest income IDR 6,873,557m, per the company's 1Q26 presentation, 23 April 2026). Likewise, `other_income_net` of IDR 5,743,384m is a *residual* (revenue minus interest income), net of net insurance income of IDR 80,461m, and is **not** identical to the filed "jumlah pendapatan operasional lainnya" of IDR 6,793,096m. All "margins" below therefore follow the fact-sheet convention and are not comparable to bank-standard NIM or CIR figures quoted from the company presentation; where the presentation figures are cited they are labelled as external context. We also note that the fact sheet models no separate non-operating line, so pretax margin equals operating margin exactly (59.59%) — a modelling artefact, not evidence that nothing moved below the operating line.

## 1. Executive Summary

BBCA delivered Q1 2026 net profit of IDR 14,689,799m (+3.84% YoY from IDR 14,146,990m) on fact-sheet revenue of IDR 30,335,632m (+2.94% YoY), with EPS of IDR 119 versus IDR 115 (+3.48%). On the bank-standard presentation, net interest income was essentially flat (IDR ~21.1tn, -0.05% YoY per external research) while non-interest income rose 14.2% YoY to ~IDR 6.6tn and total operating expenses were virtually unchanged (+0.1%), producing pre-provision operating profit growth of ~4.8% to ~IDR 19.3tn and a cost-to-income ratio of 27.3% versus 28.5% — positive jaws of roughly 3 percentage points. Provision expense rose sharply (+19.5% YoY to IDR 1,232,275m per the filed statement), which is why PPOP growth of ~4.8% was cut down to net profit growth of +3.84%. The honest summary of the quarter: **an opex-and-fees quarter, not a spread quarter.**

The spread story was in fact negative. External research (Brights, 24 April 2026; Indo Premier, 24 April 2026) reports consolidated NIM fell ~40bp YoY to 5.4%, at the bottom of management's FY26 guidance of 5.4–5.6%, driven by asset yields falling ~45bp YoY (corporate loans, ~32% benchmark-linked) while cost of funds improved only ~10bp on a CASA surge to 85.2% of funding. Loan growth of 5.6% YoY badly undershot the 8–10% FY26 guidance, with consumer loans contracting 2.0% YoY — the first outright segment contraction — dragged by vehicle lending (-19.7% YoY). Asset quality was fine at the headline (gross NPL 1.8% vs 2.0% a year ago) but coverage thinned (NPL coverage 174.6% vs 183.8% at Dec-25) and the cost of credit at ~60bp ran above the 40–50bp management guidance.

The balance sheet grew but equity shrank, and for a benign reason. Total assets rose 3.4% from FY25 year-end to IDR 1,640,830,566m, driven by a massive re-deployment into interbank placements and reverse repos (placements with Bank Indonesia and other banks roughly tripled from IDR 9.8tn gross to IDR 27.3tn; securities purchased under resale agreements rose from IDR 5.3tn to IDR 21.6tn) as excess liquidity sat un-loaned. Total equity fell 7.93% to IDR 259,358,793m — fully explained by the FY2025 dividend accrual (IDR 41,304,508m total, IDR 336/share, ~72% payout) plus ~IDR 1.1tn of treasury stock accumulated under the Rp5tn buyback that commenced 12 March 2026 — a recurring seasonal pattern, not capital erosion. Consolidated CAR remains an extraordinary 27.52% (Note 49f), NSFR 159.9% and LCR 305.7% per external context.

The flagged anomaly — net profit attributable to non-controlling interests rising 561% — is a base-effect artefact: NCI profit grew from IDR 859m to IDR 5,676m, an absolute increment of ~IDR 4.8bn, or 0.04% of group net profit. Only two subsidiaries are less than wholly owned (BCA Sekuritas and Asuransi Jiwa BCA, both 90%); the most plausible mechanism (medium confidence) is the IFRS 17/PSAK 117 restatement lifting net insurance income from IDR 2,668m to IDR 80,461m at BCA Life — a restatement artefact, not a step-change in subsidiary earnings power. The parent's own EPS growth (+3.48%) is the cleaner read on core earnings.

No market-data stub was provided, so we do not compute P/E, P/B, or a target price. External context: the stock closed March 2026 at Rp6,450, down ~20% in the quarter to a two-year low and roughly -30% YTD by early July 2026 (recursivegains.com, 6 May 2026) — a de-rating on loan-growth deceleration and NIM compression, not on reported profit. Broker consensus remained constructive (Brights BUY, TP Rp10,900; Indo Premier BUY; Samuel BUY TP Rp8,600). Given the fact-sheet evidence — fortress capital, 48.4% net margin on the fact-sheet convention, +36% operating cash flow, fee-mix resilience — against real NIM and loan-growth headwinds, we frame this as **HOLD (medium risk)** pending a second-quarter confirmation that loan growth and CoC are tracking guidance; scoring and recommendation detail are in §13–14.

Revenue growth of +2.94% is modest for an Indonesian bank carrying an 8–10% loan-growth guidance. The composition matters more than the total: interest income grew under 1% YoY (NIM compression, flat NII per external context), while the residual other-income line grew +12.6%. Per the company presentation (23 April 2026, external context), fees and commissions rose +14.2% to ~IDR 5.5tn and trading income +13.3%; on the filed-statement basis, fees and commissions income net was IDR 5,061,371m vs IDR 4,658,813m (+8.65%) and FVTPL income IDR 1,048,919m vs IDR 912,191m (+15.0%).

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

**Volume.** Consolidated loans reached Rp994tn, +5.6% YoY but only +0.1% QoQ (external context, BCA press release 24 April 2026) — against FY26 guidance of 8–10%, implying a material second-half acceleration is required. Mix per external research: corporate Rp483.8tn (+9.1% YoY, 48.7% of book), commercial Rp145.2tn (+5.7%), SME Rp131.1tn (+5.4%), consumer Rp221.4tn (-2.0% YoY — the first outright segment contraction, dragged by vehicle lending at Rp53.9tn, -19.7% YoY, -4.8% QoQ). Fact-sheet corroboration: total loans receivable (gross) of IDR 970,701,203m at 31 Mar 2026 vs IDR 970,233,234m at 31 Dec 2025 — essentially flat QoQ (+0.05%), confirming the QoQ stagnation from the filed statements themselves. The maturity mix shifted visibly out of the 1–5 year bucket (IDR 228,230,349m → 197,917,215m) into the ≤1-year bucket (IDR 289,482,904m → 317,424,403m), consistent with a cautious, shorter-duration new-origination profile.

**Price.** The fact sheet discloses average effective yields: loans in Rupiah at 7.12% (FY2025: 7.43%) and foreign currencies at 4.99% (5.26%) — a visible yield decline that matches the external estimate of ~45bp asset-yield compression. Deposit costs moved the other way: customer time deposits in Rupiah at 2.87% (3.02% prior) and FX 1.88% (2.02%); savings Rupiah 0.08% (0.09%). Yield compression across placements was severe (Bank Indonesia/call money Rupiah 4.06% vs 4.87%; FX time deposits 0.74% vs 3.00%) — the flip side of the tripled placement balances in §1.

**Funding.** Third-party funds rose 8.3% YoY to Rp1,292.4tn with CASA +11.2% to Rp1,089tn and time deposits -5.1%; CASA ratio 85.2% vs 82.9% a year ago; LDR 74.1% vs 76.1% (external context). Fact-sheet corroboration: customer deposits IDR 1,276,408,911m vs 1,233,799,081m (+3.45% QoQ), with demand deposits 447,811,289m (+3.9% QoQ) and savings 634,224,104m (+4.3% QoQ) against time deposits 194,373,518m (-0.15% QoQ).

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

- **Geographic mix (segment table).** Jawa/Java dominates with IDR 25,221,376m of external sales (83.4% of the IDR 30,235,416m disclosed regional total) and IDR 1,431,352,997m of assets (87.3% of IDR 1,638,204,566m of regional assets). Sumatera IDR 1,353,151m sales / IDR 105,302,152m assets; East Indonesia IDR 839,502m / IDR 64,939,050m; Kalimantan IDR 567,961m / IDR 39,236,367m. The overseas-operation segment shows nil for all Q1 2026 figures versus small amounts in Q1 2025, suggesting the segment became inactive or was reclassified — an open item (internal_must_explain flags that net income per region is not disclosed, only PBT per region and total group net profit of IDR 14,689,799m).
- **Customer/counterparty mix.** Loans by economic sector (Note 12c, totals IDR 970,701,203m / 970,233,234m): manufacturing IDR 211,666,885m, trading/restaurants/hotels IDR 192,199,002m, business services IDR 172,198,097m, household IDR 158,189,441m — a diversified, non-concentrated corporate book.
- **Related-party revenue.** Interest and sharia income from related parties IDR 157,847m (0.64% of interest income; prior-year IDR 153,603m, 0.63%) — immaterial to the revenue line.
- **Insurance.** Net insurance income of IDR 80,461m vs IDR 2,668m is an IFRS 17/PSAK 117 restatement artefact in the comparative, not underlying growth — flagged in §3.3.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The two structural questions for revenue are NIM and loan growth, and both are guidance-challenged. FY26 guidance (held unchanged post-print, external context: NIM 5.4–5.6%, CoC 40–60bp, loans 8–10%) versus realised Q1: NIM at the bottom of range, CoC at the top of range, loans at 5.6% YoY. Management flagged that corporate yield pressure has begun to ease and upward loan repricing is possible (external context, Brights 24 April 2026). Watch items: (1) the Rp110tn of uncommitted unused credit facilities to customers (IDR 110,125,417m vs 100,451,029m at Dec-25) plus committed unused facilities of IDR 353,742,136m represent a large pipeline if demand returns; (2) the restructured book of IDR 25,351,106m (vs 25,851,079m) with its worst tier (substandard+doubtful+loss) rising ~1.8% QoQ to Rp8.70tn per external research; (3) fee momentum — wealth AUM Rp319tn +14% YoY, myBCA users +57% YoY with 42% of digital transaction volume via myBCA (external context) — is the highest-quality part of the revenue mix and the most defensible against rate compression. Post-period-end market context (external, low weight): the stock's -30% YTD move by early July 2026 reflects the market pricing exactly this loan-growth/NIM deceleration.

## 3. Profitability Analysis

### 3.1 Gross Margin Analysis

Not applicable / not disclosed. Banks do not report COGS or gross profit; the computed gross margin is null by construction. The nearest economic analogue is net interest income (interest income IDR 24,592,248m less interest expense IDR 3,483,815m = IDR 21,108,433m in Q1 2026 vs IDR 21,118,560m in Q1 2025, **-0.05% YoY**, a fact-sheet-arithmetic figure consistent with the flat NII reported externally). NIM per external context: 5.4%, down 40bp YoY and 20bp QoQ; risk-adjusted NIM 4.8% vs 5.3%.

Note the fact-sheet "operating profit" of IDR 18,077,072m equals PBT exactly because the model has no separate non-operating line — a modelling artefact. It is also stated *before* provisions in the fact-sheet convention, whereas the company presentation deducts provisions to arrive at pre-tax profit; the IDR 1,232,275m provision charge (Note 32, +19.5% YoY) and IDR 384,566m of recoveries sit conceptually between this line and net profit. Operating-profit growth of +3.56% (computed ratio) is therefore a pre-provision figure; on the company basis, PPOP was ~IDR 19.3tn, +4.8% YoY, with CIR 27.3% vs 28.5% (external context). Personnel expenses fell to IDR 4,733,979m from IDR 4,880,589m (-3.0%) while G&A rose +4.4% — though within G&A, communication expense fell sharply to IDR 166,444m from IDR 364,476m. Interest coverage (operating profit / interest expense) is 5.19x (computed ratio) — not meaningful for a bank but reported verbatim per the ratios block.

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

- **Interest income** IDR 24,592,248m (+0.92% YoY), including *negative* interest from discounting impaired financial assets of IDR (2,865)m versus +IDR 11,950m a year ago — a small but negative credit-quality echo inside the income line.
- **Interest expense** IDR 3,483,815m (+7.26% YoY) — the growth here is not customer-deposit cost (which fell; see §2.2) but the wholesale/other-bank funding line: Note 19 shows other-bank deposits of IDR 4,297,657m vs 3,966,077m, and borrowings average effective rate rose… actually fell (Rupiah 5.34% vs 6.32%); the driver of interest-expense growth is not fully decomposed in the fact sheet and is flagged as an open item.
- **Other income, net (residual)** IDR 5,743,384m (+12.56% YoY). Composition from filed notes: fees and commissions income — net IDR 5,061,371m (prior IDR 4,658,813m; +8.65%), FVTPL income IDR 1,048,919m (prior IDR 912,191m; +15.0%), other operating expense "others" IDR (682,806)m-level items and other operating income IDR 6,793,096m in total — the residual mapping between these is an internal_must_explain item and we present both without double-counting: the filed total of other operating income is IDR 6,793,096m vs IDR 6,019,206m-implied prior; the fact-sheet residual IDR 5,743,384m is stated after netting insurance expense of IDR (587,546)m and other components.
- **Net insurance income** IDR 80,461m vs IDR 2,668m — a **restatement artefact** of IFRS 17/PSAK 117 in the comparative (1Q25 restated onto the new basis with almost no carried net insurance result), not ~2,900% growth. Must not be read as growth.
- **FX.** No FX gain/loss line is separately disclosed in the fact sheet; the FX sensitivity table (Note 41.i) shows a ±5% Rupiah move would impact PBT by ±IDR 30,025m vs ±IDR 11,027m at Dec-25 — a material increase in FX sensitivity driven by the larger foreign-currency monetary position (Rp107.3tn assets / Rp102.6tn liabilities vs Rp94.4tn / Rp89.0tn).

Versus peers, BBCA's NCI structure is unusually trivial (0.09% of total equity), so profit retention is effectively 99.96% attributable to the parent — a structurally clean minority picture.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

Scored on evidence: (1) FY2025 dividend IDR 41.3tn at 72% payout — shareholder-first; (2) Rp5tn buyback, ~Rp1.1tn executed in the first three weeks — immediately accretive to EPS and consistent with a stock that management believed cheap at Rp8,200; (3) **zero loan growth in the quarter** — the paradox: the bank allocated ~Rp22tn of incremental balance sheet to reverse repos and placements rather than loans. That is either disciplined risk pricing (refusing to chase sub-hurdle corporate yields that have compressed ~45bp) or demand deficiency; either way, capital allocation is currently optimized for fee businesses, securities, and shareholder returns rather than balance-sheet growth. With CET1 at 26.39% against a 9.99% requirement, there is ~Rp150tn+ of theoretical excess capital — the ongoing question is whether it should be returned faster.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 4/10

Revenue +2.9% YoY on the fact-sheet convention with interest income +0.9%; loan growth 5.6% YoY versus 8–10% guidance and 0.1% QoQ; the growth engine is currently the fee/wealth stack and BCA Syariah — real, but too small to move a Rp1.6tn balance sheet.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: N/A (no market data)

### 13.5 Risk Profile

**Medium.** The bank-specific failure modes (deposit run, capital shortfall, FX blow-up) are essentially absent — this is one of the best-capitalized, most liquid banks in emerging Asia. The live risks are cyclical and franchise-level: NIM at the guidance floor, loan demand weakness, consumer-book contraction, coverage erosion, and tax-dispute tail liabilities. The largest single risk to the equity story is a multi-quarter continuation of flat loans with provisions at 60bp, which would compress EPS growth toward zero and undermine both the buyback rationale and the ~3x book multiple.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

Indonesia's highest-quality banking franchise is going through a yield-cycle trough, not a credit event. NIM at 5.4% is the floor of management guidance, and with corporate yields easing (management's own comment, external) and 85.2% CASA, the spread stabilizes in Q2–Q3; the Rp464tn of unused committed and uncommitted facilities converts as corporate capex demand returns, putting loans back toward 8–10% and NII back to growth; fees (+14%) and wealth (+14% AUM) continue to compound as the structural offset; CoC normalises back to 40–50bp as the consumer re-pricing cycle completes; and the Rp5tn buyback plus quarterly dividends return the ~Rp150tn theoretical excess capital while EPS compounds at high-single digits. On this path, a 22%+ ROE is sustained and the external de-rating reverses.

### 14.2 Bear Case

The fee engine cannot outrun the balance sheet forever. Loans stay at ~5% growth; excess liquidity keeps leaking into 4–5% placements, dragging NIM below 5.4% and outside guidance; provisions remain at 60bp as the auto-loan and SME books continue to sour (Stage 3 already rising); EPS growth decays from +3.5% toward zero; and a bank whose equity fell 7.9% in a quarter (dividend + buyback) while earning 22% ROE is structurally shrinking its balance-sheet earnings base relative to payout — the multiple compresses toward ~2x book, and even that re-prices another 30–40% lower from external Rp6,450. Tax-dispute tail (Rp1.5tn+ open across FY2016–FY2021) is noise at this scale but headlines badly.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **Q2 2026 loan growth** — anything at or above ~8% YoY confirms guidance; ~5% again breaks the FY26 loan thesis.
2. **NIM print** — stabilisation at ≥5.4% vs a slide below the band; watch reverse-repo and placement balances (this note's §9.2 table) as the tell.
3. **Cost of credit** — a return to ≤50bp and NPL coverage stabilising ≥175% would restore the provision narrative; a second quarter at 60bp with coverage <170% escalates the concern.
4. **First quarterly interim dividend** (June 2026, per the new AGMS authorization) — a yield-support event in a falling tape.
5. **Consumer/auto-loan inflection** — the vehicle book (-19.7% YoY) is the swing segment for both growth and credit.
6. Buyback pace under the Rp5tn authorization through March 2027.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (medium risk), no target price.** With no market-data stub supplied, we do not set a target range; the decision framework is: BBCA's Q1 2026 is an in-line, high-quality, low-growth quarter — fee-led earnings (+3.8% NP), flat loans, spread at the guidance floor, capital and liquidity beyond reproach. Own it for the franchise, the payout, and the fortress balance sheet; do not add for growth until the Q2 loan and NIM prints confirm the second-half acceleration management has guided. **Review trigger: the Q2 2026 report — specifically loan growth ≥7% YoY, NIM ≥5.4%, and CoC ≤60bp; missing two of the three downgrades the thesis to a pure-yield hold and demands a fresh valuation view with live market data.**
