# PT Pulau Subur Tbk (PTPS) — Q2 2026
---

## 1. Executive Summary

PT Pulau Subur Tbk reported H1 2026 revenue of IDR 31.18 billion, a 13.9% contraction from the prior-year period, as Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) volume and/or pricing weakened against an already soft comparable. Despite the top-line decline, net profit rose 3.0% to IDR 15.86 billion, driven by a sharply lower tax charge—IDR 1.72 billion versus IDR 3.65 billion in H1 2025—and tight cost control that limited the operating profit drop to 7.1%. Gross margin compressed 813 bps to 49.8%, but operating margin actually expanded to 56.1% because operating profit exceeded gross profit, implying net other operating income or cost credits below the cost-of-sales line that are not separately disclosed.

The balance sheet remains pristine. Total liabilities collapsed 60.2% from December 2025 to just IDR 2.42 billion, cutting the debt-to-equity ratio to 0.013x and leaving the company effectively unlevered. Cash stands at IDR 64.75 billion—37x current liabilities—and working capital expanded to IDR 79.38 billion. The only material liability is a IDR 411.9 million vehicle lease with PT Dipo Star Finance. Equity grew 3.4% to IDR 186.89 billion, underpinned by retained earnings.

Cash flow quality deteriorated. Operating cash flow fell 41.7% to IDR 11.08 billion, with supplier payments jumping 48.1% even as revenue declined. Collections remained robust at 101.0% of revenue, but the FY2025 dividend of IDR 9.75 billion—approved May 2026—consumed nearly all operating cash, pushing reported free cash flow to IDR -10.35 billion. Capex was light at IDR 1.22 billion, mostly directed to non-PPE items, suggesting maintenance mode.

No market data were provided, so valuation multiples are indeterminate. On a book-value basis, BVPS is IDR 86.24 and H1 EPS is IDR 7.32. The investment case is therefore binary: the balance sheet is fortress-like, but revenue concentration—effectively a single customer—and an unresolved IDR 42.57 billion construction commitment for a 10-ton/hour palm oil mill create asymmetric downside. We assign a **HOLD (Qualified)** pending price discovery and clarity on customer and contract risk.

*Reporting basis: Indonesian Rupiah (IDR) in full amounts. The current interim period ended June 30, 2026 is unaudited; the comparative balance sheet as of December 31, 2025 is audited.*

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

FFB production for H1 2026 was 9,225.8 MT. No prior-period volume or realized pricing data are disclosed, so a volume-versus-price decomposition is not available. The 13.9% revenue decline therefore reflects an unidentified mix of lower volume, weaker CPO/FFB prices, or both. No foreign-exchange tailwind was noted; all operations are domestic IDR.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is extreme and is the defining feature of the revenue profile. PT Gelumbang Agro S. generated IDR 30,670,001,320 in revenue—effectively the entirety of the IDR 31.18 billion top line. Trade receivables from PT Gelumbang Agro Sentosa were IDR 1,417,000,000, suggesting the two names may be related entities within the same buyer group. No export sales, segment splits, or domestic/external breakdowns are disclosed.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

With no reserve disclosures and only 9,225.8 MT of FFB production in H1, the revenue runway is entirely dependent on the offtake agreement with PT Gelumbang Agro S. and spot CPO market dynamics. A material post-period-end catalyst is the IDR 42.57 billion construction contract with PT Sejahtera Jaya Abadi (SJA) for a 10-ton/hour CPO mill; the contracted execution period of April 2024 to May 2025 has already elapsed, yet the commitment remains disclosed as of June 30, 2026. Watch items: (i) renewal or amendment of the dominant customer contract; (ii) any progress or impairment signal on the SJA mill; and (iii) H2 FFB volume data.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths
- **Fortress leverage**: D/E of 0.013x and loans/equity of 0.002x; effectively debt-free.
- **Extreme liquidity**: Current ratio of 46.4x and cash ratio of 37.0x; cash covers all obligations many times over.
- **Profit resilience**: Net profit grew 3.0% despite a 13.9% revenue drop, aided by tax efficiency.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
- **Cash conversion cycle of 7.4 days**: DSO 4.1 days, DIO 3.4 days, DPO 0.2 days—near-immediate cash realization.
- **Collections exceeding revenue**: 101.0% collections ratio indicates working capital is not tied up in receivables.
- **Minimal payables reliance**: The company does not depend on supplier financing.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- **No litigation**: Note 29c confirms no material litigation as of June 30, 2026.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Customer concentration**: PT Gelumbang Agro S. accounts for effectively all revenue. Any delay, default, or pricing renegotiation would instantaneously impair the top line and OCF.
- **HIGH — Construction commitment overhang**: The IDR 42.57 billion SJA mill contract (136% of H1 revenue) has an elapsed execution window. Completion risk, cost overruns, or impairment could materially dent equity.
- **MODERATE — OCF deterioration**: Operating cash flow fell 41.7% YoY, and the FY2025 dividend consumed 88% of H1 OCF, leaving minimal reinvestment buffer.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **MODERATE — Lack of diversification**: No disclosed segments, no export sales, no downstream revenue. The company is a pure FFB merchant fully exposed to CPO spot volatility.
- **MODERATE — Scale constraints**: 9,225.8 MT FFB in H1 implies a very small plantation footprint with limited economies of scale and fixed-cost absorption.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **MODERATE — Tax authority discretion**: A tax amnesty asset (IDR 5.12 billion) and a material discrepancy in prior-year tax estimates (Note 15c: estimated current tax Rp 433 million versus reconciliation of Rp 3.91 billion) raise questions about tax reporting consistency.
- **LOW — Related-party governance**: Small but present related-party rents to shareholder PT Sekawan Kontrindo and payables to associate SEAL create potential conflict-of-interest scenarios.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- **Not assessable**: Absent market data, no P/E, P/B, or EV/EBITDA benchmark can be established. If shares trade at any meaningful premium to BVPS (IDR 86.24), the thin float and binary customer risk may not be adequately priced in.

Without market multiples, scoring is inherently speculative. On fundamentals, the earnings yield looks reasonable relative to book value, but the business is too concentrated and too small to award a high valuation score absent a price.

### 13.5 Risk Profile
Asymmetric and front-loaded. Downside is severe if the sole customer falters or the mill contract sours; upside is capped by the lack of volume growth and reinvestment. The risk/reward skew is unfavorable without a clear entry price or catalyst.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
If CPO prices recover and FFB volume stabilizes, the company’s low fixed-cost base and tax efficiency could drive outsized EPS growth from any incremental revenue. The SEAL associate may declare dividends or expand milling capacity, providing non-operating income upside. The balance sheet can absorb significant stress before equity is impaired.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Loss of the sole customer would eliminate nearly all revenue and OCF. The IDR 42.6 billion mill commitment may become a stranded asset or require further cash calls. OCF is already down 41.7%, and the dividend is eating cash reserves. A prolonged CPO downturn would compress the already-thin gross margin and force a dividend cut or balance-sheet drawdown.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. Disclosure of customer contract renewal or pricing terms with PT Gelumbang Agro S.
2. Update on the PT Sejahtera Jaya Abadi mill construction (completion, cost revision, funding).
3. H2 2026 FFB production data and CPO price realization.
4. Emergence of broker coverage or market-making that establishes a benchmark price and liquidity.

### 14.4 Recommendation
**HOLD (Qualified)**. PTPS exhibits exceptional balance-sheet strength and enviable margins, but the combination of single-customer concentration, declining revenue, a massive construction overhang, and no observable market valuation makes it uninvestable on a risk-adjusted basis without a clear entry price.
