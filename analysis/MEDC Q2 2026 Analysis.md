[[MEDC Q1 2026 Analysis]]

# PT Medco Energi Internasional Tbk (MEDC) — Q2 2026
---

**Reporting basis.** All company figures in this note are in **US Dollars (full units)** drawn from MEDC's consolidated interim financial statements for the **six months ended 30 June 2026 (H1 2026)**, audited, sourced from the canonical fact sheet (IDX XLSX filing). The comparative income-statement and cash-flow column is **H1 2025** (unaudited in the prior filing); the comparative balance-sheet column is **31 December 2025 (FY2025 year-end, audited)**. Where a discrete Q2 2026 (three-month) figure is shown, it is derived as H1 2026 less Q1 2026, with the Q1 2026 inputs taken from [[MEDC Q1 2026 Analysis]] and the research brief — these are clearly labelled and are not fact-sheet-native. Market data was **not provided** in the stub, so all market-based valuation multiples are reported as **not disclosed/not computed** rather than estimated.

A structural point that governs everything below: the fact sheet's `period_end` is 30 June 2026 but the income statement is **cumulative six-month**. "Q2 2026" throughout this note therefore means the second quarter embedded in the H1 statements; all ratios in the fact sheet (ROE, ROA, asset turnover, DSO/DIO/DPO) are computed on H1 figures, annualized where stated.

## 1. Executive Summary

MEDC delivered a step-change half. H1 2026 revenue of **US$1,414.7m** grew **+23.4%** YoY (H1 2025: US$1,146.9m), gross profit rose **+30.2%** to US$588.5m with gross margin expanding to **41.6%** from 39.4%, and PBT more than doubled to **US$477.4m** (+151.5%). Net profit attributable to owners of the parent reached **US$275.3m** (H1 2025: US$30.7m), taking basic EPS to **US$0.01115** from US$0.00124 — a near-eightfold increase. Discrete Q2 was even stronger than Q1: derived from H1 minus Q1 (per [[MEDC Q1 2026 Analysis]]), Q2 revenue was ~US$746m vs ~US$668m in Q1, with Q2 gross margin at ~47.8% vs 34.7% in Q1 as realized oil prices jumped (research brief: ~US$98.8/bbl in Q2 vs ~US$75.1 in Q1).

The bottom line, however, is **not what the headline suggests**. Per the external research (management earnings call and release, high confidence), the single biggest driver of the +697% net profit jump is the **share of profit from associates and joint ventures**, which swung from a US$11.8m loss in H1 2025 to a US$124.1m profit in H1 2026 (+/− US$135.9m), of which **~US$104.2m is attributed to PT Amman Mineral Internasional (AMMN)** as the Batu Hijau smelter ran at full capacity. Management quantified the ex-AMMN core at ~US$171m vs ~US$62m — a strong ~3x improvement, but far below the optical 9x. AMMN equity income is **non-cash at the MEDC level and untaxed in MEDC's own P&L**, which also flatters the effective tax rate optics (39.8% in H1 vs a Q1-implied ~55%). This is the central earnings-quality caveat of the quarter.

The balance sheet executed a deliberate liquidity build. Cash nearly tripled off the year-end base to **US$1,326.5m** (31 Dec 2025: US$569.1m), driving current assets up 48.7% to US$2,425.1m. But the build is **debt-funded**: consolidated gross debt rose from US$3,520.2m to US$4,128.4m (+17.3% QoQ) on committed-facility drawdowns (~US$922m raised vs ~US$324m repaid per external estimates), plus a US$200m re-tap of the 8.625% 2030 Senior Notes and a June 2026 call on IDR Bond V Phase I Seri B that pulled long-dated paper into current maturities. Interest-bearing loans on the balance sheet rose from US$3,759.0m to US$4,261.7m. Management frames this as pre-funding for growth (Sakakemang, Batam power expansion, Cendramas Malaysia) — external estimates show restricted-group net debt/EBITDA actually **improved to ~1.4x** — but consolidated D/E at 2.57x is up from 2.14x (computed: 6,002.6/2,360.1 at FY2025) and the negative carry (interest income US$13.7m vs interest expense US$162.9m) is real.

Valuation cannot be assessed from the stub — no market data was provided, so P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA and dividend yield are **not computed**. The fundamental picture supports a constructive stance: H1 operating cash flow of **US$514.7m** (+46.6%), free cash flow of **US$461.5m** (fact-sheet ratio), proved reserves of **362.0 mmboe** at 30 June 2026, and proved+probable of **542.1 mmboe**, with reserve life far exceeding a decade at H1 production of ~26.7 mmboe over the half (proved reserves production during H1 2026 per the reserves note). On balance-sheet and earnings-quality considerations, we rate MEDC **HOLD (positive bias)** pending normalization of the AMMN contribution and the debt-funded cash build into committed capex. Key catalysts and review triggers are in §14.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet contains no production volume or realized price table (production list is empty), so volume/price attribution relies on the external research (high confidence, in-period, management-sourced):

- **Realized oil** averaged ~US$98.8/bbl in Q2 2026 vs ~US$75.1 in Q1 2026 and ~US$69.4/bbl in H1 2025 (+25% YoY) — Brent spiked on Middle East tensions in April–May 2026 (April high ~US$126/bbl, May monthly average ~US$107) before falling back in June (~US$85) ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?graph_id=1414596)).
- **Realized gas** ~US$8.9/mmbtu in Q2 2026 vs ~US$7.2 in Q1 and ~US$7.0 in H1 2025 (+15.5% YoY).
- **Volumes** grew ~19% to ~170.1 mboepd (top of the 165–170 mboepd guidance), against a near-fixed cost base — unit cash cost of ~US$8.4/boe in H1 (+4.2% YoY) and ~US$7.7/boe in Q2 (−14.5% QoQ). This combination — price spike on top of volume growth on a fixed cost base — is the whole gross-margin story.
- Volume adds: the incremental **24% Corridor PSC working interest** (46%→70%, effective with the Fortuna International Barbados acquisition completed 28 July 2025, giving the first full six-month contribution in H1 2026), Forel/Terubuk start-ups in South Natuna Sea Block B, Senoro Phase 2A full onstream (June 2026), Bualuang Phase 1 (Thailand) onstream in Q2, and Bisat C debottlenecking in Oman Block 60.
- Power: sales volumes rose ~16.5% to ~2,322.9 GWh (renewables ~590.1 GWh / 25.4%) but segment revenue rose only ~1.1% — blended tariff/fuel pass-through per kWh fell, so power was a **volume and diversification contributor, not a margin driver**.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Geographically (fact sheet segment disclosure), foreign revenue of US$853.7m (60.3%) exceeded Indonesian revenue of US$561.1m (39.7%) in H1 2026; a year earlier the split was US$637.9m / US$509.0m (55.6% / 44.4%) — the Corridor consolidation and Thai/Oman volumes pushed the mix further external. Customer concentration (trade receivables basis, 30 June 2026): Shell International Eastern Trading 19%, Sembcorp Gas 15%, Gas Supply Pte Ltd 12%, PT Perusahaan Gas Negara (PGN) 10%, Petroleum Development Oman 8% — gas offtakers dominate the receivables book, consistent with the long-dated GSA portfolio (Corridor to GSPL/PGN through 2028, South Sumatera to PLN/Pupuk Sriwidjaja, Senoro-Toili to Donggi Senoro LNG). Related-party service revenue is small (US$3.9m in H1 2026 per Note 34c, vs US$1.0m in H1 2025). Revenue by type is 92.5% oil & gas sales, 3.1% electricity, 1.4% O&M and other services, 2.1% lease/finance income — the group remains overwhelmingly an upstream producer.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The GSA portfolio provides a contracted underlay (major Corridor contracts to GSPL and PGN run to 2028; Pertamina Hulu Rokan to 2028; Pupuk Sriwidjaja Palembang to 2033; PLN Tarakan extended to 2030), but the Q2 revenue surge was substantially **price-led**, and the price leg is fading: Brent fell from the ~US$107 May average to ~US$85 in June and remained ~US$103–114 into September 2026 per the research brief (post-period context). Volumes are the durable leg: proved reserves of 362.0 mmboe (30 June 2026; 361.4 mmboe excluding the Simenggaris held-for-sale block) against ~26.7 mmboe of H1 production imply a proved reserve life of ~13.5 years at current rates; proved+probable of 542.1 mmboe and contingent resources of 1,154.2 mmboe give a long runway. Watch items: (i) the **Simenggaris JOB divestment completed July 2026** (post-period) — any disposal result lands in Q3 and removes ~649 mboe of proved held-for-sale reserves; (ii) the **Cendramas PSC (Malaysia, 50%)** becomes effective 23 September 2026, adding ~7 mboepd not yet in guidance; (iii) the Sakakemang PSC acquisition (45% WI, closed November 2025) is the principal growth capex target behind the raised 2026 oil & gas capex guidance of US$450–475m (post-period, per research brief); (iv) LIBOR-replacement amendments to USD-LIBOR-referencing GSAs/COSPAs remain pending SKK Migas direction (Note 46).

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

- **Interest income fell 39.2%** to US$13.65m (H1 2025: US$22.47m) despite cash nearly tripling off the year-end base — the research brief attributes this to lower interest-earning balances in H1 (the build landed in Q2), part of the yield migrating into the revenue-level finance income line, and deposit yields falling faster than debt cost. Negative carry on the enlarged balance sheet is a real drag.
- **Interest expense was essentially flat** at US$162.9m (H1 2025: US$165.8m) despite gross debt ending 19% higher — the Q2 drawdowns carried interest for only part of the half; expect a full-quarter step-up in H2.
- **Other income net swung to +US$6.2m from −US$10.9m** (+157% as flagged). Per the research (medium confidence), this is a composition of: gross other income up ~64% to US$19.4m (including an FX gain of US$14.2m in H1 2026 vs nil in H1 2025); other expenses lighter at US$13.2m vs US$22.7m; and the other-gains line swinging to +US$6.0m from −US$18.3m on **fair-value marks on marketable securities (+US$7.5m vs −US$8.0m)** — i.e., largely **non-cash mark-to-market**, not a settlement or disposal gain. No divestment gain sits in H1: the Simenggaris sale completed only in July 2026.
- **Associates/JV share: +US$124.1m in H1 2026 vs −US$11.8m in H1 2025** (research, high confidence) — of which ~US$104.2m from AMMN (Q2 alone ~US$70.7m vs ~US$33.5m in Q1). Also embedded in PBT per Note 37b/37c figures. This line is the single largest PBT driver and the single largest earnings-quality caveat.

Related-party other receivables halved YoY to US$101.7m (Jun 2025: US$197.4m), with the Medco Daya Natuna balance down to US$62.6m from US$128.9m — cash-call receivables from joint operations (MDN/MDM/BDM/MDEN) carry late-payment interest at benchmark +2–3%.

- **Other advances fell 31.2% to US$78.1m** (Dec 2025: US$113.7m). The research plan flags this as **must-explain**: candidates are project advances unwinding as Forel/Terubuk/Senoro 2A moved to execution and held-for-sale reclassifications, but no external disclosure confirms a driver — treat as an open question, not a fact.

The H1 severance charge of US$7.9m (nil in all prior periods) reflects this transition. ### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary

From the earnings call/release (per research, high confidence): management explicitly quantified the AMMN contribution (~US$104.2m of the US$124.1m associates' swing) and the ex-AMMN core (~US$171m vs ~US$62m); attributed the gross debt rise to "additional loan drawdown" under committed facilities; raised oil & gas capex guidance to US$450–475m and power capex to up to US$50m; and flagged an active portfolio-optimization review (targeting >US$200m of non-core disposals over five years, no committed timeline). Actions in the half: the US$200m 8.625% 2030 notes re-tap (April), the IDR Bond V Phase I B call (June), CCS contracts with seven banks (April–May), and pre-funding of the growth program.

### 12.3 Governance Observations

- The extensive related-party web with the Medco Daya entities (same controlling family/group) is disclosed in detail and appears arm's-length-priced (JO cash calls at benchmark +2–3%, office lease at market ROU), but the sheer density of it (receivables, payables, crude purchases, advances, leases) warrants standing scrutiny; note PT Bank Woori Saudara ceased to be a related party in 2025, reducing one strand.
- Fact-sheet data-quality caveats: multiple subsidiary/joint-operation tables are docling-misaligned with reconstructed values (Lematang/Malaka asset splits, Medco LLC fragmentation) — treat subsidiary-level asset figures as indicative, and the writer flags (per internal must-explain list) that the owner-attributable comparative (US$30.7m) and NCI allocation do not fully reconcile to the equity note without the full rollforward.
- Positive: audited interim statements, detailed sensitivity disclosures, reserve certifications by independent engineers (NSAI, GCA, McDaniel, Sproule, DeGolyer), and prompt subsequent-event disclosure (Note 56).

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

The H1 2026 pattern is textbook **pre-funding for growth**: borrow (~US$922m) → hold cash (US$1.33bn) → spend in H2 on Sakakemang and power. Simultaneously: modest shareholder returns (US$45m dividend, ~20% of prior-year profit), treasury-share transfers for the share-award programs (US$2.6m, 46.3m shares), early repayment of expensive/maturing paper (Oak Tree, Bell, IDR tranches), and NCI dividends (~US$2.0m). The unresolved capital-allocation question: with US$450–475m of capex committed and the Cendramas/Sakakemang pipeline, the US$1.2bn undrawn facilities plus cash give ~2x coverage — adequate, but no slack for a second FIBI-sized acquisition without re-levering past 2.6x D/E.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 7/10

Liquidity is exceptional (cash 0.93x current liabilities, ~US$2.6bn total liquidity), OCF conversion is strong, and the DB plan is fully funded. Deductions: consolidated leverage of 2.57x D/E with rising derivative liabilities, negative carry (interest income US$13.7m vs expense US$162.9m), and an H2 capex program that will consume much of the buffer. The restricted-group ~1.4x net debt/EBITDA is credible but is management's frame, not the auditor's.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 8/10

Production at the top of guidance (~170 mboepd), unit costs falling QoQ, Q2 gross margin of ~47.8%, 94.4% cash collection, Corridor/Senoro/Bualuang start-ups landing on schedule, and power CODs (ELB 109 MW) hitting plan. Deduction: organic reserve replacement below 1.0x in the half, and the Simenggaris exit removes producing volumes (closed July 2026, post-period).

### 13.3 Growth Score: 7/10

Sakakemang, Cendramas (effective Sept 2026), the Batam power expansion (Siemens units 2027–28), Ijen stage 2, and the Oman debottlenecks give a genuine multi-year ramp. The AMMN stake adds uncontrollable but currently powerful earnings torque. Deduction: near-term organic growth is acquisition-heavy; the drilling-based organic pipeline needs to prove out in the revisions line.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: n/a

No market data provided; we decline to score what we cannot measure. On fundamentals alone the earnings quality is strong-but-inflated (headline annualized EPS ~US$0.0223 vs ~US$0.0139 recurring core).

### 13.5 Risk Profile

**MEDIUM-HIGH.** Commodity-price beta (oil fell ~15% off the May peak into June), leverage in absolute terms (US$6.2bn+ of debt instruments), a structurally heavy Indonesian fiscal regime, an unwaived Sarulla default overhang, Middle East operational tail risk (Oman/Yemen, unquantified per Note 52), and earnings concentration in an associate (AMMN) MEDC does not control. Offsetting: contracted gas revenue, investment-grade offtakers, deep liquidity, and a governance transition completed without operational disruption.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

- The **recurring core tripled** (ex-AMMN ~US$171m vs ~US$62m) on volume growth, cost discipline, and the first full Corridor 70% half — this is operational, not financial, improvement.
- Q2 proved the margin torque of the model: ~47.8% gross margin on a ~US$8/boe cash cost base; even at normalized ~US$75–85 Brent, the Corridor GSAs and Oman/Natuna volumes sustain a mid-30s% gross margin.
- The US$1.33bn cash plus US$1.2bn undrawn facilities fund Sakakemang and the power build-out without equity dilution; RG net debt/EBITDA of ~1.4x is well inside investment-grade norms for the sector.
- AMMN's smelter ramp is a multi-year earnings escalator at zero incremental capital, and the 362 mmboe proved reserve base (13.5-year life) with 1.15 bnboe of contingent resources underwrites decades of runway.
- Post-period catalysts: Simenggaris disposal gain in Q3, Cendramas consolidation from late September, and a >US$200m non-core divestment program.

### 14.2 Bear Case

- **H1 2026 is the peak of the cycle as reported**: Q2-peak oil (~US$98.8/bbl realized) plus peak smelter contribution (US$70.7m AMMN in Q2 alone) plus mark-to-market gains — every one of those legs is mean-reverting, and H2 comparisons are against this base.
- Re-levered balance sheet meeting fading prices: gross debt +19% YoY, interest expense stepping up in H2, and ~US$400m+ of H2 capex — the combination squeezes H2 FCF toward zero and D/E toward 2.7x+.
- Organic reserve replacement below 1.0x; the growth story is increasingly bought (FIBI at 1.4x book-equivalent goodwill, Sakakemang with contingent consideration) rather than drilled.
- Structural drags: ~40% ETR forever, Sarulla's unwaived defaults, the PSC fiscal regime's DMO/FTP mechanics, and an unquantified Middle East escalation risk sitting on Oman/Yemen operations.
- Earnings quality: ~40% of headline owners' profit is untaxed, non-cash associate income from an entity MEDC neither controls nor consolidates.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **Q3 2026 results (Simenggaris disposal close + Cendramas consolidation)** — first clean read on the ex-AMMN run-rate at post-spike oil prices.
2. **Brent trajectory through H2 2026** (~US$103–114 in September per FRED — supportive if sustained; a slide to the US$80s compresses the Q2 margin story).
3. **Sakakemang development spend and first reserve recertification** — the organic-replacement test.
4. **AMMN quarterly earnings** — each US$10m swing in the associate's half-year run-rate moves MEDC's owners' EPS by ~7%.
5. **Non-core divestment announcements** (>US$200m program) and any further debt tender as rates allow.
6. **Dalle Energy Batam expansion PPA conversion** (conditional → executed) and Ijen stage-2 progress.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (positive bias) — medium-high risk.** The fundamental picture — a tripled recurring core, top-of-guidance volumes, ~US$461m of H1 FCF, a 13.5-year proved reserve life, and ~US$2.6bn of total liquidity — is decisively better than a year ago, and the equity deserves credit for it. But the reported +799% EPS is substantially associate-income and price-spike torque, the balance sheet has been deliberately re-levered into an H2 capex wall, and with no market data provided we cannot certify that the price already discounts the peak-quarter optics. We would upgrade on evidence of (i) organic reserve replacement ≥1.0x, (ii) H2 recurring net income holding above ~US$150m ex-AMMN at sub-US$90 Brent, or (iii) net debt/EBITDA holding ≤1.5x through the capex program. **Target price range: not set (no market data provided).** **Review trigger: Q3 2026 results** — specifically the Simenggaris disposal result, the post-spike realized-price deck, and the pace of Sakakemang capex drawdown.
