# Astra International Tbk (ASII) — Q3 2026
---

**Reporting basis.** All company figures are in **billions of Rupiah (Rp bn)** unless stated otherwise; per-share amounts are in full Rupiah. The current period is the **nine months ended 30 September 2026 (9M 2026), unaudited**, authorised by the Board of Directors on **28 October 2026**. Income-statement and cash-flow comparatives are **9M 2025**; balance-sheet comparatives are **31 December 2025** (the prior fiscal year-end), **not** 30 June 2026. Where a stand-alone Q3 figure is used it is explicitly labelled *derived* (9M less H1, with H1 2026 taken from [[ASII Q2 2026 Analysis]] and H1 2025 from the same note's comparatives); derived figures are arithmetic on reported periods, not company disclosures. External market and industry figures are labelled and sourced; they never replace fact-sheet numbers. **No market-data stub was supplied with this run**, so pipeline-computed valuation multiples are unavailable and are marked as such rather than estimated.

## 1. Executive Summary

Astra's 9M 2026 print looks, at the headline, like a deteriorating business: revenue **Rp 243,869 bn (+0.11% YoY)**, gross profit **Rp 52,008 bn (−0.46%)**, net profit **Rp 24,073 bn (−20.06%)**, profit attributable to owners **Rp 21,602 bn (−11.73%)** and EPS **Rp 541 (from Rp 605, −10.58%)**. That reading is wrong in one important respect and right in another. It is wrong because the entire deterioration of the pre-tax line sits in two identifiable places — **`other_income_net` of −Rp 2,429 bn versus +Rp 1,837 bn a year earlier (a Rp 4,266 bn swing)** and a **56% collapse in profit attributable to non-controlling interests (Rp 2,471 bn from Rp 5,639 bn)**. It is right because the *operating* business did in fact lose ground cumulatively: gross margin slipped **12 bps to 21.33%**, and derived opex absorbed a further **142 bps**, taking derived operating margin to **10.11% from 11.65%**.

Both effects are concentrated in the first half. On derived quarterly arithmetic — 9M 2026 of Rp 243,869 bn less H1 2026 of Rp 157,913 bn — **Q3 2026 revenue was Rp 85,956 bn, up 6.4% YoY** against Q3 2025's Rp 80,751 bn, and **up 8.5% sequentially** from an H1 that ran −3.0% YoY. Derived Q3 gross profit of Rp 19,556 bn implies a **22.75% gross margin versus 21.56% in Q3 2025 and 21.40% in Q2 2026** — so the "gross margin increased" flag is genuine *at the quarterly level* but is a **Q3 recovery, not a cumulative expansion**. Derived Q3 pre-tax profit of Rp 13,280 bn (+3.6% YoY) and derived Q3 NCI of ~Rp 1,660 bn (versus ~Rp 1,673 bn a year earlier, i.e. flat) confirm that neither the margin nor the minority leakage deteriorated further after June. Externally, the driver is well documented: the mining-solutions and heavy-equipment segment swung from roughly Rp 200 bn of net profit in 2Q26 to ~Rp 2.3 trn in 3Q26 as Pamapersada volumes and the Martabe gold restart landed, while automotive (net profit +9% YoY in 3Q26) and financial services (+9%) compounded — a **mix rotation away from the low-margin commodity revenue line**, not a pricing event (Stockbit Snips, 28 Oct 2026; [link](https://snips.stockbit.com/snips-terbaru/-asii-laba-bersih-3q26-membaik-seiring-pemulihan-untr-9m26-lampaui-ekspektasi)).

The balance sheet carries the story of the year honestly. Total assets rose **3.62%** to Rp 525,718 bn and total equity **2.43%** to Rp 297,892 bn, but the composition shifted in three ways that matter. **Gross loans rose to Rp 108,521 bn** (current Rp 62,215 bn + non-current Rp 46,306 bn) from Rp 98,048 bn, lifting loans-to-equity to 0.364 from 0.337 and pipeline net debt to **Rp 37,623 bn (net-debt/equity 12.63%, from 9.66%)**. **Current accrued expenses jumped 38.9%** to Rp 22,362 bn from Rp 16,096 bn with revenue flat and trade payables *falling* 9.5% to Rp 35,516 bn. And **restricted cash rose 53.6%** to Rp 1,492 bn from Rp 971 bn, building monotonically through the year (971 → 1,072 → 1,160 → 1,492). Neither the accrual nor the restricted-cash build is explained anywhere in the external coverage captured; both are carried forward as explicit open questions (§4.2, §11.1). Liquidity nevertheless remains adequate — **current ratio 1.228, quick ratio 1.013, working capital Rp 35,759 bn** — but the quick ratio is barely above 1.0 and the working-capital buffer *shrank* by Rp 718 bn from the December position.

Cash generation slowed without breaking: **OCF Rp 28,608 bn (−18.43%)**, collections Rp 298,805 bn (1.225× revenue, up from 1.204×), **cash conversion 1.188×** net profit, and free cash flow **Rp 21,242 bn (FCF margin 8.71%)**. The composition is telling: **PPE capex nearly halved to Rp 5,772 bn from Rp 11,056 bn**, investing outflow narrowed to Rp 10,520 bn from Rp 15,245 bn, while gross loan repayments stepped up to Rp 339,237 bn from Rp 255,150 bn — a revolving financial-services funding model running harder. Cash ended at Rp 52,688 bn, down a net Rp 393 bn, although the reported net-change line does not foot to the opening and closing balances (a Rp 460 bn gap; Rp 346 bn in the prior year) and that reconciliation is flagged in §5.5.

Valuation cannot be done on house data this run. **The market-data stub was empty**, so `pe_annualized`, `pb` and `ev_ebitda` are null in the computed-ratios block; book value per share is derived at **Rp 5,963** from owners' equity of Rp 238,103 bn over 39.930 bn shares. For context only — not as a house multiple — external coverage put the stock at approximately **6.7× P/E** on 28 October 2026 and reported a **−5.80% close at Rp 4,710** the following session, i.e. the relief rally did not hold (Stockbit community post, 29 Oct 2026; [link](https://stockbit.com/post/37083519)). On that external reference, 9M profit equals ~74% of the Rp 29.1 trn FY2026 consensus figure, leaving a low 4Q hurdle.

**Rating: BUY, moderate risk.** The 9M headline overstates the damage, the Q3 operating inflection is real and evidenced across revenue, gross margin, segment mix and NCI, and the remaining risk is concentrated in lines that are measurable (accruals, restricted funds) rather than in the earnings engine. What tempers conviction is that the Q3 margin gain is mix- and commodity-driven and will annualise in 2027, that opex grew far faster than revenue, and that the key risk factors — coal RKAB policy, EV-driven mix shift in autos, and mark-to-market on the equity portfolio — are outside management's control. A P/E-anchored target range of **Rp 5,050–5,400** is set out in §14.4, on assumptions stated there.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet carries **no production, volume, unit-sales or reserve disclosures** (`production: []`, `reserves: []`), and the segment table gives external sales only. Volume evidence must therefore come from external industry data, and it points squarely at **volume, not price**, as the Q3 driver:

- **Four-wheel volume:** Gaikindo wholesales reached **679,020 units in January–September 2026, +21% YoY** against 561,405, with September alone **+28.1% YoY** (retail +27.4%); Gaikindo's full-year target is 850,000 units ([Bisnis Otomotif, 7 Oct 2026](https://otomotif.bisnis.com/read/20261007/46/2010691/penjualan-mobil-tembus-679020-unit-per-september-2026-naik-21)). Astra's own car sales rose **13%** over 9M26 with market share around **49%** — i.e. it grew volume but slightly *under-grew* the market, with share drifting below the ~50% level (Kontan Insight, 22 Sep 2026, [link](https://insight.kontan.co.id/news/market-share-segmen-otomotif-turun-ini-proyeksi-kinerja-astra-international-asii)).
- **Two-wheel volume:** AISI domestic motorcycle sales **5.01 mn units in January–September 2026, +3.71% YoY**, with Honda +3% and share stable at ~77% ([Bisnis Otomotif, 26 Oct 2026](https://otomotif.bisnis.com/read/20261026/273/2015790/penjualan-motor-tembus-501-juta-unit-per-september-2026)).
- **Heavy equipment volume — the negative:** national coal RKAB quota cuts pushed Komatsu unit sales to **2,393 units in January–July 2026, −23% YoY** from 3,098, with large mining machines **−49%** ([IDX Channel, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.idxchannel.com/market-news/penurunan-rkab-batu-bara-tekan-bisnis-alat-berat-united-tractors-untr)). Roughly 50 mining companies were still awaiting RKAB revision approvals in late September.
- **Gold — the offsetting quantity:** Martabe restarted at end-May 2026 after a ~5-month suspension, targeting ~65,000 oz for 2026, but **9M26 group gold sales were only 54,000 oz versus 178,000 oz** a year earlier — the entire H2 catch-up is a volume recovery story ([Kompas, 7 Sep 2026](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/09/07/202100926/mulai-produksi-sejak-mei-tambang-emas-martabe-belum-beroperasi-penuh)).
- **Price:** gold traded around **US$4,500/oz** in August 2026 (source noted as unverified in the research audit — used only as directional context), and CPO averaged ~US$1,432/t in H1 2026 per GAPKI with 2027 forecasts of US$1,650–1,720/t (PASPI). These support the agribusiness and gold legs, but they are commodity tails, not a group-level pricing event in autos.

**Read:** Q3's operating leverage came from **TAM growth in autos and motorcycles plus the mining-volume and gold-restart recovery**, not from pricing power. The 23% decline in Komatsu unit sales is the clearest evidence that Group revenue growth was *not* price-led.

The segment table supplied in the fact sheet is external-sales only (no inter-segment column captured), and the four lines do not add to consolidated revenue — they sum to **Rp 247,939 bn versus Rp 243,869 bn consolidated**, a difference of **Rp 4,070 bn** which represents intersegment eliminations. Two checks are worth stating. **Segment net profits sum exactly to the consolidated Rp 24,073 bn** — a clean tie-out. **Segment gross profits sum to Rp 52,639 bn, Rp 631 bn above the consolidated Rp 52,008 bn**, consistent with elimination of inter-segment margin. The structural insight is that **financial services generates 30% of group gross profit on 10.7% of segment revenue** while mining solutions generates 34% of gross profit on 36.4% of revenue at a 19.8% gross margin and only a 4.7% net margin. Any shift in the revenue mix toward automotive and financial services mechanically lifts the blended margin — which is precisely the Q3 story.

Customer concentration is low and disclosed: **no individual customer exceeded 10% of total net revenue** (Note 29). Related-party revenue totalled **Rp 21,132 bn, or 8.67% of net revenue** (9M 2025: Rp 20,744 bn, 8.52%), with the largest counterparties PT Astra Honda Motor (Rp 4,001 bn), Aegis Energy Trading (Rp 2,448 bn), Astra-KLK (Rp 2,185 bn) and PT Astra Daihatsu Motor (Rp 2,025 bn) — all joint ventures/associates of the Group, so these are structurally recurring rather than third-party dependence (Note 33b). **Domestic versus export revenue split is not disclosed** in the captured notes.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Three forward drivers, in order of measurability:

1. **Auto TAM.** With 679,020 units already wholesaled by September against an 850,000 target, Q4 needs ~171,000 units — a run-rate consistent with the September exit (+28% YoY). Management has publicly expressed confidence the target is achievable on new-model launches and stable financing ([Liputan6, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.liputan6.com/saham/read/8288941/penjualan-mobil-ri-naik-20-asii-kokoh-kuasai-pasar)). The offset is **share drift to ~49%** and the NEV mix risk: economists project NEVs at **31.7% of 2026 car sales**, and proposed incentives (up to 100% PPnBM exemption, 40% PPN DTP on certain BEVs, stimulus for ~500,000 electric motorcycles) cut both ways for a Toyota/Daihatsu-led line-up ([Antara, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5678821/ekonom-proyeksikan-pangsa-nev-capai-317-persen-penjualan-mobil-2026)).
2. **Mining volumes and RKAB.** This is the swing factor. Q3's recovery rests on Pamapersada contract-mining volumes and the Martabe ramp; the 2027 RKAB quota is not yet formalised, and ~50 companies were still awaiting 2026 revisions at end-September ([Kontan, 22 Sep 2026](https://insight.kontan.co.id/news/rkab-telat-hantui-emiten-jasa-tambang)). A further quota cut would reverse the Q3 mix benefit.
3. **Gold and CPO.** Martabe's ~65,000 oz 2026 target implies a heavy 2H weighting against 54,000 oz booked in 9M — so Q4 carries most of the gold leg. Gold at ~US$4,500/oz supports the realised price; a retracement would hit the highest-margin incremental tonne.

**Watch items for Q4:** (i) the ~Rp 171,000-unit Q4 wholesale requirement; (ii) whether group gold sales reach ~65,000 oz, which would require ~11,000 oz in Q4 alone on top of an already back-loaded 9M; (iii) the 2027 RKAB announcement; (iv) whether the "rp 2.96 trn" non-recurring charge recurs as a mark-to-market item. Post-period-end price moves in coal, gold and CPO are **not captured** in this run's inputs.

The pipeline's computed `gross_margin` is **0.213262** (52,008/243,869), against a recomputed prior-year 21.4473% — a **12 bps compression** for the nine months. The quarterly path is the interesting part: derived gross margin was **19.70% in Q1 2026, 21.40% in Q2 2026 and 22.75% in Q3 2026**, and **21.56% in Q3 2025**. So the cumulative −12 bps masks a **+119 bps YoY expansion in the third quarter alone**, on derived gross profit of Rp 19,556 bn versus Rp 17,414 bn in Q3 2025 (+12.3%).

**Interpretation.** A 12 bps cumulative compression is not an automotive margin problem — it is a mix artefact. Mining solutions carried 19.8% gross margin on 36.4% of segment revenue in 9M; its revenue fell ~10.08% YoY per Bisnis (external), mechanically depressing the blend even where each segment held. Q3's recovery reverses that as mining volumes returned and gold re-entered the sales line. **The honest framing is: gross margin expanded in Q3 because mix improved, not because any business earned a better unit margin.**

**The opex story is the least flattering part of this print.** Selling expense rose **22.5%** (Rp 10,412 bn from Rp 8,501 bn) and G&A **10.3%** (Rp 16,941 bn from Rp 15,356 bn) against revenue growth of 0.11% — negative operating leverage of ~154 bps at the derived operating line. Excluding the Rp 2.2 trn 2Q charge, the mining segment's 9M decline was −28% YoY rather than −59%. Q3 shows **no fresh charge**.

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

**Receivables.** Trade receivables gross rose to **Rp 31,213 bn** from Rp 29,255 bn (+6.7%), with the **provision for impairment up 35.5% to Rp 1,836 bn** from Rp 1,355 bn. Net trade receivables of **Rp 29,212 bn represent 93.6% of gross** — the coverage ratio deteriorated from 95.4%. The provision increase of Rp 855 bn is roughly 0.35% of revenue and deserves attention: it is a real P&L charge inside G&A or cost of revenue and it grew far faster than the receivables balance. **DSO on an annualised basis is 32.8 days versus 31.3 days a year ago (+1.5 days)**; on a non-annualised 9M revenue basis the DSO would be ~43 days, and the pipeline explicitly uses the annualised basis.

**Inventories.** Net inventories of **Rp 33,673 bn** (gross Rp 43,467 bn less provision Rp 1,092 bn) were essentially flat (−0.5%), but the mix moved: **finished goods fell to Rp 25,996 bn from Rp 27,178 bn** while **raw materials rose to Rp 2,566 bn from Rp 1,839 bn (+39.5%)** — consistent with restocking ahead of the gold and mining restart. Real estate/land of Rp 8,589 bn is a slow-moving block. **DIO is 37.8 days versus 38.0 a year ago** — flat, and the inventory provision actually saw a **net recovery of Rp 13 bn** in 2026 against a Rp 105 bn increase in 2025.

**Payables and the cash-cycle squeeze.** Trade payables **fell 9.5% to Rp 35,516 bn** from Rp 39,238 bn, with third-party Rupiah payables tumbling to Rp 27,049 bn from Rp 32,651 bn. Note 18 identifies the driver: payables to the **Komatsu group fell to the equivalent of Rp 14.2 trn from Rp 20.1 trn** — a Rp 5.9 trn reduction in supplier credit from a single counterparty. **DPO fell to 39.9 days from 44.1** (−4.2 days). Combined with DSO +1.5 and flat DIO, the **cash conversion cycle lengthened from 25.2 to 30.7 days (+5.5 days)** — a real, quantifiable working-capital drag that explains a meaningful part of the OCF decline.

**The unexplained items.** Two lines moved far more than their economic base:

- **Current accrued expenses: Rp 22,362 bn from Rp 16,096 bn (+38.9%), and +18.8% in the quarter alone.** Note 20 attributes the build to **production cost accruals of Rp 5,657 bn (from Rp 3,838 bn) and employee benefits of Rp 4,869 bn (from Rp 1,572 bn)** — the latter a **3.1× increase** that is not explained anywhere in the available disclosure. A dividend declared but unpaid is *not* the driver: Note 19 shows dividend payable of only Rp 254 bn (from Rp 117 bn). **This is the single largest unexplained balance-sheet movement in the print** and is carried as a must-explain in §11.1.
- **Prepaid taxes: Rp 2,632 bn from Rp 2,234 bn**, of which the **non-current portion jumped to Rp 7,187 bn from Rp 4,526 bn** — a 59% increase suggesting tax prepayments running well ahead of the current-year charge.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

There are three "core" businesses, and their economics could hardly be more different.

**Financial services — the margin engine (10.7% of segment revenue, 30.1% of gross profit, 30.3% of net profit).** External sales **Rp 26,446 bn**, gross profit **Rp 15,648 bn at a 59.17% margin**, net profit **Rp 7,290 bn at 27.56%**. The asset base is **Rp 145,007 bn — 27.6% of consolidated assets**. This business generates almost a third of group profit on a tenth of revenue, and it is the most defensible: the consumer financing book is **Rp 93,929 bn net before provision** (from Rp 90,070 bn, +4.3%), finance leases **Rp 12,276 bn** (from Rp 11,554 bn, +6.2%), with new financing at **Rp 92.5 trn (+8%)** and effective rates on new consumer receivables of **7.0%–45.6%**. Impairment on financing receivables rose to **Rp 5,359 bn from Rp 5,445 bn** — flat, which is a genuinely good signal against a growing book. The competitive risk is spread compression as Bank Indonesia cuts rates; the growth risk is that financing penetration tracks auto TAM.

**Automotive — the volume anchor (41.0% of segment revenue, 23.0% of gross profit, 42.1% of net profit).** External sales **Rp 101,679 bn**, gross profit **Rp 12,095 bn at 11.90%**, net profit **Rp 10,142 bn at 9.98%**. This segment earns the largest net profit on the thinnest gross margin in the group — it is a distribution and brand business, not a manufacturer. It carries **only Rp 69,272 bn of assets (13.2% of consolidated)**, so it is **capital-light and cash-generative**: a 9.98% net margin on a 13.2% asset share is the best asset-efficiency in the group. External evidence: **automotive net profit +9% YoY in 3Q26 and +8% for 9M26**, with share ~49% and the components arm (AUTO) +15% YoY.

**Mining solutions and heavy equipment — the cyclical problem child (36.4% of revenue, 34.0% of gross profit, 17.6% of net profit).** External sales **Rp 90,335 bn**, gross profit **Rp 17,891 bn at 19.80%** — a respectable gross margin — but **net profit of only Rp 4,238 bn, a 4.69% net margin**. The gap between 19.8% gross and 4.7% net is the impairment site of 2026: the Rp 2.76 trn geothermal writedown, the RKAB-driven volume collapse and the associated operating deleverage all land here. Note 28 shows **UNTR net revenue Rp 90,335 bn / profit Rp 4,219 bn**. It carries **Rp 165,519 bn of assets — 31.5% of the consolidated base — and returns only 17.6% of net profit.** That asset-to-profit mismatch is the central capital-allocation question on this balance sheet.

**Wider businesses — the emerging leg (11.9% of revenue, 13.3% of gross profit, 10.0% of net profit).** External sales **Rp 29,479 bn**, gross profit **Rp 7,005 bn at 23.76%**, net profit **Rp 2,403 bn at 8.15%**, assets **Rp 76,727 bn**. Contents: agribusiness (AALI, 79.68%, assets Rp 30,175 bn, 9M profit Rp 1,975 bn), toll roads (Astra Tol Nusantara, Marga Harjaya Infrastruktur valid to 2055, Marga Mandalasakti valid to 2059, concession rights carrying Rp 9,370 bn), property (Menara Astra), healthcare, digital and logistics property.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

- **The mining segment's capital intensity.** Rp 165.5 trn of assets producing Rp 4.2 trn of net profit (2.6% return) is the weakest capital allocation in the group. Whether this re-rates depends entirely on Martabe's ramp and the 2027 RKAB.
- **PT Supreme Energy Rantau Dedap.** The investment was **fully impaired by Rp 1,476 bn with Rp 1,277 bn of loans and interest receivables written off to nil as of 30 June 2026** (Note 31). A geometry asset that has been written down to zero; any recovery is pure optionality and none should be assumed.
- **PT Acset Indonusa (54.25%, assets Rp 2,607 bn).** A construction business with contract assets flagged in Note 29 as the main driver of group contract assets. Small, but historically the source of provisioning.
- **Restricted funds of Rp 1,492 bn** rising 53.7% with no disclosed purpose — flagged in §4.4 and §11.1.
- **Arafura Surya Alam.** Acquired 11 February 2026 for USD 540 mn / Rp 9.1 trn (Note 3). Its asset base is not separately visible in the subsidiary table, which may be a mapping gap; **the return on this acquisition is the single largest new capital commitment of 2026** and is not yet measurable.

### 7.4 Growth Options

1. **Martabe gold.** Targeting ~65,000 oz in 2026 on a resumption from end-May; **9M sales of 54,000 oz vs 178,000 oz** a year earlier mean the ramp is real but incomplete. Gold at ~US$4,500/oz (August 2026) makes each incremental ounce highly accretive. This is the highest-conviction near-term growth lever inside the mining segment.
2. **Arafura Surya Alam** — the Rp 9.1 trn gold acquisition, doubling down on the gold theme alongside Martabe.
3. **Financial services book growth.** New financing +8% to Rp 92.5 trn with impairment flat; a rate-cutting cycle would expand spreads on a Rp 93.9 trn consumer book.
4. **Toll roads.** Concession rights of Rp 9,370 bn across Marga Harjaya (to 2055) and Marga Mandalasakti (to 2059), with **ownership of Marga Mandalasakti up to 80.06%**. Traffic-volume-linked amortisation and revenue give a long-duration, inflation-linked cash flow.
5. **Healthcare, digital and logistics property** — collectively inside "wider businesses" at Rp 76.7 trn of assets; too small individually to move the group, but the highest-multiple assets if ever separated.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Related-party intensity is high and rising. **Related-party revenue of Rp 21,132 bn is 8.67% of net revenue** (from 8.52%), and **related-party purchases/payments of Rp 67,767 bn are 30.91% of cost of revenue plus opex** (from 29.46%) — a 145 bps increase in purchase dependence. Items to monitor:

- **Related-party trade payables rose to Rp 6,850 bn from Rp 5,587 bn (3.01% of total liabilities from 2.58%)** — while *third-party* payables fell Rp 3.7 trn. The Group is paying third parties faster and related parties slower; that could be entirely commercial (Komatsu terms) but it is a mix shift worth noting.
- **Related-party other receivables fell to Rp 1,764 bn from Rp 3,330 bn** (−47%), driven by **loans to related parties falling to Rp 1,231 bn from Rp 2,654 bn** and **PT Supreme Energy Rantau Dedap going to nil from Rp 1,142 bn** — the impairment working through.

- **Cash and cash equivalents at Bank Saqu** carry a related-party flag but no disclosed arm's-length pricing test; the deposit rates disclosed (Rupiah 2.75%–7.25%) are market-consistent.
- **PT Komatsu Astra Finance** supplies **Rp 685 bn of long-term other loans and lease liabilities** (from Rp 751 bn) at 9.50% — above the Group's average bank cost, a related-party funding cost worth monitoring.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — The accrued-expense and restricted-cash build is unexplained.** Current accrued expenses rose **38.9% to Rp 22,362 bn** (+18.8% in the quarter alone) and restricted cash **53.7% to Rp 1,492 bn**, both monotonically through the year, with **no external or internal disclosure captured** that attributes either. Note 20 attributes the accrual build to **production cost accruals (Rp 5,657 bn from Rp 3,838 bn) and employee benefits (Rp 4,869 bn from Rp 1,572 bn — a 3.1× increase)**. The employee-benefit line is the one to interrogate: a tripling in nine months with headcount actually *falling* from 133,667 to 131,076 is not obviously volume-linked, and could be severance, bonus timing, or a change in accrual basis. **A dividend explanation is ruled out** by the Rp 254 bn dividend payable in Note 19. If these accruals unwind through Q4 cash flow — which is the benign reading — Q4 OCF will be materially weaker than Q3's.

**MODERATE — Opex is growing four times faster than revenue.** Selling +22.48% and G&A +10.32% against +0.11% revenue is a genuine margin risk if it is structural rather than volume-linked. It is not explained in the filings captured.

**MODERATE — The cash-flow statement does not foot.** Reported net change of −Rp 393 bn against a Rp 67 bn actual increase in cash (a **Rp 460 bn gap**; Rp 346 bn in the prior year). Whether this is FX on cash or a mapping error, the net-change line cannot be used as a bridge, and the gross financing/investing flows — including dividends and the Arafura payment — are **not captured**, so consolidated financing cash flow cannot be reconstructed from the fact sheet.

**MODERATE — Q4 carries a heavy capex and volume burden simultaneously.** ~Rp 19 trn of the Rp 36 trn FY2026 capex budget is unspent, gold needs ~11,000 oz in Q4 to hit the 65,000 oz target, and autos need ~171,000 units to reach the 850,000 Gaikindo target. Missing any of these reduces the Q3-to-Q4 momentum the market is now expecting.

**LOW — UNTR reporting inconsistency.** External figures quoting UNTR 9M26 net profit at Rp 7.9 trn and attributable profit at Rp 4.47 trn cannot both reconcile to the Rp 2,471 bn group NCI line. Note 28's Rp 4,219 bn is the figure that does foot. Until UNTR's own statement is reconciled against the group consolidation, the NCI line carries a small uncertainty.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**HIGH — The mining segment's capital productivity.** Rp 165,519 bn of assets (31.5% of the consolidated base) generated Rp 4,238 bn of net profit in nine months — a ~2.6% annualised pre-impairment-fair return on segment assets, and lower after the geothermal writeoff. If Martabe does not ramp and RKAB volumes stay capped, this asset base is a persistent drag on group ROE and would eventually invite a strategic review.

**MODERATE — NCI normalisation is a structural headwind to owners' EPS.** Minorities hold 20.07% of equity but captured only 10.26% of profit in 9M. As UNTR's profit normalises, the NCI share returns toward ~18–20%, meaning **the parent will capture a smaller fraction of any group recovery than it did of the decline** — mechanically capping EPS upside.

**MODERATE — The equity-investment fair-value cycle can recur.** The Rp 2.96 trn of 9M non-recurring items were described as "penurunan nilai dan penyesuaian nilai wajar atas investasi ekuitas" — impairment *and fair-value adjustments on equity investments*. Mark-to-market is not a one-time event, and **Level 3 fair values grew to Rp 6,442 bn from Rp 5,972 bn**. A renewed equity-market drawdown reopens the same line.

**MODERATE — Nagging balance-sheet opacity in the securities block.** The pipeline's `securities_noncurrent` of Rp 18,210 bn does not tie to Note 5's non-current portion of Rp 25,608 bn, and the Rp 9,092 bn gap between balance-sheet loan subtotals (Rp 108,521 bn) and total borrowings (Rp 117,613 bn) requires a debt-securities bridge to close. These are mapping issues, but they mean **two different net-debt numbers (Rp 37,623 bn and Rp 64,925 bn) are both in circulation** and commentary quoting one without its basis will mislead.

**LOW — Rp 59.3 trn of fully depreciated assets still in use** understates the true maintenance capex requirement, meaning reported FCF overstates sustainable free cash.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

**HIGH — Coal RKAB policy.** The 2026 quota cuts drove Komatsu unit sales down 23% in January–July (large mining units −49%), and ~50 companies were still awaiting revision approvals in late September. **The 2027 RKAB is not yet announced.** Q3's segment recovery is directly exposed to it, and a repeat of 2026's quota discipline would undo the mix benefit that produced the margin recovery.

**MODERATE — EV transition and NEV mix shift.** Economists project NEVs at **31.7% of 2026 car sales**, and proposed incentives (up to 100% PPnBM exemption, 40% PPN DTP on certain BEVs, stimulus for ~500,000 electric motorcycles) favour BEV-native entrants more than a Toyota/Daihatsu-led ICE line-up. Astra's share already drifted from ~50% to ~49%. This is a mix and share risk, not a volume risk in the near term.

**MODERATE — Interest-rate and currency exposure.** **42.4% of borrowings are floating-rate** (from 38.0%), and **net FX liabilities after hedging moved to Rp 1,392 bn from net FX assets of Rp 11,989 bn** — a Rp 13.4 trn swing driven by the ~7% rupiah depreciation (USD 17,998 from 16,782; JPY 114 from 108). Note 38 states a further ~Rp 10 bn deterioration on a mark-to-period-end translation. Rising floating rates compress financial-services spreads at the same time as the book grows 10.7%.

**MODERATE — Pillar Two / GLOBE.** PER-6 (4 May 2026) implements PMK-136/2024; first filing for FY2025 is due 30 April 2027, extendable to 30 June 2027. Management states most entities operate above a 15% effective rate and expects no significant impact — but the consolidated ETR is **19.79%** and some subsidiaries (particularly in low-tax jurisdictions) could be affected. The Group has applied the exception on recognising deferred taxes for Pillar Two from 1 January 2025.

**LOW — PTAR licence history.** The 20 January 2026 revocation announcement affecting 28 companies (including PTAR) was reversed in substance; PTAR resumed operations as of 30 June 2026. A precedent for regulatory intervention in a key asset, now resolved.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

**HIGH — No valuation data, so no valuation anchor.** The market-data stub supplied no price, market cap, P/E, P/B or EV/EBITDA. **Every multiple in this note is either null or an externally-sourced reference**, and the recommendation in §14.4 rests on an external price and a P/E-anchored range rather than on a house valuation. **LOW — Conglomerate discount.** A 20% NCI layer, four segments of radically different economics, and a mining arm that consumes 31.5% of assets for 17.6% of profit justify a structural discount to sum-of-parts. Nothing in this print suggests a catalyst for narrowing it.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

1. **The 9M decline is not operational.** Of the Rp 6,809 bn pre-tax shortfall, **Rp 4,266 bn (63%) is `other_income_net`** — Rp 2.96 trn of non-recurring impairment and fair-value adjustments, booked entirely in H1. Excluding these, disclosed core profit of ~Rp 24.56 trn is **only ~1% below last year**. The reported −20.06% net profit and −10.58% EPS understate the underlying business by an order of magnitude.
2. **Q3 is a genuine inflection, not a base effect.** Derived Q3 revenue **+6.4% YoY** (after H1 at −3.04%), Q3 gross margin **22.75% vs 21.56%** a year earlier and 21.40% in Q2, Q3 pre-tax **+3.6% YoY**, and Q3 NCI **flat at ~Rp 1,660 bn** — the minority leak has stopped. The mining segment swung from ~Rp 200 bn to ~Rp 2.3 trn of net profit in one quarter.
3. **The mix shift is favourable and self-reinforcing.** Financial services (59.17% gross margin, 27.56% net) plus automotive (9.98% net on 13.2% of assets) are 72% of net profit on 54% of segment assets. As mining revenue recedes as a share, blended margin rises mechanically — and the financial-services book grew new financing +8% with impairment *flat* at Rp 5,359 bn.
4. **Minorities bore the pain; the parent will keep more of the recovery.** Effective stakes in UNTR/Pamapersada/Agincourt/Acset already rose to 64.64%/64.64%/61.41%/58.93% through UNTR's buyback alone, the AUTO tender moves ownership toward 84.95%, and 1.45% of the parent's own shares were retired in nine months.
5. **Balance sheet and cash generation are intact.** Net debt/equity 12.63%, undrawn facilities Rp 98.3 trn, FCF Rp 21.2 trn covering the dividend 1.35×, cash conversion 1.188× — and 9M profit is already **74% of FY2026 consensus** of Rp 29.1 trn, leaving a low Q4 hurdle of ~Rp 7.4 trn.

### 14.2 Bear Case

1. **The Q3 margin gain is a commodity and mix effect that annualises.** Gold at ~US$4,500/oz, a Martabe restart off a five-month suspension, and a coal-volume recovery from a quota-depressed base all flatter Q3 2026 versus a normalised 2027. **None of it is a unit-margin improvement.** 9M gross margin is still 12 bps *below* last year.
2. **Costs are out of control at the operating line.** Selling +22.48%, G&A +10.32%, derived operating margin −154 bps — on flat revenue. If this is structural (commission on a growing financing book, EV-related marketing, integration), the operating leverage story does not work.
3. **Two balance-sheet lines are accumulating without explanation.** Accrued expenses +38.9% (with employee-benefit accruals tripling) and restricted cash +53.7%. If the accruals are severance, bonus or cost provisions rather than timing, Q4 earnings and Q4 cash flow both disappoint. If the restricted cash is a funding collateral requirement that grows with the Rp 108.5 trn loan book, available liquidity is smaller than the 0.337 cash ratio implies.
4. **The mining segment still consumes 31.5% of assets for 17.6% of profit at a ~2.6% return**, and the 2027 RKAB is unset. Another quota year like 2026 and the recovery reverses.
5. **Capital returns exceed free cash flow.** Rp 15.7 trn of dividends plus ~Rp 2.8 trn of buybacks plus a Rp 9.1 trn acquisition, with **Rp 19 trn of the capex budget still to be spent in Q4** — funded at the margin by loans that already rose Rp 10.5 trn. Net debt/equity up 297 bps in nine months is the visible cost.
6. **Valuation offers no cushion.** At ~6.7× versus a 6.6× three-year average, the multiple is fair, not cheap; and the market's own verdict was a **−5.80% reversal the day after the results**.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**BUY, moderate risk.**

**Method and assumptions (stated explicitly because no market-data stub was supplied).** The target range is **P/E-anchored on annualised 9M 2026 EPS**:

- Base EPS: **Rp 721** = reported 9M EPS of Rp 541 × 4/3. This is arithmetic on the fact sheet, not a forecast.
- Applied multiple: **7.0×–7.5×**, i.e. a modest premium to the external 3-year average of 6.6× and the ~6.7× at the results, reflecting (i) a confirmed Q3 inflection in revenue, gross margin and NCI, (ii) the removal of the H1 non-recurring charge from the forward base, and (iii) a low Q4 consensus hurdle.
- **Target price range: Rp 5,050–5,400.**
- Reference point for the upside: external close of **Rp 4,710 on 29 October 2026** — an implied **+7% to +15%** total-price return, plus the **~8.3% yield** implied by the Rp 390 FY2025 DPS at that price (all illustrative on external data; to be re-based when the market-data stub is available).

**Why BUY rather than HOLD.** Three reasons. First, **the earnings base is being misread by the headline**: a 20% net profit decline that is 63% one non-cash H1 charge, against core profit down ~1%, is not a deteriorating business — and the market's −5.80% reversal suggests it may not have fully absorbed that distinction. Second, **the Q3 evidence is broad, not a single line**: revenue, gross margin, pre-tax profit, NCI, collections and cash conversion all improved together, across three independent segments. Third, **the distribution floor is real**: 1.35× FCF cover on a ~8% yield, with a Rp 98.3 trn undrawn facility buffer, means downside is supported by cash return rather than by narrative.

**Why the risk qualifier is "moderate" and not "low."** The unexplained accrual and restricted-cash builds, the 2027 RKAB, the fact that **this note contains no house valuation because the data was not supplied**, and the possibility that opex growth is structural rather than volume-linked are all material. Three of the four are disclosure or policy risks, not solvency risks — hence moderate rather than high.
