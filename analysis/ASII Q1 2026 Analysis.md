**Reporting basis.** Consolidated interim financial statements for the three months ended 31 March 2026, **unaudited**, authorised for issue by the Board of Directors on 29 April 2026. All figures in **billions of Rupiah (Rp bn)** unless otherwise stated; per-share amounts are in full Rupiah and dividend totals in trillions. ## 1. Executive Summary

Astra's Q1 2026 is a story of one segment overwhelming an otherwise resilient conglomerate. Revenue fell 5.6% year-on-year to Rp 78,668 bn and gross profit fell 9.2% to Rp 15,494 bn, but pre-tax profit dropped 24.2% to Rp 8,069 bn and net profit 24.9% to Rp 6,423 bn. The entire decline is traceable to the heavy-equipment, mining, construction and energy (HEMCE) segment, where revenue fell 16.7% to Rp 28,554 bn, gross profit fell 31.7% to Rp 4,828 bn and segment profit collapsed 79.1% to Rp 691 bn from Rp 3,305 bn. The two proximate causes are external and documented: the suspension of the Martabe gold mine (PT Agincourt Resources), which cut group gold sales to 4,000 oz from 57,000 oz, and a lower national coal RKAB quota for 2026 that reduced Komatsu heavy-equipment deliveries by 20% to 1,107 units and Pamapersada overburden removal by 7% to 236 mn bcm (source: United Tractors Q1 2026 business report, IDX, [link](https://www.idx.co.id/StaticData/NewsAndAnnouncement/ANNOUNCEMENTSTOCK/From_EREP/202604/511de0c779_5fa69412b9.pdf)). Everything else — financial services (+8.7% gross profit), agribusiness (+24.1%), automotive segment profit (+2.5% despite lower revenue), infrastructure, IT and property — grew.

We explicitly **reject the "margin increased" framing** implied by the deterministic ratio flags. On every externally verifiable baseline, both margins fell: gross margin 19.70% vs 20.47% in Q1 2025 (−77 bps) and against FY2025 audited gross margin of 22.09% (revenue Rp 323,392 bn, COGS Rp 251,948 bn, gross profit Rp 71,444 bn per the IDX filing of 27 February 2026, [link](https://www.idx.co.id/StaticData/NewsAndAnnouncement/ANNOUNCEMENTSTOCK/From_EREP/202502/20260227003847-59490-0/Astra%20Account%20December%202025.pdf)); net margin 8.16% vs 10.26% (−210 bps) and against ~10.1% for FY2025. Our segment-weighted decomposition (detailed in §3.1) attributes **+22 bps to mix** — financial services and agribusiness gaining weight as HEMCE shrank — and **−96 bps to segment-level rate**, of which HEMCE alone contributes −134 bps. There is no pricing-power story in this quarter; there is a mix-and-volume story.

The balance sheet grew 2.1% to Rp 517,804 bn, funded almost entirely by debt: total balance-sheet loans rose 9.6% to Rp 107,409 bn while cash fell 6.8% to Rp 49,046 bn, lifting fact-sheet net debt from Rp 28,085 bn at 31 December 2025 to Rp 40,562 bn and net-debt-to-equity from 9.7% to 13.8%. On the company's own Note 33 basis (which includes lease liabilities and debt securities, total borrowings Rp 116,835 bn), gearing rose to 23% from 20% and, critically, the **non-financial-services block flipped from net cash of Rp 7,218 bn to net debt of Rp 1,814 bn** — the first time in recent memory that the industrial core has not been self-funding. That flip was driven by the Rp 12,560 bn investing outflow against only Rp 2,678 bn of PP&E capex, i.e. an acquisition, not maintenance or growth capex.

Operating cash flow halved to Rp 6,274 bn (from Rp 13,381 bn), a 97.7% cash-conversion ratio that looks adequate only because net profit itself fell; collections were 99.8% of revenue, so this is an earnings problem, not a collection problem. Free cash flow fell to Rp 3,596 bn (FCF margin 4.6%) from Rp 8,957 bn (10.7%). Working capital was the offset — receivables and inventories both fell — but payables fell faster, worsening the cash cycle by 1.3 days to 25.9 days.

Valuation: **no market data was provided for this run**, so P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA and dividend yield are not computed and are not estimated. For orientation only, book value per share is Rp 5,809 and annualised Q1 EPS is Rp 584. FY2025 dividends of Rp 390 per share (Rp 98 interim paid 31 October 2025; Rp 292 final payable 25 May 2026, record date 6 May 2026) are a stated fact but their yield is not computable without a price. **Recommendation: HOLD with an accumulate bias** — the franchise is intact and the drag is identifiable and, per post-period reporting, partly reversible (Martabe restart guided for May 2026), but the Q1 evidence does not yet prove that HEMCE normalises rather than re-rates lower, and one-off charges recurred into H1 2026.

Revenue declined Rp 4,693 bn. On a segment basis (Note 30, pre-elimination, external sales) the decline is entirely HEMCE: −Rp 5,707 bn. Automotive was essentially flat at −Rp 135 bn, financial services added Rp 542 bn, agribusiness Rp 478 bn, IT Rp 39 bn and property Rp 133 bn, while infrastructure lost Rp 102 bn. Eliminations were −Rp 629 bn of revenue in Q1 2026 versus −Rp 688 bn a year earlier.

Revenue recognised from contracts with customers was Rp 68,917 bn (point-in-time Rp 55,746 bn; over time Rp 13,171 bn) against Rp 74,062 bn, with the balance of revenue from financial services (Rp 8,493 bn) and rental (Rp 1,258 bn) — Note 27. Insurance revenue embedded in the total was Rp 2,500 bn (Q1 2025: Rp 2,400 bn).

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The volume/price split is legible only at segment level, and it differs sharply by segment.

**HEMCE — volume, not price.** Komatsu heavy-equipment sales fell 20% to 1,107 units with mining accounting for roughly 50% of the mix; Pamapersada overburden removal fell 7% to 236 mn bcm; coal sales rose to 4.0 mn t from 3.2 mn t but could not offset the gold and equipment shortfall. Gold sales collapsed 93% to 4,000 oz from 57,000 oz because there were no Martabe sales after the operation was suspended (United Tractors Q1 2026 business report, IDX, and [wartaekonomi](https://wartaekonomi.co.id/read610037/bisnis-tambang-grup-astra-tertekan-di-kuartal-i-2026-united-tractors-hingga-emas-martabe-turun-laba)). The regulatory cause is the lower 2026 national coal production quota under RKAB, which cut mining customers' capex and contracting volumes ([detikFinance](https://finance.detik.com/bursa-dan-valas/d-8675303/produksi-batu-bara-penjualan-alat-berat-untr-turun-terkendala-rkab)); the price backdrop was not supportive either, with the HBA high-CV 6,322 GAR benchmark set at US$102.87/t for the second February 2026 period and the ministry's HBA grid falling across grades ([Bisnis](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20260218/44/1953601/harga-batu-bara-acuan-hba-periode-kedua-februari-2026-kompak-turun)). This is a double volume hit with a negative price overlay.

**Gold — a price tailwind fully neutralised by zero volume.** Gold traded at record levels through the quarter: US$4,432/oz on 5 January 2026, then a historic US$5,583.88/oz on 29 January 2026 ([detik.com](https://www.detik.com/jateng/bisnis/d-8331182/harga-emas-dunia-hari-ini-cetak-rekor-tertinggi-sepanjang-sejarah)), with World Gold Council data showing Q1 2026 demand value at a record US$193 bn, +74% year-on-year on roughly flat tonnage ([[Gold May 2026 Update]]). Losing ~53,000 oz of sales into a ~US$5,000/oz market is on the order of US$2.6 bn of forgone revenue — the single most expensive operational interruption of the quarter. The zero-cost collar derivative liability of Rp 5,793 bn on gold sales (Note 19; total derivative liabilities Rp 5,811 bn vs Rp 3,606 bn) further caps the recovery upside on whatever volume returns.

**Automotive — market share, not market size.** Gaikindo wholesales *rose* 1.7% to 209,021 units in Q1 2026, yet Astra group share slipped to roughly 47–49% (~101,613 units) from the >50% level historically held ([Kompas](https://otomotif.kompas.com/read/2026/04/14/080200615/otomotif-grup-astra-tergerus--toyota-daihatsu-isuzu-turun)). Toyota-Astra Motor described a ~14% sequential correction from February to March 2026, partly Lebaran timing ([Kontan](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/toyota-ungkap-penjualan-mobil-pada-kuartal-i-2026-alami-penurunan-begini-alasannya)). Two-wheeler (AISI) volumes fell 4.1% to 1.68 mn units. So automotive's flat-to-down revenue against a growing four-wheel market is a share and mix event, and price competition from Chinese entrants is the working hypothesis (medium confidence; no company disclosure of discount levels).

**Financial services and agribusiness — volume and price both supportive.** Consumer financing receivables grew to Rp 87,715 bn from Rp 85,144 bn and finance lease receivables to Rp 11,901 bn from Rp 11,554 bn (Note 7), so the +6.8% revenue growth is book growth plus stable yields (new Rupiah consumer financing rates 7.0%–44.7%). Agribusiness revenue rose 6.8% on CPO strength: CPO was MYR 4,574/t, +12.94% year-to-date as of late April 2026, with the April 2026 export-levy reference price set at US$989.63/MT ([Kontan](https://investasi.kontan.co.id/news/kinerja-emiten-sawit-tak-merata-saat-harga-cpo-naik-cek-rekomendasinya); [Antara](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5504165/permintaan-global-dan-geopolitik-dorong-kenaikan-harga-cpo)).

**FX.** The rupiah closed the quarter at Rp 16,993/USD (fact sheet exchange-rate note). Q1 2026 realised FX effects are not separately disclosed; the group's post-hedge net foreign-currency position collapsed to Rp 1,243 bn-equivalent from Rp 11,989 bn (Note 36) — a swing worth flagging but **not** convertible into a revenue conclusion without the FX line, which is not captured in the fact sheet.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

No single customer exceeded 10% of net revenue (Note 27) and no single third-party supplier exceeded 10% of net revenue (Note 28), so there is no disclosed concentration risk at the top of the book. Related-party revenue nevertheless rose to Rp 7,713 bn, **9.80% of total net revenue** (Q1 2025: Rp 7,324 bn, 8.79%), with the largest counterparties Aegis Energy Trading (Rp 1,689 bn, 2.16%), PT Astra Honda Motor (Rp 1,249 bn, 1.59%) and Astra-KLK (Rp 855 bn, 1.09%). Related-party purchases and payments were Rp 20,982 bn, **29.00% of cost of revenue plus operating expenses** (Q1 2025: Rp 22,343 bn, 30.14%), led by AHM (Rp 7,905 bn, 10.93%) and Toyota-Astra Motor (Rp 6,713 bn, 9.28%) — Note 31c. The group is a vertically integrated ecosystem; roughly three-tenths of its cost base is transacted with entities it does not consolidate. That is structurally normal for Astra and not a red flag, but it means consolidated margin is partly a function of transfer pricing with JVs whose earnings also arrive as associate income.

Compositionally, the quarter marks a visible rotation: HEMCE fell from 40.8% to 36.0% of segment revenue and from 41.3% to 31.0% of segment gross profit, while financial services rose to 32.2% of gross profit (from 26.9%) and agribusiness to 7.5% (from 5.5%). The group is becoming, marginally, more of a financial-services and consumer story and less of a commodities story — but the rotation is being forced by HEMCE weakness, not chosen.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Three quarters of the forward revenue question reduce to HEMCE, and the news is mixed-to-better. Martabe received Ministry of Environment approval to resume operations in March 2026 ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20260326154648-4-721644/tambang-emas-martabe-diizinkan-beroperasi-lagi-ini-reaksi-agincourt)), but mining had not restarted at that date, and United Tractors guided a **May 2026** restart ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20260416163323-4-727366/sempat-disetop-tambang-emas-martabe-beroperasi-lagi-mei-2026)) — after the quarter closed. That makes the gold line a Q2 event, not a Q1 recovery. Set against a record gold price and a new gold asset (Doup, §7.4), the gold franchise should be revenue-accretive from Q2 — but note that post-period reporting shows precisely this pattern: H1 2026 revenue of Rp 157.9 trn (−3% YoY) with net profit Rp 12.53 trn (−19%), i.e. only partial recovery, alongside Rp 2.4 trn of non-recurring fair-value/impairment losses ([Bisnis](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20260730/192/1992424/astra-asii-mampu-cetak-laba-rp1253-triliun-di-semester-i2026)). **This is post-period evidence and is used only as an outcome check, not as an in-period explanation.**

The coal/RKAB constraint is structural rather than a one-quarter air pocket: the quota mechanism governs customers' capex and therefore Komatsu unit sales and Pamapersada volumes for the whole of 2026. Automotive revenue sustainability depends on whether share stabilises in the high-40s; financial services revenue should keep compounding with the receivables book but is rate-sensitive on funding cost. Watch items for Q2: (i) the Martabe restart date and the realised volume; (ii) whether Komatsu unit sales stabilise after the −20% quarter; (iii) any RKAB revision; (iv) whether free-cash-flow generation returns above Rp 8 trn/quarter, which is the threshold at which the non-financial net-debt flip reverses.

The FY2025 comparison matters for anyone anchoring on the annual number: at 22.09% for FY2025, the Q1 2026 print of 19.70% is 239 bps below the prior full year, so there is no defensible reading in which gross margin improved.

### 3.2 Operating Margin Analysis

**Important limitation:** the fact sheet carries no `operating_profit` line and the ratios block returns `operating_margin: null`. The Rp 6,310 bn figure above is an analyst derivation from three disclosed lines and **does not bridge to reported PBT**: 6,310 − 923 (interest expense) − 19 (other income, net) = 5,368 vs reported PBT of 8,069, a Rp 2,701 bn gap. Part of that gap is share of JV/associate results (Rp 1,902 bn per Note 30 as reported in the research brief), leaving ~Rp 799 bn unexplained — most plausibly gross interest income from the financing book (the fact sheet's `interest_income: 0.0` appears to be a capture gap: Note 31d states related-party interest income of Rp 75 bn equals 9.06% of total interest income, implying total interest income of roughly Rp 828 bn) plus FX. The derived operating margin should therefore be read as a **trend indicator only**, and the true operating line is an open item.

G&A is the under-flagged swing of the quarter. It rose Rp 1,142 bn (+23.4%), roughly six times the Rp 200 bn increase in selling expense, and consumed 7.65% of revenue versus 5.85% a year earlier. External evidence points to a one-off/structural cocktail rather than pure inflation: Rp 723 bn of non-recurring charges booked in United Tractors' nickel and geothermal businesses in the quarter ([wartaekonomi](https://wartaekonomi.co.id/read610037/bisnis-tambang-grup-astra-tertekan-di-kuartal-i-2026-united-tractors-hingga-emas-martabe-turun-laba)), plus consolidation of a newly acquired subsidiary (Doup, §7.4) and idle-site costs at Martabe with no offsetting revenue. No public source breaks the G&A line down; the split remains **unverified** and is on our list of items to resolve.

The headline observation is how *stable* finance cost is: Rp 923 bn of interest expense against Rp 116,835 bn of gross borrowings implies a blended cost of roughly 3.2% annualised — plausible for a book that is majority Rupiah bank debt at 4.1%–7.1% but heavily diluted by cheap syndicated USD facilities at Term SOFR + 0.68%–0.95% and by AAA-rated multifinance bond coupons of 4.75%–6.90%. The JIBOR discontinuation after 31 December 2025 and the transition to Compounded IndONIA (Note 33) moved most floating exposure onto a benchmark that was falling, which is the single best explanation for financial services' margin expansion (§3.1). **Hypothesis, medium confidence:** the FS margin gain is funding-cost-led rather than yield-led, and therefore reverses if IndONIA rises.

Other income net was a small charge of Rp 19 bn (Q1 2025: −Rp 15 bn), composed per Note 29 of administration income on vehicles Rp 293 bn, commission income Rp 101 bn, gain on sale of fixed assets Rp 55 bn, and other expenses net of Rp 468 bn. Commission income jumped to Rp 50 bn in related-party terms (from Rp 6 bn) received from Isuzu Astra Motor and Toyota-Astra Motor (Note 31e) — a material one-off to verify and probably not recurring.

The group also carries an insurance book measured under the PSAK 74 / IFRS 17 contract-boundary model (FCF plus CSM, LRC and LIC including IBNR), with no amounts broken out in the portions of the notes captured here. That is a modelling opacity rather than a profitability concern at this scale.

The PBT margin fell more than twice as far as the net margin, because the effective tax rate rose 76 bps to 20.40%. Taxable income is stated to be based on preliminary calculations, and deferred tax assets of Rp 1.3 trn on Rp 6.1 trn of unused tax losses (expiring up to 2031) remain unrecognised — i.e. management itself does not expect to monetise those losses.

The gap between the 24.9% net-profit decline and the 15.6% owners'-profit decline is the NCI collapse: NCI absorbed 35.0% of the incremental decline. NCI's share of group profit fell to 8.9% from 19.0%. As shown in §4.5, this is almost entirely the United Tractors identity.

Liquidity is adequate but no longer comfortable at the margin. The current ratio of 1.21x and quick ratio of 1.02x mean the group's inventory and receivable balances barely cover its current liabilities before cash — and current liabilities rose 2.1% while current assets were flat, driven by a Rp 8,670 bn (+15.2%) increase in current loans to Rp 65,742 bn. Cash fell 6.8% and the cash ratio dropped to 0.32x.

Two caveats. First, a large share of current assets is financing receivables (Rp 52,227 bn current per Note 7) generated by the financial-services subsidiaries, which are matched by borrowings and are not liquid in the conventional sense; the "current ratio" for this group overstates true liquidity. Second, the residual "other current assets" line of Rp 79,570 bn is not itemised in the fact sheet and is where financing receivables, other receivables and derivative assets sit.

**Data discrepancy flagged:** the fact sheet carries inventories at Rp 30,430 bn, while Note 10 in the source notes describes net inventories of Rp 39,137 bn (from Rp 42,506 bn) with finished goods at Rp 23,515 bn. The Rp 8.7 trn gap looks like a current/non-current split (or a captive-financing reclassification), not an error in the total group inventory base. We use the fact sheet for ratio purposes and flag that DIO of 35.3 days may understate true inventory days by 8–10 days.

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

**Receivables.** Gross trade receivables were Rp 28,523 bn with allowance of Rp 1,404 bn, net Rp 27,119 bn, of which Rp 27,023 bn current (Note 6). DSO on an annualised basis was **31.3 days**, deteriorating 0.9 days from 30.5 days at 31 December 2025. A Rp 4.3 trn over-60-day bucket against a Rp 1.4 trn allowance implies a coverage ratio of ~32% on the impaired bucket — acceptable for a book that is largely secured automotive and heavy-equipment trade terms, but the trend is the wrong direction.

**Inventories.** DIO improved 1.75 days to **35.3 days**, with finished goods falling to Rp 23,515 bn from Rp 27,178 bn. The provision was flat at Rp 1,121 bn (increase Rp 3 bn, write-off Rp 1 bn). No inventories are pledged as collateral; Rp 22.2 trn is insured. Given HEMCE's demand slump, falling finished-goods inventory is a positive — the group did not build into the downturn.

**Payables and other.** Trade payables fell 10.4% to Rp 35,140 bn, and DPO shortened 2.2 days to **40.8 days** — the group paid suppliers faster than it collected, which directly cost cash. Third-party payables include the Komatsu group at USD 0.5 mn plus Rp 16.0 trn, secured by letters of credit (Note 18). The cash conversion cycle therefore **worsened 1.3 days to 25.9 days** (DSO 31.3 + DIO 35.3 − DPO 40.8).

**Other receivables — a real flag.** Note 31i shows other receivables from related parties jumping to **Rp 8,132 bn from Rp 3,330 bn**, of which Rupiah Rp 5,897 bn (from Rp 1,108 bn) and foreign currency Rp 2,235 bn (from Rp 2,222 bn). This is most plausibly declared-but-unpaid JV dividends (AHM paid the group Rp 3,894 bn of dividends in the period), i.e. a timing item, not a credit event — but it also mechanically inflates total receivables and should reverse in Q2. Total other receivables in the maximum-credit-exposure table rose to Rp 11,289 bn from Rp 6,362 bn (Note 33).

**Other advances** of Rp 4,294 bn (from Rp 4,168 bn) and **prepaid taxes** of Rp 2,424 bn (from Rp 2,234 bn) are small and stable.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

There is no longer a single crown jewel; there are two, and their trajectories diverged violently this quarter.

**Financial services — the new profit engine.** Segment revenue Rp 8,537 bn (+6.8%), gross profit Rp 5,015 bn (+8.7%, a 58.7% gross margin), segment net profit Rp 2,310 bn (+5.8%), assets Rp 139,078 bn (+3.2%). Financing receivables were Rp 99,616 bn (from Rp 96,698 bn): consumer financing Rp 87,715 bn and finance lease Rp 11,901 bn. Consumer financing is secured by Motor Vehicle Ownership Certificates, lent at 7.0%–44.7% for Rupiah, written off once overdue beyond 150 days, and measured for impairment on a three-stage expected-credit-loss model. Only Rp 125 bn of consumer receivables is pledged for bank loans. This segment now generates **36.0% of group net profit on 10.8% of revenue** and is the single largest profit contributor. Its constraint is funding cost, not demand.

**Automotive and mobility — the resilient scale franchise.** Revenue Rp 32,958 bn (−0.4%), gross profit Rp 3,738 bn (11.3% margin), segment net profit Rp 2,465 bn (+2.5%), assets Rp 75,474 bn. Segment profit *grew* on flat revenue despite losing ~1–3 pp of national market share, which suggests mix and cost discipline rather than pricing. The JV earnings inside this (AHM profit Rp 2,350 bn from Rp 2,480 bn; ADM profit Rp 853 bn from Rp 674 bn, with ADM revenue Rp 15,441 bn from Rp 15,096 bn and total comprehensive income Rp 897 bn from Rp 700 bn, Note 11) were mixed: two-wheelers softened on an AISI −4.1% market and AHM's carrying value fell sharply on a Rp 3,894 bn dividend upstream, while Daihatsu improved materially.

**HEMCE — the drag.** Revenue Rp 28,554 bn (−16.7%), gross profit Rp 4,828 bn (−31.7%, margin 16.9% from 20.6%), segment net profit Rp 691 bn (−79.1%), assets Rp 165,608 bn (+3.2%). Within it: United Tractors revenue Rp 28,554 bn and profit Rp 643–685 bn (vs Rp 3,187–3,298 bn); Agincourt/Martabe with essentially zero Q1 sales; Pamapersada at 236 mn bcm overburden (−7%); and gold, nickel and coal mining properties of Rp 12,151 bn, Rp 5,362 bn and Rp 4,333 bn respectively. HEMCE still holds 36.7% of group assets and generates only 10.8% of group net profit — the worst return on assets of any large segment this quarter.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

**Martabe's licence.** PT Agincourt Resources was among 28 companies whose business licences were listed as revoked in a 20 January 2026 Minister of the State Secretariat press release; PTAR states it has not received official written notification. On 11 February 2026 the Minister of Energy and Mineral Resources said the government would evaluate PTAR's licence; the Ministry of Environment approved resumption in March 2026 (reported 26 March); mining had not restarted at that date; United Tractors guided a May 2026 restart ([CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20260326154648-4-721644/tambang-emas-martabe-diizinkan-beroperasi-lagi-ini-reaksi-agincourt); [CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20260416163323-4-727366/sempat-disetop-tambang-emas-martabe-beroperasi-lagi-mei-2026)). Management states no material impact on the 31 March 2026 or 31 December 2025 financial statements. The reduction to 4,000 oz of group gold sales versus 57,000 oz suggests the impact ran through revenue rather than through a provision — i.e. lost margin, not a write-down. This is a live regulatory overhang: the licence was revoked once on an environmental/regulatory trigger, and nothing disclosed precludes a recurrence.

**Suspended/idled assets.** No other material idle asset is disclosed. The Group states that until 31 March 2026 no business combination occurred (§4.4 flag), while 2025 acquisitions were PT Pratista Industrial Properti Satu and Dua (100% each, warehousing, Rp 1.3 trn) and 83.67% of PT Mega Manunggal Property (September 2025, plus 7.76% in December, total consideration Rp 4.1 trn, effective 91.43%).

**Unrecognised tax assets.** Rp 1.3 trn of deferred tax assets on Rp 6.1 trn of accumulated losses (from Rp 5.7 trn) remain unrecognised, expiring up to 2031. The carriers of those losses are not disclosed, but the fact that management has not recognised them is an explicit statement that it does not expect those entities to become profitable in time — a subsidiary-level value question.

**PT Agincourt environmental remediation.** A reclamation provision of only Rp 95 bn sits on the balance sheet (from Rp 105 bn). That is thin relative to a 15,649 bn asset base and to Indonesian mining rehabilitation practice, and it is worth monitoring if the licence review reopens.

### 7.4 Growth Options

**Doup gold project (PT Arafura Surya Alam).** Acquired 12 February 2026 for USD 540 mn (~Rp 8.85 trn) via UT/Danusa at 99.99%. ~38.2 mn t at 1.28 g/t (~1.6 mn oz) with a designed 3 mn t/yr plant. This is a genuine second gold leg, materially larger than the Martabe exposure in reserve terms, and it lands in a record gold price environment. Against that: it is non-producing (Q1 depreciation of Rp 210 bn on mining properties confirms it), it adds ~37% NCI leakage at the UT level, and the zero-cost collars cap upside. It is a 2027–2028 earnings story, not a 2026 one.

**Financial services book growth.** Consumer financing receivables grew Rp 2.6 trn in a quarter; new-issue bond coupons of 4.75%–5.90% (Federal International Finance VII Tahap III, SAN Finance V Tahap II) are below the existing book's average, so incremental spread should be positive. This is the most reliable compounder in the group.

**Agribusiness.** CPO at MYR 4,574/t (+12.9% YTD) with an April 2026 export reference price of US$989.63/MT. Segment gross profit rose 24.1% on 6.8% revenue growth — operating leverage is working. Bearer plants of Rp 7,365 bn at a 20-year straight-line life, uninsured, no borrowing cost capitalised in 2026 (vs Rp 7 bn in FY2025), so the segment is self-funding.

**Digital mobility and healthcare.** PT Astra Digital Mobil: Toyota Motor Asia (Singapore) acquired 40% for USD 120 mn (~Rp 2.0 trn); ADI retained 60% and control (Note 3b(ii)). That is an external validation of the digital mobility asset at a meaningful mark, and a rare disclosed fair value for a non-listed Astra business — worth remembering as a sum-of-the-parts anchor.

**Infrastructure.** Segment revenue fell 13.7% to Rp 641 bn but gross profit rose to Rp 434 bn (67.7% margin, from 52.5%) and segment profit +28.8% to Rp 385 bn. Toll concessions run to 2055 (Marga Harjaya Infrastruktur) and 2059 (Marga Mandalasakti). Capital commitments of only Rp 12 bn — essentially complete.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

The group's economics are heavily intermediated by JVs it does not consolidate. Related-party revenue was 9.80% of the total (Rp 7,713 bn) and related-party purchases 29.00% of cost of revenue plus opex (Rp 20,982 bn). The top purchase counterparties are the automotive manufacturers themselves — AHM Rp 7,905 bn (10.93%) and Toyota-Astra Motor Rp 6,713 bn (9.28%) — meaning roughly one-fifth of the cost base is paid to two 50/50-style JVs whose **own profits flow back as associate income, not as consolidated margin**. This is the central accounting subtlety of the Astra model: consolidated gross margin is structurally depressed by buying from JVs at transfer prices and then augmented below the operating line by Rp 1,902 bn of equity-accounted results. Any margin analysis that ignores the associate line misreads the business.

Items to monitor in the related-party book:
- **Other receivables from related parties up to Rp 8,132 bn from Rp 3,330 bn** (1.57% of total assets), driven by a Rp 5,074 bn dividend receivable and AHM's balance rising from Rp 128 bn to Rp 3,961 bn. Verify this is declared-dividend timing and not an artefact.
- **Commission income from Isuzu Astra Motor and Toyota-Astra Motor of Rp 50 bn versus Rp 6 bn** — a one-off to confirm.
- **Trade payables to related parties fell to Rp 4,901 bn (2.18% of total liabilities) from Rp 5,587 bn**, with Toyota-Astra Motor down from Rp 679 bn to Rp 191 bn and Daihatsu from Rp 1,008 bn to Rp 732 bn.
- **Net finance lease receivables from related parties** of Rp 57 bn (0.01% of total assets), from Rp 63 bn.
- **Long-term other loans and lease liabilities to related parties** of Rp 793 bn, of which Rp 785 bn is PT Komatsu Astra Finance (Note 31m) — a related-party financier inside the group's own dealer network.
- **Post-employment plans:** payments to Dana Pensiun Astra 1 and 2 totalled Rp 206 bn, or 2.92% of employee benefit expenses (from Rp 206 bn / 2.99%). Employee benefit obligations rose to Rp 11,816 bn from Rp 11,167 bn with the current portion flat at Rp 1,213 bn (Note 21).
- **Cash at PT Bank Saqu Indonesia** (a JV/associate) of Rp 1,709 bn, 0.33% of total assets — a related-party deposit that is immaterial but should be disclosed as such.

Eliminations were −Rp 629 bn of revenue, Rp 544 bn of cost of revenue, −Rp 85 bn of gross profit and −Rp 2,994 bn of assets in Q1 2026 (2025: −688, 616, −72, −2,703). The small elimination base confirms that most intercompany trade runs through **unconsolidated JVs**, whose transactions eliminate only on the equity-accounted line, not in the consolidation.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

- **Balance sheet capacity remains substantial.** Total equity of Rp 293,123 bn against net debt of Rp 40,562 bn (fact-sheet basis) or Rp 67,789 bn (company basis) is gearing of 13.8%–23%. Even after the Doup acquisition and the Rp 8.7 trn increase in current loans, ASII is not a leveraged company.
- **Unused credit facilities of Rp 90.7 trn** — nearly two-thirds of the entire gross borrowing base — including Rp 7.4 trn of supplier-financing lines. Refinancing risk is low despite the 61% current maturity profile.
- **Cash generation even in a bad quarter.** Rp 6,274 bn of OCF and Rp 3,596 bn of FCF in a quarter with a 79% profit decline in the largest segment, a 93% gold volume collapse and 23% higher G&A. The group did not burn cash on an operating basis.
- **Collections discipline.** 99.8% collections-to-revenue, DIO improved 1.8 days, working capital reduced Rp 3,209 bn, and no inventory pledged. Revenue is converting to cash on a like-for-like basis.
- **Earnings diversification and low single-segment dependency.** Seven reportable segments, no customer above 10% of revenue, no supplier above 10% of revenue, and no segment above 40% of gross profit. A 79% profit collapse in the biggest industrial segment still left the group at an 8.2% net margin.
- **Hard, disclosed asset values.** Book value per share of Rp 5,809; JV/associate investments of Rp 70,551 bn carried on the equity method; and an external mark on a digital asset (40% of PT Astra Digital Mobil to Toyota Motor Asia for USD 120 mn / ~Rp 2.0 trn).

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- **Auto segment profit grew despite losing share.** Segment net profit +2.5% to Rp 2,465 bn on revenue that fell 0.4% in a market that grew 1.7% — i.e. the segment absorbed volume loss without margin destruction, implying pricing and cost discipline.
- **Financial services is compounding profitably.** Revenue +6.8%, gross profit +8.7%, net profit +5.8%, receivables +3.0%, at a 58.7% gross margin. The book is secured, seasoned and appropriately provisioned (allowance Rp 5,030 bn on Rp 87,715 bn of net consumer receivables).
- **Agribusiness operating leverage is working.** Gross profit +24.1% on revenue +6.8% — 220 bps of margin expansion from CPO strength and self-funding (nil borrowing cost capitalised in 2026).
- **Rapid, disclosed incident response at Martabe.** Licence restored in March 2026 after a January 2026 revocation, with a May 2026 restart guide — a four-month recovery for a regulatory interruption, and no provision taken, implying the site was preserved.
- **Inventory discipline into a downturn.** Finished goods cut Rp 3,663 bn (13.5%) rather than built, avoiding an obsolescence charge. Inventory provision essentially flat at Rp 1,121 bn.
- **Subsidiary-level buybacks that accrete to ASII.** UT repurchased 45.8 mn of its own shares for Rp 1.4 trn in Q1 2026 (and 59.1 mn for Rp 1.7 trn in 2025) — raising the group's effective interest to 62.93% without a rupiah of parent cash.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- **A vertically integrated ecosystem that is hard to displace.** 29% of cost of revenue and opex transacted with related parties, with the manufacturing JVs (AHM at 50%, ADM at 31.87%) supplying the distribution and financing arms. Market position in four-wheel (47–49% share) and two-wheel vehicles, plus the dominant heavy-equipment franchise, is not replicable.
- **Counter-cyclical capital allocation.** Buying Doup (1.6 mn oz reserve, 3 mn t/yr plant design) for USD 540 mn in a record gold price environment is buying low-cycle-cost ounces at the right point in the commodity cycle — and doing it via a subsidiary that carries minority partners, sharing the risk.
- **Financial services as a capital-recycling engine.** Multifinance converts vehicle demand into a Rp 99,616 bn receivables book funded by AAA-rated bonds and bank lines, generating 36% of group profit.
- **Governance continuity with fresh leadership.** The 23 April 2026 AGMS appointed Priyono Sugiarto as President Commissioner and Rudy Gidion as President Director, with a board including independents Sri Indrastuti Hadiputranto, Muliaman Darmansyah Hadad and Muhamad Chatib Basri; Jardine Cycle & Carriage's 50.11% anchor remains.
- **Policy alignment.** Pillar Two (PMK-136) is not expected to bite because most entities sit above the 15% effective rate; the group applied the 3% listed-company rate reduction; PSAK 107/109 amendments are assessed as having no significant impact.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — HEMCE does not normalise in Q2.** The Martabe restart is guided for May 2026 but the RKAB coal quota constraint persists all year. If Komatsu unit sales stay at ~1,100/quarter and overburden at ~236 mn bcm, HEMCE profit stays near Rp 700 bn/quarter instead of Rp 3,300 bn. At that run-rate the group's annualised net profit is ~Rp 24–26 trn rather than the FY2025 base. This is the dominant near-term risk. Post-period evidence is not reassuring: H1 2026 revenue fell 3% and net profit 19% despite the Martabe restart, with a further Rp 2.1–2.4 trn of non-recurring geothermal/nickel losses ([Liputan6](https://www.liputan6.com/bisnis/read/8258405/astra-catatkan-pendapatan-rp-1579-triliun-semester-i-2026)).

**HIGH — The G&A line is not explained.** A Rp 1,142 bn (+23.4%) jump that consumes 7.65% of revenue versus 5.85% is either an Rp 723 bn one-off plus consolidation costs (benign, reverses) or a structurally higher cost base (not benign). Management has not decomposed it publicly. Until it is decomposed, the Q1 earnings base is of unknown quality.

**MODERATE — The Rp 292/share final dividend (~Rp 11.7 trn) lands in Q2** against an operating cash flow that has just halved. It is affordable on the Rp 49 trn cash balance, but it converts the non-FS net-debt position from temporary to structural if H2 does not recover.

**MODERATE — Short-term refinancing concentration.** 61% of the Rp 107,409 bn loan book is current, with most facilities amortising during 2026 including specific maturities in April, May, June and September 2026. Rp 90.7 trn of undrawn facilities cover it, but continuous access to the bank and bond markets is a structural dependency, and the fixed-rate share fell to 56.1% from 62.0%.

**MODERATE — Foreign-currency position swing.** Post-hedge net FX assets collapsed to Rp 1,243 bn-equivalent from Rp 11,989 bn (Note 36), with USD net moving from 728.8 mn to 61.0 mn and the JPY position widening to a 2,084.99 mn deficit. A Rp 20 bn sensitivity is disclosed, so the P&L effect is small — but the collapse in net FX assets removes a natural balance-sheet hedge against rupiah weakness.

**LOW — Receivables ageing.** Over-60-day trade receivables up 34.7% to Rp 4,336 bn with allowance coverage of only ~32% on that bucket. Small in absolute terms against Rp 27,119 bn net receivables.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**HIGH — Regulatory risk in mining is now realised, not hypothetical.** PT Agincourt's licence was listed as revoked on 20 January 2026 (28 companies named in a State Secretariat press release), the licence was only restored in March 2026 after ministerial intervention, and mining had not restarted by the reporting date. A single administrative act erased an entire quarter of gold sales at a record gold price. Nothing disclosed prevents recurrence, and the group's mining concessions expire across 2026–2044 with renewal at the state's discretion. For a group that has just committed ~Rp 8.85 trn to a new gold asset (Doup), this is the most important structural risk in the file.

**HIGH — The coal/quota dependency of the industrial core.** HEMCE revenue is 36% of group revenue and a function of Indonesian coal production quotas, mining customers' capex, and thermal coal prices. The 2026 RKAB cut is a policy variable, not a market one, and it flows directly into Komatsu deliveries and Pamapersada volumes. This is a structural cap on the growth of the group's largest revenue segment regardless of commodity prices.

**MODERATE — Automotive market-share erosion.** Group share slipped to ~47–49% from >50% while the market grew 1.7%. Chinese-brand EV and ICE entry plus discounting is the working hypothesis (medium confidence, not corroborated by company disclosure). A structural share loss in the four-wheel franchise would be a slow, permanent de-rating driver.

**MODERATE — Contract liabilities and the order book.** Contract liabilities fell only slightly to Rp 4,688 bn (from Rp 4,890 bn), mainly automotive, with contract assets rising to Rp 1,512 bn net (from Rp 1,381 bn) mainly from Acset construction contracts. The order-book signal is neutral, not deteriorating — but it does not point to a Q2 rebound.

**MODERATE — Intercompany dependence cuts both ways.** 29% of cost of revenue plus opex is transacted with related parties, the largest being the manufacturing JVs whose profits arrive as equity-accounted income (Rp 1,902 bn). Any adverse transfer-pricing or tax treatment of that structure would hit consolidated margin and the associate line simultaneously.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

- **PSAK/Pillar Two.** PSAK 107 and 109 amendments effective 2026 are assessed as having no significant impact; PSAK 118, the PSAK 338 amendment and PSAK 413 are effective 2027 and still being evaluated. PMK-136 (OECD Pillar Two) applies from 1 January 2025 with first reporting for FY2025 due 30 June 2027; management believes no significant impact because most entities are above the 15% threshold. The Q1 2026 ETR of 20.40% *rose* 76 bps despite the 3% listed-company reduction, so the interaction of geographic mix with the new tax architecture deserves monitoring.
- **Interest-rate regime change.** JIBOR was discontinued after 31 December 2025 and the group transitioned most loan agreements to Compounded IndONIA by 31 March 2026. With the fixed-rate share down to 56.1% and Rp 51,235 bn floating, any IndONIA rise hits financial-services spread directly — the segment that is currently carrying the group.
- **CPO export policy.** The April 2026 export-levy reference price of US$989.63/MT and any change to the levy/duty schedule can move AALI's realised margin quickly. Agribusiness is now 7.5% of gross profit and growing, so this matters more than it did.
- **Environmental/permitting regime.** Martabe's revocation was linked to a November 2025 Sumatra disaster. The framework for licence suspension has widened from mine-level compliance to disaster-related culpability, which increases tail risk across the mining portfolio.
- **Buyback and shareholder-register mechanics.** The final dividend total depends on shares entitled at the 6 May 2026 record date given the ongoing buyback (419.7 mn treasury shares, Rp 2.8 trn). This creates minor uncertainty in the absolute cash outflow, not in the per-share amount.
- **Governance of the parent.** Jardine Cycle & Carriage (Singapore, subsidiary of Jardine Matheson Holdings of Bermuda) holds 50.11%. Minority shareholders have no path to control and limited ability to influence capital allocation, including the decision to deploy Rp 8.85 trn into a gold mine.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

- **No market data in this run.** P/E, P/B and EV/EBITDA are not computed; any conclusion about cheapness or expensiveness is therefore conditional and this note explicitly does not make one.
- **Earnings quality.** The reported net margin of 8.16% contains at least ~Rp 1.2 trn of identified non-recurring charges (Rp 723 bn UT nickel/geothermal plus the Martabe revenue loss) and an unexplained Rp 1,142 bn G&A increase. Without a clean baseline, any multiple applied to reported EPS overstates the price being paid.
- **Book value quality.** Of Rp 232,765 bn of owners' equity, Rp 70,551 bn is equity-accounted JV/associate carrying value (AHM's carrying value alone fell to Rp 4,947 bn from Rp 7,599 bn after a dividend upstream) and Rp 21,846 bn is mining properties now carrying a Rp 7,032 bn acquisition uplift not yet generating depreciation or revenue. These are mark-sensitive, not cash-backed, components.
- **The premium-supporting net-cash position is gone.** Non-financial services flipped from Rp 7,218 bn of net cash to Rp 1,814 bn of net debt. Historically ASII's industrial block could be valued with net cash added; that adjustment is now zero or negative.
- **NCI leakage on the recovery.** Only ~60% of the UT/Doup/Agincourt recovery accrues to ASII shareholders. A sum-of-the-parts built on 100% of segment profit would overstate the equity value by a wide margin.
- **Cyclical earnings mean low multiples look high.** At a trough EPS, a P/E that looks full may be a trough multiple. Without a price we cannot arbitrate; the correct discipline is to value on normalised earnings and on book.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

The bull case is that Q1 2026 is a clean, identifiable, largely reversible trough, and that the market is being handed a diversified Indonesian franchise at a cyclical low in its largest segment.

1. **The drag is exogenous and dated.** HEMCE's Rp 2,614 bn segment-profit decline has two named causes: the Martabe suspension (gold sales 4,000 oz vs 57,000 oz) and the 2026 RKAB quota cut (Komatsu −20%, overburden −7%). The Martabe licence was restored in March 2026 with mining guided to restart in May 2026. A Q2 restart restores the gold line into a record gold price — US$5,583.88/oz was printed on 29 January 2026, and Q1 2026 global demand hit a record US$193 bn (+74% YoY) per the World Gold Council ([[Gold May 2026 Update]]).
2. **Everything else grew.** Financial services gross profit +8.7% and net profit +5.8%; agribusiness gross profit +24.1%; automotive segment net profit +2.5% on flat revenue; infrastructure segment profit +28.8%; property segment profit +271%; IT +47%. Ex-HEMCE gross margin *improved* 98 bps to 21.19%. The remaining 64% of revenue is performing.
3. **Doup converts gold from a single mine to a franchise.** ~1.6 mn oz reserve at ~38.2 mn t / 1.28 g/t with a 3 mn t/yr plant design, acquired for USD 540 mn, sits alongside Martabe's Rp 15,649 bn asset base. Two gold assets, one of them new, in a structurally higher gold price regime, with the group's mining-properties carrying value at Rp 21,846 bn.
4. **The balance sheet can absorb the cycle.** Rp 90.7 trn of undrawn facilities, 23% gearing, Rp 49 trn of cash, covenants complied. This is a company that can wait two quarters for a recovery, pay Rp 390/share, and keep buying back stock.
5. **NCI means the recovery is leveraged to owners.** Because UT is 59.5% owned, the down-quarter cost ASII shareholders only ~60% of the UT decline (owners' profit fell 15.6% against net profit −24.9%). The corollary is that only ~60% of the rebound accrues — but the *reported* equity earnings line is not the only channel: the group also receives cash dividends upstream (Rp 3,894 bn from AHM alone) and has been raising its effective interest via subsidiary buybacks (UT effective now 62.93%).
6. **Valuation support from book.** Book value per share is Rp 5,809 on an audited equity base of Rp 232,765 bn, and the group has a long history of trading around book-to-modest-premium. At a trough in earnings, book is the more stable anchor.

### 14.2 Bear Case

1. **The "trough" may be a re-rating.** The RKAB quota mechanism is a policy variable, not a price cycle. If Indonesian coal production quotas stay constrained, Komatsu unit sales do not return to the 1,400+/quarter run-rate and Pamapersada volumes stay flat. HEMCE's Rp 691 bn segment profit on Rp 165,608 bn of assets is a 1.7% return on segment assets — value-destroying if sustained. Post-period H1 2026 data (revenue −3%, net profit −19%, with a further Rp 2.1–2.4 trn of non-recurring geothermal/nickel losses) does not support a clean Q2 recovery.
2. **G&A is unexplained and may be permanent.** +Rp 1,142 bn (+23.4%) to 7.65% of revenue. If Rp 723 bn is one-off, the residual Rp 419 bn is a permanent 53 bps of revenue — roughly Rp 1.7 trn annualised of lost profit with no revenue offset. Management has not decomposed it.
3. **Regulatory tail risk is now demonstrated.** A single ministry press release on 20 January 2026 removed a gold mine from production for a quarter. The group's mining concessions expire across 2026–2044, reclamation provisioning is only Rp 95 bn, and the group has just added a second gold asset in the same jurisdiction. Any investor in ASII is underwriting Indonesian permitting risk at scale.
4. **The industrial net-cash buffer is gone.** Non-FS net debt of Rp 1,814 bn from net cash of Rp 7,218 bn. Historically ASII's industrial block was valued with net cash added. That support has been spent on a non-producing gold mine and returned to shareholders.
5. **Only ~60% of the recovery reaches ASII shareholders.** A bull case built on UT/Doup/Agincourt recovery must apply a 37–40% minority haircut. The segment profit of Rp 691 bn belongs 59.5% (62.93% effective) to ASII, not 100%.
6. **Automotive share loss may be structural.** Group share at ~47–49% from >50% in a market that grew 1.7%. Two-wheeler volumes −4.1%. If Chinese-brand competition is resetting the market's price architecture, the automotive segment's 11.3% gross margin has further to fall — and automotive is now the largest profit contributor at Rp 2,465 bn.
7. **We cannot value it.** No price data means no multiple. An investor cannot conclude "cheap" from this note.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD — accumulate on evidence, not on hope. Risk qualifier: HIGH dependency on commodity-volume normalisation and Indonesian mining permitting; not a SELL given franchise diversification, gearing of 23%, and Rp 90.7 trn of undrawn facilities; not a BUY until Q2 demonstrates Martabe volume, HEMCE segment profit above Rp 1,500 bn, and a decomposed G&A line.**

**Target price range: Rp 5,800–6,970 — illustrative only.** Because no market data was supplied, we cannot publish a price target in the conventional sense. The range above is derived transparently from the single hardest number in the pack — book value per share of Rp 5,809 — at 1.0x–1.2x P/B, and is presented as a valuation *framework* to be re-based once a price is available. For reference, annualised Q1 2026 EPS is Rp 584; normalising out the ~Rp 1.2 trn of identified one-off charges would lift that materially, but we decline to publish a normalised EPS because the G&A split is unverified. **Any published target requires a share price, a market capitalisation and an FY2026 EPS assumption — none of which are in this run's data.**

Positioning logic: the Q1 print is bad but legible, the balance sheet is intact, and the largest single drag has a dated, guided resolution (May 2026). That argues against selling into the print. But three things must be proven before adding: (i) that Martabe actually restarts and delivers volume; (ii) that HEMCE segment profit is a trough rather than a new run-rate; and (iii) that the Rp 1,142 bn G&A increase is substantially non-recurring. Two of those three are unproven and the third is undisclosed.

**Review trigger.** Re-underwrite this note on the **Q2 2026 report** (expected August 2026) on any of: gold sales below 30,000 oz (bear case confirmed); HEMCE segment profit below Rp 1,000 bn (structural de-rating); G&A above 7.0% of revenue for a second consecutive quarter (cost base reset); Komatsu unit sales below 1,000 (quota constraint binding); or a new mining-licence action against any group asset. Conversely, upgrade the stance if HEMCE segment profit exceeds Rp 1,500 bn, automotive share recovers above 50%, and free cash flow returns above Rp 8 trn in a single quarter.
