# PT Alamtri Minerals Indonesia Tbk (ADMR) — Q2 2026 Earnings Forecast

> **Context:** Based on KAI's first aluminium exports in June 2026 (35,063 MT to US and South Korea), target of 350,000 tons for FY2026. This forecast incorporates the aluminium segment's debut into ADMR's consolidated financials alongside the continuing coal business.

## Executive Summary

ADMR's Q2 2026 marks a **transformational quarter** — the first period with aluminium revenue following KAI's commercial production debut. After Q1 2026's pure coal profile ($267.5M revenue, $86.0M net profit), the consolidated P&L now includes the KAI aluminium smelter. However, the revenue uplift is partially offset by the **accounting shift at KAI from pre-operational to commercial**: interest on $969M debt and depreciation on $1.7B+ assets begin flowing through the income statement rather than being capitalized.

The article [[Alamtri ekspor aluminium ke AS dan Korea Selatan, target 350.000 ton]] confirms 35,063 MT shipped in June (31,494 MT to US via Mercuria; 3,569 MT to South Korea via Vitol), with a full-year target of 350,000 tons. The shipments occurred during a period of record US Midwest premiums due to Strait of Hormuz supply disruptions, though LME prices have since pulled back from the April peak of $3,672/t to ~$3,100/t.

**Forecast Summary (Base Case):**

| Metric | Q1 2026 (Actual) | Q2 2026 (Forecast) | QoQ Change | Q2 2025 (Est.) | YoY Change |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|------------|----------------|------------|
| **Revenue** | $267.5M | **~$380–410M** | +42–53% | ~$244M | +56–68% |
| **Gross Profit** | $125.2M | **~$155–175M** | +24–40% | ~$98M | +58–79% |
| **Operating Income** | $107.0M | **~$105–130M** | −2 to +22% | ~$86M | +22–51% |
| **Net Profit (Parent)** | $87.7M | **~$70–95M** | −20 to +8% | ~$75M | −7 to +27% |
| **Net Margin** | 32.8% | **~18–23%** | −10 to 14pp | ~31% | −8 to 13pp |

**Key Insight:** Revenue surges 40%+ QoQ driven by aluminium, but net profit may be **flat to down** due to the accounting shift at KAI — interest and depreciation expense on $969M debt and $1.7B+ assets transition from capitalization to P&L expensing upon commercial production. The aluminium segment is revenue-accretive but may be profit-dilutive in its first quarter of operations.

### 1.2 Revenue Recognition Assumption

Critical for Q2 P&L: whether shipments are recognized on FOB or CIF terms.

| Shipping Term | Revenue Recognition | Impact on Q2 2026 |
|---------------|---------------------|--------------------|
| **FOB (Free on Board)** | Recognized when loaded (June 10 ¹ June 29) | ✅ Included in Q2 |
| **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)** | Recognized upon arrival (July in Texas/Incheon) | ❌ Deferred to Q3 |

**Forecast assumes FOB terms** (revenue recognized in Q2). This is the conservative assumption for Q2 — if CIF, aluminium revenue shifts to Q3 and Q2 profit may be higher (no aluminium costs, only pre-operational expenses). Management should clarify on the Q2 earnings call.

## 3. Profitability Forecast

### 3.1 The Accounting Shift: Capitalization → Expensing

This is the **single most important factor** for Q2 2026 profitability. KAI's transition from pre-operational to commercial production triggers:

| Item | Pre-Commercial (Q1 2026) | Post-Commercial (Q2 2026E) | Impact |
|------|--------------------------|----------------------------|--------|
| **Interest on $969M debt** | Capitalized (balance sheet) | Expensed through P&L | **−$5–15M/quarter** |
| **Depreciation on $1.7B+ assets** | Capitalized / not started | Expensed through P&L | **−$5–15M/quarter** |
| **Direct production costs** | Capitalized | Expensed (COGS) | **−$60–80M/quarter** on full run-rate |

For Q2 2026, only **one month** (June) of commercial operations is included (assuming June was the commercial production declaration date), so the quarterly impact is:
- Interest: ~$5M for June (annualized: $969M × 6% ÷ 12 ≈ $4.8M/month)
- Depreciation: ~$5M for June (annualized: $1.7B ÷ 25 years ÷ 12 ≈ $5.7M/month)
- Production COGS: ~$20–30M for June (scaled from full quarter estimate of ~$80M)

### 3.2 Cost Structure Estimate for Aluminium Production

| Cost Component | $/MT Estimate | Basis |
|----------------|---------------|-------|
| Alumina (~1.93t per t Al) | ~$600–700 | Alumina at ~$320–370/t in Q2 |
| Energy (power for smelting) | ~$500–800 | KIPI coal-fired power; captive energy |
| Carbon anodes & consumables | ~$200–300 | Industry benchmark |
| Labor & overhead | ~$100–150 | Estimated for Indonesian operation |
| **Total COGS/t** | **~$1,400–2,000** | Wide range for commissioning smelter |

- Base (COGS $1,700/t): **47%** → Gross profit $53M on 35K MT

### 3.5 Bear Case Expanded: The "Commissioning Quarter" Worst Case

The bear case is the most realistic **risk scenario** for a first-quarter-of-production smelter:
- Lower realized aluminium prices ($2,800/t — discount for new supplier, no premium capture)
- High commissioning costs ($2,000+/t COGS — yield losses, learning curve, one-off startup expenses)
- CIF shipping terms deferring 50% of volume to Q3
- Full $15M interest expense for the quarter (commercial production declared at start of Q2)
- Startup disruption costs, inventory build, working capital consumption

In this scenario, the aluminium segment generates ~$25M gross profit on ~31,500 MT but is offset by ~$20M in incremental interest/depreciation/opex, contributing negligibly to net profit. The coal business shoulders all profitability.

## 9. Conclusion & Recommendation

### Q2 2026: A Transitional Quarter

ADMR's Q2 2026 is best understood as a **transition quarter** — the first period where KAI's aluminium operations are visible in the consolidated financials, but at an early stage of ramp-up. The headline revenue growth (~+45% QoQ) is impressive, but the bottom-line impact is tempered by the accounting shift at KAI (capitalization → expensing) and the one-month contribution window.

**Investment Implications:**
- **Revenue transformation is real** — KAI shipped 35K MT to blue-chip traders in its first month
- **Profitability inflection in Q3 2026** — Full quarter of aluminium volume with cost base stabilizing
- **Leverage peak approaching** — KAI capex continues; expect DER to peak in Q2-Q3 before aluminium cash flow begins deleveraging
- **Valuation support** — At an annualized ~$380M NP (based on Q2 + Q3E trajectory), ADMR trades at a significant discount to diversified mining peers

### Recommendation: **WATCH → ACCUMULATE**

The successful first exports materially de-risk the KAI investment thesis. The key awaitable is the Q2 2026 earnings print, which will reveal:
1. Aluminium segment gross margin (single most important number)
2. Interest capitalization policy (when commercial production was declared)
3. Production ramp guidance for H2 2026
4. Working capital position (first export receivables cycle)

If Q2 shows aluminium gross margins above 40%, the diversification thesis is validated and ADMR deserves a re-rating from "coal pure-play with a risky aluminium project" to "diversified minerals producer with a growing aluminium franchise."
