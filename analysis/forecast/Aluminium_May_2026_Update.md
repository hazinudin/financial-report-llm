# Aluminium Market Forecast Update (May 2026)

## Executive Summary

The aluminium market has experienced an unprecedented supply shock following the US-Israel conflict with Iran that began on February 28, 2026. What Mercuria's Nick Snowdon characterized as a **"black swan" event** — the largest single supply shock to a base metals market since 2000 — has fundamentally repriced the aluminium complex.

**Current Status (as of May 6, 2026):**
- **LME 3-month price**: ~$3,385/t (as of March 6; pulled back from 4-year high of $3,672/t on April 16)
- **Market structure**: Acute backwardation (~$90 spot vs 3-mo), widest since March 2007
- **Global deficit**: Minimum 2M tons through year-end (Mercuria base case), could widen to 2.9M+ tons under severe disruption scenarios

---

## 1. Price Forecast Scenarios

### Base Case ($3,200–$3,600/t through H2 2026)
**Assumptions:**
- Partial restart of Gulf smelters in Q3–Q4 2026
- Strait of Hormuz remains intermittently open with elevated insurance/logistics costs
- No further military escalation targeting production infrastructure

**Supporting Analysis:**
- **World Bank** (April 28, 2026): Forecasts average $3,200/t for 2026 (+21.6% YoY), expects decline to $3,000/t in 2027
- **Fastmarkets** (March 2026): Full-year 2026 forecast at $3,073/t; Q1 2026 at $3,155/t; 2027 at $2,966/t
- **Goldman Sachs**: Base case average $3,150/t in H1 2026; expects Indonesian supply ramp-up in H2 to raise inventories
- **ING**: Revised forecasts higher; base case ~2M ton deficit; prices elevated but not materially higher from current levels

### Bull Case ($4,000+/t)
**Assumptions:**
- Strait of Hormuz remains closed through May–June 2026
- Additional smelter attacks or prolonged shutdowns at Alba, Qatalum, EGA
- No meaningful restart of Gulf capacity before Q1 2027

**Supporting Analysis:**
- **Citi**: $3,600/t base for next three months; $4,000/t achievable in bull-case scenario if Hormuz remains closed
- **Commerzbank**: $4,000/t if Hormuz closure persists through May
- **Kpler** (April 2, 2026): Strikes on Alba and EGA enhance possibility of $4,000/t; production losses of 1.13–1.79Mt in 2026 from just these two facilities
- **ING**: Middle East escalation could push prices above $4,000/t given region's 9% share of global production and larger share of traded metal

### Bear Case ($2,800–$3,000/t)
**Assumptions:**
- Rapid de-escalation and ceasefire holds
- Gulf smelters restart faster than expected (Q3 2026)
- Demand destruction at current price levels
- Indonesian supply ramps up in H2 2026

**Risk Factors:**
- Premium compression if conflict de-escalates sharply
- US tariff policy changes
- Chinese export quota adjustments

## 2. Supply Disruption Assessment

### Gulf Smelter Status (Updated May 2026)

| Smelter | Country | Capacity (ktpa) | Status | Restart Timeline |
|---------|---------|-----------------|--------|------------------|
| **Alba** | Bahrain | 1,600 | Force majeure; ~30% capacity | 6–12 months (Norsk Hydro estimate) |
| **Qatalum** | Qatar | 640 | Controlled shutdown; ~60% operating | 6–12 months |
| **EGA Al Taweelah** | UAE | 1,500 | Operations halted (missile strike damage) | Up to 12 months (CEO statement) |
| **EGA Jebel Ali** | UAE | 1,200 | Operational but rerouting exports | N/A |
| **Ma'aden Ras Al Khair** | Saudi Arabia | 900 | Operational (domestic bauxite buffer) | N/A |
| **Sohar** | Oman | 400–450 | Operational | N/A |

**Cumulative Impact**: ~3M tons affected (~50% of GCC production)

### Critical Constraints to Restart

1. **Alumina Supply Bottleneck**: Gulf region produces only ~3% of global alumina and ~1% of bauxite
   - Alba imported only 0.02Mt alumina in March vs. 0.24Mt/month average requirement
   - EGA imported ~0.07Mt via Oman in March vs. 0.42Mt/month needed for 2.6Mt annual output
   - Red Sea ports lack alumina handling infrastructure; overland routes capacity-constrained

2. **Technical Restart Challenges**:
   - Once electrolytic pots cool, only ~20% of curtailed capacity can return in Q4 under optimistic scenario
   - Full restart requires specialized equipment/components amid conflict-related supply chain disruption
   - Norsk Hydro (Qatalum operator): 6–12 months to full restart

3. **Logistics Cost Inflation**:
   - 70–80% of Gulf output can be rerouted via Oman/Saudi/Fujairah, adding $75–125/t in logistics costs
   - Insurance premiums elevated even during ceasefire period

## 3. Inventory & Market Tightness

### LME Stocks (as of late April 2026)
- **Total registered + off-warrant**: ~583,000t (lowest since 2020)
- **Russian metal share**: 58–92% of warranted stock (untouchable for Western buyers due to self-sanctioning)
- **Effective non-Russian inventory**: ~335,000t (as of April 27) = ~1.5 days of global production
- **Historical context**: LME inventories historically hovered 0.8–1.2M tons; current "realistic balance" range 300–600kt

### Global Supply Constraints (Beyond Middle East)

| Constraint | Impact |
|------------|--------|
| **China capacity cap** | 45M ton annual limit; running at 44.5M annualized (~97% utilization); production growth slowing to ~2% |
| **China net importer** | Imported record 2.5M tons primary Al in 2025; semi-fabricated exports down ~10% YoY |
| **Mozal (Mozambique)** | Moving to care & maintenance from March 2026 (power supply failure) |
| **Century (Iceland)** | Partial shutdown October 2025 |
| **US/Europe idled capacity** | Little available to restart; competing with data centers for power |

---

## 4. Demand Outlook

### Structural Tailwinds
- **EVs / Electrification / Renewables**: ~2.5% annual demand growth
- **Packaging**: Resilient demand (60% of aluminium foil consumption); sustainability trends favor aluminium over plastics
- **US Project Vault**: Government initiative to build strategic metals stockpile could tighten domestic supply further

### Headwinds
- **US tariffs**: 50% tariffs on aluminium imports → Midwest premium at record $1.14/lb ($2,521.50/t) → demand destruction risk
- **EU CBAM** (effective Jan 1, 2026): Adds cost layer to imported semi-fabricated products
- **Construction sector**: Lagging due to China property slump and weak residential activity in advanced economies
- **High interest rates**: Suppressing capital-intensive demand

## 6. Investment Implications & Trading Strategy

### Upside Risks (Bullish)
1. **Escalation risk**: Any renewed military action targeting additional smelters (EGA Jebel Ali, Ma'aden, Sohar) could push deficit to 2.9M+ tons
2. **Hormuz closure duration**: Every additional month of full closure adds ~0.5M tons to deficit
3. **Restart delays**: Technical challenges could push Gulf capacity return to H2 2027
4. **Chinese policy**: No swing capacity available at 45M ton cap; China already net importer
5. **Speculative positioning**: Risk premiums remain embedded; momentum trading could extend rally

### Downside Risks (Bearish)
1. **Ceasefire holds**: Two-week ceasefire (brokered by Pakistan, effective April 8) reduces immediate escalation risk
2. **Demand destruction**: At $3,500+/t, marginal consumers exit market
3. **Indonesian supply ramp-up**: Goldman Sachs expects new Indonesian capacity in H2 2026 to raise inventories
4. **Russian metal flow**: If de-escalation occurs, Western buyers may resume purchasing Russian aluminium, easing tightness
5. **Premium compression**: If conflict resolves, premiums could fall sharply, pulling LME price lower

### Key Watchpoints
1. **Diplomatic progress**: Ceasefire durability; Iran-Israel-US negotiations
2. **Smelter restart timelines**: Alba/Qatalum quarterly updates; EGA damage assessments
3. **Chinese output data**: Monthly production vs. 45M ton cap; export quotas
4. **LME inventory draws**: Pace of drawdown; Russian metal share
5. **Premium signals**: US Midwest, Europe, Japan quarterly benchmarks
6. **Demand indicators**: Chinese semi-fabricated exports; European automotive production data

## 8. Conclusion

The aluminium market has entered a **structurally tight regime** driven by:
1. **Realized supply shock**: 3M tons of Gulf capacity offline (not just at-risk)
2. **Historically low inventories**: Effective non-Russian stocks at 1.5 days of global production
3. **No swing capacity**: China at cap, Europe/US energy-constrained
4. **Structural demand growth**: Electrification, EVs, renewables provide 2.5% annual tailwind

**Base case view**: LME aluminium averages **$3,200–3,400/t through H2 2026**, with upside risk to **$4,000/t** if Hormuz remains closed or additional smelter damage occurs. Downside limited to **$2,800–3,000/t** only under rapid de-escalation and faster-than-expected restart scenarios.

**Risk/reward**: Upside skewed. The Mercuria base case of 2M ton deficit assumes partial restarts; any escalation could push to 2.9M+ tons. Western buyers (US 22% ME import share, Europe 18.5%) have no buffer. China cannot fill the gap.

**Strategic recommendation**: Maintain long bias; use pullbacks below $3,000/t as buying opportunities. Monitor ceasefire developments and smelter restart timelines closely.
