## Executive Summary

ESSA's Q2 2026 is shaped by two opposing forces: a **35–40 day plant turnaround** (begun May 6) that cuts quarterly production to ~110–130K MT, and an **explosive ammonia price surge** driven by the Strait of Hormuz closure. Per Stockbit's Closed Door Meeting with management (Jun 2, 2026), the **Southeast Asia reference price — ESSA's actual sales basis — is ~$750/MT**, with the Q2 2026 average at ~$710/MT. This is dramatically higher than Northeast Asia spot prices (~$360/MT) used in the initial forecast, and transforms the quarter from an expected earnings dip into a **potential earnings inflection point**.

The ~38,000 MT of undelivered inventory at end of Q1 (per management) provides a substantial volume cushion, partially offsetting the production loss. Combined with windfall pricing, Q2 2026 net profit could **exceed Q1 2026** despite the turnaround — a counterintuitive outcome driven by the 54% QoQ price increase outweighing the 19% volume decline.

Management's guidance of 3–6 months for price normalization (even if the conflict resolves) suggests elevated pricing continues through H2 2026, supporting Stockbit's view that consensus estimates (~$69M FY2026E at ~$415/MT ammonia) have significant **upside risk**.

## 5. Ammonia Price Analysis (Revised with SE Asia Reference)

### The SE Asia Reference Price Correction

The initial forecast used Northeast Asia (NE Asia) spot prices from IMARC as the basis for estimating ESSA's realized price. The Stockbit CDM reveals that ESSA's actual sales basis is the **Southeast Asia reference price**, which is significantly higher:

| Benchmark | Pre-War | Q2 Peak (Apr–May) | Post-SoH (Jun 17+) | Q2 Average |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|-------------------|------------|
| **NE Asia spot (IMARC)** | $300/MT ($0.30/kg) | $370–380/MT | $330/MT | ~$360/MT |
| **SE Asia reference (ESSA's basis)** | ~$480/MT | ~$750/MT | ~$600/MT (est.) | **~$710/MT** |
| **Middle East FOB** | ~$480/MT | ~$700/MT | ~$570/MT | ~$657/MT |
| **India reference** | ~$510/MT | ~$880/MT | — | — |

**Why SE Asia trades at a premium to NE Asia:**
1. SE Asia is a net ammonia importer with limited regional production capacity
2. Logistics costs from Middle East to SE Asia are higher than to NE Asia
3. The war disrupted Middle East supply specifically to Asian markets
4. ESSA, as a local SE Asia producer, commands a premium (no shipping from Middle East needed)
5. India's fertilizer season (May–June) pulls regional supply toward India at ~$880/MT, tightening SE Asia availability

ESSA's ammonia is sold under a long-term offtake agreement with **Genesis Corporation** (Japan), a related party under common control (Laroya family). ## 11. Key Risks & Catalysts (Revised)

### Upside Catalysts

1. **Windfall pricing persists:** Management's 3–6 month normalization guidance means elevated pricing continues through Q3 2026 (at minimum). If the ceasefire remains fragile and SoH normalization stalls, Q3–Q4 pricing could exceed expectations.
2. **Inventory monetization timing:** ESSA has ~38K MT of undelivered inventory. If a larger portion is sold during the Q2 peak (vs. carrying to Q3), Q2 earnings exceed the base case.
3. **Gas cost lag:** If gas cost adjustments lag ammonia price movements (gas renegotiated quarterly while ammonia adjusts monthly), ESSA captures a temporary margin expansion during the price surge.
4. **Consensus upgrade cycle:** Stockbit identifies "upside risk to consensus" — if Q2 results beat expectations, analysts revise FY2026 estimates upward, driving a re-rating.
5. **Post-turnaround efficiency:** The 9% production improvement is management guidance; actual improvement could be higher if the turnaround optimizes the KBR system beyond expectations.
6. **Capital allocation:** With ~$195M projected cash at Q2 end and windfall profits, ESSA could announce: (a) special dividend, (b) share buyback, (c) accelerated blue ammonia/SAF investment, or (d) new energy sector project (management evaluating per CDM).
7. **Blue ammonia acceleration:** Feasibility study completion (end 2026, $120–150M capex) could be a multi-year growth catalyst. The US-Iran crisis has accelerated Japan/Korea's strategic imperative to diversify ammonia supply away from Middle East LNG-dependent production — validating ESSA's blue ammonia investment thesis.

### Downside Risks

1. **Gas cost pass-through (DOMINANT RISK — REVISED):** The Stockbit CDM confirmed gas cost follows ammonia price. If gas costs increase proportionally with ammonia prices, the margin expansion from higher prices is significantly compressed. The degree of pass-through is the single most important variable for Q2 margins.
2. **Price normalization acceleration:** If SoH fully normalizes faster than management's 3–6 month guidance (e.g., mines cleared quickly, Qatar repairs progress), prices could collapse to pre-war levels (~$480/MT) by Q4 2026, compressing H2 earnings.
3. **Market treats windfall as "peak earnings" (KEY RISK — per Stockbit):** "Pasar memperlakukan windfall sebagai peak earnings, sehingga harga saham tak terapresiasi mesgi kinerja membaik." If the market assigns a cyclical low P/E to windfall profits, the stock may not re-rate despite strong earnings. This risk is **exacerbated by the hawkish Fed pivot** (markets pricing in Sept 2026 rate hikes) and the **JCI liquidity shock** (index at 6,162, Meridian warns of break below 5,500 due to DSI export regime implementation), which historically pressure cyclical commodity valuations. This is the **#1 risk to stock performance** per Stockbit.
4. **Demand destruction:** Management warns that >$800/MT is unsustainable (similar to 2022 when prices spiked to $1,600/MT then collapsed). If prices remain above $800/MT for an extended period, fertilizer demand could contract, leading to a sharp price correction.
5. **Turnaround overrun:** 5-year-old plant (last turnaround 2021) may uncover additional maintenance items. However, management confirmed on-schedule execution as of June 2, reducing this risk.
6. **Gas contract renewal (2027):** Contract expires December 2027. Management is "optimistic" on renewal, but any renegotiation at higher gas prices (removing the floor advantage) would structurally compress margins.
7. **NCI dilution:** The 30% NCI in PAU means ~30% of the windfall ammonia profits flow to minority shareholders. At $33M consolidated net profit, ~$10M goes to NCI — a permanent structural drag.

## 13. Investment Thesis (Revised)

### From "Earnings Dip" to "Windfall Quarter"

The initial forecast expected Q2 2026 to be an earnings trough due to the plant turnaround. The Stockbit CDM data **fundamentally changes this narrative**: Q2 2026 is not a dip — it's a **windfall quarter** where ammonia prices surged 54% QoQ (SE Asia reference: $455 → ~$700/MT) while the 38K MT inventory cushion offsets much of the production loss.

**The revised narrative:**
1. **Q1 2026** ($26.76M NP) was the *opening act* — only 1 month of war pricing captured
2. **Q2 2026** (~$30M NP) is the *windfall quarter* — full war peak pricing + inventory monetization
3. **H2 2026** (~$68M NP) is the *normalization phase* — post-turnaround production recovery (+9%) partially offsets declining prices
4. **FY2026** (~$125M NP) is a *transformative year* — 127% above FY2025's $55M

### The "Peak Earnings" Debate

Stockbit identifies the key stock risk: "pasar memperlakukan windfall sebagai peak earnings." This is the central tension:

**Bull view (Stockbit/This forecast):** Windfall pricing persists for 3–6 months (management guidance). Structural floor from Qatar damage, SoH not fully normalized, ceasefire fragile. FY2027 could still see elevated pricing if normalization is slow. Consensus estimates ($69M) are far too low → estimate upgrades drive re-rating.

**Bear view:** Ammonia prices have already peaked (June reversal confirmed). SoH reopening is ongoing. Prices will normalize to ~$400–480/MT by H2 2026. FY2026 is a cyclical peak — the market should assign a low P/E (6–7x) to windfall earnings. The stock won't re-rate despite strong Q2 results.

**This forecast's assessment:** The truth likely lies between. Q2 2026 will be the earnings peak quarterly, but H2 2026 will still deliver strong profitability (~$68M) due to post-turnaround production recovery. FY2027 pricing depends on geopolitical normalization speed — management's 3–6 month guidance suggests H1 2027 could still see above-normal pricing. The stock should be valued on a **normalized FY2027 earnings power** of ~$80–100M (assuming prices settle at ~$450–500/MT with +9% production), implying a P/E of ~10–12x — supporting IDR 1,100–1,300 fair value.

### Strategic Positioning

Regardless of the peak earnings debate, ESSA's structural strengths are enhanced by the windfall:
- **Fortress balance sheet:** ~$195M cash (post-Q2), zero debt, strong cash generation
- **Expansion funding:** Blue ammonia ($120–150M capex), SAF, energy projects — all self-funded from windfall cash flow
- **Strategic validation:** The US-Iran crisis validates ESSA's blue ammonia investment thesis (Japan/Korea seeking non-Gulf, low-carbon supply security)
- **Contract pipeline:** 18 months of visibility (Genesis offtake until Dec 2027, gas supply until 2027)
- **KBR technology advantage:** Efficient gas-to-ammonia conversion provides cost advantage through the full price cycle
