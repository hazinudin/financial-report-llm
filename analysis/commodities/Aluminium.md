# Aluminium Market Research Briefing

> [[Aluminium faces 'black swan' supply shock, Mercuria says]]
> [[Al price forecasts hit $4,000t on Middle East conflict]]

## 1. The Mercuria Thesis (FT Commodities Summit, Apr 21, 2026)

Nick Snowdon, Head of Metals & Mining Research at Mercuria, characterized the current aluminium supply disruption as a **"black swan" event** — the **largest single supply shock to a base metals market since 2000**. Key claims:

- **Minimum 2M ton deficit** through year-end (conservative estimate, assumes partial restart of smelters this quarter)
- Total global stock only ~3M tons (visible inventory ~1.5M tons) — **extremely limited buffers**
- Deficit could widen if alumina flows via Strait of Hormuz remain constrained

## 3. Gulf Smelter Status (Cumulative Impact: ~3M tons affected)

| Smelter | Status |
|---|---|
| **Alba (Bahrain)** | Force majeure declared; running at ~30% capacity (~300kt curtailed) |
| **Qatalum (Qatar)** | Controlled shutdown; operating at ~60% (~260kt curtailed); Norsk Hydro says 6–12 months to full restart |
| **EGA Al Taweelah (UAE)** | Operations halted |
| **Other GCC** | 70–80% of output can be rerouted via Oman/Saudi Arabia/Fujairah, adding $75–125/t in logistics costs |

The GCC region produced **~6.16M tons in 2025** (~8.35% of global supply). With ~3M tons currently impacted, roughly **50% of regional production** is disrupted.

## 4. Strait of Hormuz — Macro Impact

- Vessel traffic through Hormuz **down ~95%** (from ~125/day to ~5/day)
- Affects alumina imports to Gulf smelters alongside finished metal exports
- Rerouting via overland routes (Oman, Fujairah, Saudi) partially mitigates but adds cost and delay (a few days to 1 week)

## 5. Global Supply Constraints (Beyond the Middle East)

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **China capacity cap** | 45M ton annual limit; running at 44.5M annualized (~97% utilization); production growth slowing to ~2% |
| **China net importer** | Imported record 2.5M tons primary Al in 2025; semi-fabricated exports down ~10% YoY |
| **US/Europe** | Little idled capacity to restart; competing with data centers for power |
| **Mozal (Mozambique)** | Moving to care & maintenance from Mar 2026 (power supply failure) |
| **Century (Iceland)** | Partial shutdown Oct 2025 |
| **LME stocks** | Combined registered + off-warrant ~583,000t (lowest since 2020); 58% of warranted stock is Russian (untouchable for Western buyers) |

## 6. Demand Drivers

- **EVs / Electrification / Renewable Energy**: Structural tailwind (~2.5% annual demand growth)
- **US tariff effects**: 50% tariffs on Al imports → Midwest premium at record → demand destruction risk at elevated prices
- **EU CBAM** (effective Jan 1, 2026): Adds cost layer to imported semi-fabricated products

## 8. Key Investment Implications

- **Upside risk skewed**: The Mercuria base case of 2M ton deficit assumes partial restarts; any escalation (wider smelter damage, prolonged Hormuz closure) could push to 2.9M+ tons
- **Western exposure**: US (22% ME import share) and Europe (18.5%) are most exposed; low inventories provide no buffer
- **China cannot fill the gap**: At the 45M ton cap and already a net importer, China has no swing capacity
- **Premium compression risk**: If the conflict de-escalates, premiums could fall sharply; however, tariffs (US) and CBAM (EU) provide a structural floor
- **Cost curve anchor**: ~90% of global producers clustered around $2,400/t cost curve — historically, deficit markets trade well above this level
- **Watchpoints**: (1) Ceasefire/diplomatic progress on Iran; (2) Alba/Qatalum restart timelines; (3) Chinese output data; (4) LME inventory drawdown pace; (5) Demand destruction signals at current price levels
