## 1. The Mercuria Thesis (FT Commodities Summit, Apr 21, 2026)

Nick Snowdon, Head of Metals & Mining Research at Mercuria, characterized the current aluminium supply disruption as a **"black swan" event** — the **largest single supply shock to a base metals market since 2000**. Key claims:

- **Minimum 2M ton deficit** through year-end (conservative estimate, assumes partial restart of smelters this quarter)
- Total global stock only ~3M tons (visible inventory ~1.5M tons) — **extremely limited buffers**
- Deficit could widen if alumina flows via Strait of Hormuz remain constrained

## 3. Gulf Smelter Status (Cumulative Impact: ~3M+ tons affected)

| Smelter | Status | Restart Update (Jul 17) |
|---|---|---|
| **Alba (Bahrain)** | Force majeure; running ~35% | Alumina feed resuming via Oman; partial pot relining begun |
| **Qatalum (Qatar)** | Operating ~65% but at acute risk | **Qatar Transport Ministry suspended all maritime activity (Jul 11)**; Ras Laffan LNG halted; alumina feed now threatened |
| **EGA Al Taweelah (UAE)** | Operations halted | Damage assessment complete; long-lead parts ordered; air defenses activated Jul 11 |
| **Other GCC** | Rerouting via Oman/Saudi continues | Logistics costs re-escalating as Hormuz formally closed; 70–80% output still rerouted |

The GCC region produced **~6.16M tons in 2025** (~8.35% of global supply). With ~3M tons currently impacted and Qatar's blanket maritime suspension adding new uncertainty, roughly **50% of regional production is disrupted and at risk of deepening**.

## 4. Strait of Hormuz — Macro Impact

- **July 11, 2026**: The IRGC struck the Cyprus-flagged container ship M/V GFS Galaxy in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran formally **re-declared the Strait CLOSED until further notice** — the first formal closure declaration of the entire conflict.
- The **June 17 US-Iran MOU** (60-day toll-free window) was **nullified on day 24** by the IRGC strike. The brief Jun 18–Jul 8 "reopening" (284 vessels transited) has been annihilated.
- **July 11**: US launched a third round of strikes on ~140 Iranian military targets. Iran retaliated against Prince Hassan Air Base (Jordan) and activated air defenses across **Qatar, Bahrain, and UAE** — the first time all four Gulf states were involved in a single exchange.
- **Qatar Transport Ministry issued an urgent advisory suspending ALL maritime vessel activity until further notice** — the first blanket Gulf-state maritime suspension of the entire conflict. This directly threatens Qatalum alumina feedstock and Ras Laffan LNG feed gas.
- Vessel traffic has collapsed: only ~10 commercial transits/24h (down from ~100–138 pre-war); daily tanker average ~21; 7-day total tankers 127 (down −77% vs prior 7d).
- Rerouting via overland routes (Oman, Fujairah, Saudi) continues, but logistics costs are re-escalating sharply as Hormuz closes again.

## 6. Demand Drivers

- **EVs / Electrification / Renewable Energy**: Structural tailwind (~2.5% annual demand growth)
- **US tariff effects**: 50% tariffs on Al imports → Midwest premium at record → demand destruction risk at elevated prices
- **EU CBAM** (effective Jan 1, 2026): Adds cost layer to imported semi-fabricated products

## 8. Key Investment Implications

- **Risk rotation**: Dominant risk has sharply reversed from "premium compression on de-escalation" back to **"acute supply escalation"** and **"Strait of Hormuz closure premium."**
- **Western exposure**: US and Europe are now re-exposed to the "panic" buffer as Hormuz is formally closed again and Qatar has suspended all maritime activity.
- **China cannot fill the gap**: At the 45M ton cap, China has no swing capacity. Gulf losses are now structurally irreplaceable in H2 2026.
- **Premium re-escalation**: US tariffs (50%) and EU CBAM already provided a floor; Hormuz re-closure and Qatar suspension are now pushing premiums back toward April panic levels.
- **Cost curve anchor**: ~90% of producers clustered around $2,400/t — market remains well above this floor even in a correction.
- **Watchpoints**: (1) US-Iran conflict escalation (fourth US strike round?); (2) Qatar maritime suspension duration; (3) Alba/Qatalum restart milestones; (4) LME non-Russian inventory draws; (5) Fed September dot plot; (6) CITA (Indonesia) Q2 earnings print.
