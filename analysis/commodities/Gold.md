Gold surged 55% in 2025, breaking $4,000/oz for the first time in October. After peaking at $5,595 in January 2026, the metal corrected to $4,098 in early February amid Middle East conflict volatility. A brief recovery to ~$4,695 in May 2026 has since reversed sharply; gold has fallen back to ~$4,176 as of early July—its lowest level since the February floor.

**Dual drivers of the May–July decline:**
1. **Fed hawkish pivot**: Core inflation surged to 4.2%, markets began pricing in rate hikes for September 2026, and the dollar strengthened to ~102.
2. **Geopolitical de-escalation**: The June 17 US-Iran MOU (60-day negotiation window) and gradual Strait of Hormuz reopening (284 vessels transited since Jun 18) have triggered a partial safe-haven premium unwind—mirroring the price reversals seen in [[Ammonia]] and [[Aluminium]] markets.

**Key dynamic**: Investment demand (bars, coins, central banks) is driving the market, while jewelry demand is being suppressed by elevated prices. Asian investors are the primary drivers of bar and coin growth.

**Central bank buying sensitivity (Meridian Research)**: Historically, every additional **100 tonnes of central bank net buying** correlated with roughly a **3% increase in gold prices** across the majority of observations. Q1 2026's 244t print therefore implies a structural price floor sustained by sovereign demand, even as ETF flows turn negative. Correlations can break down for a quarter (Q1 2026 was likely one such period given the simultaneous price correction), but structurally sovereign demand remains one of the strongest long-term pillars.

**Q2 2026 early indicators**: ETF outflows accelerated through May–June as profit-taking kicked in after the 42% YoY gain. J.P. Morgan notes that "gold is on the back burner for most investors at the moment" amid Fed-hike fears. Central bank activity remained bifurcated—**Turkey sold ~70t** (tactical gold-currency swaps for FX liquidity), **Russia sold ~22t**, while **Poland (+31t), Uzbekistan (+25t), and China (+7t)** continued buying. Net central bank demand remains structurally positive but volatile.

## 3. Supply Side

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Annual mine supply** | ~2,500–3,500 metric tonnes/year (1–1.5% growth) |
| **Recycling** | Increasing at elevated prices; partially offsets mine constraints |
| **Central bank reserves** | Structural shift toward gold diversification; not cyclical |
| **ETF holdings** | US fund outflows in March 2026 suggest profit-taking |

Unlike base metals (e.g., [[Aluminium]]), gold's supply is relatively inelastic. The rally is **demand-driven**, not supply-constrained.

**Structural supply constraint (Meridian Research)**: Normalizing the capex cycle of miners within the GDX portfolio, the industry remains far from historical capex euphoria. Post-2013, mining companies became extremely disciplined with capital allocation—**years of underinvestment created a structural supply pipeline problem.** Gold supply cannot rapidly respond to higher prices because development takes years. This inelasticity is precisely why gold bull markets historically correct on the demand side (positioning flushes) but rarely on the supply side—there is no quick supply response to cap a price rebound.

## 4a. Long-Term Structural Framework (Meridian Research)

### The 225-Year Commodity Supercycle Lens

Gold is fundamentally a **macro regime asset**. Over the last 225 years, commodity supercycles were rarely isolated market events—they were typically tied to:
1. **Wars** — direct military escalation, supply shocks, and resource reallocation
2. **Fiscal deterioration** — sovereign debt crises, deficit monetisation
3. **Monetary debasement** — currency devaluation, reserve fragmentation
4. **Supply shocks** — structural underinvestment meeting demand surges

From the Napoleonic Wars to the 1970s stagflation era, commodities historically outperformed **when trust in financial assets and monetary stability weakened.** The current build-up of geopolitical narratives, US fiscal concerns, and central bank diversification fits this historical pattern.

### Is Gold Structurally Topping?

Meridian's view: **No.** Major gold tops historically occur when geopolitical fear evolves into a "truly systemic and gut-wrenching event"—the kind involving direct military escalation, severe economic dislocation, liquidity stress, and forced liquidation across **all** asset classes (gold included). As of mid-2026, we are still far from that stage. What we have is a build-up of tail risks that "may eventually fade or may become the early stages of a much larger macro regime shift."

### Secular Bull Corrections Are Normal, Not Exceptional

Historically, secular gold bull markets never moved in straight lines. **Corrections of 20–30% were normal, not exceptional.** At the $5,595 nominal peak, a USD 800–1,000 pullback "suddenly feels catastrophic psychologically, even though historically it would simply resemble another cyclical retracement inside a broader uptrend." The current ~25% drawdown from the January 2026 high fits this historical norm.

- **CME July probability**: 66.3% chance rates hold at 3.50–3.75% — markets no longer pricing in cuts; instead, **hike expectations building for September 2026**
- **Core inflation**: 4.2% (up sharply from 3.3% in May) — energy-driven inflation complicates Fed calculus
- **Fed trajectory**: Four dissenters in May have grown into a broader hawkish contingent; JOLTS job openings at 2-year highs, strong NFP expected for June
- **Real rates**: ~0.45% (nominal 4.65% – inflation 4.2%) — turning less negative; reduced gold support

**J.P. Morgan view**: "Growing worries that the Fed might have to respond to energy-driven inflation with hikes" have put gold "on the back burner for most investors."

### Geopolitical Risk
- **Middle East conflict**: Ceasefire in place since Apr 8; **US-Iran MOU signed Jun 17** establishing 60-day negotiation window on nuclear program & SoH reopening
- **Strait of Hormuz**: Gradually reopening since Jun 18 — 284 vessels transited per Kpler data, but volumes remain **below pre-conflict average** (~100–138 vessels/day); mines and damaged port infrastructure require clearance
- **US-Iran talks**: Pakistan-brokered 14-point MOU under negotiation; Qatar & Pakistan mediators established a "communication line" to ensure safe passage; Iranian leadership reportedly divided on 12–15 year enrichment moratorium
- **Sanctions relief**: US partially lifted sanctions on Iranian oil exports in late June; China lifted urea export restrictions
- **Trade concerns**: Tariff disputes persist but USD safe-haven flows have strengthened the dollar, offsetting some gold support

**Critical change from May**: The dominant narrative has shifted from **"prolonged Hormuz closure"** to **"fragile but real de-escalation."** The MOU and SoH reopening have triggered a partial safe-haven premium unwind in gold, similar to the rapid price reversals in [[Ammonia]] (−10.8% from Apr peak) and [[Aluminium]]. However, J.P. Morgan cautions that "these themes are on hold until more clarity arrives around a resolution of the Iran conflict." A breakdown in the 60-day negotiation window would quickly restore the risk premium.

## 6a. Gold as a Rupiah Hedge — Meridian Research View

> *"Is not our preferred Rupiah hedge short term."* — Meridian Research

### The Short-Term Bearish Setup
Three conditions must align to pressure gold, and all three are currently in place:
1. **Higher US real yields** — increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold
2. **Stronger USD** — tightens global financial conditions, especially for EMs dependent on USD funding
3. **Tight global liquidity** — emerging market currencies weaken disproportionately during late-cycle USD strength

US 1-year inflation swaps and median inflation expectations have surged as oil remains above $100/barrel, forcing markets to reconsider the entire "Fed easing" narrative. This is the same dynamic driving the Fed-hike pricing referenced in §6 above.

### Cross-Asset Variance: Why Gold Is a Noisy Hedge

| Asset | Weekly Variance |
|---|---|
| **Gold (XAU/USD)** | ~1.3% |
| **USD/IDR** | ~0.2% |

Gold is **not a low-volatility hedge.** During historical gold bull markets, weekly variance in gold prices averaged ~1.3%, while USD/IDR variance averaged only ~0.2%. If gold corrects 2–3% during a positioning flush while USD/IDR only rises modestly, the real hedge performance in Rupiah terms suddenly looks much less attractive. Indonesian investors hedging IDR depreciation face **basis risk**: the gold hedge can whip around far more than the FX exposure it is meant to offset.

### Positioning Was Crowded

The last five years created one of the strongest gold momentum environments in decades—massive performance chasing, ETF inflows, and macro uncertainties pushed positioning increasingly crowded. Crowded positioning unwinds violently; the May–July 2026 correction is a textbook example. At current nominal prices, a USD 800–1,000 pullback feels psychologically catastrophic even though it historically resembles just another cyclical retracement inside a broader uptrend.

### Meridian's Rupiah / JCI View (Context for Gold Hedging)
- **USD/IDR**: Turned bullish at 16,800; now at **17,748**. Medium-term target **Rp21,000/USD**.
- **JCI**: Turned bearish at 8,250; now at **6,162**, firmly bearish, with potential to break below 5,500. Banks remain the main pressure point. The DSI-related liquidity pull (see §7 below) likely opened the door for another leg lower.
- **Implication**: The Rupiah depreciation gold is meant to hedge is real and ongoing, but **holding foreign FX directly is a cleaner short-term hedge than gold** at current gold volatility. The bigger gold story is a 2027–2030 structural play, not a Q3 2026 hedge.

## 7. Gold in the Indonesian Context

### ARCI — Primary Gold Producer
- **Q1 2026 production**: 29 koz (+38% YoY) — strongest Q1 on record
- **FY 2026 forecast**: 155–168 koz (base case ~160 koz)
- **Revenue impact (revised)**: At ~$4,200/oz (current H2 assumption), FY 2026 revenue drops to ~$670M; at $3,900/oz bear-case, revenue falls to ~$620M. The ~$500/oz correction from May levels erases ~$80M in expected annual revenue.
- **Seasonal pattern**: Q1 trough (0.69x avg), Q3 peak (1.21x avg) — driven by Indonesia's monsoon cycle in North Sulawesi
- **Margin pressure**: The progressive royalty regime (7%–16%) hits the top bracket at prices above ~$4,000/oz. With gold currently ~$4,176, ARCI remains in the top bracket, but the lower realized price compresses unit margins significantly versus the May 2026 scenario.

### Regulatory Headwinds
- **Progressive gold royalty**: 7%–16% based on price brackets (up from 3.75%–10%)
- At current gold prices, ARCI faces the top bracket, compressing margins
- Revised mining law introduces ownership and licensing uncertainty

### Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI) — New Commodity Export Regime (Meridian Research)

On **May 20, 2026**, President Prabowo Subianto announced that Indonesia's strategic commodity exports (coal, CPO, ferro-alloys — the top-3 export contributors) would be funnelled through a single state body, **Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI)**, to combat under-invoicing and transfer pricing (an estimated $908B hemorrhaged overseas).

| Phase | Dates | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1** | Jun 1 – Aug 31, 2026 | DSI acts as mandatory auditor/price police — verifies export contract prices vs. benchmarks; exporters still transact directly |
| **Phase 2** | Sep 1, 2026 onwards | DSI becomes the **sole exporting body** — foreign buyers can only purchase from DSI; producers sell to DSI, which resells to foreign buyers |

**Why this matters for gold equities (even though gold is not a Phase-1/Phase-2 commodity):**
1. **JCI liquidity shock**: The DSI announcement triggered a JCI sell-off (now at 6,162, with Meridian warning of a break below 5,500). Banks are the main pressure point. Reduced equity market liquidity spills into reduced appetite for risk assets including ARCI.
2. **Indonesian macro risk premium**: DSI inserts a SOE intermediary between producers and foreign buyers, posing contract novation risk, margin compression risk, and quality specification problems. While gold is not yet in scope, the precedent of state intervention in commodity export channels increases regulatory uncertainty for all Indonesian resource companies.
3. **APKINDO historical mirror**: Indonesia's prior plywood cartel (APKINDO) succeeded for a decade (70% global share) before collapsing under IMF conditionality in 1998. The lesson—coal/CPO/ferro-alloys are globally substitutable, unlike plywood was. If DSI stumbles in its ~100-day build-out window, Indonesia-wide sentiment deteriorates further.
4. **USD/IDR tailwind for Rupiah-hedged gold investors**: Meridian's medium-term USD/IDR target of Rp21,000 (from 17,748 today) supports the case for Rupiah-based gold exposure over the long term, even if the short-term FX hedge case is weak (see §6a).

### Investment Thesis
The [[ARCI keystats robertgunawan Stockbit post]] analysis highlights a long-term thesis of gold reaching $8,000/oz over several years. At current levels (~$4,200), this implies ~90% upside to that target. However, the near-term path is clouded by the Fed-hawkish correction. ARCI offers leveraged exposure — every $100/oz change in gold price has an outsized impact on earnings given the high fixed-cost base. The stock is now a **higher-conviction, higher-volatility play**: if gold recovers toward J.P. Morgan's $6,000 Q4 target, ARCI's operating leverage creates exceptional upside; if CoinCodex/LiteFinance bearish targets (~$2,900) materialize, margin compression from the top-bracket royalty becomes severe.
