# Aluminium Price Forecast Update (July 2026)

## Executive Summary

The aluminium market has violently reversed from a **premium compression phase** back to an **acute supply escalation phase** following the collapse of the US-Iran ceasefire and the formal re-closure of the Strait of Hormuz on **July 11, 2026**. The "black swan" supply shock that pushed LME prices to a 4-year high of $3,672/t in April is no longer a fading memory — it has been reactivated by the IRGC strike on the M/V GFS Galaxy, Iran's formal closure declaration, and Qatar's unprecedented blanket maritime suspension.

The **dual catalysts** for the violent reversal:
1. **Geopolitical Re-escalation**: The June 17 US-Iran MOU was **nullified on day 24** (Jul 11). The Strait of Hormuz, which had briefly reopened (284 vessels transited Jun 18–Jul 8), is now **formally closed until further notice**. Qatar's Transport Ministry suspended **all maritime vessel activity** (Jul 11), directly threatening Qatalum alumina feedstock and Ras Laffan LNG feed gas. The July 11 exchange also saw Iran retaliate against Jordan and activate air defenses across Qatar, Bahrain, and UAE — the first time all four Gulf states were drawn into a single exchange.
2. **Macroeconomic Headwinds (Persistent)**: A hawkish Fed pivot (core CPI at 4.2%, September rate hike priced in) and a strengthening DXY (~102) continue to weigh on dollar-denominated commodities. However, unlike the July 3 environment where macro headwinds dominated the narrative, the supply shock is now severe enough to override the DXY drag — mirroring the divergence seen in [[Ammonia_July_2026_Update]].

**Current Status (as of July 17, 2026):** LME 3-month prices have re-escalated to the **$3,400–$3,600/t** range, reversing the July 3 compression. The market is no longer in "fragile normalization" — it is back in **crisis regime**. The path to $4,000/t, which required an "MOU collapse" as a bull-case assumption on July 3, is now the **base-case trajectory** unless diplomacy rapidly intervenes.

## 2. Scenario Forecasts (Revised July 17, 2026)

### Bull Case ($4,200–$4,800+/t through H2 2026)
**Assumptions:**
- Strait of Hormuz remains formally closed for 6+ months; sustained regional war.
- Qatar maritime suspension extends indefinitely; Qatalum forced into deeper shutdown.
- Alba restart fails due to alumina feed disruption from Oman logistics bottleneck.
- China holds at 45M ton cap; no swing capacity available.
- Fed blinks on inflation and pivots back to rate cuts, weakening the USD.

**Supporting Analysis:**
- **Citi/Commerzbank**: $4,000/t targets are now base case; bull case extends to $4,500/t+ if Gulf losses deepen beyond ~3M tons.
- **Mercuria**: The structural 2M+ ton deficit widens to 3M+ tons if Qatar joins Alba/EGA in force majeure.
- **ING**: Reversed from "premium compression" to "acute tightness" view; $4,200/t achievable if non-Russian LME stocks draw below 40,000t.

### Base Case ($3,600–$4,200/t through H2 2026)
**Assumptions:**
- Strait of Hormuz remains closed or operates under "contested transit" (toll/escort/military convoy) with intermittent interruptions.
- Oman route-management proposal gains partial traction by Aug–Sep, but Iran demands tolls or restricts access.
- Alba partial restart proceeds slowly (~6–9 months); Qatalum holds at ~65% but cannot ramp higher due to Qatar maritime suspension.
- EGA Al Taweelah remains offline through 2026 (12+ month repair timeline).
- Fed implements one 25bp hike in Sep/Nov; DXY firm at 100–105.
- LME stocks remain critically low; Russian metal share stays >90%, keeping "usable" inventory near zero.

**Supporting Analysis:**
- **Citi/Commerzbank**: $4,000/t is now the working base case given the Jul 11 closure and Qatar suspension.
- **World Bank/Goldman Sachs**: Prior $3,000–$3,200/t forecasts are being revised upward to $3,600–$3,900/t as the "normalization" thesis collapses.
- **ING**: "The premium compression trade is dead. The market is pricing acute shortage again."

### Bear Case ($2,800–$3,400/t)
**Assumptions:**
- Rapid diplomatic breakthrough: Iran accepts comprehensive nuclear curbs + verified sanctions sequencing within 4–6 weeks.
- SoH reopens under new international administration (UN/IMO mandate).
- Qatar lifts maritime suspension and accelerates Ras Laffan repair beyond 3–5 year baseline (unlikely but possible).
- Alba and Qatalum announce full restart timelines by Q4 2026.
- Fed aggressively hikes 2-3 times in H2 2026; DXY breaks above 105, overwhelming the supply shock.
- Massive surge in non-Russian LME registered stocks (unlikely given China cap).

**Supporting Analysis:**
- Even in this best-case diplomatic scenario, the **briefs.co long-run structural floor** (~$2,800–$3,000/t) applies because Qatar Ras Laffan's 3–5 year repair timeline means Gulf ammonia/urea/alumina feedstock faces a structural methane shortage regardless of shipping access.

## 4. Inventory & Market Tightness

- **LME Registered Stocks**: Combined stocks have slightly declined to ~595kt as non-Russian metal is drawn down. The "usable" (non-Russian) buffer is now critically low — **<2 days of global production**.
- **Russian Metal Share**: Now ~93% of warranted stocks. Western buyers' inability to access this metal means the effective inventory is virtually zero for US/EU consumers.
- **China Capacity**: Still running near the 45M ton cap (~97% utilization). China remains a net importer, meaning it cannot act as a swing producer to offset Gulf losses. If anything, China's own import dependence is a source of upside price pressure.

## 5. Demand Outlook & Regional Premiums

### Structural Drivers
- **Tailwinds**: EV and renewable energy demand continues to grow (~2.5% p.a.).
- **Headwinds**: 
  - **US Tariffs**: 50% tariffs are causing genuine demand destruction in some industrial segments.
  - **EU CBAM**: Implementation costs are beginning to hit semi-fabricated import margins.

### Premium Re-escalation
The "panic" premiums of April, which had compressed through early July, are now **re-escalating**. The US Midwest premium has jumped from ~$0.88/lb (Jul 3) back toward ~$1.02/lb as the risk of a total Gulf shutdown has been reactivated by the Jul 11 Hormuz closure and Qatar maritime suspension. The **structural floor** from US tariffs (50%) and EU CBAM remains in place, but the "panic premium" on top of that floor is rebuilding rapidly.

- **US Midwest Premium**: Re-widening toward $1.00–$1.10/lb. Tariff floor + supply panic.
- **Europe (Rotterdam) Premium**: Re-widening toward $520–$580/t. CBAM floor + Hormuz closure.
- **Japan Q3 Benchmark**: Re-rising toward $300–$320/t as Asian buyers scramble for non-Gulf supply.

## 6. Indonesia / CITA Linkage

**CITA (PT Cita Mineral Investindo Tbk)** as an aluminium extruder is sensitive to both LME prices and regional premiums.
- **Margin Impact**: The violent swing from $3,600 $\to$ $3,100/t (Jul 3) $\to$ $3,450/t (Jul 17) creates margin whipsaw. The Jul 3 dip offered temporary raw material cost relief, but the Jul 11 re-escalation restores pricing power for finished extrusions while also raising input costs.
- **CBAM Risk**: As a regional player, CITA's export competitiveness to the EU is under pressure from the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), which adds a cost layer to non-EU aluminium.
- **DSI Context**: Aluminium extrusions are not currently in Phase 1 or 2 of the Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI) export regime, but the broader "nationalization" sentiment of DSI creates a general risk premium for Indonesian resource-linked equities. The Jul 11 Hormuz re-closure and Qatar suspension amplify this by making non-Gulf supply sources more strategically valuable.

## 8. Investment Implications & Watchpoints

### Tactical Strategy
- **Bias**: Structurally bullish short-term and long-term. The Jul 11 re-escalation has removed the de-escalation discount.
- **Key Levels**: Support at $3,200–$3,400/t; Resistance at $3,800–$4,200/t.
- **Trade**: The "premium compression" trade is dead. Monitor LME non-Russian stock draws and Qatar maritime suspension duration for upside acceleration.

### Key Watchpoints (July–September 2026)
1. **Qatar maritime suspension duration**: The first blanket Gulf-state maritime suspension (Jul 11). If extended, Qatalum feedstock is at risk and premiums spike further.
2. **US CENTCOM posture**: Fourth strike round? Or pivot to defensive blockade? Escalation vs. containment shapes the supply-shock severity.
3. **Oman diplomatic track**: Any route-management proposal that gains US + Iranian traction could reopen Hormuz under a toll/escort regime.
4. **Smelter Restart Milestones**: Alba partial restart progress; Qatalum stability under Qatar suspension; EGA Al Taweelah repair orders.
5. **LME Inventory**: Any significant draw in non-Russian stocks (<50,000t) would signal acute tightness and accelerate price spikes.
6. **Fed July 29 / September Meeting**: Guidance on rate hikes will drive DXY, but supply shock may now dominate macro headwinds.
7. **CITA Earnings**: Q2 2026 print for clues on margin resilience amid price volatility ($3,100 → $3,450/t swing).

## 9. Conclusion

The aluminium market has violently reversed from a **corrective regime** back to a **crisis regime**. The "black swan" supply shock, which had been partially discounted during the Jun 18–Jul 8 Hormuz reopening window, has been fully reactivated by the July 11 IRGC strike on the M/V GFS Galaxy, Iran's formal Strait of Hormuz closure declaration, and Qatar's unprecedented blanket maritime suspension.

**Base case**: LME aluminium averages **$3,600–$4,000/t through H2 2026**. The path to $4,000/t no longer requires a new catalyst — the catalyst (MOU collapse + Hormuz re-closure + Qatar suspension) has already occurred. The only question is whether diplomacy can partially reverse the damage before Q4, or whether the market enters a sustained $4,000+/t regime by year-end.
