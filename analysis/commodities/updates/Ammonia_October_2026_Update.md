# Ammonia Price Update — The Chinese Supply Wall (October 21, 2026)

> **Status:** This note **supersedes the price thesis** in [[Ammonia_July_2026_Update]] (July 17, 2026). The July note called the April–May peak "no longer the cycle top" and argued for an upward revision of ESSA's H2 realized price to $600–650/MT. That call is **invalidated**. Ammonia benchmarked at a **12-month low in early October 2026 — below pre-conflict levels — with the Middle East still largely absent from the market.**

---

## Executive Summary

The ammonia complex made a 12-month low on October 8, 2026, giving back **12–15%** and trading **below pre-war levels**, despite the fact that Strait of Hormuz disruption continues to remove most Middle Eastern product from international markets (global anhydrous ammonia trade is **−14% YTD**). That is the single most important fact in this note: **seaborne supply lost to geopolitics was more than replaced from ex-Middle East sources.**

Attribution, ranked by evidence weight:

1. **China (primary, structural)** — 2025 output 77.7 Mt (+6.1%), **+5.38 Mt of new capacity in 2026 (4.02 Mt in H2)**, supply growth **4.9% vs demand 3.9%**. Domestic prices at a 5-year low pushed surplus into exports: **H1-26 exports 588.7 kt, +870.77% YoY** (record), with a further **+78% MoM** surge reported in the latest month. This is capacity-driven oversupply finding an export window, not a temporary arbitrage.
2. **India (secondary, input-constrained)** — Platts ammonia delivered India fell to **$465/MT CFR West Coast / $455/MT CFR East Coast (Aug 20)**, below the pre-war $495/$485 and −48% from the **$885/MT April peak**. The cause is **sulphur scarcity cutting DAP output** (India complex fertiliser production **−28%**, imports **−48.5%** in the June quarter) — *not* a collapse in farm-gate demand. Indian **urea** imports actually surged (25.1 lt Apr–Jun vs 8.4 lt prior year).
3. **Benchmark repricing and destocking (mechanical)** — Tampa settled **$545/MT CFR in October**, the **fourth consecutive monthly decline** from a **$825/MT May peak (−33%)**; Q3 seasonal lull plus comfortable distributor inventories kept buyers on the sidelines.

What the July note missed: it treated the June–July move as a **geopolitical oscillation** and put "China floods the market" at *Low-Moderate* probability. In reality the Chinese export wave was already in the trade data by mid-August — a **supply-cycle** problem wearing a geopolitical costume.

Tactically, October is now **firming** (SE Asia FOB low-$400s, IFA Crossroads reports firmer SE Asia FOB, European gas at its highest since December 2022) because Chinese export availability tightened in late September and an **extended maintenance turnaround in Indonesia** was reported on October 6 — but this is a supply-side bounce off a low base, not a regime change.

## 2. Driver 1 — China: Capacity, Not Opportunity

The Chinese leg is the load-bearing part of the thesis and it is **structural**:

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| China ammonia output 2025 | 77.687 Mt, **+6.1% YoY** | CNFIA |
| 2026 capacity additions | **+5.38 Mt** (4.02 Mt in H2-26) | Longzhong |
| Implied supply vs demand growth | **+4.9% vs +3.9%** | Longzhong |
| China H1-26 ammonia exports | **588.7 kt, +870.77% YoY** (record) | China Customs |
| Export peak month | May, **185 kt (+163% MoM)** | China Customs |
| Export destinations | South Korea, **India**, Morocco, Vietnam | China Customs |
| China domestic H1 average | RMB 2,247/t, **−6.53% YoY (5-yr low)** | SunSirs |
| Latest export pulse | **+78% MoM** — "unprecedented wave" | OPIS (8 Oct) |

**Mechanism:** domestic delivered-to-port cost ≈ RMB 2,400/t versus international $550–650/MT in Q2 → export arbitrage became the only outlet for surplus. Chinese producers were *price-takers off a domestic cost floor*, which caps how far international prices can fall but also means they keep shipping until domestic prices hit cash cost.

**Why this beats the geopolitical story:** Middle East supply losses were real and are ongoing (global trade −14% YTD through May; North American and Chinese shipments "surged"). Yet prices went **below pre-war levels**. If the market were still a war-supply story, that is arithmetically impossible without a large, offsetting supply source. China is that source.

**Second-order lens for ESSA:** China's nitrogen policy is not uniform. Beijing **cut its 2026 urea export quota 34% (−1.7 Mt vs 2025) and set FOB floors of $430–440/t** (Sep 18). Ammonia got the opposite treatment — an open export window. Any future administrative tightening of *ammonia* exports is the single largest upside catalyst for ESSA's pricing that is not geopolitical.

## 3. Driver 2 — India: A Sulphur Problem Dressed as a Demand Problem

| Metric | Value | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia delivered India (Platts) | **$465 WC / $455 EC CFR** | 20 Aug 2026 |
| Pre-war reference | $495 WC / $485 EC | 27 Feb 2026 |
| April peak | **$885/MT CFR** | Apr 2026 |
| India complex fertiliser output | **−28%** (1.92 Mt) | Jun-26 quarter |
| India complex fertiliser imports | **−48.5%** (491 kt) | Jun-26 quarter |
| India urea imports | **25.1 lt** vs 8.4 lt | Apr–Jun 2026 vs 2025 |

The causal chain is **sulphur, not affordability**: Hormuz disruption curtailed sulphur/sulphuric acid availability, DAP producers cut run rates, and ammonia offtake fell with them. Icra: *"constrained sulphur availability is keeping the demand for ammonia also under check as both are needed to manufacture NPK grades."* A trader on the binding constraint: *"It is not about the sulfur prices now, now is about the sulfur availability — where are they going to source sulfur?"*

**Three implications that matter for the model:**

- **Do not model this as demand destruction.** DAP prices are at record highs (~$935/MT CFR; Mosaic guides Q3 DAP $820–840 FOB). This is a *supply-side* cut in a product with strong underlying demand.
- **It is reversible.** Indian buyers expected improvement from September as the government subsidised sulphur procurement; QC Intel already reported **firming Indian delivered prices on firmer demand and SE Asian supply constraints (8 Oct)**.
- **Ammonia nitrate/phosphate demand is the swing leg, not urea.** ESSA sells industrial/fertiliser ammonia into the Japanese offtake and the regional index — the phosphate complex is the marginal price-setter for the Asian index, which is why sulphur headlines lead ESSA's realised price.

## 4. Driver 3 — Benchmark Repricing, Destocking and Seasonality

- **Tampa:** $825 (May) → $555 (Sep, −$80 MoM) → **$545 (Oct)** = **four consecutive declines, −33%**, "the steepest sustained Tampa decline since the post-2022 pullback."
- **Systematix (Aug 10):** *"a genuine trend change, with the market shifting from the US-Iran war-driven scarcity to a surplus,"* expecting further downside through the December quarter before Rabi demand and supply cuts support prices.
- **Q3 seasonal lull** after spring application, weak industrial demand, comfortable distributor inventories → buyers on the sidelines, no bid to catch falling prices.
- **October firming** now visible on: Chinese export availability slowing (QC Intel, 16 Sep), extended **Indonesian** turnaround reported (6 Oct), European gas at its **highest since December 2022** driving European curtailments and pulling Atlantic basin ammonia away from Asia.

## 6. ESSA Implications

### 6.1 Where realised price actually landed

| Period | Realised ammonia | Source / derivation |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 | $455/MT | Q1 report |
| H1 2026 | **$531/MT, +70% YoY** | idnfinancials (28 Jul 2026) |
| Q2 2026 (implied) | **≈ $607/MT** | 2×531 − 455 |
| Q3 2026 (est.) | **≈ $470–510/MT** | Jul–Sep spot path |
| Q4 2026 (est.) | **≈ $440–480/MT** | Spot low-$400s + October firming + contract lag |

Contract structure matters in both directions: the Genesis offtake is indexed to the **SE Asia reference with minimal discount** (near-parity established in Q1) but with an index **lag**, so ESSA's realised price trails spot by roughly a quarter. That cushioned Q3; it will equally cap any Q4 recovery.

### 6.2 Sensitivity (from the Q2 forecast methodology)

- **$10/MT ≈ $1.5M revenue and $1.2M net profit per ~150 kt quarter.**
- Versus the July note's $600/MT H2 assumption, a **$100/MT miss ≈ −$15M revenue and −$12M net profit per quarter.**
- Two consecutive quarters at ~$460–480 instead of $600 → roughly **−$25–30M of H2 net profit** vs. the revised July base case. Against H1 net profit of $30.2M, that is a material step-down in the run-rate, not a wipeout.

### 6.3 Offsets that remain intact

- **Zero bank debt**, ~$195M projected cash post-Q2 → the balance sheet is not the risk at these prices; **earnings momentum** is.
- **Post-turnaround volume uplift** (+9% to ~2,530 MTPD) means ESSA ships more tonnes into a lower price — margin-per-tonne compression is partially diluted at the top line.
- **USDIDR ~18,000+** remains a translation tailwind for a USD-revenue/rupiah-cost structure.
- October's **Indonesian turnaround extension** (attributed to an Indonesian ammonia plant — most likely PAU; **unconfirmed**) is *bullish price, bearish ESSA volume* if it is ESSA's own plant. Resolve before Q4 volume modelling.
- Sell-side is not capitulating: **Ciptadana set an IDR 620 target (Oct 13)**, entry IDR 585, stop IDR 560.

### 6.4 Next hard datapoint

**Q3-2026 results (late October):** the first realised price printed in the post-collapse market. Watch (i) realised ammonia $/MT vs spot path, (ii) whether the turnaround cost and SAF delay are treated as one-offs, (iii) inventory movements, (iv) any language change on the Genesis index formula.

## 7. Revised Scenarios — Q4-26 / H1-27 Ammonia (SE Asia reference)

| Scenario | SE Asia ammonia | Prob. | Assumptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear — Chinese export discipline breaks down** | **$380–430/MT** | 30% | Chinese restarts continue (liquid ammonia fell 13% w/w to 16 Oct); Indian phosphate run rates stay at seasonal lows; no sulphur fix; European gas retreats; Tampa drifts to $500 |
| **Base — bottomed, low-level range** | **$440–500/MT** | 45% | Chinese export availability stays patchy; Indian Rabi demand recovers modestly; European gas keeps Atlantic basin product home; Indonesian/Middle East outages provide episodic support |
| **Bull — administrative or physical squeeze** | **$520–600/MT** | 25% | China restricts ammonia exports (cf. the 34% urea quota cut) *or* a major Indonesian/Middle East outage; European gas spikes further; sulphur availability unlocks Indian phosphate run rates at high DAP prices |

**Anchors worth remembering:** China's domestic cost line (~RMB 2,050–2,400/t, i.e. roughly **$290–340/MT**) is the soft floor for the global complex — the market cannot trade far below Chinese cash cost for long, but it can trade there for a while. Pre-war SE Asia was ~$460–480; the 2022 cycle top was ~$1,600.

**Key asymmetry:** the July note assumed the war premium would ratchet **up**. The evidence now says the **supply cycle dominates**, so price risk is skewed to the downside and the realistic upside is **administrative (Chinese policy) or physical (outage)**, not geopolitical.

## 9. Conclusion

The 2026 ammonia shock has resolved the way most supply shocks resolve when a low-cost marginal producer has spare capacity: **Chinese oversupply absorbed the war premium**. Capacity additions of 5.38 Mt against 3.9% demand growth gave China both the volume and the domestic-price incentive to export 588.7 kt in H1 (+870.77% YoY), and India's sulphur-constrained phosphate complex removed the marginal bid on top of it. The result is a 12-month low, below pre-war levels, with the Middle East still off-market.

For ESSA the read-across is a **de-rating of the earnings base, not of the balance sheet**: realised price of ~$607/MT in Q2 and an estimated ~$470–510 in Q3 gives H2 net profit a materially lower ceiling than the July revision implied. The stock's support at these levels rests on zero debt, ~$195M cash, volume recovery and a blue-ammonia option — not on an ammonia price that "catches up to urea."

**The thesis to hold from here:** ammonia is now a **cost-floor + Chinese-policy** market, not a **war-premium** market. Trim H2 realised assumptions to **$450–520/MT SE Asia**, treat the October firming as a low-base supply bounce, and make Chinese ammonia export policy — not Strait of Hormuz headlines — the primary swing factor for 2027.
