### Urea-Ammonia Spread Analysis
- Urea peaked at ~$200/t premium to ammonia in April 2026
- Spread driven by: urea is solid (dry bulk vessels), ammonia is liquid (pipelines available)
- Urea disruption immediate and visible; ammonia impact from LNG cuts propagates over weeks/months
- **Original thesis (Apr 2026):** Ammonia likely to catch up to urea pricing as prolonged LNG outages hit production — rated "High probability"
- **Interim outcome (Jun 2026):** Spread compressed from the **urea side** — urea plunged −30 to −53% from peak as SoH reopened and China lifted export bans. Ammonia declined more modestly (−10 to −20%). Spread had narrowed from ~$200/t to ~$60/t (ME FOB urea ~$630 vs ammonia ~$570)
- **Update (Jul 17, 2026):** With the July 11 IRGC re-closure and US third-round strikes, urea is the **first responder** again (+17.8% MoM vs ~flat ammonia index). The original "ammonia catches up to urea" thesis may yet play out on the second leg — but only if the LNG feedstock shock (Qatar Ras Laffan damage, ~3–5 yr repair per S&P Global / Maritime Executive) eventually outweighs shorter-cycle tanker panic. The oscillating pattern (war → ceasefire → war) now favours the original thesis on a 6–12 month horizon even though it inverted during the brief June window.

### India Tender Signal
- India (world's largest urea importer) tendered 2.5M MT in single purchase (5% of global trade) — at the April peak
- Transaction price: $935-$960/t vs. $510/t two months ago (+83%)
- Demonstrated urgency to secure fertilizer for planting season at the height of the crisis
- **Update (Jun 2026):** India had been taking its time before issuing another import tender, focusing on securing additional gas supplies to prevent further declines in domestic production (ICIS). With urea prices falling back toward pre-war levels, the urgency premium had evaporated.
- **Update (Jul 2026):** India initiated its **second 7-million-tonne global urea import tender** (announced Jun 2 per ChemAnalyst, supplies landing Jun–Jul for kharif). The government has already contracted ~2.5M MT urea and ~5M MT DAP from suppliers outside the SoH region. Spot prices surged from ~$447/t to nearly $947/t (per Aparna S. Sharma, Additional Secretary, Ministry of Fertilizers). However, the Ministry of Agriculture has **revised Kharif demand downward** — urea demand cut by 400K MT (to 19.4M MT) and DAP by 600K MT (to ~6M MT) on El Niño-affected cropping pattern reassessment. Net: India entered the July re-escalation with substantial already-contracted volumes for kharif, providing a partial buffer; the next tender round (likely rabi season) will land squarely into the re-escalation price spike.

### The "Oscillating Crisis" Pattern — A New Market Regime

Unlike the 2022 Russia-Ukraine shock — a one-directional structural step-up — the 2026 US-Iran conflict is exhibiting an **oscillating pattern**: war (Feb–Apr) → ceasefire (Apr 8) → MOU (Jun 17) → re-escalation (Jul 8 ceasefire collapse, Jul 11 IRGC strike). Each ~7-week oscillation re-inflates and then partially deflates the war premium, producing a series of local price peaks, troughs, and new high-legs.

Implications:
- **Spot traders** have been whipsawed — buying-the-trough (Jun) on the "normalization" thesis has been decisively stopped out within ~3 weeks
- **Structural floors persist**: Qatar Ras Laffan damage (3–5 yr repair) and Middle East supply-chain trust erosion are invariant across oscillations. briefs.co (Jul 5, written before re-escalation) projected a long-run urea floor of $532/short ton (~13% above pre-crisis) — the floor is independent of any individual ceasefire oscillation
- **Insurance & freight premia**: War-risk insurance at 8.0× pre-crisis (straits.live, Jul 17). Each oscillation ratchets up baseline shipping, insurance, and risk premia — the "overshoot" baseline never fully reverts
- **Forecast implications**: H2 2026 base case for ammonia should be revised **UPWARD** — the [[ESSA_Q2_2026_Earnings_Forecast]] "slow normalization" assumption (Q3 ~$600/MT, Q4 ~$500/MT) now looks too pessimistic; management's "3–6 month normalization" guidance is likely a floor under realized price, not a ceiling
- **Iranian rial pressure (873k open-market, 7d +8.85%)** signals domestic economic stress that may push Iranian leadership toward either capitulation or further escalation — both scenarios are bullish for fertilizer prices in the short-to-medium term

### What Can Re-Open the Strait

Per the original Jun 19 deal terms (CNBC), Iran allowed 60 days of toll-free transit; "after the 60-day toll-free period ends, Iran will hold talks with Oman and the Gulf states on how to administer the strait." The toll-free window opened Jun 18 and Iran abrogated it on Jul 11 (day 24 of the 60 days). With the IRGC formal closure declaration on Jul 11 (first of the entire conflict per Windward), the prior arrangement is nullified. Oman's tentative route-management proposal remains the only diplomatic pathway; US has stated "nuclear talks cannot progress until the Strait is secure." Any reopened framework would now likely include tolls, verifiable transit guarantees, and a broader Gulf security commitment — meaning the Jul–Aug SoH regime, if it reopens, will carry a structurally higher cost of transit than the Jun 18–Jul 8 window.

### Implications for the Original Thesis (Updated Jul 17, 2026)

The original analysis (and [[Rupiah & Ammonia - Meridian Research]]) identified the **Strait of Hormuz reopening as the key risk** — rated "Low probability, not expected soon." The risk **materialized briefly** (Jun 18 – Jul 8) and has now **re-closed**. The urea-ammonia spread mean-reversion thesis ("ammonia likely to catch up to urea pricing," rated "High probability") **inverted during the June window** (urea plunged toward ammonia) but is now **re-opening** as urea surges first on the July re-escalation — ammonia is the lagged follower, and the original "high probability" thesis may yet play out on the second leg of the cycle, particularly given the 3–5-year Qatar LNG feedstock constraint. The ESSA "champion stock" tailwind from elevated ammonia prices — which appeared to be "unwinding" through June — is now **re-emerging** rather than unwinding. The April–May peak is no longer the cycle top; a new high-leg is forming with urea leading and ammonia the lagged follower.
