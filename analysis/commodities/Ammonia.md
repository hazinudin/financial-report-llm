**Key observation (updated Jul 2026):** Ammonia prices surged 23.3% to USD 0.37/kg in April 2026 on the US-Iran war supply shock, peaked in May (~0.38/kg), then reversed sharply to USD 0.33/kg (Northeast Asia, IMARC June 2026) — a **−13.2% MoM decline** — following the June 17 US-Iran MOU and gradual Strait of Hormuz reopening. ## US-Iran War Impact — Supply Shock (2026)

### Strait of Hormuz Closure
- SoH accounts for ~30% of global urea and ammonia exports
- 20% of global LNG trade passes through the strait
- Asian nations receive 35% of urea and 64% of ammonia imports from Middle East

### Supply Disruptions
| Event | Impact |
|---|---|
| QatarEnergy force majeure | LNG exports stranded |
| Iran plant shutdowns | 7 urea/ammonia plants offline (fear of Israeli military targets) |
| China export ban | Nitrogen-potassium blend fertilizer exports halted |
| Russia export ban | Ammonium nitrate exports banned until at least April 21 |
| Australia | Largest ammonia plant stopped production until late May |
| India | Multiple fertilizer plants (urea + ammonia) shut down due to LNG feedstock shortage |

**Total supply removed:** ~3.3M MT from Saudi, Iran, and Qatar (top ammonia exporters)

### Price Surge & Reversal

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonia (Middle East) | $480/t | $700/t | ~$570/t³ | **+46%** | −18.6% |
| Urea (Middle East) | $490/t | $907/t | ~$630/t | **+85%** | −30.4% |

> **Note:** Urea has reverted much closer to pre-war levels than ammonia. The urea-ammonia spread has compressed sharply (see updated analysis below).

### Urea-Ammonia Spread Analysis
- Urea peaked at ~$200/t premium to ammonia in April 2026
- Spread driven by: urea is solid (dry bulk vessels), ammonia is liquid (pipelines available)
- Urea disruption immediate and visible; ammonia impact from LNG cuts propagates over weeks/months
- **Original thesis (Apr 2026):** Ammonia likely to catch up to urea pricing as prolonged LNG outages hit production — rated "High probability"
- **Actual outcome (Jun 2026):** Spread compressed from the **urea side** — urea plunged −30 to −53% from peak as SoH reopened and China lifted export bans. Ammonia declined more modestly (−10 to −20%). The mean-reversion occurred, but via urea falling rather than ammonia rising. Spread has narrowed from ~$200/t to ~$60/t (ME FOB urea ~$630 vs ammonia ~$570)

### India Tender Signal
- India (world's largest urea importer) tendered 2.5M MT in single purchase (5% of global trade) — at the April peak
- Transaction price: $935-$960/t vs. $510/t two months ago (+83%)
- Demonstrated urgency to secure fertilizer for planting season at the height of the crisis
- **Update (Jun 2026):** India is now taking its time before issuing another import tender, focusing on securing additional gas supplies to prevent further declines in domestic production (ICIS). With urea prices falling back toward pre-war levels, the urgency premium has evaporated.

### 2026 vs. 2022 Shock Comparison
| Dimension | 2022 (Russia-Ukraine) | 2026 (US-Iran) |
|---|---|---|
| Shock vectors | Single (natural gas to Europe) | **Three simultaneous** |
| Trade disruption | Regional (Europe) | Global (SoH closure) |
| LNG impact | Europe cut off | 20% of global LNG exports stranded |
| Production cuts | European plants | Global (Iran, India, Australia, China, Russia) |
| Ammonia peak | $1,600/t | Approaching 2022 levels |
| Resolution speed | Years (structural gas shift) | **~3 months** (ceasefire Apr 8 → MOU Jun 17 → SoH reopening) |

> **Key difference:** The 2022 shock was structural (permanent gas pipeline rerouting); the 2026 shock is proving more transient — the ceasefire and SoH reopening have triggered a rapid price reversal within ~3 months of the peak. However, structural damage to Qatar's Ras Laffan hubs means the recovery will not be fully V-shaped.

## Ceasefire & SoH Reopening (June 2026) — The Reversal

> **Update (Jul 2026):** The single largest bearish catalyst for nitrogen fertilizer markets since the conflict began has arrived. The war premium that drove the April–May price spike is now unwinding.

### Timeline of De-escalation

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Feb 28, 2026 | US & Israel launch strikes on Iran; SoH effectively closed |
| Mar–Apr 2026 | Prices surge 30–85%; China bans fertilizer exports; Russia bans AN exports |
| Apr 8, 2026 | Ceasefire put in place; extended multiple times |
| May 7, 2026 | Trump signals productive discussions; 14-point MOU under negotiation (Pakistan-brokered) |
| Jun 4, 2026 | Urea futures plunge to ~$416/t (−28% over the month) |
| **Jun 17, 2026** | **US-Iran MOU signed** — 60-day negotiation window on nuclear program & SoH reopening |
| Jun 18+ | SoH traffic resumes — 284 vessels transit (Kpler); cargoes include crude, LNG, fertilizer |
| Jun 26, 2026 | Urea futures at ~$366/t (−22% MoM; 7.8% below year-ago) |
| Late Jun 2026 | US partially lifts sanctions on Iranian oil exports; China lifts urea export restrictions |

### Strait of Hormuz — Gradual Reopening

- 284 vessels transited from Jun 18 (day after MOU) per Kpler maritime data
- Still **below pre-conflict average** of ~100–138 vessels/day
- US Navy providing guidance for a southern route cleared of mines
- Iran permitting limited transit via a northern passageway
- Ships carrying **crude oil, LNG, fertilizer, and other goods** now crossing
- US-Iran "communication line" established to prevent misunderstandings and ensure safe passage (Qatar & Pakistan mediators)
- Hundreds of ships still waiting in the Gulf; full normalization will take weeks–months

Sources: IMARC Ammonia Price Index (Jun 2026); Trading Economics; University of Arkansas weekly survey; StoneX; IFDC Global Fertilizer Markets (Week 13)

**Key takeaway:** Urea has reverted to near pre-war levels far faster than ammonia. Fertilizer markets overall remain 22–28% above pre-war (IFDC, early Jun), but the gap is closing rapidly. US ammonia fell an additional 5.81% w/w in early June as buyers stepped back (ChemAnalyst, Jun 10).

### Structural Overhangs That Persist

Despite the ceasefire, several factors prevent an immediate return to pre-war normalcy:

1. **Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG & fertilizer hubs damaged** — repairs will take months to years (GIS Reports); this constrains LNG feedstock availability for Gulf ammonia/urea production
2. **SoH not fully normalized** — transit volumes still well below pre-conflict levels; mines and damaged port infrastructure require clearance
3. **Ceasefire is fragile** — verification of nuclear curbs and sequencing of sanctions relief remain sticking points; 12–15 year enrichment moratorium under discussion; Iranian leadership reportedly divided
4. **India's kharif season** — delayed Middle Eastern shipments have already impacted planting; yield effects will persist into 2026–2027
5. **World Bank projection** — 31% increase in global fertilizer prices for 2026 (Commodity Markets Outlook), even accounting for some normalization

### Implications for the Original Thesis

The original analysis (and [[Rupiah & Ammonia - Meridian Research]]) identified the **Strait of Hormuz reopening as the key risk** — rated "Low probability, not expected soon." **That risk has now materialized.** The urea-ammonia spread mean-reversion thesis ("ammonia likely to catch up to urea pricing," rated "High probability") has **inverted** — urea plunged back toward ammonia, not the reverse. The ESSA "champion stock" tailwind from elevated ammonia prices is now **unwinding**, though prices remain above pre-war levels and structural damage to Gulf infrastructure provides a partial floor.

## Demand Drivers

### 1. Fertilizer (Primary — ~70% of demand)
- Urea, ammonium nitrate, ammonium sulfate production
- Haber-Bosch process attributes 40-50% of global food production
- Crop yield falls 4-7% for every 10% reduction in nitrogen fertilizer
- [[Indonesia Fertilizer Market]]: USD 9.37B (2026) → USD 11.49B (2031), CAGR 4.15%
- Government subsidy program: 9.5M metric tons allocated annually
- Indonesia's rice self-sufficiency target by 2026 driving nitrogen demand
- Palm oil replanting program (180,000 hectares/year) locking in fertilizer demand through 2030

### 2. Industrial Chemicals
- Nitric acid, caprolactam, ammonium nitrate (explosives for mining)
- Growing chemical manufacturing in China, Japan, South Korea

### 3. Clean Energy / Green Ammonia
- Ammonia as hydrogen carrier for power generation and shipping
- Japan and South Korea leading ammonia-based fuel technology
- Yara/ACME agreement: 100,000 tons renewable ammonia annually from Oman
- BASF/AM Green MoU: 100,000 tons green ammonia annually from India
- Japan's Hydrogen Society Promotion Act: JPY 3 trillion CfD funding

### 4. Mining Explosives
- Ammonium nitrate from ammonia used in rock blasting
- Australia, China, Indonesia mining operations

## Supply Dynamics

### Key Constraints (Easing but Not Resolved)
- **US-Iran ceasefire / SoH partial reopening** — primary shock now unwinding (Jun 2026)
  - SoH transit resuming (284 vessels since Jun 18); still below pre-war average
  - US-Iran MOU (Jun 17) sets 60-day negotiation window; sanctions partially lifted on Iranian oil
  - 3.3M MT supply from Saudi, Iran, Qatar gradually returning — but Qatar's Ras Laffan hubs damaged (months–years to repair)
- **Natural gas feedstock costs** — primary input for Haber-Bosch process
  - Regasified LNG at USD 16.77/MMBtu (vs. USD 6 subsidized in Indonesia) — easing as LNG flows resume
  - Adds USD 50–70 per ton to urea production cost
  - LNG shortage forcing plant shutdowns globally (India, Australia, Iran) — some restarts expected as feedstock returns
- **Plant maintenance turnarounds** — scheduled shutdowns tighten supply
- **Geopolitical risks** — ceasefire is fragile; verification of nuclear curbs and sanctions sequencing remain unresolved; Iranian leadership divided
- **Trinidad production cuts** — reducing global supply
- **Export bans — REVERSED:** China **lifted** urea export restrictions (Jun 2026), returning to global markets; Russia's ammonium nitrate ban (until Apr 21) has expired

### Major Producers in Asia Pacific
- **China** — largest producer and consumer, dominates regional market
- **Indonesia** — PT Pupuk Indonesia (state-owned), [[ESSA]] (private), PT Pupuk Kalimantan Timur
- **India** — expanding green ammonia capacity (Hygenco: 1.1M MT target)
- **Japan** — net importer, strategic partnerships for supply security
- **Australia** — emerging green ammonia exporter

## Indonesia-Specific Dynamics

### ESSA's Strategic Position
- **Sole listed ammonia producer in Indonesia** — was direct beneficiary of supply shock; now facing price reversal
- De-levered balance sheet (zero bank debt, $164.6M cash), best-in-class utilization rates (121% in Q1 2026) — these structural strengths persist regardless of price cycle
- **Ammonia price tailwind UNWINDING:** NE Asia prices fell from 0.37 to 0.33 USD/kg (−10.8%) since the April peak; still ~10% above pre-war but declining. Q2 2026 also impacted by 35–40 day plant turnaround in May
- Positioned as "champion stock" for market recovery (Meridian Research) — thesis now under pressure as the war premium evaporates
- **Rupiah tailwind (partially intact):** USDIDR hit all-time high near 17,300 (May 2026), driven primarily by domestic factors (Prabowo's MBG programme, fiscal expansion breaching 2.8-3.0% of GDP, foreign SBN outflows of IDR 38.3Tn). Some war risk premium may unwind with de-escalation, but the dominant driver is domestic policy confidence erosion
- BI's FX intervention is purely reactive (damping volatility, not pre-empting), steadily draining reserves — no credible policy path for Rupiah to sustainably trade below 16,500
- **Meridian Research positioning:** Long USD, ESSA; Short/Avoid JCI — expects continued JCI deterioration and big bank selling pressure. The USD leg remains intact; the ESSA leg faces commodity price headwinds
- ~~Key risk: Strait of Hormuz reopening (real opening) would remove primary price support; not expected soon~~ **→ MATERIALIZED (Jun 2026):** SoH has begun reopening after the Jun 17 US-Iran MOU. Primary price support is being removed. Remaining floor: structural damage to Qatar's Ras Laffan (months–years to repair), SoH not fully normalized, ceasefire fragile

### Rupiah Macro Context ([[Rupiah & Ammonia - Meridian Research]])
- USDIDR at record highs (~17,300 in May 2026) despite solid macro fundamentals (real rates attractive vs EM peers, reserves covering 5-6 months of imports)
- Confidence erosion driven by aggressive quasi-fiscal programs (MBG) with no funding clarity since Prabowo's Oct 2024 election victory
- Foreign ownership of SBN in steady retreat (IDR 38.3Tn outflow YoY)
- CDS spreads 20-40 bps wider than EM peers — currency pricing policy trajectory in real time
- **Update (Jul 2026):** The US-Iran de-escalation may partially unwind the war-related risk premium in EM currencies, but the Rupiah's weakness is dominantly driven by domestic policy confidence erosion (MBG programme, fiscal expansion) — not the Iran conflict. The Rupiah tailwind for ESSA is thus **more durable** than the ammonia price tailwind
- Implication for ESSA: Weak Rupiah continues to boost USD-denominated ammonia revenue when converted, **partially offsetting** the declining ammonia price. The two tailwinds are now moving in opposite directions

## Green Ammonia — Emerging Opportunity

> **Update (Jul 2026):** The US-Iran war has demonstrated the vulnerability of fossil-fuel-dependent fertilizer supply chains. GIS Reports notes that the conflict is likely to act as a catalyst for green ammonia investment, as governments worldwide "having experienced fossil fuel weaponization, pour trillions into nuclear, solar and large-scale energy storage." Fertilizer may shift to green ammonia produced from renewable energy, bypassing dependence on natural gas. This structural tailwind for green/blue ammonia projects is a **permanent legacy** of the 2026 crisis, independent of the ceasefire outcome.

- MoU signed with JOGMEC, Mitsubishi Corporation, and ITB
- Feasibility study for CCUS conversion of entire 700,000 MT/year capacity
- Targets blue ammonia production for Japanese market
