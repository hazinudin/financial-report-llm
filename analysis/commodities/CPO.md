CPO has held firm between RM4,400–4,650/ton since the start of 2026, well above the RM3,900–4,000 lows seen earlier in the year. The Malaysian Palm Oil Council (MPOC) reaffirmed its structural call on June 22, 2026 that prices will hold above RM4,400 well into 2026, supported by **tightening Indonesian exportable surplus**, the B50 launch, and **rising El Niño risk**. Unlike the 2022 spike to ~RM7,000/ton (driven by Indonesia's temporary export ban—a transient policy shock that did not spark sustained CPO equity re-rating), the current support is structural, anchored by domestic biodiesel demand policy.

## 2. B50 Biodiesel Mandate — Confirmed & Live

> **Launched July 1, 2026.** Indonesia became the **first country in the world** to mandate a 50% palm oil/diesel blend. — President Prabowo, June 24, 2026 (Gorontalo)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **[[B50]] definition** | 50% palm-oil-based biodiesel + 50% conventional diesel |
| **Effective date** | **July 1, 2026** |
| **Full rollout** | October 1, 2026 (all filling stations sell B50) after 3-month staged transition |
| **Diesel import halt** | Indonesia stopped importing diesel fuel as of July 1, 2026 |
| **Annual diesel use avoided** | ~4 million kilolitres |
| **Subsidy savings (first 6 months)** | ~Rp48 trillion (~USD 2.81 billion) |
| **Annual CPO demand under B50** | ~23.3M tonnes (vs ~14.8M under B40 → **+8.5M tonne increase**) |
| **Additional CPO allocated** | 3.5M tonnes ready; 5.3M tonnes total incremental directive (Agriculture Min.) |
| **Blenders** | ~30 biofuel entities; PT Pertamina + AKR control ~70% of distribution |
| **CPO production base** | ~46–49M tonnes/year (Agriculture Min / Oil World) |

The B40 programme (in effect through end-June 2026) already displaced ~3.3M kL of diesel and cut ~38.9M tonnes CO₂e. Indonesia's Energy Minister Bahlil Lahadalia framed B50 as central to energy independence amid Middle East oil-supply risk; an **E20 bioethanol mandate** is slated for 2028 with sugarcane bioethanol production starting in Merauke (South Papua) in 2027. The roadmap runs through to B100.

### Open question from earlier notes (now answered)
- *"What is the [domestic] consumption?"* — Indonesia's CPO demand under B50 = **~23.3M tonnes/yr**; up **+8.5M tonnes versus B40**. Agriculture Min. Amran Sulaiman reports national CPO production of ~46M tonnes/yr, with exports growing from 26M→32M tonnes — meaning B50 is being absorbed **on top of expanding exports** rather than crowding them out, at least in the near term.
- *"Starting from second half of 2026 (need to check on this)"* — **Confirmed**: July 1, 2026 (Reuters, Jakarta Globe, Antara, Fuels & Lubes).

## 3. Supply Tightening & Trade Flow Shift

MPOC expects Indonesia's **exportable surplus to tighten from late Q3 into Q4 2026**, driven by:
1. **B50 demand pull (from July 1)** — domestic redirection rising
2. **Stagnant 2026 production** — Oil World: ~49.4M tonnes (flat YoY)
3. **Jan–Apr 2026 consumption + exports up ~15%** (+2.2M tonnes)

Oil World projects **Indonesia's exports to fall by 1.7M tonnes** during Apr–Sep 2026, while **Malaysia's exports rise ~0.4M tonnes** as global buyers reroute sourcing for supply stability. Jakarta Globe cited a study estimate that the B50 programme could cut Indonesia's palm oil export value by **~USD 2.7 billion**.

**Secondary supply risks:**
- **Fertilizer prices rising** (US–Iran geopolitical tensions) — may compress fertilizer application by Indonesian & Malaysian growers, impacting future yields with a lag
- **El Niño developing June–July 2026**, expected to persist into early 2027 (Malaysian Met. Dept.). Production impact typically emerges **9–12 months after onset** — i.e., visible in H2 2027

**Commentary**: The market is in a **"supportive-but-capped"** regime — structural biodiesel demand and weather risk underpin, while elevated importer inventories and weak biodiesel arbitrage cap further upside. This explains the tight RM4,400–4,650 trading band.

## 5. Export Policy Shock — SOE Monopoly from Jan 1, 2027

> Indonesia's Trade Ministry announced **Permendag 16/2026** on June 9–10, 2026, tightening palm oil export channels ahead of 2027.

| Phase | Dates | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Transition** | Jun 1 – Dec 31, 2026 | Existing permits remain valid; DMO/transfer framework being introduced |
| **Full enforcement** | **Jan 1, 2027 onwards** | **All palm-oil derivative exports only via designated state-owned exporters** holding Export Permits (PE) |

**Commodities covered:** CPO, RBDPO, RBDPL, UCO (used cooking oil), palm residue.

**Acquisition of export rights:** SOEs secure rights via (a) fulfilling **DMO** obligations or (b) formal transfer of rights from private producers to the SOE.

This sits inside the broader **Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI)** regime announced by President Prabowo on May 20, 2026, which funnels strategic commodity exports (coal, CPO, ferro-alloys — the top-3 export contributors) through a single state body to combat under-invoicing / transfer pricing (estimated USD 908B hemorrhaged — see [[Gold & DSI - Meridian Research]]). Phase 1 (Jun–Aug 2026): DSI audits export prices. Phase 2 (Sep 1, 2026+): DSI becomes sole exporter.

**Implications for CPO:**
- Contract novation risk for private producers; margin compression risk
- **CPO exporters earn USD but pay IDR costs → benefit from weak Rupiah**; however, the SOE-mandated channel may capture part of that FX margin for the state
- Policy evaluation review scheduled within 3 months, led by Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs
- Refer to APKINDO historical precedent: Indonesia's plywood cartel succeeded (70% global share) before collapsing under IMF conditionality in 1998 — palm oil is **globally substitutable**, unlike plywood — so DSI/Permendag's bargaining power is structurally weaker

## 7. Key Investment Implications

1. **CPO price is structurally supported, not spike-driven.** The RM4,400+ floor is policy-anchored (B50 demand) rather than the transient shock that produced the 2022 RM7K peak. Expect price to hold in a **RM4,400–4,650 near-term band** with upside probes if El Niño strengthens or Indonesia accelerates export curbs.
2. **Watch Indonesia's exportable surplus** in late Q3–Q4 2026 — the inflection point when B50 demand begins crowding out exports is the key catalyst. Until then, Malaysia picks up rerouted demand (~+0.4M tonnes Apr–Sep).
3. **Jan 1, 2027 SOE export regime is the structural reset.** Equity positioning should discount a margin transfer from producers to the state; the contract-novation and pricing-power risk means producer equities may underperform the underlying commodity.
4. **Biodiesel economics are a swing factor.** If gasoil stays below palm oil, the export curb rationale weakens (Indonesia loses the domestic-vs-export arbitrage that justifies DMO). If gasoil recovers above palm oil, B50 economics improve sharply → supports both price and policy permanence.
5. **El Niño optionality is largely a 2027 story** (9–12 month lag), worth monitoring but not a 2026 catalyst.

### Watchpoints
- (1) Indonesia exportable surplus prints in Q3/Q4 2026;
- (2) Permendag 16/2026 3-month policy review;
- (3) El Niño ONI updates (Jul–Aug onset window);
- (4) Gasoil vs CPO price spread (biodiesel economics);
- (5) India/China vegetable oil inventory drawdown;
- (6) USD/IDR & JCI liquidity (Meridian's break-below-5,500 target);
- (7) [[$PTPS]] earnings sensitivity to B50 offtake and DSI margin compression.
