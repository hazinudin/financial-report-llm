# Map Aktif Adiperkasa Tbk (MAPA) — Q1 2026
---

## 1. Executive Summary

Map Aktif Adiperkasa Tbk (MAPA) delivered an unaudited Q1 2026 performance characterized by robust top-line momentum, significant operating leverage, and a doubling of operating cash flow. Revenue reached IDR 4,954.7 billion, up 14.7% year-on-year, while gross profit expanded 16.6% to IDR 2,379.7 billion, pushing the reported gross margin to 48.03%. Operating profit surged 28.9% to IDR 603.4 billion, and net profit attributable to owners jumped 39.6% to IDR 470.6 billion, translating to basic earnings per share of IDR 17 versus IDR 12 in the prior-year quarter—a 41.7% advance. The balance sheet grew 2.7% quarter-on-quarter to IDR 14.57 trillion in total assets, funded almost entirely by equity, which rose 4.9% to IDR 9.28 trillion. The equity ratio stands at 63.7%, and all disclosed credit facilities were undrawn as of March 31, 2026.

The working-capital profile, however, sends a mixed signal. Trade receivables compressed 21.7% sequentially to IDR 298.7 billion, driving days-sales-outstanding to an exceptionally low 5.5 days. Conversely, inventories expanded 5.4% quarter-on-quarter to IDR 5,962.7 billion, consuming 41% of the balance sheet and stretching days-inventory-outstanding to 109.8 days. Trade payables contracted 7.3% to IDR 1,411.4 billion, narrowing the natural funding offset to inventory. Cash and cash equivalents declined a net IDR 34.3 billion to IDR 1,423.5 billion, but the cash-flow statement reveals strong underlying generation: collections from customers hit IDR 5,044.6 billion, exceeding revenue by 1.8%, and operating cash flow more than doubled to IDR 601.8 billion. Free cash flow, defined as operating cash flow less capex, printed at IDR 710.6 billion.

Valuation metrics are not determinable because no market data stub was provided; consequently, P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, and market capitalization are not disclosed. On an operational basis, the multi-brand retailer—whose subsidiaries distribute New Balance, Adidas, Converse, Hoka, Birkenstock, Dr. Martens, Steve Madden, Skechers, and others—demonstrated healthy cash conversion and margin expansion. The absence of a price anchor, combined with elevated inventory intensity and a sharp acceleration in capex (+71% year-on-year), keeps the rating at HOLD with a Moderate Risk qualifier. Key monitorables for the next quarter are inventory turnover, brand-license continuity, and any activation of the undrawn USD 40 million revolving credit facility with DBS Bank.

Consequently, it is not possible to state whether the 14.7% advance was driven by unit volume, ticket size, mix shift toward higher-priced brands, or pass-through of input costs. The analyst must treat the top line as a black-box aggregate until management provides segmental or operational commentary in future filings.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration, domestic versus external revenue splits, and segment composition are not disclosed in the canonical fact sheet; the three-month geographic revenue table, however, is available in the filing's Note 32 (printed page 69 / PDF page 72). The customer list is empty, and the segment array is null. Without concentration metrics, the dependency on any single wholesale account or geographic market cannot be quantified.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

No commodity-price or policy-driver commentary is included in the inputs, and post-period-end price moves are not disclosed. Reserve or store-runway data is absent. The forward outlook therefore rests on the following watch items: (i) the trajectory of inventory days, which at 109.8 days could signal forward-loading of stock ahead of anticipated demand or, conversely, slowing sell-through; (ii) the status of brand distribution agreements, particularly the New Balance operation housed in Planet Sports, Inc. (PSIPH), which carries the largest subsidiary asset base; and (iii) the undrawn DBS USD 40 million and HSBC Vietnam USD 6 million facilities, which provide liquidity but, if drawn, would signal working-capital strain.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

Collections from customers exceeded revenue, producing a 1.018x collections ratio and underscoring high earnings quality. Operating cash flow more than doubled, and cash conversion reached 1.27x net profit. The current ratio of 2.25x and equity ratio of 63.7% describe a conservatively capitalized balance sheet. All significant credit facilities—DBS USD 40 million, HSBC Vietnam USD 6 million, and a raft of domestic bank lines—were undrawn as of March 31, 2026, providing pristine liquidity. Interest expense on bank loans dropped to zero per Note 26, removing a material cash-flow drag.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

Days-sales-outstanding of 5.5 days is exceptionally low for any retail or wholesale business, implying a cash-sale or advance-payment model with minimal credit risk. Operating leverage was pronounced: operating profit growth of 28.9% on revenue growth of 14.7% indicates that the store base and corporate cost structure are absorbing incremental sales efficiently. Gross margin printed at 48.03%, with COGS growing slower than revenue, suggesting procurement discipline or favorable brand-pricing power.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

MAPA’s subsidiary network spans more than a dozen global brands across footwear, accessories, and athleisure, reducing single-brand dependency. Geographic diversification extends beyond Indonesia into Vietnam (MMA Vietnam), Malaysia (MAM, MASM), and Singapore (MASS, MAS), creating a Southeast Asian platform. Free cash flow of IDR 710.6 billion in a single quarter, combined with zero loan repayments and undrawn credit lines, leaves management with ample optionality for brand acquisitions, store expansion, or dividend hikes.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

- **HIGH — Inventory intensity:** Inventories of IDR 5,962.7 billion represent 41% of total assets and 109.8 days of inventory. If Q2 sell-through disappoints, markdowns could compress the 48.03% gross margin rapidly. The sequential build of 5.4% in a quarter when revenue grew 14.7% year-on-year suggests stocking ahead of demand, but the mismatch versus payables compression is a red flag.
- **MODERATE — Payables compression:** Trade payables fell 7.3% quarter-on-quarter to IDR 1,411.4 billion while inventories rose. This reduces spontaneous financing and may indicate supplier tightening or shorter credit terms.
- **MODERATE — Capex acceleration:** PPE additions rose 71% year-on-year. Without a maintenance-versus-growth split, investors cannot judge whether this is expansionary or catch-up spending. If demand softens, returns on these assets will lag.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

- **MODERATE — License concentration:** Planet Sports, Inc. (PSIPH) holds IDR 2,118.5 billion in assets for the New Balance distribution. This single subsidiary is 14.5% of group assets. Distribution agreements are the core intangible asset; none are disclosed as perpetual, and renewal terms are not provided. Loss or non-renewal of a major brand would impair both revenue and asset values.
- **LOW — NCI volatility:** NCI swung from a loss of IDR 1.3 billion to profit of IDR 2.1 billion. While immaterial, the volatility indicates that minority-owned operations are marginal and could become a drag in a downturn.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

- **MODERATE — Tax timing volatility:** Article 21 taxes payable spiked from IDR 491 million to IDR 40,511 million between December 2025 and March 2026 per contextual notes. If this reflects a recurring timing mismatch or a new compliance obligation, cash-flow predictability will suffer.
- **LOW — FX exposure:** Foreign subsidiaries generate non-IDR cash flows, yet no hedge disclosures are provided in the canonical schema. A sharp IDR appreciation would compress the IDR value of overseas earnings, while depreciation would raise the cost of imported inventory.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

No market data is available, so quantitative overvaluation cannot be asserted. However, the structural valuation concern is that a 41.7% EPS growth print may attract momentum capital, pricing the stock at a multiple that assumes sustained operating leverage. If inventory turns slow and gross margin reverts, the implied P/E could contract sharply. Investors should demand a valuation discount until market data confirms the multiple.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

MAPA’s multi-brand, multi-country platform is firing on all cylinders. Revenue growth of 14.7% is amplified by gross margin expansion and SGA leverage into 41.7% EPS growth. Cash conversion above 1.2x and FCF of IDR 710.6 billion provide the firepower to raise dividends, acquire new brands, or fund store rollouts without touching debt. The undrawn USD 40 million DBS facility and zero bank-loan interest expense signal a pristine credit profile. If inventory turns stabilize and new brands (Hoka, Vivaia) scale, the earnings trajectory could re-rate the stock on any market-data pullback.

### 14.2 Bear Case

The inventory build is the canary in the coal mine. At 109.8 days and 41% of assets, any demand deceleration will force markdowns, collapsing the 48% gross margin. Trade payables are already compressing, removing a natural hedge. Capex is accelerating into an uncertain demand environment. A lost brand license—particularly New Balance via PSIPH—would write down 14.5% of assets and an unquantified share of revenue. Without disclosed hedging, FX and input-cost volatility are unmitigated. If the market has already priced in the 41.7% EPS beat, the risk/reward skews negative.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

- **Q2 2026 inventory print:** A reduction in DIO would confirm sell-through strength; an increase would signal markdown risk.
- **Brand license renewals / new additions:** Any announcement on Hoka ramp, Vivaia expansion, or New Balance contract extension.
- **Dividend policy for FY2026:** With Q1 FCF covering the 2024 full-year dividend 6x, a payout raise would be a tangible positive.
- **Activation of undrawn credit facilities:** Drawing on the DBS or HSBC lines would signal M&A or working-capital stress.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Moderate Risk).** MAPA’s operational momentum is undeniable—revenue +14.7%, EPS +41.7%, OCF doubled—but the absence of market data prevents valuation confirmation, and the inventory-heavy balance sheet (IDR 5.96 trillion, DIO 110 days) is a near-term concern. The equity-funded, low-leverage structure limits downside, yet the lack of a price anchor and the risk of inventory markdowns counsel patience.
