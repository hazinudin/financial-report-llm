## 1. Executive Summary

ELSA delivered a top-line expansion of 8.9% year-on-year in H1 2026, with revenue reaching Rp7,586 billion against Rp6,965 billion in the prior-year period. However, gross profit contracted by 0.4% to Rp721.7 billion, indicating margin compression as cost of revenues grew faster than sales. The entire profit upside was generated below gross profit: general and administrative expenses fell by Rp40.8 billion, interest expense dropped by Rp26.8 billion, and other income swung from a Rp4.3 billion loss to a Rp39.5 billion gain. The result was a 29.2% surge in net profit to Rp434.7 billion and basic EPS of Rp59.56, up from Rp46.10.

The balance sheet remains fortress-like in liquidity but increasingly tethered to the Pertamina ecosystem. Cash rose to Rp2,886 billion, producing a net cash position of Rp2,858 billion. Yet trade receivables ballooned to Rp4,136 billion, of which context notes indicate only Rp136 billion is third-party; the remaining Rp3,999 billion is owed by related parties, primarily Pertamina entities. Total assets grew 4.0% to Rp11,401 billion, while equity expanded 2.1% to Rp5,426 billion, delivering an annualized ROE of 32.4%. No interim dividends were paid, and capital commitments more than doubled to Rp320 billion, signaling an aggressive capex pipeline ahead.

Valuation ratios cannot be assessed because market data was not provided. Without a quoted price, market capitalization, or EV, we cannot compute P/E, P/B, or EV/EBITDA. The operational story is one of Pertamina-captive growth with strong cash generation, but the concentration risk is extreme and the lack of market data prevents a definitive valuation anchor. We assign a HOLD with a Pertamina-concentration qualifier pending live market inputs and evidence that third-party revenue can be rebuilt.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet contains no production, reserve, or segment-level volume data (`production: []`, `segments: []`), so a volume/price split is not disclosed. What is observable is the customer ledger: PT Pertamina Patra Niaga accounted for Rp3,341 billion (44.0% of sales) and PT Pertamina Hulu Indonesia for Rp749 billion (10.0% of sales). ### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is the defining feature of revenue composition. The top two customers are both Pertamina group entities under common control or parentage. Context notes indicate that aggregate trade receivables are split into third parties (Rp136,365 million) and related parties (Rp3,999,457 million), meaning 96.7% of billed and unbilled trade receivables are intra-group. Domestic versus external geographic splits are not disclosed, but the customer roster implies overwhelmingly domestic, Pertamina-centric activity.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

No commodity price assumptions, policy drivers, or post-period-end contract awards are disclosed. Reserve life and production runways are not provided (`reserves: []`). A critical watch item is the Rp320,356 million in outstanding capital expenditure commitments (up from Rp132,769 million at December 31, 2025), which suggests management is positioning for contract execution rather than organic reserve-based expansion. The sustainability of the revenue line therefore hinges on continued Pertamina logistics, drilling support, and fuel-trading allocations. Any retendering or insourcing decision by the Pertamina parent would represent a material discontinuity.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

PT Elnusa Petrofin (EPN) is the crown jewel by asset mass, holding Rp5.53 trillion—48.5% of group total assets. EPN’s activity spans fuel stations (SPBU), depots, transportation, and trading of fuel and chemicals. This subsidiary is the economic engine that drives both revenue and receivables, given its trading volume. No subsidiary-level profitability, license schedules, or reserve tables are disclosed, so EPN’s standalone margin contribution is not disclosed.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

PT Elnusa Geosains Indonesia (EGI) is not yet operating (`belum beroperasi`) and holds only Rp13,084 million in assets; it is a dormant call option on upstream geoscience work. The KSO Elnusa-WAS joint operation (51% owned) was ended on March 21, 2025, yet still carries Rp21,239 million in assets and recorded equity income of Rp4 million in H1 2026 versus a loss of Rp183 million in H1 2025. EFK holds a surface pump repair contract with an active addendum of Rp44,970 million expiring April 10, 2026; two earlier addendums (Rp88,425 million and Rp97,267 million) have expired. The absence of renewed large EFK contracts is a watch item.

### 7.4 Growth Options

No explicit second-leg growth targets are disclosed. The Rp320,356 million capex commitment (more than double the December 2025 level) and the sharp increase in non-current advances for fixed assets to Rp132,420 million suggest organic capacity expansion, likely within EPN’s depot and logistics network or ETSA’s vessel fleet. Whether these investments translate into third-party revenue or simply deeper Pertamina dependency is not stated.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Related-party transactions dominate the balance sheet and P&L. Trade receivables billed to PT Pertamina Patra Niaga are Rp325,317 million, with unbilled receivables of Rp1,634,936 million. PT Pertamina EP, PT Pertamina Hulu Energi, and PT Pertamina Hulu Indonesia add further millions in billed and unbilled claims. On the payable side, PT Pertamina Patra Niaga is owed Rp247,829 million. Finance lease receivables from related parties total Rp183,045 million non-current and Rp30,681 million current. Intercompany eliminations are not detailed. The critical monitoring point is the payment velocity of these receivables: DSO of 49.75 days is acceptable only if the counterparty is solvent, but the concentration means default or delay risk is non-diversifiable.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

The balance sheet is deleveraged and liquid. Net cash of Rp2,858 billion and a loans-to-equity ratio of 0.025x provide ample headroom for the Rp320 billion capex commitment. The current ratio of 1.45 and quick ratio of 1.36 indicate no refinancing urgency. Cash conversion of 1.75x and a collections ratio of 95.6% demonstrate that earnings are backed by cash. Annualized ROE of 32.4% and ROA of 15.6% are robust for a capital-intensive services and trading platform. Unused credit facilities exceed US$100 million across BNI, BRI, MUFG, ICBC, and others, providing contingent liquidity.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

Revenue grew 8.9% in a period when global oilfield services markets remain competitive, suggesting that ELSA’s Pertamina affiliation secures contract flow. Days inventory outstanding is exceptionally low at 5.94 days, consistent with a trading and logistics model rather than a manufacturing footprint. The 46.5% reduction in interest expense indicates successful liability management, including the full repayment of the Sukuk Ijarah and the PT Bank Mandiri short-term loan.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

ELSA is embedded in the Pertamina value chain at multiple points: downstream fuel logistics (EPN), marine transport (ETSA), fabrication (EFK), and upstream support (PND, EOS). This integration creates high switching costs for the counterparty and provides revenue visibility that independent contractors lack. The low-cost operator strategy referenced in [[ELSA KSO-LCO Low Cost Operator Strategy]] aligns with Pertamina’s upstream revival agenda, potentially locking in maintenance and logistics spend.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — Receivables concentration and unbilled exposure.** Third-party trade receivables collapsed to Rp136,365 million while related-party receivables surged to nearly Rp4 trillion. Unbilled receivables from PT Pertamina Patra Niaga alone are Rp1,635 billion. Any delay in Pertamina’s internal payment cycles would instantly strain reported liquidity despite the net cash position.

**MODERATE — Capex execution risk.** Capital commitments more than doubled to Rp320,356 million. If these projects face delay or cost overrun, the anticipated return on invested capital may not materialize in H2 2026.

**LOW — Inventory obsolescence.** The inventory allowance is static at Rp9,461 million with no new provision, suggesting management is comfortable with stock quality.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**HIGH — Pertamina-group dependency.** The majority of revenue, receivables, and payables are intra-group. ELSA is effectively a captive service provider. A strategic decision by Pertamina to consolidate vendors, insource logistics, or alter fuel-trading margins would re-rate the entire revenue line.

**MODERATE — Subsidiary opacity.** No segment profitability is disclosed. EPN contributes nearly half of group assets, yet its standalone margins, return on assets, and debt load are not reported. Investors cannot verify whether the group’s 32% ROE is generated by EPN’s trading book or by upstream services.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

**MODERATE — Fuel pricing and policy risk.** EPN trades BBM and chemicals; any government-mandated fuel price caps or distribution obligation changes could compress trading margins without warning.

**MODERATE — Covenant compliance on Sharia financing.** The PT Bank Syariah Indonesia facility (Rp85,733 million, 7% per annum) carries covenants requiring a minimum current ratio of 1.0x and a maximum debt-to-equity ratio of 3.0x. While currently met, a working capital shock from delayed Pertamina receivables could tighten headroom. The MUFG facility (maturity December 2026) carries similar covenants, and the ICBC facility caps interest-bearing debt/EBITDA at 4.0x.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

No market data were provided, so traditional multiples cannot be computed. If the stock is pricing in continued Pertamina protection and high dividend yields, the suspension of the H1 2026 dividend and the spike in capex could trigger a re-rating. Conversely, if the market is already pricing structural dependency risk, the absence of a price prevents us from judging the margin of safety.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

Pertamina’s upstream revival and downstream logistics expansion continue to funnel contract volume to ELSA, particularly through EPN and ETSA. The low-cost operator strategy highlighted in [[ELSA KSO-LCO Low Cost Operator Strategy]] positions the group to win maintenance and construction work as Pertamina accelerates field activity. With net cash of Rp2.9 trillion, annualized ROE above 32%, and minimal leverage, ELSA can fund capex internally while maintaining dividend capacity. A resumption of the H1 dividend in H2 would signal confidence in cash flow sustainability.

### 14.2 Bear Case

Gross profit declined in absolute terms despite higher revenue, confirming that pricing power is weak. The entire profit beat came from cost cuts and non-recurring other income, not operational leverage. Third-party receivables have collapsed, meaning ELSA is increasingly a captive finance vehicle for Pertamina’s working capital. If Pertamina delays payments or renegotiates trading margins, the 49.75-day DSO could lengthen abruptly, and the Rp4 trillion receivables pile would become a liquidity trap. The absence of a dividend in H1 may also signal that management sees tighter cash conditions ahead.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **H2 2026 dividend declaration** — a return to the Rp278 billion H1 2025 payout level would validate free cash flow durability.
2. **New Pertamina logistics contract awards** — evidence that EPN’s depot and SPBU footprint is expanding, not just maintaining.
3. **EFK contract renewals** — resolution of the April 2026 expiration and any new surface pump repair mandates.
4. **Third-party revenue disclosure** — any uptick in non-Pertamina sales would reduce concentration risk.
