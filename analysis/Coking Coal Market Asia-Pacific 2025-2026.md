# [[ Coking Coal]] Market: Asia-Pacific Region | 2025-2026 Outlook

## Executive Summary

The Asia-Pacific coking coal market is at an inflection point. Demand is structurally supported by India and Southeast Asia's blast furnace capacity expansion, while China—the dominant consumer—shows flat-to-declining met coal demand. Supply-side constraints are emerging from Australia (weather, royalties, permitting delays) and structural shifts in China's import sourcing (from seaborne to overland via Mongolia/Russia). Australian PLV HCC prices are forecast at **$200-240/t range** for 2025-2026, with significant intra-year volatility. The seaborne market is tightening toward 2026, creating potential for price upside in H2 2026 and beyond.

## 2. Demand Analysis

### 2.1 Global Met Coal Demand

- **2025:** Global met coal demand projected at ~1,114 Mt, broadly stable vs. 2024 (IEA)
- **Outlook to 2030:** Gradual decline to ~1,061 Mt (IEA), with China down 77 Mt offset by India (+26 Mt) and Indonesia (+12 Mt)

### 2.2 China (~67% of global met coal demand)

- Crude steel production fell **3.1% YoY** in Jan-Jul 2025 (594.5 Mt)
- Domestic coking coal output rose ~1.62% in H1 2025 to ~231 Mt
- **2025 total coking coal imports projected at ~113-115 Mt** (down 6-9 Mt YoY)
- Import structure shifting: Mongolia + Russia share rose from 71% (2024) to **78% (2025)**
  - Mongolia: ~58-59 Mt (50% of imports) — record volumes
  - Russia: ~31-32 Mt (28% of imports)
  - US coal imports collapsed due to retaliatory tariffs (28% tariff from May 2025); cumulative Jan-Oct imports only 2.9 Mt, down 65.7% YoY
  - Australian imports volatile, dependent on price arbitrage
- **2026 outlook:** China's domestic coking coal production expected to decline 5-8% due to safety restrictions and the "276 working days" policy; imports likely stable to slightly higher, favoring cheaper Mongolian/Russian coal
- Beijing's push for higher-quality steel output may underpin demand for premium coking coal

### 2.3 India (the key growth engine)

- Crude steel production: **94.9 Mt** in Jan-Jul 2025 (up 9.8% YoY)
- Per capita steel consumption: ~104 kg (less than half global average) — enormous growth runway
- Capacity roadmap: **190 Mt -> 300 Mt by 2031 -> 500 Mt by 2050**
- India's met coal imports remain strong and growing; Australia is the dominant supplier
- Indian steel sector saw industrial coal demand **up 6%** in H1 2025
- AMNS India announced plans to triple capacity to 25-26 Mt/yr by 2030

### 2.4 Japan & South Korea (mature, declining)

- **Japan:** Coal demand projected at 153 Mt in 2025 (moderate decline); 8% decline expected in 2026
- **Korea:** 17% YoY decline in H1 2025 coal consumption; total demand forecast at 103 Mt in 2025, 4% decline in 2026

### 2.5 Southeast Asia (emerging growth)

- Current crude steel production: **71.9 Mt**; expected to grow by **40-50 Mt over next 5 years**
- New blast furnace investments in Indonesia, Vietnam, and Philippines
- ASEAN coal demand: 4.5% annual growth through 2030 (IEA)
- Indonesia coal demand: 268 Mt in 2025 (up 7%), 284 Mt in 2026; smelting industry growing
- Vietnam: +5 Mt coal demand in 2026, driven by additional coal-fired power generation

## 3. Supply Analysis

### 3.1 Australia (~60%+ of seaborne exports)

| Metric | FY 2023-24 | FY 2024-25 (est.) | FY 2025-26 (forecast) |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Met coal exports | 151 Mt | ~161 Mt (revised down) | 174-175 Mt |
| Export earnings | A$61B | A$54B | A$41B |

**Supply challenges:**
- Mine approvals now average **1,371 days** (up 60% since 2019)
- Queensland progressive royalty regime: at $200/t, royalty ~$34/t ($10 more than pre-2022); at $250/t, ~$54/t ($20 jump)
- Incidents: Anglo American's Grosvenor underground fire; Broadmeadow East, Byerwen disruptions
- Weather: Cyclone season (through Feb), La Nina risk for QLD/NSW mine operations
- BMA suspended Saraji South coking coal mine (Nov 2025) due to low prices and high royalties
- Anglo American restarting Moranbah North; seeking buyer for coal portfolio in early 2026
- Coronado facing financial and safety challenges at Curragh complex

### 3.2 Mongolia

- **2025 record exports:** ~90 Mt total coal (up 7% YoY), ~58-59 Mt coking coal to China
- 2026 target: 90 Mt exports (all-time high)
- Gashuunsukhait-Gantsmod cross-border railway under construction — medium-term logistics catalyst
- Pricing highly sensitive to Chinese demand signals; "V-shaped" 2025 with H1 weakness and H2 recovery
- Mongolia proposed increasing coal exports to China to **100 Mt**

### 3.3 Russia

- Steady coking coal exports to China: ~26.5 Mt in Jan-Oct 2025 (up 4.3% YoY)
- Projected annual coking coal exports to China: ~31-32 Mt
- Russia signaled potential to increase total coal exports to China to 100+ Mt

### 3.4 Seaborne Supply Outlook

- **Critical:** Global seaborne supply unlikely to keep pace with demand beyond 2026 (per industry analyst)
- New project pipeline insufficient; rising costs, stricter regulations, and long approval timelines limiting development pace
- Low-cost producers remained profitable in 2025, but marginal producers face margin pressure

## 4. Trade Flows & Geopolitical Factors

### 4.1 Key Structural Shifts

| Flow | Trend |
|------|-------|
| China -> seaborne premium HCC | **Declining** — Chinese buyers largely absent at elevated prices; reliance on Mongolia/Russia |
| China -> Mongolia/Russia | **Increasing** — combined share rose to 78% of imports (from 71%) |
| Australia -> India | **Stable-to-growing** — India remains key buyer for Australian premium HCC |
| Australia -> Japan | **Relatively stable** |
| US -> China | **Zero** since May 2025 — retaliatory tariffs (28%) |
| China exports | Marginal (0.03% of total export value) — uncompetitive vs. seaborne |

### 4.2 Geopolitical & Policy Headwinds

- **US tariffs on NE Asian automakers** depressed steel demand and coking coal imports
- **China-US trade friction:** 28% tariff on US coking coal since May 2025; imports effectively ceased
- **Decarbonization policies:** EU CBAM, ESG pressure on coal financing, coal phase-outs in advanced economies
- **Queensland royalties:** Progressive regime discouraging marginal investment
- **Mine permitting:** Australia averaging ~4 years for approval

## 5. Key Risk Factors

### Upside Risks (bullish for prices)
1. **Seaborne supply shortage beyond 2026** — insufficient new project pipeline
2. **Australian weather disruptions** (La Nina, cyclones) limiting exports
3. **India restocking** after H1 2025 destocking cycle
4. **China supply cuts** (276 working day policy, safety inspections) driving import demand
5. **Mongolian logistics constraints** limiting ramp-up of exports

### Downside Risks (bearish for prices)
1. **Global recession / steel demand decline** from trade war escalation
2. **India safeguard measures** limiting steel imports -> potential demand impact
3. **Aggressive EAF/scrap adoption** reducing BF-BOF dependence
4. **China economic slowdown** deeper than expected
5. **New Queensland supply** coming online faster than expected
6. **Green steel commercialization** (longer-term risk; hydrogen DRI still years/decades away at scale)

## 6. Investment Implications

### Supply-Demand Balance

The market moves from **loosely balanced in 2025** toward **structural tightness by late 2026-2027** as Indian and Southeast Asian blast furnace additions outstrip new supply from Australia and Canada. Mine approval delays, royalty regimes, and ESG-related financing constraints are effectively constraining the supply response.

### Price View

We see Australian PLV HCC trading in a **$190-230/t range** through mid-2026, with a probable move toward **$220-270/t in H2 2026** as restocking coincides with supply scarcity. The Fitch $170/t forecast appears too bearish given the tightening supply outlook.

### Key Producers Positioning

- **BHP** — expanding Blackwater Mine (A$1.2B investment); dominant Queensland producer
- **Whitehaven Coal** — acquired Daunia and Blackwater mines from BHP Mitsubishi Alliance
- **Anglo American** — exiting coal; selling portfolio to Peabody (up to US$4.9B), seeking new buyer for Moranbah North
- **Glencore** — acquired 77% of Elk Valley Resources from Teck (US$6.93B); facing weather/safety constraints at Curragh
- **Arch Resources + CONSOL Energy** — merged to form Core Natural Resources (US$5.2B entity, June 2025)

## 7. Market Size & Growth

- Global metallurgical coal market: **USD 126.35 billion (2025)** -> **USD 166.34 billion (2034)**, CAGR 3.06%
- Asia-Pacific was the **largest region** with ~33% global market share (USD 41.7 billion) in 2025
- China market: ~USD 31.26 billion (24.7% of global); India: ~USD 4.58 billion (3.6%)
- Hard Coking Coal (HCC) dominates with 52.2% product-type market share
- BF-BOF steelmaking still accounts for ~70% of global steel output, requiring coking coal through at least 2040
