# PT Multi Hanna Kreasi Indonesia Tbk (MHKI) — Q2 2026
---

**Reporting Basis:** All financial figures are presented in Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). For readability, income-statement and balance-statement values are expressed in IDR billion (1 Bn = 1,000,000,000) unless otherwise noted; minor balances are expressed in IDR million (1 Mn = 1,000,000). The financial statements are **unaudited** interim reports for the period ended 30 June 2026, with the prior-year quarter ended 30 June 2025 for flow statements and 31 December 2025 for the balance-sheet comparative.

## 1. Executive Summary

MHKI’s Q2 2026 results reveal a sharp deterioration in operating earnings power masked by anomalous tax accounting and a still-robust balance sheet. Revenue contracted 19.1% year-on-year to Rp81.5 billion, while gross profit collapsed 39.1% to Rp31.5 billion as cost of goods sold rose 1.9% despite lower volumes. Net profit plunged 76.9% to Rp4.6 billion, producing a net margin of just 5.6%—down from roughly 19.7% in Q2 2025. The quarter’s most analytically urgent distortion is a negative effective tax rate of –20.3% (a tax benefit of Rp1.2 billion), down from a larger benefit of Rp5.5 billion in the prior-year quarter. Without this tax benefit, net profit would have been materially lower.

The balance sheet remains fortress-like: total assets are flat at Rp508.4 billion, equity rose 0.8% to Rp487.4 billion, and leverage is negligible (debt-to-equity 4.3%). Liquidity is extreme, with a current ratio of 13.6× and cash of Rp109.6 billion—more than 13× total loans. However, working capital signals stress: trade receivables fell 40.3% (a welcome reversal of FY2025’s surge), but inventories jumped 34.0% and prepaid taxes exploded 1,594% to Rp3.3 billion. Operating cash flow was negative at –Rp1.7 billion, and free cash flow was deeply negative at –Rp13.5 billion after Rp11.8 billion of capex, roughly in line with management’s disclosed Rp48 billion full-year revitalization budget for Bekasi and Lamongan.

No market data (price, market cap, or multiples) was provided for this review, making valuation impossible. The combination of collapsed profitability, negative free cash flow, unresolved tax anomalies, and zero valuation visibility warrants a **HOLD (Qualified)** stance. The thesis is on hold until Q3 2026 results clarify whether revenue stabilizes and the tax position normalizes.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Metric | Q2 2026 (IDR Bn) | Q2 2025 (IDR Bn) | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 81.5 | 100.8 | –19.1% |

Revenue of Rp81.5 billion represents a steep deceleration from the prior-year quarter and is roughly 38% of the FY2025 full-year run-rate on a quarterly basis, indicating that the contraction is not merely seasonal. The company generated Rp81.2 billion in cash collections from customers, yielding a collections-to-revenue ratio of 99.6%, so the revenue decline is not an artifact of accrual timing.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

Management does not disclose segment revenue, shipment tonnage, unit pricing, or volume data in the interim statements. Consequently, the split between volume contraction and price/mix effects is **not disclosed**. External context from S&P Global Manufacturing PMI offers a plausible demand-side explanation: Indonesian manufacturing contracted to 46.9 in June 2026 (from 50.0 in May), the deepest drop in a year, driven by weak domestic and export demand and rising input costs (*BRIEF INSIGHT: PMI Manufaktur RI Kembali Kontraksi*, Bisnis.com, 2026-07-02, https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20260702/257/1984981/brief-insight-pmi-manufaktur-ri-kembali-kontraksi-alarm-bagi-industri-nasional). MHKI’s hazardous waste and industrial services revenue is tied to industrial client activity; the PMI contraction provides external context for lower waste volumes and deferred freight/logistics contracts.

The rupiah depreciated to USD/IDR 17,856 at 30 June 2026 from 16,782 at 31 December 2025 (Bank Indonesia mid rate). Because MHKI’s revenue is overwhelmingly domestic IDR-denominated, FX translation is not a revenue tailwind.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Management has budgeted capex of approximately Rp48 billion for 2026 to revitalize processing facilities and develop operational facilities in Bekasi and Lamongan, East Java (*Anggarkan Capex Rp48 Miliar, MHKI Genjot Revitalisasi Fasilitas Pengolahan*, Warta Ekonomi, https://wartaekonomi.co.id/read618288/anggarkan-capex-rp48-miliar-mhki-genjot-revitalisasi-fasilitas-pengolahan; Viv.co.id, https://www.viv.co.id/142646/anggarkan-capex-rp48-miliar-mhki-genjot-revitalisasi-fasilitas-pengolahan). This suggests a strategic bet on capacity expansion, but the revenue ramp timeline is undisclosed. The 34% inventory build in Q2 2026 may indicate stockpiling for anticipated goods-revenue contracts that have been delayed, creating a working-capital squeeze. Reserve and production disclosures are **not provided**. Key watch items: (i) whether Q3 2026 collections hold above 95% of revenue, (ii) inventory turnover given the surge, and (iii) any new long-term customer contract announcements post-period-end.

\*Prior-period gross margin derived from disclosed prior-year revenue and COGS.

Gross margin collapsed 1,264 basis points to 38.6%, a level that is structurally lower than FY2025’s implied full-year margin and far below what would be expected for a specialized waste-treatment franchise. The fact that COGS rose 1.9% while revenue fell 19.1% indicates severe operating deleverage—fixed processing costs or higher unit input costs (energy, chemicals, transportation) absorbed against a smaller revenue base. Internal notes do not explain the specific input-cost changes or pricing actions in Q2 2026, nor the impact of the Lamongan facility ramp-up on unit costs (open question M3). The revenue mix between service revenue (58% in FY2025 per external research) and goods revenue in Q2 2026 is **not disclosed**, preventing a mix-shift analysis (open question M2).

Operating profit and operating margin are **not disclosed** in the interim statements or computed ratios. ### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

Interest income surged 145.6% to Rp3.6 billion, reflecting deployment of the company’s large cash balance into higher-yielding deposits or instruments as Bank Indonesia rates likely increased. Interest expense rose 38.1% to Rp839.7 million despite non-current loans decreasing 30.1%, suggesting higher rates or new short-term borrowing (open question C2). The net interest surplus is therefore roughly Rp2.8 billion (analyst calculation), but the exact composition of interest income sources and yields is **not disclosed** (open question C1).

Other income net was –Rp41.3 million, improved from –Rp157.6 million in Q2 2025 but still a minor drag. The specific items comprising this line are **not disclosed** (open question O1).

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

**Receivables / DSO:** Days sales outstanding improved to 21.5 days (annualized), down from an estimated 29.1 days at FY2025 based on the prior-year revenue run-rate. The past-due-but-not-impaired receivables balance collapsed from Rp17.7 billion at 31 December 2025 to Rp4.0 billion at 30 June 2026 (context note), a dramatic cleanup. The allowance for impairment rose modestly to Rp847 million from Rp807 million. Whether the reduction reflects aggressive collections, factoring, or write-offs of prior-period balances is **not disclosed** (open question W1). Notably, four customer balances (Sorini Agro, Adi Siswanto, Star Energy, Creative Scrap) dropped to zero; internal notes do not confirm collection versus write-off.

**Inventories / DIO:** Days inventory outstanding stood at 13.1 days (annualized). The 34% inventory increase to Rp11.7 billion is unexplained. It could reflect raw materials for processing, finished goods for resale, or work-in-progress for specific projects (open question W2). The inventory is insured to PT Malacca Trust Wuwungan Insurance Tbk for Rp8.0 billion; no inventory is pledged as collateral.

**Prepaid Taxes:** Prepaid tax Article 28 appeared at Rp3.3 billion in Q2 2026 versus zero in FY2025, while tax payable Article 29 vanished to zero from Rp1.2 billion. This massive swing—prepaid taxes up 1,594%—is the balance-sheet counterpart to the income-statement tax anomaly and remains unresolved (open questions T2, T3).

**Other Advances:** Note 7 discloses advances increased to Rp58.6 billion from Rp35.2 billion, a 66% surge. This item is not broken out in the face-sheet current-asset summary but represents a material off-line working-capital lock-up.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks — HIGH

– **Revenue and profit collapse:** A 19% revenue drop with 39% gross profit decline indicates severe operating deleverage. If Q3 2026 does not stabilize, full-year earnings will fall far below FY2025.  
– **Negative free cash flow:** –Rp13.5 billion quarterly FCF, annualized to –Rp54 billion, is unsustainable relative to Rp109.6 billion cash. The cash burn rate must decelerate post-capex.  
– **Tax anomaly:** The –20.3% ETR and Rp3.3 billion prepaid tax surge are unexplained. If the tax benefit reverses, reported net profit could turn negative.

### 11.2 Structural Risks — MODERATE

– **Customer concentration:** PT PLN’s Rp23.5 billion receivable and Jinhua Zinc’s Rp5.3 billion exposure dominate the trade book. Loss or delay of either contract would amplify revenue volatility.  
– **Related-party exposure:** Net payables to PT Multi Hanna Transportindo (Rp2.9 billion) and undisclosed operational financing with the parent create governance and cash-flow routing risks.  
– **Lean headcount:** Only 18 permanent employees for a Rp300+ billion PPE base implies heavy reliance on subcontractors or automation; operational disruption risk is concentrated.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks — MODERATE

– **Manufacturing recession:** Indonesia’s manufacturing PMI at 46.9 (June 2026) signals shrinking industrial activity, directly pressuring waste volumes and freight demand (Bisnis.com, 2026-07-02).

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

If industrial activity rebounds in H2 2026 and the Lamongan facility begins contributing revenue, MHKI could exit 2026 with a higher revenue run-rate and normalized margins. The net-cash balance sheet and minimal debt provide optionality to weather the downturn and fund accretive expansion. Regulatory tightening on B3 waste disposal strengthens the moat for licensed operators.

### 14.2 Bear Case

A prolonged manufacturing recession (PMI sustained below 50) would keep waste volumes and freight demand depressed. Capex overruns at Lamongan/Bekasi, combined with negative free cash flow, would erode the Rp109.6 billion cash buffer faster than expected. The tax anomaly could reverse, wiping out already-thin reported net profit. Customer concentration to PLN and industrial exporters creates asymmetric downside.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **Q3 2026 revenue trajectory:** A sequential stabilization or recovery would signal that Q2 was the trough.  
2. **Lamongan facility commissioning:** Any disclosure of operational start dates or capacity utilization.  
3. **Tax note clarification:** H1 2026 audited notes must explain the –20.3% ETR and Rp3.3 billion prepaid tax surge.  
4. **New customer contracts:** Replacement of vanished 2025 receivables (Sorini, Creative Scrap) with new long-term accounts.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Qualified).** No market data prevents a price target or multiple-based rating. Fundamentally, MHKI is solvent and liquid, but its earnings power has collapsed and its capital allocation is cash-negative. Investors should await (i) Q3 2026 results showing revenue stabilization, (ii) resolution of the tax anomaly in audited notes, and (iii) a disclosed revenue contribution from Lamongan before committing fresh capital.
