## 1. Executive Summary

BCA delivered H1-2026 consolidated net profit of IDR 29,545,621m (EPS Rp 240), up 1.8% YoY on H1-2025's IDR 29,022,969m — a modest print that masks a sharply two-sided quarter. Revenue (total interest, sharia income and other income) grew 2.5% YoY to IDR 61,243,239m, but the mix deteriorated: net interest income was essentially flat to down (external: NII −0.6% YoY to ~IDR 42.5tn per NH Korindo Sekuritas, 30 Jul 2026) as the cost of funding repriced faster than asset yields, while interest expense rose 7.8% YoY to IDR 7,252,822m. The margin story is one of compression, not expansion: consolidated NIM slipped to ~5.7% in Q2 (−44bp YoY) and bank-only NIM fell from 5.4% in Q1 to 5.3%, below the 5.4–5.6% FY26 guidance band (external, company presentation, 28 Jul 2026; NH Korindo; BNI Sekuritas via brights.id, 29 Jul 2026). The held-48.2% "net margin" in the fact sheet is net profit margin on gross revenue — not NIM — and its resilience was provision-led, not spread-led.

The quality flag on the H1 margin is important: Q2 standalone provisioning expense fell ~38% QoQ with IDR 0.7tn of loan recoveries, taking gross cost of credit to ~0.4% (from ~0.6% in Q1), inside the 40–60bp guidance but near-cycle-low and unlikely to repeat (external, company presentation, 28 Jul 2026). NIM, by contrast, troughed in June and recovered to 5.5% in July with NII +4% YoY (post-period, medium confidence, Bisnis.com, 20 Aug 2026) — supporting management's 2H26 recovery thesis built on corporate-loan repricing (+50–100bp on new bookings), higher SBN/SRBI reinvestment yields, and assumed further BI hikes (policy rate was raised 50bp in May and 25bp twice in June to 5.75% by 18 June 2026, per Bank Indonesia news releases).

The balance sheet grew strongly but capital-lightly: total assets +4.6% YtD to IDR 1,660,579,336m, gross loans +4.4% half-year to IDR 1,012,705,846m (first crossing IDR 1,000tn per BCA's 29 Jul 2026 press release), deposits +2.8% to IDR 1,268,824,684m with CASA at ~85% — while equity fell 3.9% YtD to IDR 270,667,746m, entirely explainable by the FY2025 dividend (Rp 336/share, ~IDR 41.3tn, ~72% payout, residual paid 8 April 2026) and the Rp 5tn buyback approved at the 12 March 2026 AGMS. QoQ equity actually rose ~4.4% (external, company presentation). CAR remains very strong (consolidated 27.39%, CET-1 26.27% — parent-only disclosure), and NPLs are low (gross NPL 1.86% vs 1.71% at Dec-25; net 0.75%). No market data stub was provided for this run, so no P/E, P/B, EV or target multiple can be computed; press commentary notes valuation near its lowest since 2008 (external, Kontan, 17 Aug 2026), but we present no price-based numbers.

Thesis: a best-in-class franchise (42m accounts, wealth AUM IDR 339tn +16% YoY, fee income +11% YoY) earning through a cyclical deposit-cost trough with near-cycle-low provisions flattering the interim margin. On the fact-sheet financials alone, we assess the quarter as operationally solid, financially robust, but margin-challenged, and frame the recommendation around franchise durability rather than near-term earnings momentum.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

*(For a bank, "revenue" here is total interest, sharia income and other income before funding costs — IDR 61,243,239m for H1-2026.)*

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Metric (IDR m) | H1-2026 | H1-2025 | % Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue (interest + sharia + other income) | 61,243,239 | 59,758,680 | +2.5% |
| Interest and sharia income | 49,765,390 | 49,313,964 | +0.9% |
| Interest and sharia expense | 7,252,822 | 6,728,967 | +7.8% |
| Net interest/sharia income | 42,512,568 | 42,584,997 | −0.2% |
| Other income, net | 11,477,849 | 10,444,716 | +9.9% |

Top-line growth of +2.48% is decelerating versus the +2.94% reported in Q1-2026 ([[BBCA Q1 2026 Analysis]]), and stands well below the 8–10% FY26 loan-growth guidance. The composition is the story: gross interest income rose just +0.9% YoY — average effective loan yields fell to 7.12% (Rupiah) from 7.43% at Dec-2025 per Note 12 — while interest expense grew 7.8% as BI's May–June 2026 hikes (to 5.75%) transmitted into deposit and money-market costs. All the H1 revenue growth came from the other-income line, i.e., fee and trading, not spreads.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

**Volume.** Gross loans grew from IDR 970,233,234m at Dec-2025 to IDR 1,012,705,846m at 30-Jun-2026 (+4.4% in six months, ~+8.9% annualized), crossing IDR 1,000tn for the first time (BCA press release, 29 Jul 2026). By type: working-capital loans +4.5% half-year (IDR 452,857,451m), investment loans +7.2% (IDR 382,550,457m), consumer loans −1.5% (IDR 154,687,552m), credit cards −1.7% (IDR 19,409,888m), employee loans flat. Sectorally, growth was led by manufacturing (+7.6% HtD to IDR 227,644,972m), business services (+6.5% to IDR 186,561,803m), trading/restaurants/hotels (+2.4%), utilities (+9.4%) and mining (+14.9%); household activities contracted −1.5%. The mix is tilting to wholesale/short-tenor — loans up to 1 year grew 19.0% half-year (IDR 344,520,808m) while >1–5 year loans fell 11.9% — consistent with the external read that corporate bookings were being made at higher new-booking yields (NH Korindo, 30 Jul 2026) but not yet earning through.

**Price.** Asset yields compressed: Rupiah loan yield 7.12% vs 7.43%; FX loans 5.12% vs 5.26%; bills receivable Rupiah yield 8.22% vs 10.00%. Funding costs on the other side rose (external: deposit repricing after the tightening cycle; BI at 4.75% through April, hiked to 5.50% on 9 June and 5.75% on 18 June 2026 — Bank Indonesia news releases). The resulting consolidated NIM ~5.7% in Q2 (−7bp QoQ, −44bp YoY) and bank-only 5.3% (vs 5.4% in Q1) is the CoF-trough quarter (external, high confidence).

**FX.** FX-denominated loans are only 5.4% of gross (IDR 54,037,014m vs IDR 95,866,883m Rupiah, Note 12), so FX translation is not a revenue driver. FX sensitivity of PBT to a ±5% rupiah move rose materially to IDR 56,286m (from IDR 11,027m at Dec-2025) on a larger net open position — but the NOP of IDR 1,125,726m is still only 0.44% of capital, well inside the 20% regulatory limit.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

By product (interest/sharia income only, Note 40): Loans IDR 33,654,005m (67.6% of the IDR 49,765,390m total; Q2-2025: IDR 33,811,983m, i.e., −0.5% YoY — the loan book is growing but yielding less), Treasury IDR 13,820,198m (27.8%; +4.5% YoY from IDR 13,212,637m), Others IDR 2,291,187m (4.6%, flat). Fee-based income and others totalled IDR 11,499,448m in H1-2026 vs IDR 10,486,040m in H1-2025 (+9.7%) per the segment table — consistent with the external +11.2% fee & commission growth to ~IDR 11tn (company presentation, 28 Jul 2026). Geographically, Java is the franchise: IDR 50,668,814m of external revenue (90% of the IDR 56,258,874m total) and IDR 32,625,994m of pre-tax profit; Sumatera, Kalimantan and East Indonesia are profitable but small; the overseas operation (H1-2025: IDR 6,774m revenue, IDR −4,373m loss) is not reported for H1-2026. Counterparty mix of credit exposure: corporate IDR 811,455,492m (~50%), government/BI IDR 459,262,109m (~28%), individuals IDR 274,774,967m (~17%), banks IDR 66,689,339m (~4%) — a wholesale-heavy book, reflected in the related-party/duktail orientation of the Group's customer base (Hartono-family-affiliated entities are numerous depositors and borrowers, Note 45; aggregate related-party loans fell to 0.95% of gross from 1.18%).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The H1 pattern — flat NII, fee-led growth — is sustainable only if (a) asset yields reprice up, and (b) fee momentum is structural rather than cyclical. On (a): management's 2H26 NIM recovery case (5.4–5.6%) rests on corporate-loan repricing (+50–100bp on new bookings), reinvestment of ~IDR 443tn of investment securities at higher SBN/SRBI yields (BI securities yields were already 5.18% at Jun-2026 vs 6.40% on the amortised-cost BI-securities book at Dec-2025 — wait, that comparison is Dec-2025 vs Jun-2026 in the other direction; note the 5.18% is the current average effective yield on the large IDR 114,426,425m BI-securities holding, still below the ~7% reinvestment rates in money markets), and two assumed further BI hikes. Post-period evidence (medium confidence) supports this: July NIM 5.5% vs June's 5.2%, NII +4% YoY (Bisnis.com, 20 Aug 2026). On (b): fee income is anchored by ~42m accounts, transaction volumes up ~60% over three years, and wealth AUM of IDR 339tn (+16% YoY) — durable, but Q2 fee income was flat QoQ (−0.2%) after Q1's +14.2% YoY surge, i.e., deceleration, not acceleration (company presentation, 28 Jul 2026). Watch items: consumer-credit contraction (credit cards, consumer financing receivables fell from IDR 9,466,498m to IDR 9,342,986m), Stage-3 ECL on off-balance commitments rising to IDR 31,454m from IDR 8,100m, and the FX-sensitivity step-up.

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

FVOCI unrealised losses swung to IDR 2,535,031m in H1 (vs gain IDR 1,121,121m in H1-25) on the rate backup — but these flow through OCI, not PBT. FX PBT sensitivity ±IDR 56,286m on a ±5% move (see §2.2).

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

(i) The repo position at 30-Jun (IDR 48.3tn securities-sold-under-repo against IDR 48.8tn of government bonds with 1–7 July buybacks) — a large, short-dated liquidity arbitrage unwound days after period end; economically neutral but worth monitoring for recurrence. (ii) The legacy KUT (BI rural credit) of IDR 577m with contractual dates 2000 still "in process of closing" — an amusing footnote, immaterial. (iii) The unresolved tax disputes (fiscal 2016–2024, see §11.3) with IDR 1.9tn+ parked in other assets pending objection. (iv) Consumer-credit contraction (credit cards, consumer financing) — is the retail-lending engine stalling or deliberately de-risked?

### 7.4 Growth Options

BCA Syariah (+7.6% half-year assets, IDR 20.7tn) and Bank Digital BCA (+10%, IDR 20.8tn) are the designated second legs; BCA Life (+16.4% assets, premiums +36% YoY external) under PSAK 117 is scaling; the myBCA ecosystem (RDN/securities account opening, credit cards, travel tickets embedded — external) converts the 42m-account base into fee perimeters. Quarterly interim dividends (AGM authorized up to three for 2026) plus the Rp 5tn buyback are the shareholder-return architecture.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

The Hartono-family constellation (Dwimuria Investama Andalan, 54.94% controlling shareholder; Robert Budi Hartono now sole ultimate controller after the passing of Bambang Hartono, being recorded with OJK) is a large related-party cluster: aggregate related-party loans fell to IDR 9,653,832m (0.95% of gross, from 1.18%/IDR 11,485,252m), related-party deposits rose to IDR 4,636,182m (0.37%), unused related-party credit facilities jumped to IDR 11,651,725m (2.61% from 1.39%), bank guarantees to related parties IDR 757,109m (2.51% from 1.10%). Related-party income is small (interest income 0.58% of total; pension contribution expense 79.78% — that is the Dana Pensiun BCA, i.e., mostly the employer plan). The PT Grand Indonesia office lease (28,166.88 sqm to 2035, ROU IDR 220,076m) is the largest related-party asset. BMPK legal-lending-limit compliance was affirmed (Note 49b). Monitor: the fast growth in unused related-party credit facilities and guarantees.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **MODERATE — NIM still below guidance:** bank-only NIM 5.3% in Q2 vs 5.4–5.6% FY26 band; recovery depends on 2H corporate repricing and further BI hikes — a macro assumption, not a company control.
- **MODERATE — provision-led margin quality:** Q2 CoC ~0.4% is near-cycle-low with IDR 0.7tn recoveries; FY26 guidance 50bp with 60bp downside if consumer/SME stress builds (external).
- **LOW — taxes payable −46% QoQ:** mechanical April–June settlement (PPh instalments, THR payroll, fiscal-2024 dispute offset); no earnings effect.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **MODERATE — fee deceleration:** Q2 fee income flat QoQ (−0.2%) after Q1's +14.2% YoY; if fee growth fades, the jaws story weakens while NIM is still repairing.
- **MODERATE — consumer credit contraction:** consumer loans −1.5%, credit cards −1.7%, consumer financing receivables −1.3% half-year — the retail risk discipline is prudent but shrinks a high-margin book.
- **LOW — FX sensitivity step-up:** ±5% rupiah move now worth ±IDR 56,286m of PBT (from ±11,027m); NOP still only 0.44% of capital.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **HIGH — tax disputes:** fiscal 2024 SKP/STP of IDR 1,223,357m underpayment (IDR 1,147,817m under objection, parked in other assets); fiscal 2016/2017/2018/2021 appeals with a cumulative IDR 2.3tn+ at stake across years, partially decided. Management does not expect material adverse effect on the disputes in flight, but the pattern of recurring assessments is itself a risk.
- **MODERATE — rate cycle transmission:** BI at 5.75% (and held to Sep–Oct 2026); if hikes stall while deposit competition persists, CoF pressure persists without the asset-side offset.
- **LOW — regulatory capital:** Pillar Two (PMK-136/2024) — the Group states no exposure. Lending limits, RR/MPLB all compliant.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- No market data was provided; we cannot state whether the shares price in the NIM trough or the franchise value. External commentary (Kontan, 17 Aug 2026) places valuation near 2008 lows — if accurate, the market is discounting the margin miss and the 1.8% profit-growth softness more than the 42% ROE. The risk for a premium-multiple bank is that a guidance miss (NIM) resets the multiple faster than earnings recover.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
The Q2 NIM trough (5.3% bank-only) marks the cycle low: deposit costs peak as BI hikes fully transmit, the IDR 443tn securities book and IDR 344.5tn sub-1-year loans reprice up, and corporate bookings land at +50–100bp. Post-period evidence (July NIM 5.5%, NII +4% YoY — medium confidence) already validates this. Meanwhile the fee engine (IDR 339tn wealth AUM, +16% YoY; 42m accounts) and the buyback (share count −0.3% in H1, compounding) deliver low-to-mid single-digit EPS growth through the trough, and the 43% annualized ROE on a franchise trading (per external commentary) near 2008-low valuations offers both earnings recovery and multiple recovery. Buyback + quarterly interims provide a floor.

### 14.2 Bear Case
The margin miss is structural, not cyclical: digital disintermediation and competition compress both spreads and fees; CoC normalizes back toward 60bp+ as consumer/SME stress builds; the BI hiking cycle stalls so the 2H repricing thesis fails; profit growth stalls at ~1–2% while the market de-rates a premium multiple further. The tax-dispute pattern (recurring IDR-trillion assessments) and the related-party web are governance discounts. Equity is shrinking (−3.9% YtD) while distributions exceed earnings — at some point a 27% CAR becomes a 25% CAR, still fine, but the buffer-burn direction matters.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. 2H-2026 NIM recovery into the 5.4–5.6% guidance band — the July 2026 monthly data is the first checkpoint (already showing 5.5%).
2. FY2026 dividend decision (Q3 interim authorization: up to three quarterly interims; the September AGM cycle) and buyback continuation/pause (an October 2026 press release indicates the buyback continued — post-period, unverified).
3. Resolution of the fiscal-2024 tax objection (IDR 1,147,817m parked in other assets).
4. BI policy path — each hike accelerates the treasury-book reinvestment tailwind.
5. Full-run-rate PSAK 117 reporting cycle (comparatives clean from FY2026).

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (low risk tolerance) / accumulate on weakness — no target price computable without market data.** The financial position is impeccable and the franchise best-in-class, but the in-period evidence shows margin compression, provision-flattered profits, and decelerating fee growth; the recovery thesis rests on macro assumptions (BI hikes, corporate repricing) and post-period indications of only medium confidence. With no market data stub for this run, we deliberately state no target price range; the review trigger is the Q3-2026 report: if bank-only NIM prints ≥5.5% with CoC ≤50bp, upgrade to BUY; if NIM stays ≤5.4% or CoC exceeds 60bp, the margin-miss thesis extends and the rating should be reassessed lower.
