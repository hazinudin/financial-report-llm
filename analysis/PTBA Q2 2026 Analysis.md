# PT Bukit Asam Tbk (PTBA) — Q2 2026 (Six Months Ended June 30, 2026)
---

**Reporting basis.** All company figures are drawn from PTBA's unaudited interim consolidated financial statements for the six-month period ended June 30, 2026 (completed and approved for issuance by the Board of Directors on 28 August 2026), expressed in **millions of Rupiah (IDR mn)** unless otherwise stated; per-share amounts are in full Rupiah. The "Q2 2026" filing is a semester statement: P&L and cash-flow comparatives are H1 2025, while the balance-sheet comparative is December 31, 2025 (prior fiscal year-end). Single-quarter (2Q26) figures in this note are derived arithmetically as H1 minus Q1-2026 disclosed figures (Q1 revenue Rp9,929,722mn; Q1 gross profit Rp1,543,782mn, per [[PTBA Q1 2026 Analysis]] and the pipeline DB) and are labelled as derived throughout. No market data stub was provided; all valuation ratios are shown as not computable.

## 1. Executive Summary

PTBA delivered a dramatic H1 2026 result that is, on its face, one of the strongest half-years in the company's recent history. Revenue rose a modest **+7.7% YoY to Rp22,027,901mn**, but gross profit exploded **+89.0% YoY to Rp4,247,161mn**, lifting the gross margin from 10.99% to **19.28%** (+829 bps). PBT nearly tripled to Rp3,271,733mn (+198.7%), net profit attributable to owners rose **+218.2% to Rp2,649,899mn**, and basic EPS jumped from Rp72 to **Rp230**. The expansion was concentrated in Q2: derived 2Q26 gross profit of ~Rp2,703,379mn implies a ~22.3% quarterly gross margin versus ~15.5% in Q1 2026 and a very weak H1 2025 base — a sequential re-rating consistent with the HBA 6,322 kcal GAR benchmark moving from roughly US$100–103/t in Q1 2026 into the US$121–132/t range by mid-2026 (external context, per [[ITMG Q2 2026 Analysis]]), against only a partial offset from a fuel-cost spike.

The balance sheet strengthened materially. Cash nearly doubled from year-end to **Rp6,875,929mn** (from Rp4,522,194mn at 31 Dec 2025), while total bank loans fell to Rp2,985,269mn — leaving PTBA in a substantial **net cash position of Rp4,307,128mn (net debt/equity −0.17)**. The structure of the debt shifted too: the short-term BRI facility (Rp1,950,000mn at year-end) was fully repaid, while the Mandiri/BRI/BNI syndicated investment credit rose to Rp2,371,766mn (drawn under the Rp3.56tn facility financing the Tanjung Enim–Kramasan coal logistics corridor) — a terming-out of the maturity profile rather than pure deleveraging, with H1 loan repayments of Rp3,201,219mn. Equity grew +12.0% to Rp25,338,577mn.

Operationally, the story is price-led, not volume-led, and the quarter is corroborated by the external coal tape: post-period HBA prints of US$126.87/t (Sep H1) and US$126.81/t (Oct H2) suggest 2H ASP carries a lagged tailwind, though DMO volumes (25% of production at US$70/t for public electricity supply) cap the transmission. The "Others" segment (power JVs, mining services) contributed Rp878,318mn of H1 net profit — a third of the group total — driven by the HBAP (Sumsel 8) joint venture's sharp H1 2026 turnaround (JV profit Rp1,102,054mn vs Rp427,847mn), while BPI remained loss-making and dragged NCI to a negative Rp6,542mn.

With annualized ROE of ~44%, cash conversion of 1.75x, H1 free cash flow of Rp3,478,686mn, and a declared FY2025 dividend of Rp115/share (total Rp1,318,481mn, paid July 2026), PTBA enters 2H 2026 with rare financial flexibility for a coal producer. Valuation cannot be assessed here (no market data provided). On fundamentals we rate the quarter strongly; the open questions are margin sustainability against fuel costs and the DMO/export-policy regime (Permentrad 15/2026 routes exports through PT Danantara Sumber Daya Indonesia from January 1, 2027). **Recommendation: BUY (cyclical/commodity risk qualifier), target range not computable without market data** — see §14.

Revenue growth of +7.7% on a nearly flat-volume backdrop (Q1 production was −22% YoY on rainfall, per [[PTBA Q1 2026 Analysis]]) points squarely to price: realized ASPs re-rated across both export-linked and HBA-linked domestic contracts. The geographic mix confirms it — Vietnam (+105% YoY to Rp3,140,951mn), Cambodia (+204% to Rp1,874,455mn) and Thailand (+17% to Rp1,037,008mn) grew fastest, while the Philippines collapsed −66% to Rp406,314mn and India fell −33% to Rp1,512,053mn — a rotation toward spot-priced Southeast Asian buyers.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet discloses no production/sales tonnage for the period (`production: []`), so a clean volume/price split is **not disclosed** in the canonical inputs. What can be said:

- **Price.** PTBA's DMO obligation is set at 25% of current-period coal production (Kepmen ESDM 399/2023), with DMO coal for public electricity supply priced at **US$70/t FOB vessel (6,322 kcal GAR)** — capped revenue. The residual ~75% re-rates with HBA/spot. External context: HBA 6,322 kcal GAR moved from ~US$100–103/t in Q1 2026 to US$121–132/t over June–July 2026, and printed US$126.87/t in Sep H1 2026 (per [[ITMG Q2 2026 Analysis]]; source verified by that note's research). This is the most plausible driver of the derived ~680 bps qoq gross-margin expansion.
- **Volume.** Q1 2026 production was 6.62 Mt, −22% YoY on rainfall, with sales inventory-funded. The +21.8% qoq derived revenue growth and the H1 inventory drawdown (Rp4,883,306mn → Rp4,186,041mn) are consistent with production recovery plus continued stockpile release, but tonnage is not confirmed in this filing's extracted disclosures.
- **FX.** PTBA carries net monetary USD assets of US$138.1mn (Rp2,476,655mn equivalent) at June 30, 2026 vs Rp2,400,811mn at year-end — a modest rupiah-depreciation translation tailwind on USD-denominated receivables and debt-instrument investments; no hedging is in place (natural hedge only, Note 35).

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

- **Related-party concentration is high but declining.** Related-party sales (Note 30b) totaled Rp9,398,907mn — **43% of consolidated revenue** (H1 2025: 48%). The largest related-party customer is the PLN Group at Rp6,888,859mn, followed by MIND ID Trading Pte. Ltd (Rp1,139,296mn) and the HBAP JV itself (Rp785,911mn). Exports to Vietnam, Cambodia and India are largely third-party.
- **Geography (H1 2026):** Indonesia Rp9,428,115mn (42.8%), Vietnam Rp3,140,951mn (14.3%), Bangladesh Rp2,711,436mn (12.3%), Cambodia Rp1,874,455mn (8.5%), India Rp1,512,053mn (6.9%), Thailand Rp1,037,008mn (4.7%), with Korea, China, Malaysia, Philippines and Taiwan making up the balance.
- **Segment split:** the Coal segment produced external sales of Rp21,672,370mn (before elimination) with gross profit of Rp4,170,170mn; "Others" contributed Rp2,975,339mn of external sales (pre-elimination) and Rp345,262mn of gross profit. Intercompany eliminations of Rp2,619,807mn in revenue reflect internal coal transfers into power JVs and trading subsidiaries.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Supportive but capped. Post-period benchmark prints (HBA US$126.87/t Sep H1, US$126.81/t Oct H2, −3.4% in Oct H1 — per [[ITMG Q2 2026 Analysis]]) argue for a lagged but real 2H ASP tailwind on export-linked tons. Offsets and watch items:

1. **DMO cap**: 25% of production is locked at US$70/t, structurally compressing blended ASP versus pure exporters.
2. **Export-channel regime change**: Minister of Trade Regulation 15/2026 designates coal a strategic commodity and routes exports through PT Danantara Sumber Daya Indonesia from January 1, 2027 (PTBA may export directly until December 31, 2026) — a material 2027 uncertainty.
3. **DHE SDA rules**: Government Regulation 21/2026 requires ≥30% of export proceeds retained onshore ≥3 months (conversion capped at 50%); PP 8/2025 requires 100% of FX proceeds placed in the Indonesian financial system for ≥12 months. Neither impairs revenue recognition but affects cash deployability.
4. **Reserve runway / license security**: PP 25/2024 provides 10-year rolling IUP extensions with PTBA's ≥30% non-dilutable stake preserved — a long-run strategic positive. Mine-level reserve tonnage is not disclosed in this filing.
5. **Contract anchor**: the PLTU 10,000 MW Tahap I program (H1 sales of Rp4,261mn-equivalent per Note 29a scale, contracts to 2044) plus the Tanjung Enim–Kramasan rail/logistics build-out (TLS VI & VII 91.5% complete, estimated completion September 2026) underpin multi-decade domestic offtake.

The margin story is twofold: (i) a weak H1 2025 base — COGS in H1 2025 exceeded H1 2026 in absolute terms despite lower revenue, implying cost one-offs and weak ASPs in the base period; and (ii) genuine 2026 price-led expansion. ### 4.2 Working Capital Components

But composition matters: gross receivables rose to Rp4,972,550mn, with **overdue 1–30 days jumping to Rp1,109,528mn from Rp225,151mn** — the PLN Group related-party balance rose to Rp2,107,603mn (from Rp1,225,584mn at year-end) while MIND ID Trading USD receivables collapsed to Rp87,872mn from Rp419,146mn (collected). PLN receivables carry state-utility payment-cycle risk but low credit risk.
- **Inventories / DIO: 17.3 days (annualized)** — down 14.3% in absolute terms; the NRV/obsolescence allowance rose Rp14,264mn in the period. Inventory-funded sales flattered margins in H1.
- **Prepaid taxes: nil** (year-end Rp264,487mn) — Article 28A prepayments fell to Rp57,708mn and were reclassified entirely non-current (Rp3,494,089mn of VAT/prepayments vs Rp3,043,015mn). This is a classification and tax-payment-timing effect, not a working-capital release.

### 4.5 Non-Controlling Interests (NCI)

NCI equity fell from Rp133,087mn to **Rp112,587mn**. The movement: IPC (49%-owned) NCI opening Rp137,550mn, share of net loss Rp12,181mn, dividends declared Rp13,958mn, closing Rp111,411mn — **IPC's minority absorbed a net loss**, which also explains the negative NCI profit line in the P&L (group NCI loss of Rp6,542mn vs +Rp6,858mn profit in H1 2025, a −195% swing). IPC-Palaran's near-fully-amortized mining properties suggest an asset at end-of-life incurring closure and care costs. Minor NCI (<5% stakes) swung from −Rp4,463mn to +Rp1,176mn. Note BPI (59.75%) and HBAP (45%) are joint ventures accounted by equity method, so their results hit "other income," not NCI. PTBA's structure effectively concentrates JV upside with owners while IPC minorities bear their share of that subsidiary's losses.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
- Four-decade-life producing asset base (Air Laya, Banko Barat, Banko Tengah, Muara Tiga Besar) with rolling 10-year IUP extensions and ≥30% non-dilutable ownership guaranteed under PP 25/2024.
- Integrated rail–port logistics chain (Tanjung Enim–Kramasan, TLS VI & VII 91.5% complete) that lowers delivered cost and creates third-party logistics optionality.
- Long-dated PLN offtake (contracts to 2044) anchoring 43%-of-revenue domestic book.
- Cost discipline: supplier payments fell 12.4% YoY; segment D&A declined.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths
- Power-JV vertical integration: HBAP's H1 2026 breakout (profit Rp1.1tn at JV level) converts coal price risk into stable IPP cash flows; HBAP net assets Rp17.1tn.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **MODERATE — Supplier-payment catch-up.** The Rp1.87tn accrued-expense build (KAI Rp1.72tn + PAMA Rp4.36bn-scale per Note 29c) defers cash outflows into 2H 2026; OCF and FCF will compress as these settle, plus ~Rp931mn of payables-settled capex (Rp931,233mn) turns cash.
- **MODERATE — TLS VI & VII completion slippage.** Estimated completion moved from June to September 2026 (91.5% complete); further slips delay the logistics-cost payoff and keep CIP carrying capitalized interest.
- **LOW — PLN receivables aging.** Overdue 1–30 days receivables jumped to Rp1,109,528mn; watch collection before it seasons.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — Coal price cyclicality.** The 44% ROE and 19% gross margin are cycle-peak readings driven by HBA re-rating; a retrace to 2025-level benchmarks would compress H1-like profit by well over half, mechanically.
- **HIGH — Energy transition / demand erosion.** Japan coal demand −8% expected in 2026, Korea −4% (external, per [[Coking Coal Market Asia-Pacific 2025-2026]]); seaborne thermal demand beyond 2026 faces structural pressure even as near-term supply tightens.
- **MODERATE — Depleting subsidiary mines.** IPC-Palaran near-fully amortized and loss-making; BBK suspended — both carry reclamation tails.
- **LOW — End-of-life obligations.** Rp2.34tn reclamation/mine-closure provision and Rp721bn of guarantees are well-provisioned but long-dated cash drains.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **HIGH — Export channel nationalization.** Permentrad 15/2026 mandates coal exports through PT Danantara Sumber Daya Indonesia from January 1, 2027 (PTBA exempt until December 31, 2026) — margin-taking, counterparty and execution risk on the ~57% of revenue that is exported.
- **MODERATE — DMO regime.** 25% of production at US$70/t caps blended ASP; any DMO share increase directly dilutes margins.
- **MODERATE — DHE SDA retention.** PP 21/2026 (30% of export proceeds onshore ≥3 months, ≤50% conversion) and PP 8/2025 constrain offshore cash deployability — mostly a friction, not a P&L item.
- **MODERATE — Tax disputes.** A long tail of PBB/VAT/PPh disputes (2012–2024 vintages, Prabumulih/Lahat/KPP WPB 3) remains in appeal; the 2012 PBB PK was decided against the Company in the current period. Amounts are individually modest relative to earnings but cumulative exposure is non-trivial and unsettled.
- **LOW — PP 39/2025** restrictions on affiliate mining-services arrangements (SBS relevance).

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- Not computable — no market data. ## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
- H1 annualized EPS of ~Rp460 against a book value of Rp2,190/share; if even 60% of the price-driven margin holds into 2H (post-period HBA ~US$127/t supports it), FY2026 owners' profit approaches Rp5tn.
- Net cash of Rp4.3tn plus an undrawn Rp3.56tn facility give PTBA optionality no listed thermal-coal peer has entering a supply-scarce market (seaborne supply unlikely to keep pace with demand beyond 2026, per [[Coking Coal Market Asia-Pacific 2025-2026]]).
- HBAP's breakout turns PTBA into a quasi-utility/coal hybrid, dampening EPS volatility through the cycle; the rail corridor completes in September 2026, cutting delivered cost and unlocking third-party logistics revenue.
- Dividend re-rating: a payout restored toward FY2024 levels on FY2026 earnings implies a step-change in yield.

### 14.2 Bear Case
- The entire H1 beat is HBA-price beta with a low 2025 base; any 2H benchmark normalization (Oct H1 already −3.4% m/m) compresses the 19% gross margin back toward low-teens or worse, and the FY2025 dividend cut shows the downside.
- Export-channel nationalization from January 2027 (Permentrad 15/2026) inserts a state intermediary into 57% of revenue with unknown margin and counterparty terms.
- 2H cash-flow air pocket: ~Rp2.8tn of accrued supplier/transport obligations plus payables-settled capex must settle in cash, while DMO at US$70/t caps the upside on a quarter of volume.
- PLN receivables seasoning, IPC/BBK end-of-life drag, BPI's unexplained Rp359,699mn other-expense swing, and the unresolved Kramasan readiness sign-off.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. **TLS VI & VII completion (September 2026)** — mechanical ramp of the Kramasan corridor and logistics cost curve.
2. **2H HBA prints** — US$127/t September print supports lagged ASP re-rating on export-linked tons; conversely, sequential softening caps it.
3. **FY2026 dividend proposal (typically H1 2027)** — payout decision against a ~Rp5tn annualized earnings base.
4. **Danantara export-channel implementation details** ahead of the 1 January 2027 mandate.
5. **Kramasan terminal operational-readiness signing** with KAI Logistik.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**BUY (with commodity-cycle risk qualifier).** PTBA enters 2H 2026 with cycle-peak earnings power, a fortress net-cash balance sheet, a completing growth-asset in the rail corridor, and a power-JV leg that structurally lowers earnings volatility. The core risks — coal-price mean reversion and the 2027 export-channel regime change — are real but are balance-sheet-absorbable at current leverage. **Target price range: not computable (no market data provided)**; on fundamentals, any price implying ≤8x normalized (mid-cycle) EPS of ~Rp300–350/share or ≤1.5x book would offer an attractive entry. **Review trigger:** two consecutive HBA periods below US$100/t, adverse Danantara export-channel terms, or a 2H OCF shortfall below Rp1.5tn as the accrued-expense tail settles — any of these moves the thesis to HOLD.
