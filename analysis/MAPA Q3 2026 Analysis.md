# PT MAP Aktif Adiperkasa Tbk (MAPA) — Q3 2026
---

*All figures in Rp million unless stated otherwise. Statements are unaudited interim financial statements for the nine months ended 30 September 2026 ("9M26"), with the comparative balance sheet at 31 December 2025 and comparative income/cash flows for the nine months ended 30 September 2025 ("9M25"). Q3 standalone figures, where cited, are derived via 9M-minus-H1 arithmetic or from clearly labelled external sources. No market data stub was provided for this report — all valuation figures are "not disclosed" and no target price range is issued.*

## 1. Executive Summary

MAPA's 9M26 report is dominated by a single structural event: the consolidation of Sports Direct Malaysia Sdn. Bhd. ("SD(M)") acquired by wholly-owned Singapore subsidiary Athletica International Holdings Pte. Ltd. (AIH) from Frasers Group Trading Limited effective 1 July 2026 for USD 148.9 million. The purchase price allocation added roughly Rp 1.8 trillion of goodwill and intangible assets (goodwill Rp 563,479 million total vs Rp 59,527 million at end-2025; license/distribution rights Rp 1,351,060 million vs Rp 32,072 million), drove total assets up 34.5% to Rp 19.07 trillion, total liabilities up 65.3% to Rp 8.82 trillion, and introduced two persistent P&L drags: 10-year amortization of acquired license/distribution rights booked in selling expenses, and non-cash discount accretion on the deferred share-purchase payable (Rp 41,467 million of finance cost in 9M26 alone).

Operationally, the quarter was solid rather than spectacular. Revenue grew 17.8% to Rp 16,433,452 million (9M), gross margin expanded to 47.08% from ~46.70% (fact-sheet arithmetic: 9M gross profit +18.8%), and net profit rose 25.5% to Rp 1,442,445 million with EPS of Rp 51 vs Rp 41. But the group outperformed a genuinely weak macro backdrop — Bank Indonesia's retail sales index contracted through Q2 2026 and recovered to only +1.1% YoY in July — and external research (Stockbit, 27 Oct 2026) attributes a large slice of Q3 growth to the SD(M) consolidation (Malaysia revenue stepping from ~Rp 118–141 billion/quarter to ~Rp 654 billion, making Malaysia the #2 market at ~10% of revenue). Growth is real but substantially inorganic.

The balance sheet shift is the key investor debate. Loans jumped to Rp 4,803,625 million (Rp 2,096,029 million current, Rp 2,707,596 million non-current) from Rp 1,889,151 million at end-2025, and a new "liabilities for shares purchase" line of Rp 1,908,902 million carrying value (Rp 540,704 million current, Rp 1,368,198 million non-current) appears with no prior-year comparative. Leverage metrics deteriorated mechanically: D/E 0.86x, loans/equity 0.47x — but cash of Rp 1,611,949 million covers the Rp 569,603 million of drawn bank loans, cash conversion is excellent (OCF Rp 1,949,909 million; OCF/net profit 1.35x), and free cash flow was Rp 1,282,371 million. The group remains covenant-comfortable (current ratio min 1, net debt/equity max 2, net debt/EBITDA max 3.25 across most facilities, all asserted met).

Without a market-data stub, no P/E, P/B, EV or target price can be computed. On fundamentals: 9M26 delivered ~77% of full-year consensus per Stockbit, ETR normalized to 25.3% after H1's anomalous tax credit, and the Q4 run-rate now carries a full quarter of amortization and discount interest. Maintaining **HOLD (Moderate Risk)**, consistent with the Q2 2026 view; the acquisition converts MAPA into a materially larger, more levered regional platform whose earnings quality must be proven over 2–3 quarters of clean consolidated trading.

Revenue growth of +17.8% for 9M26 is the headline, but composition matters more than the topline. External research (Stockbit, 27 Oct 2026) estimates Q3 2026 group revenue grew ~+23% YoY, with Malaysia alone contributing ~Rp 654 billion in Q3 versus ~Rp 141 billion in Q3 2025 and ~Rp 118 billion in Q2 2026 — i.e., roughly Rp 500+ billion of incremental quarterly revenue arrived on 1 July with SD(M). Implied standalone Q3 group revenue is ~Rp 6,310.8 billion (9M Rp 16,433.5 billion minus H1 Rp 10,122.8 billion, per internal H1 records). Indonesia itself grew only ~12% YoY in Q3 per the same external source — respectable against BI retail sales of +1.1% YoY in July 2026, but far below the group headline.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

MAPA does not disclose store counts, same-store sales, units or pricing metrics in the parsed statements, so volume-vs-price decomposition is not directly observable. What can be said:

- **Consolidation effect (dominant in Q3).** SD(M) consolidation from 1 July 2026 lifts the Malaysian geography from ~3% of revenue to ~10% (external, Stockbit). The Frasers–MAP Active framework announced March 2025 (Reuters) targeted 350+ Sports Direct stores across India and Southeast Asia — a multi-year runway, not a current contribution figure.
- **Seasonality.** Q3 is MAPA's seasonal peak (back-to-school plus Indonesia's August independence-day promotional season), supporting full-price sell-through — consistent with the +70 bps YoY Q3 gross margin to 46.8% per external research.
- **Currency headwind, not tailwind.** The rupiah weakened against all major invoicing currencies (conversion rates used: USD 17,998 vs 16,782; EUR 20,427 vs 19,753; SGD 14,072 vs 13,069 — Note 37). Translated foreign operations inflate revenue in rupiah terms but also inflate liabilities; this is translation, not demand.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

- **Geographic mix (9M26 net revenues):** Indonesia Rp 12,237,029 million (74.5%); Philippines Rp 1,830,848 million (11.1%); Malaysia Rp 913,962 million (5.6%, now understated as a run-rate because SD(M) only consolidated for one quarter); Thailand Rp 827,899 million; Singapore Rp 304,932 million; Vietnam Rp 279,644 million; Cambodia Rp 39,138 million. Versus 9M25, Philippines grew +10.5%, Malaysia +133.8% (acquisition), Vietnam +73.7%, Thailand +21.1%, while Singapore contracted −18.7% and Indonesia grew +15.0%.
- **Customer concentration: none.** Note 25 explicitly states no single customer exceeds 10% of net revenues, and Note 38A states no significant credit exposure to any single counterparty. Trade receivables of Rp 301,772 million net against Rp 16.4 trillion revenue (DSO ~5 days annualized) confirms the overwhelmingly cash-retail model.
- **Consignment commission** within retail sales was Rp 73,707 million (9M25: Rp 73,362 million) — essentially flat, a de minimis piece of the mix.
- **Brand concentration remains the structural issue:** revenue rides on licensed distribution rights (New Balance, Sports Direct/USC, Skechers, Converse, Hoka, Birkenstock, Dr. Martens, etc.) that prohibit marketing competing products and can be terminated on breach. The Company has notified brand principals of its brand-acquisition plans — an explicit exclusivity/conflict watch item (see §11).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

External macro evidence supports caution, not a demand recovery narrative: Bank Indonesia's retail sales survey showed contraction for three consecutive months in Q2 2026, rebounding to only +1.1% YoY in July (Aug estimate +0.5%, Sept forecast +1.3–1.4%); consumer confidence fell from 127.0 (Jan) to 116.8 (Jul) before recovering to 118.5 (Aug); inflation rose from 2.88% (Jul) to 3.28% (Sep) with core at 2.92%; BI held its policy rate at 5.75% all quarter (Samuel Sekuritas 3Q26 Macro Report, 5 Oct 2026). MAPA's outperformance is company-specific (Malaysia consolidation + seasonal strength + apparent share gain in sports/lifestyle), not sector beta.

Forward watch items: (1) SD(M)'s contribution run-rate — 9M26 net profit at ~77% of consensus (Stockbit) means Q4 must clear a high bar driven by the acquired entity's peak-season trading; (2) full-quarter amortization and discount-interest drag persists into Q4 and FY2027; (3) IDR/USD above Rp 18,000 in July 2026 vs the Rp 16,825 rate used to size the USD 148.9 million consideration creates ongoing translation pressure on the acquisition liability; (4) the 350+ store Southeast Asia/India rollout (Reuters, Mar 2025) frames multi-year runway but no store-count or standalone SD(M) financials are disclosed — treat as not disclosed.

### 4.5 Non-Controlling Interests (NCI)

NCI profit went from a **loss of Rp 13,104 million in 9M25 to a near-zero loss of Rp 101 million in 9M26** — the "99% collapse" is a shrinking minority loss, not a buyout. NCI equity is essentially unchanged (Rp 120,848 million vs Rp 128,296 million), inconsistent with any stake purchase; AIH is 100%-held, so the SD(M) deal added no NCI. The economic read: minority-held vehicles (90%-held Athletica SD Holdings and MAPA FTL Adiperkasa at 90%, 51%-held Aldo/Noble Footwear entities) stopped bleeding, mechanically lifting profit attributable to owners. This is a genuine earnings-quality improvement but also a flag that regional minority units were loss-making a year ago — watch whether they now contribute positively.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

The retail segment is the crown jewel: external revenue Rp 15,373,163 million (93.5% of group), segment result Rp 1,897,836 million (12.3% margin), segment assets Rp 16,930,700 million. It is built on exclusive distribution/license rights for premium sports and lifestyle brands across seven markets, increasingly diversified beyond Indonesia (non-Indonesia Southeast Asia now ~25% of revenue post-acquisition). Asset intensity is the defining trait — inventories (Rp 7.27 trillion, 38% of assets) and lease-backed ROU assets (Rp 2.75 trillion) dominate — and DSO of ~5 days shows an essentially cash-collected customer base. The associated PT Adi Sport Retailindo (ASR, 50%, Adidas retail Jakarta) contributes a modest Rp 2,807 million equity-pickup on Rp 90,307 million carrying value.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

- **Brand-acquisition plan:** the Company has notified brand principals of plans to acquire brands — a potential conflict with exclusivity clauses that prohibit marketing competing products; any misstep could terminate key license agreements.
- **Non-retail segment:** external sales Rp 1,060,289 million with segment result of Rp 199,071 million (18.8% result margin) on only Rp 1,582,918 million of assets — a high-return question mark; watch for what "non-retail" (wholesale/distribution to franchisees) becomes as the Sports Direct model scales.
- **Lapsed facilities:** BRI facilities (USD 35 million BG/SBLC etc.) were not extended past 19 Jan 2026; BBVA's USD 50 million facility expired 1 Sep 2026; MUFG Manila's USD 7 million PSIPH line expired 31 Jul 2026 with extension in process — housekeeping items, but they trim the undrawn liquidity pool at the margin.

### 7.4 Growth Options

The Sports Direct Malaysia acquisition is the declared second leg: provisional net assets Rp 1,884,893 million taken over, license/distribution rights Rp 1,339,657 million amortized over 10 years, goodwill Rp 498,358 million. The Reuters (Mar 2025) framework of 350+ Sports Direct stores across India and Southeast Asia, plus the India subsidiary (MAIN, established 2025) and Cambodia/Vietnam greenfields, frames the multi-year runway. Athletica SD Holdings (90%) signed the local retail agreement giving SD(M) non-exclusive Sports Direct/USC rights in Malaysia. No store-count, standalone revenue or EBITDA disclosure exists for SD(M) — not disclosed.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Related-party exposure is small in gross terms (trade receivables Rp 3,947 million = 0.02% of assets; other receivables/payables Rp 4,830 million / Rp 21,641 million) but structurally interesting: the MAP management-services agreement (IT, finance, HR, legal, distribution, licensing — fee in G&A) is a standing intra-group transfer; related-party non-retail sales were 3.46% of non-retail sales (Rp 36,634 million) and related-party merchandise purchases 0.11% of total (Rp 9,531 million). All related-party payables/receivables are current and expected to settle within 12 months. The parent MAP (68.83% holder) guarantee backstops all subsidiary bank facilities — concentration of financial liability at the parent level.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths
- OCF of Rp 1.95 trillion with 1.35x cash conversion; FCF Rp 1.28 trillion after capex and before the one-off acquisition outflow.
- Cash (Rp 1.61 trillion) exceeds drawn bank loans (Rp 569,603 million) — net cash on a bank-debt basis; covenants comfortably met.
- Annualized ROE of 20.1% at a 0.86x liabilities/equity — strong returns for a retailer.
- ETR normalized to 25.3%, confirming H1's tax credit was one-off.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
- DSO of ~5 days — a cash-retail model with negligible customer credit risk; no customer >10% of revenue.
- Organic inventory provisioning fell sharply (net additions Rp 4,928 million vs Rp 48,745 million prior) even as stock grew 28.5% — disciplined sell-through and acquisition carry-over rather than markdown accumulation.
- Q3 gross margin +70 bps YoY in a contracting retail macro (BI retail sales ~+1% YoY) — company-specific execution/share gain.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths
- Sports Direct Malaysia acquisition immediately makes Malaysia the #2 market (~10% of revenue) and anchors a 350+ store Southeast Asia/India rollout framework with Frasers.
- Multi-country, multi-brand platform (7 markets, ~12 major brands) reduces single-geography and single-brand risk versus a pure Indonesia licensee.
- Conservative dividend policy (~21% implied payout) preserves integration and expansion capacity.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Integration and P&L drag:** Rp 33,281 million amortization (9M, i.e. one quarter) plus Rp 41,467 million discount accretion will persist through Q4 and FY2027 at full run-rate; 9M26 profit at ~77% of consensus leaves a demanding Q4 (external, Stockbit).
- **HIGH — Inventory intensity:** Rp 7.27 trillion inventory, DIO 121 days, 38% of assets. A soft Q4 (macro still fragile: BI retail +0.5–1.4% YoY, CCI 118.5) triggers markdown risk directly into gross margin.
- **MODERATE — FX translation:** rupiah above Rp 18,000/USD in July vs the Rp 16,825 deal-sizing rate inflates the USD-linked acquisition liability; net FX monetary liability position widened to Rp 1,120,513 million from Rp 733,452 million.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — License dependence and exclusivity:** distribution agreements prohibit marketing competing products and are terminable on breach; the disclosed brand-acquisition plan is a live conflict-of-interest risk with brand principals.
- **MODERATE — Leverage step-up:** liabilities/equity 0.86x from 0.60x; the Rp 1.91 trillion share-purchase payable (nominal Rp 2.84 trillion over 5 years) is a genuine new obligation, partially discount-financed.
- **MODERATE — NCI history:** minority-held regional units lost Rp 13,104 million in 9M25; they are near-breakeven now, but the underlying units' standalone economics remain undisclosed.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **MODERATE — Tax disputes:** 2021 SKPKB of Rp 49,553 million recorded in FY2025 tax and paid in 2026, objection filed with DGT, no decision as of report date — contingent exposure. Pillar Two: transitional safe-harbour relief applies for FY2025 across all jurisdictions except Thailand; no material exposure currently expected.
- **MODERATE — Macroeconomy:** Indonesian retail sales growth of ~+0.5–1.4% YoY through Q3, inflation rising to 3.28% (Sep), BI rate 5.75% — a weak discretionary backdrop for FY2027 organic growth.
- **LOW — Facility churn:** BRI, BBVA and MUFG Manila facilities lapsed or unextended; JP Morgan (USD 10 million, Jul 2026) and Standard Chartered Philippines (USD 10 million, Sep 2026) are new additions.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- No market data available; the mechanical annualized EPS of ~Rp 68 embeds the seasonally strongest quarter and should not be naively annualized for a P/E.
- Goodwill now Rp 563,479 million and license rights Rp 1,351,060 million — ~10% of total assets rests on impairment-sensitive assumptions (10.02% discount rate, 1.6% terminal growth). Any SD(M) underperformance converts to impairments.
- Deferred tax liability swing to Rp 485,576 million (from Rp 134,166 million) is a direct read on the acquisition's fair-value step-up.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
- SD(M) consolidates at scale from day one: Malaysia becomes the #2 market, revenue +23% YoY in Q3, with a 350+ store SEA/India rollout as a multi-year volume engine.
- Core economics are elite for retail: 47% gross margin, ~20% annualized ROE, 1.35x cash conversion, DSO ~5 days, net cash on bank debt.
- Earnings quality improved: tax normalized to 25.3%, minority losses absorbed, organic markdown provisioning collapsed — the Rp 1.28 trillion FCF is real and recurring.
- Non-Indonesia revenue now ~25% of the mix — geographic diversification reduces single-market macro risk.

### 14.2 Bear Case
- The growth story is bought, not built: strip SD(M)'s ~Rp 500+ billion quarterly revenue contribution and organic growth is ~12% against a ~+1% retail market — share gains that may not persist.
- The balance sheet is structurally weaker: D/E 0.86x, Rp 2.84 trillion nominal of acquisition debt, Rp 1.9 trillion of impairment-sensitive goodwill/licenses, and a quick ratio of 0.51x with 121-day DIO into a fragile consumer.
- Persistent P&L drags (amortization + discount accretion) eat into gross-margin gains — SG&A ratio already up 60 bps in Q3 (external).
- Brand principals may react adversely to the brand-acquisition agenda; exclusivity breach = license termination risk on the core franchises.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
- Q4/FY2026 results: full-quarter SD(M) contribution vs the ~23% of consensus left to deliver; watch SD(M) standalone disclosure and inventory aging.
- Finalization of provisional purchase accounting (goodwill Rp 498,358 million provision is subject to revision within the measurement period).
- DGT decision on the 2021 SKPKB objection (Rp 49,553 million paid; refund possible).
- Store-opening cadence and same-store sales disclosure for the Sports Direct rollout; first India (MAIN) revenue prints.
- Any rupiah stabilization reducing the translation drag on the USD-linked acquisition liability.
- Disclosure of a market price/market cap to enable the valuation pass deferred from this report.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Moderate Risk)** — carried forward from [[MAPA Q2 2026 Analysis]]. MAPA has become a materially larger, more levered regional platform with excellent underlying cash economics, but the earnings bridge now runs through purchase accounting, and the Q4 consensus bar (~23% of FY profit) plus a weak Indonesian consumer make the near-term setup two-sided. **Target price range: not established — no market data provided.** Review triggers: Q4 2026 results with SD(M) standalone disclosure; finalization of purchase accounting; DGT objection decision; any brand-principal reaction to the acquisition agenda.
