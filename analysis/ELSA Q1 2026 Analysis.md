**PT Elnusa Tbk** delivered a resilient Q1 2026 performance, navigating a **-2.98% YoY revenue decline** while still achieving **+1.55% net profit growth** through disciplined cost management and improved working capital dynamics. ## 2. Revenue & Profitability Analysis (YoY Q1 Comparison)

### 2.1 Revenue Dynamics

Consolidated revenue declined **Rp111B (-2.98%)** YoY. The decline was entirely driven by the **Integrated Upstream O&G** and **O&G Support Services** segments, partially offset by strong growth in **Energy Distribution & Logistics**.

| Segment | Q1 2026 Revenue | Q1 2025 Revenue | Δ YoY | Share (Q1 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Integrated Upstream O&G | Rp1,005,010M | Rp1,290,792M | **-22.2%** | 27.8% |
| O&G Support Services | Rp295,217M | Rp456,741M | **-35.3%** | 8.2% |
| Energy Distribution & Logistics | Rp2,316,384M | Rp1,980,095M | **+17.0%** | 64.1% |
| **Consolidated** | **Rp3,616,611M** | **Rp3,727,628M** | **-2.98%** | 100% |

**Key Insight:** The Energy Distribution & Logistics segment (primarily PT Elnusa Petrofin with Rp5.1T in assets) has become the dominant revenue and profit driver, growing 17% YoY. This reflects increased fuel distribution volumes through Pertamina Patra Niaga, which alone contributed **Rp1.53T (42.4% of total revenue)** — up from Rp1.30T (35.0%) in Q1 2025.

The Upstream decline likely reflects reduced drilling/seismic activity from Pertamina EP (-35.3% revenue contribution) and Pertamina Hulu Rokan (-52.1%), consistent with the broader industry trend of mature field optimization over new exploration.

⚠️ **Concern:** Gross margin decline reflects the shift toward the lower-margin Energy Distribution segment (8.5% gross margin vs 11.0% for Upstream and 16.2% for Support Services). This structural mix shift may persist.

## 6. Notable Trends & Red Flags

### 🟢 Positive Trends

1. **Cash Generation Transformation:** Operating CF went from Rp284B to Rp1,042B YoY. Even excluding the one-time tax refund, the underlying cash generation improved dramatically due to better receivables management.

2. **Related-Party Receivables Declining:** Trade receivables from Pertamina group fell **Rp452B (-13.2%)** in Q1, reducing the related-party receivables as a percentage of total assets from 31.0% to 27.0%. This is a positive sign of improving payment terms from the parent group.

3. **Energy Distribution Margin Expansion:** The dominant segment improved gross margin from 7.2% to 8.5% while growing revenue 17%. This suggests pricing power or favorable product mix shifts in fuel distribution.

4. **Deleveraging Continues:** Total liabilities declined Rp130B; the Sukuk was fully repaid; bank debt is minimal. The balance sheet is cleaner than it has been in years.

5. **Dividend Payer:** FY2025 dividend of Rp39.11/share (5.01% yield) demonstrates shareholder return commitment. FY2025 cash dividend was Rp285.4B (payout ratio ~37% of FY2025 net profit of Rp718B).

### 🔴 Red Flags & Concerns

1. **Extreme Related-Party Concentration:** **76.7% of revenue** comes from Pertamina group entities. PT Pertamina Patra Niaga alone accounts for **42.4%**. While this provides revenue visibility (captive state-owned enterprise demand), it creates:
   - Pricing power asymmetry (Pertamina can squeeze margins)
   - Collection risk (though improving)
   - Revenue volatility tied to Pertamina's capex cycles

2. **Upstream Segment Deterioration:** Integrated Upstream O&G profit fell **-44.6%** YoY with margin compression (13.2% → 11.0%). This segment's revenue decline of -22.2% reflects reduced drilling activity from Pertamina EP and Hulu Rokan. If Indonesia's upstream capex slows further, this segment could become a drag.

3. **Inventory Buildup:** +28.9% inventory increase in one quarter (Rp133B) warrants monitoring. If global oil prices correct, inventory write-downs could impact margins.

4. **Rising Effective Tax Rate:** Tax rate increased from 19.7% to 21.1%, with income tax expense growing 10.9% while pre-tax profit grew only 3.4%. The company should clarify whether this reflects changes in tax treatment or one-time items.

5. **Accrued Expenses Opacity:** Rp3.35T in accrued expenses (30.4% of total assets) is a persistent structural feature. While typical for fuel distribution businesses (accrued fuel purchases from Pertamina Patra Niaga), the lack of granular disclosure on aging and terms is a transparency gap.

6. **NCI Contribution Eliminated:** Non-controlling interests contributed Rp1M to profit in Q1 2025 but **zero** in Q1 2026. This suggests the subsidiaries' minority shareholders are no longer receiving profit allocations, which could indicate restructuring or changed profit-sharing arrangements.

## 10. Conclusion & Investment Thesis

### Rating: **HOLD/ACCUMULATE** (Bias: Positive)

**Investment Thesis:**

PT Elnusa Tbk is a **structurally sound, cash-rich** Pertamina subsidiary trading at a **significant discount** (7.9x P/E, 1.07x P/B, 0.39x P/S) to intrinsic value. The Q1 2026 results demonstrate the company's ability to **grow profits despite revenue headwinds** through cost discipline and working capital optimization.

**Key Bull Arguments:**
- **Fortress balance sheet:** Rp3.2T net cash provides exceptional downside protection and optionality for growth investments
- **Improving cash conversion:** Operating CF of Rp1.04T (5.5x net profit) signals fundamental improvement in cash generation
- **Energy Distribution growth:** The dominant segment (64% of revenue, 68% of profit) is growing at 17% with expanding margins
- **Dividend yield:** 5.01% TTM yield with 37% payout ratio provides income with room for increases
- **Analyst support:** Ajaib Sekuritas Buy rating with Rp1,100 target (+32% upside)
- **Sector tailwinds:** Indonesia's upstream capex growing at 5.6% CAGR; 8 new projects onstream in 2026

**Key Bear Arguments:**
- **Revenue concentration:** 76.7% from Pertamina group creates dependency risk and limits pricing power
- **Upstream deterioration:** -22% revenue and -45% profit decline in the Integrated Upstream segment
- **Margin structure:** 9.8% gross margin is thin; any cost pressure could eliminate profitability
- **Growth ceiling:** As a Pertamina subsidiary, expansion beyond the group ecosystem is constrained
- **Stock run-up:** +56.6% YTD already prices in some improvement

### Key Catalysts to Watch:

### 11.5 Key Risks to Upside Realization

1. **Tender-driven margin pressure** — orderbook conversion is competitive; pricing could be squeezed; **the "Rp4.1T orderbook" itself is a Brights figure, not ELSA-disclosed** — contract value may differ in practice
2. **Pertamina capex discipline** — if PHE defers 2026 project onstream dates (as has happened historically), upstream recovery thesis slips
3. **KSO/LCO execution risk** — operator model shift carries execution/financial risk; precedent of Pertamina services subsidiaries underperforming on operating assets exists; **KSO/LCO scope is research speculation, not yet announced by ELSA**
4. **PDSI acquisition economics** — depends on transaction valuation & integration; only an MoU is publicly confirmed; could be EPS dilutive if priced aggressively
5. **Third-party wins remain small** — Medco/Eni contracts are positive but Patra Niaga/related-party still = ~77% of revenue; **Brights' 23% third-party mix figure should be cross-checked against ELSA's own segment disclosures**
6. **Sell-side optimism bias** — both Brights (TP Rp1,110) and Ajaib (TP Rp1,100) are initiated Buy ratings with incentives to present a constructive case; analyst forecasts in upstream services have historically overestimated capex conversion speed

### 11.6 Conclusion on Future Upside

✅ **Yes, material upside potential exists for ELSA**, but the bull thesis rests on catalysts that are **predominantly sourced from external sell-side research and have NOT yet been corroborated by ELSA's Q1 2026 financial disclosure**.

3 horizons of upside, with sourcing caveats:

1. **Near-term (FY26-FY28): Upstream activity cycle recovery** — supported by †Rp4.1T orderbook (Brights) and 8 new SKK Migas project onstreams (6 Pertamina-operated, directly addressable). Brights' +32% FY26 net profit forecast is plausible **if** Pertamina capex stays on schedule and **if** the Brights-cited orderbook is approximately correct.

2. **Medium-term (FY27+): †KSO/LCO field management + †PDSI acquisition** — these would structurally lift ELSA from low-margin services contractor into a higher-margin integrated operator. This is the **unpriced optionality** in current valuations (7.9x P/E vs Ajaib TP Rp1,100 / Brights TP Rp1,110). **Both catalysts are research-sourced — ELSA has not announced firm KSO/LCO contracts or a binding PDSI acquisition agreement.**

3. **Long-term (FY28+): Indonesia upstream growth** from Eni deepwater FIDs (confirmed by Eni press release), 13-block bidding round winners, and EOR/CEOR technology deployment (per IDNFinancials) expands TAM and customer base.

⚠️ However, the upside **remains conditional** on:
- Pertamina's capex cadence (the Q1 2026 -22.2% Integrated Upstream revenue decline shows the bear case is live today)
- Execution of KSO/LCO/PDSI catalysts (currently unannounced by ELSA)
- Accuracy of the Brights orderbook and utilization disclosures (Rp4.1T backlog, 13 HWU units — NOT in ELSA's official Q1 statements)

2026 project onstreams (PHR Minas polymer, PHE ONWJ OO-OX, PHM Sisi Nubi) need to convert into tangible ELSA revenue in H2 2026 — visible in the H1 2026 audited statements — to validate the bull case.

**Bottom line:** Base case fair value should incorporate a **modest, probability-weighted premium** for KSO/LCO + PDSI optionality (call option value), supporting the Rp920-950 base case from Section 10. The bull case Rp1,100-1,110 (from Ajaib & Brights) is **fully dependent on**:
1. Realized upstream recovery in H2 2026 ELSA financials
2. Firm announcement of KSO/LCO binding contracts by ELSA
3. Materialization of PDSI acquisition terms

Until these are company-confirmed, the bull case should be treated as optimistic research, not as base case.
