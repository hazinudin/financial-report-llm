# PT Alamtri Resources Indonesia Tbk (ADRO) — Q2 2026
---

**Reporting basis.** All figures are in **thousands of US Dollars (US$'000)**, per the statement headers, drawn from ADRO's unaudited interim consolidated financial statements for the six months ended 30 June 2026 (reviewed by the independent auditor; authorised for issue by the Board of Directors on 26 August 2026). The income statement and cash flow comparatives are the six months ended 30 June 2025; the balance sheet comparative is 31 December 2025. Where the disclosed lines are cumulative for the half (the majority), we reference the H1 2026 period and label it as such; Q2-standalone figures are derived only where the fact sheet permits (e.g., EPS vs Q1 comparatives from the research brief are flagged as external/derived, not canonical). This note covers the quarter through the H1 lens disclosed in the interim report.

## 1. Executive Summary

ADRO delivered a step-change half. H1 2026 revenue rose 16.5% YoY to US$999.2m, but the real story is the margin scissors: cost of revenue was essentially flat (+0.5% to US$576.6m) while revenue climbed, so gross profit jumped 48.7% to US$422.7m (42.3% gross margin vs 33.2% a year ago), operating profit rose 87.8% to US$381.8m (38.2% margin), and net profit grew 68.9% to US$329.2m — of which US$309.4m (+76.8%) accrued to owners and EPS nearly doubled to US$0.01074 per share. The uplift came from a strong metallurgical coal price environment, cost discipline (operating expenses cut 3.3% to US$58.3m), a US$38m YoY swing in other income, a swing to +US$33.5m JV profit (from –US$3.9m), and ~US$34m of dividend income booked in Q2 that was absent a year earlier. Tax was an expense of US$86.8m — a normal ~20.9% effective rate — and any suggestion of a "tax reversal" is a sign-convention artifact of the pipeline, not a fact.

The balance sheet is being rebuilt around the KAI aluminium smelter. Total assets grew 14.5% YTD to US$7.80bn — fixed assets up to US$2.98bn, inventories up 336% to US$505.1m (dominated by US$319.9m of unsold aluminium ingot and US$33.8m of aluminium raw materials, essentially all booked in Q2 as the smelter's first output accumulated ahead of first cargo shipments on 10 and 29 June 2026). The funding side is where the tension lives: bank loans rose 59% to US$1.18bn, lease liabilities exploded from US$76m to US$756m on a new ~US$690.7m power-plant lease from PT Kaltara Power Indonesia (KPI, a related party), and total liabilities grew 47.7% to US$2.68bn — against equity that grew only 2.5% to US$5.13bn because ADRO paid out essentially all of FY2025 earnings as dividends (US$447.5m to owners plus US$21.2m to NCI in H1) under a 99.96% payout policy.

Cash flow crystallises the strain: operating cash flow flipped from +US$333.2m in H1 2025 to **–US$42.9m**, with payments to suppliers more than doubling to US$803.8m as the smelter ramp-up absorbed cash; free cash flow was –US$52.6m. Meanwhile the group still holds US$978.2m of cash and US$693.7m of securities, leaving it in a net cash position of US$488.5m (net debt/equity of –9.5%), so the leverage build is deliberate project financing, not distress. The KAI syndicated facility's amortisation is deliberately back-loaded (no 2026 principal, US$163m due 2027, ramping to US$238m in 2030), so the headline "loans current +459%" is a base effect plus current-maturity reclassification, not a liquidity event.

Valuation cannot be scored on this note: no market-data stub was provided, so P/E, P/B and EV multiples are **not disclosed** here. As context only, the pipeline DB's latest available period (Oct 2025) showed ADRO at PER 5.65x and PBV 0.62x versus its listed affiliate ADMR at 11.76x and 2.31x — a persistent holding-company discount that the smelter ramp is designed to close. The thesis is intact and improving operationally, but the investment case now hinges on converting US$505m of aluminium inventory into revenue in H2 (first cargoes shipped end-June; ~60,675t hedged for Jul-Sep; management targeting 40,000t/month by October). We rate the quarter's fundamentals strongly positive with elevated execution and funding-complexity risk.

¹ Derived: prior H1 mining external + related-party mining sales per Note 27 (third-party mining 234,113 + related-party mining 207,493 = 441,606). Segment lines are as transcribed from the interim report's segment note.

Revenue growth of 16.5% was driven almost entirely by the mining segment — third-party coal sales nearly doubled to US$370.3m (from US$234.1m, per Note 27) on higher metallurgical coal volumes and realised prices, while related-party mining sales were roughly stable at US$210.1m. Mining services revenue was flat, reflecting contracted pit-to-port services for the group's own concessions. The aluminium smelter contributed **zero external revenue** in H1 2026 — first cargoes only left on 10 and 29 June 2026 — meaning every dollar of consolidated revenue is still coal and mining services.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet does not disclose quarterly coal ASP, tonnage, or production statistics (production and reserves arrays are empty), so volume/price attribution relies on external context, flagged as such. Per the research brief (aluna 1Q26, Kontan, BRI Danareksa via brights.id), the 2026 met-coal price environment was the tailwind: coking coal around US$226/t in mid-April 2026, +19.3% YoY, with ADMR's Q1 2026 ASP around US$183/t. The revenue/COGS scissors (+16.5% revenue vs +0.5% cost of revenue) is consistent with price-led mining revenue on a stable cost base — higher realised prices drop almost entirely to gross profit, which is exactly what the margin table in §3.1 shows.

FX was a secondary tailwind: the statements are USD-functional and the Rupiah's translation effects ran positive (net monetary asset translation would have reduced net monetary assets by only ~US$5.4m at completion-date rates per Note 34, while OCI includes +US$26,981 of cash-flow-hedge and +US$23,705 of FVOCI movements), and analysts cited FX gains among H1 profit drivers. The first aluminium hedges (~60,675t for Jul–Sep 2026, per external reporting) cover the Q3 conversion of inventory into revenue.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

- **Related-party concentration remains the defining feature**: related-party revenue was US$625.6m, or 62.6% of consolidated revenue (Note 32b) — down from 72.7% in H1 2025 as third-party coal sales scaled. The largest counterparties are AI (mining contractor group: US$369.4m, up from US$333.0m) and Adaro International Singapore (AIS: US$208.7m). This is structurally high but reflects the integrated pit-to-port model rather than arm's-length risk.
- **Destination shift**: per Note 37, sales by destination added Japan at US$30.8m (nil in H1 2025) while China went to nil (US$8.5m prior) — consistent with met-coal tonnage finding Japanese steelmakers.
- **Segment mix**: mining 58.1% of external revenue, mining services 39.8%, others 2.2%; metal processing nil. KPI's internal revenue fell to zero (US$3.2m prior).
- **Customer concentration**: AI and AIS together account for ~58% of revenue; no single supplier exceeds 10% of purchases (Note 28).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Sustainability rests on three pillars. First, met-coal pricing: the price tailwind is externally assessed as intact (BRI Danareksa, Oct 2026, "Coal Laggard with Intact Price Tailwind", BUY, TP Rp3,300), though a drought-lowered Barito River was flagged in September 2026 (post-period) as likely delaying ADMR's Q3 coking-coal shipments — a watch item, not an in-period fact. Second, policy: PP No. 24/2026 / Permendag No. 15/2026 restricts coal exports to Export SOEs after 31 December 2026; the company states the impact is not yet determinable — a genuine structural overhang on the mining revenue base. Third, the smelter: the growth option is the 500ktpa Phase 1 KAI smelter, targeting up to 350,000t of ingot sales in 2026 (raised from 300,000t) and 40,000t/month run-rate by October 2026, with offtake agreements being finalised. Reserves/runway data for the coal concessions were not disclosed in the fact sheet (the CCoW table gives concession areas — LC 46,620 ha, JC/KC ~24,988 ha each, MC 24,990 ha, SBC 24,993 ha — all amended 14 November 2017 — but no tonnage reserves). Committed forward sales cover 3.5m mt of coal and 66k mt of aluminium deliverable 2026–2029 (unreviewed), up from 1.6m mt coal and nil aluminium at 31 Dec 2025 — evidence of a real forward book.

**Sign-convention alert, material to interpretation.** The computed-ratios block carries an `effective_tax_rate` of −0.2087 because the fact sheet stores tax as a negative number and the formula divides expense by a negative PBT convention. ### 4.2 Working Capital Components

- **Receivables / DSO 38.7 days (annualized basis per ratio block).** Trade receivables of US$423.7m are 84% related-party (AI US$233.9m, AIS US$56.5m, LSA US$37.5m, SCM US$19.4m, PCS US$6.4m — Note 7a), a stable structural feature. No ECL provision was deemed necessary. Collections ran at 96.9% of revenue (US$968.4m collected vs US$999.2m billed) — healthy for a group with this related-party weight.
- **Inventories / DIO 46.1 days.** The 336% build is the half's defining balance-sheet event. Composition (per notes/context): **aluminium ingot US$319.9m, aluminium raw materials US$33.8m** — together 70% of the total — plus coal ~US$86m, fuel and lubricants US$38.9m, spares and others. Inventory was only ~US$130.8m at 31 March 2026 (external), so essentially the entire increase landed in Q2 as smelter output stacked up ahead of the first cargoes. This is working-capital investment, not distress stock — but it is also the reason operating cash flow went negative and it carries aluminium price risk until hedged/sold (~60,675t hedged Jul–Sep per external reporting).
- **Prepaid taxes US$46.5m (+91%)**: the fact sheet does not disclose composition; per the research brief (low confidence), the mechanics fit recoverable PPh 22/23/25 credits and input VAT on smelter capex and alumina imports with no offsetting revenue yet. Open item — see research plan.
- **Other receivables US$35.3m** (down from US$54.1m), 94% related-party, including KPI US$23.6m.
- **DPO 12.7 days** — the group pays its suppliers fast; trade payables fell to US$139.6m from US$171.4m (IDR 135.6m / USD 3.6m by currency).

### 4.4 Non-Current Assets Analysis

- **Fixed assets / PPE net US$2.98bn (up 26% from US$2.36bn)** — the smelter and power infrastructure build, plus crushing facilities. Non-cash additions of fixed assets under leases of US$664.7m (vs US$1.4m prior) confirm the lease-capitalisation route. Depreciation allocated to inventories (US$26.1m) will feed future COGS as ingot is sold.
- **Mining properties US$534.6m** (from US$540.6m) — amortisation continues (US$8.8m for the half, all to cost of revenue).
- **Investments**: JV/associate carrying amounts US$696.8m (BPI 485.9, TPI 127.1, KHN 83.8); non-current securities at amortised cost US$84.0m; FVOCI US$521.2m within current securities. FVTPL US$88.5m took a US$12.5m H1 loss (Note 6b) — a small drag inside "other income".
- **Restricted cash US$29.7m total** (current US$3.7m, non-current US$26.0m, +62%) — includes a DSRA placed with Bank Permata tied to an US$80m facility; the exact purpose of the non-current increase is not disclosed in the fact sheet (open item).
- **Intangibles/goodwill US$39.7m** — mining-services goodwill, unimpaired (test assumptions: 0% terminal growth, 10% post-tax discount rate).
- **Reclamation/mine closure provision US$27.1m non-current** (prior US$29.0m with a small current portion). **The "reclamation provision to nil" flag is an artifact of the current-portion line only** — the provision itself reduced modestly (–US$2.1m) via deductions and realisation; there is no derecognition event. Government-mandated reclamation guarantees (bank guarantees and time deposits across LC, MC, KC, JC, SBC, BASR) remain in place in parallel.

### 4.5 Non-Controlling Interests (NCI)

NCI equity stands at US$518.4m (marginally below US$518.5m at year-end), concentrated in AMI (US$237.6m), AP (US$127.1m) and **KAI (US$154.1m)** — the smelter is only 56% owned by the group chain, so 44% of its eventual profits accrue to minority holders (the KAI subsidiary issued shares to NCI in 2025 for US$71.9m, evidence of external smelter co-investment). NCI took home US$19.8m of H1 profit (6.0% of net profit, down from 10.3% in H1 2025 because the profit surge came from fully-owned lines) and received US$25.8m of dividends; the group also bought US$7.1m of NCI stakes. Profit-retention at KAI matters for the smelter thesis: while ramping, the 44% leakage is minimal (smelter at –US$45.5m), but at target run-rate the minority drag becomes real.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

- **The aluminium segment lost US$45.5m on zero revenue in H1** — pre-revenue commissioning costs plus, from Q2, US$26m/quarter of power-plant lease interest that begins hitting P&L before meaningful ingot revenue lands. The bridge to breakeven requires the 40,000t/month run-rate target to be met by October 2026.
- **PSM's Rp526.7bn loan is "fully provisioned" yet still outstanding** — an old-generation legacy position (secured by a pledge over all PSM shares, maturing 31 December 2026) that the group has written off but continues to consolidate; watch for any recovery as pure upside or disposal.
- **PP No. 24/2026 export-SOE restriction** on coal exports after end-2026 — impact "not yet determinable" per the company; a structural cloud over the mining segment's route to market.
- **Tanah Laut Wind Farm PPA** signed May 2023, still not effective at the last disclosure — dormant optionality.
- The smelter's aluminium price risk is only partially hedged (post-period, ADRO guaranteed up to US$100m of KAI's ISDA hedging facility — 3 July 2026, post-period).

### 7.4 Growth Options

The second leg is unambiguous: **500ktpa Phase 1 aluminium smelter ramping toward 350,000t of 2026 ingot sales** (target raised from 300,000t), first cargoes 31,494t to the US and 3,569t to Korea in June, ~60,675t hedged for Q3, offtakes being finalised. Supporting options: ABI/ATDI (battery and investment vehicles, US$4.1bn combined assets, pre-operational), the solar/Batam/Karimun power cluster (small but numerous), and KELI's integrated generation licence. Downside watch: LME aluminium easing to ~US$3,200/t and alumina at ~US$353/t (post-period external figures) compress the ramp margin versus the bull case.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Related-party economics are pervasive and should be monitored, not feared: 62.6% of revenue, 84% of receivables, and now the **KPI power-plant lease (US$690.7m, US$26.3m H1 interest — 89% of all finance costs)** — KPI is a fellow Alamtri-group entity, so the smelter's single largest cost is an intra-group transfer. The Adaro shareholder loan of US$289.9m outstanding (facility US$1,450m, down from US$389.9m) and BPI's US$140.6m CJPP support loan (no maturity, repayable on demand) add parent-level flexibility. Loans to related parties total ~US$419m carrying (US$430m fair value). The eliminations line (–US$82.3m internal revenue; segment "Others" profit of US$75.7m partly reflecting treasury/investment income) shows the consolidation machinery working; the docling-extraction caveat applies to some segment table rows.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Inventory monetisation.** US$354m of aluminium ingot and raw materials must convert to cash; OCF is negative and cash conversion −13%. Slippage in the 40,000t/month October run-rate target directly hits H2 cash and earnings.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
Coal margins hold above 40% gross through 2026 on intact met-coal pricing, while the smelter hits 40,000t/month by October 2026 and converts the US$505m inventory into revenue at better than breakeven — swinging OCF strongly positive and adding a second earnings leg that closes the holdco discount (0.62x P/B at last ingestion). Owners' ROE sustains above 20%; the FY26 dividend normalises to a sustainable payout; NCI leakage at KAI is a 2027 problem, not a 2026 one.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Aluminium ramps slowly at US$3,200/t LME against high commissioning COGS (the [[ADMR Q2 2026 Earnings Forecast]] commissioning-quarter bear case), inventory overhangs into 2027, met-coal prices mean-revert and unwind 900bps of gross margin, the export-SOE regulation bites the coal franchise, and the ~100% payout forces a dividend cut just as US$163m of KAI principal comes due in 2027. The balance sheet's net cash erodes from both directions.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. Q3 2026 results (October): first material aluminium revenue and the OCF inflection — the single most important print.
2. Achievement of the 40,000t/month ingot run-rate by October 2026 and finalised offtake agreements.
3. Clarity on PP No. 24/2026 export-SOE implementation for coal.
4. Q3 coking-coal shipment recovery after Barito River drought disruption.
5. The FY2026 dividend decision (RUPST April 2027) — payout sustainability test.
6. A market-data refresh to re-score valuation (multiples not disclosed this run).

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Moderate risk qualifier).** The operating half was excellent — +83% EPS, 42.3% gross margin, 27% annualised owners' ROE on a net-cash balance sheet — and we would be buyers on the last-ingested valuation (0.62x book). But without current market data we cannot certify that the price has not already rerated on the August earnings release, and the H2 cash-flow inflection (inventory → cash) is unproven. Hold existing positions for the smelter optionality; the Q3 report is the decision trigger. Indicative framework (not a target price — no market data): accumulate below 0.9x book value per share (US$0.144 equivalent) if the smelter run-rate is on track; review trigger if October's Q3 report shows aluminium revenue below ~US$80m for the quarter or OCF still negative.
