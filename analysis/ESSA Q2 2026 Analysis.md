## 1. Executive Summary

ESSA’s first half of 2026 is a study in operating leverage and balance-sheet optionality. Revenue surged 35.5% year-over-year to USD 186,475,598, driven almost entirely by ammonia offtake to Genesis Corporation and LPG/processing fees. Cost of goods sold rose only 21.4% in absolute terms, allowing gross profit to expand 72.5% to USD 65,705,135 and lifting the gross margin to 35.24%—a level not seen in the disclosed prior-year ratios. Net profit more than doubled to USD 42,012,497, with owners’ attributable profit at USD 30,173,657 and basic EPS at USD 1.752, up 103.2% from the prior-year USD 0.862.

The balance sheet is fortress-like. Cash ballooned to USD 189,225,543 from USD 125,760,092 at year-end 2025, producing a net cash position of USD 201,369,431 and a net debt-to-equity ratio of negative 0.363x. The current ratio stands at 3.16x, but current liabilities more than quadrupled to USD 87,357,266 from USD 19,338,171—almost entirely from accrued expenses and tax payables rather than debt, yet the jump warrants monitoring. Operating cash flow of USD 87,624,045 converted net profit at 2.09x, and free cash flow reached USD 97,595,014 after only USD 9,970,969 of capex.

The fly in the ointment is existential concentration. Two customers accounted for 100% of H1 2026 sales. Genesis Corporation—a related party under common control—bought USD 166,256,202 of ammonia, representing 89.2% of segment-level external sales. Trade receivables are similarly captive: Genesis carries USD 22,292,987 (89.16% of disclosed receivables) and a further USD 24,701,739 under common control. With no market price, market cap, or consensus multiples available as of report date, standard valuation metrics are impossible to compute. The equity is a fundamentals-driven HOLD pending price discovery, but the risk qualifier is severe: any disruption to the Genesis offtake or ammonia price normalization would collapse the earnings narrative despite the pristine balance sheet.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Metric | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 186,475,598 | 137,587,951 | +35.5% |

Revenue growth of 35.53% (pre-computed) was driven by a combination of ammonia price strength and sustained offtake volumes. The absolute increment of USD 48.9M is entirely attributable to the Ammonia and LPG/Processing Fee segments; the “Others” segment recorded zero external sales.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

Segment disclosures split revenue into Ammonia (USD 166,256,202) and LPG and Processing Fee (USD 20,219,396). No production volume, reserve, or throughput data was disclosed in the extracted notes. Management commentary from a closed-door meeting on June 2, 2026 (per [[forecast/ESSA_Q2_2026_Earnings_Forecast]]) cited a SE Asia ammonia reference price of approximately USD 750/MT, 38K inventory, and gas costs tracking ammonia prices with an expected 3–6 month normalization window. This suggests the H1 2026 revenue run-rate is priced at elevated ammonia levels rather than structural volume growth. Foreign exchange tailwinds are not quantified; the report is denominated in USD.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is absolute. The notes state that two customers accounted for 100% of total sales as of June 30, 2026. Genesis Corporation (related party) is the sole offtaker for ammonia sales (USD 166,256,202). PT Pertamina Patra Niaga and PT Pertamina EP are the remaining counterparties. Domestic versus external sales splits are not disclosed beyond the segment table. The customer mix is therefore undiversified, with no third-party export revenue visible in the extracted data.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The forward revenue trajectory hinges on ammonia price normalization. Management’s 3–6 month normalization guidance implies H2 2026 revenue could compress if spot prices retreat toward consensus assumptions of USD 415/MT (per [[forecast/ESSA_Q2_2026_Earnings_Forecast]]). Reserve life, run-of-mine schedules, and plant utilization rates are not disclosed, so volume sustainability is unverifiable. A blue ammonia capex program of USD 120–150M is flagged as a strategic option but has not reached FID. Watch items: Genesis contract renewal terms, Pertamina demand schedules, and any Ministry of Energy alteration to domestic gas allocation or cost-pass-through mechanics.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **Customer concentration (HIGH):** Two customers accounted for 100% of H1 2026 sales. Genesis Corporation (related party) represented the entirety of ammonia revenue. Any volume disruption, pricing dispute, or covenant breach would halt revenue recognition.
- **Related-party receivables (HIGH):** USD 22.3M–24.7M receivables from Genesis dominate the trade receivables line. Collection risk is entangled with affiliate liquidity.
- **Dividend execution (MODERATE):** USD 48.3M dividend payable sits unsettled; cash flow shows no H1 2026 dividend payment despite declarations. Delayed distribution or a policy shift could pressure total shareholder return.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **Single-commodity exposure (MODERATE):** 89.2% of revenue is ammonia. No hedging or downstream diversification is visible in the extracted notes.
- **NCI cash extraction (MODERATE):** PAU dividends of USD 70M in 2026 versus USD 50M in 2025 suggest aggressive distributions to NCI, potentially starving the subsidiary of reinvestment capital.
- **Dormant assets (LOW):** OBP holds zero assets and contributes nothing; ESM is nascent. Strategic optionality is unproven.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **Ammonia price normalization (MODERATE):** Management guided 3–6 month normalization from a SE Asia reference near USD 750/MT. A sustained drop toward the consensus USD 415/MT assumption would compress the 35.2% gross margin.
- **Domestic gas allocation policy (MODERATE):** Feed gas pricing tracks ammonia; any regulatory change in Indonesian gas cost pass-through mechanics would alter the cost base.
- **FX (LOW):** Reported in USD; Rupiah exposure exists but is not quantified.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- **No market data (HIGH):** Without share price, market cap, or consensus estimates, P/E, EV/EBITDA, and yield are unknowable. Investors lack a quantitative entry benchmark.
- **Earnings quality (MODERATE):** Segment-level net profits for Ammonia and LPG individually exceed group net profit, suggesting material unallocated corporate costs or eliminations that are not itemized.
