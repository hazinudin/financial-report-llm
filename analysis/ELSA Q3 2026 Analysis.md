# PT Elnusa Tbk (ELSA) — Q3 2026
---

**Basis of preparation.** All figures are in **millions of Indonesian Rupiah (IDR)** unless stated otherwise, drawn from the **unaudited** interim consolidated financial statements for the **nine months ended 30 September 2026** (comparatives: 9M 2025 for income statement and cash flow; 31 December 2025 audited balance sheet). EPS and certain note figures are stated in full Rupiah where indicated. This is a cumulative nine-month filing; "Q3 2026" in this note refers to that 9M period unless explicitly labelled "Q3 standalone". No market-data stub was provided for this run, so all market-based multiples are **not disclosed / not provided** in this note; external broker estimates are cited only as labelled external context with their sources.

## 1. Executive Summary

PT Elnusa Tbk delivered a strong nine months to 30 September 2026: revenue rose **+15.8% YoY to Rp12,131,372** (9M 2025: Rp10,477,387), net profit jumped **+29.4% to Rp681,551** (9M 2025: Rp526,562), and basic EPS expanded **+29.4% to Rp93.38** from Rp72.15 on an unchanged 7,298,500,000 weighted-average share count. Growth was concentrated in the fuel distribution & logistics segment (external sales +23.5% to Rp7,586,135) while integrated upstream services grew a modest +12.7% and oil & gas support services declined −17.5% — a mix that mechanically compresses blended gross margin, which fell to **9.47%** from a derived **~10.27%** in 9M 2025. The net margin nonetheless *expanded* to **5.62%** from ~5.03%, driven almost entirely below the gross line: G&A cut −10.3% to Rp388,939, interest expense down −37.8% to Rp44,485 as bank debt was paid down (Rp387,765 repaid in the period), an FX gain of Rp37,246 (vs Rp18,719), and a lower effective tax rate of **19.33%** (vs ~23.3%).

The balance sheet tells a more double-edged story. Elnusa is now structurally a **net-cash company**: total bank loans of Rp373,162 (Rp211,850 current + Rp161,312 non-current) against cash of Rp2,129,622, with loans-to-equity of just **0.066x** and all loan covenants (max DER 3x, min current ratio 1x, DSCR 1.1x) comfortably met — the company itself discloses a DSCR of **4.45x** and a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.1x (total-liabilities basis) versus its 3x policy ceiling. But the working-capital engine is straining: net trade receivables swelled **+40.6% since December to Rp5,106,140**, overwhelmingly Pertamina-group billing, with unbilled receivables of Rp3,706,509 (vs Rp2,601,141 at FY25) and a sharply elevated overdue 1–30-day bucket of Rp999,666 (vs Rp125,489). OCF fell −11.5% YoY to Rp522,978 despite the profit surge, cash conversion slipped to 76.7%, and cash declined Rp568,881 to Rp2,129,622 after funding Rp477,809 of capex, Rp387,765 of loan repayments, and a Rp323,251 dividend (paid July 8, 2026).

Strategically, the quarter confirms the pivot articulated in [[ELSA Q2 2026 Analysis]] and the Brights thesis ([[Brights - ELSA - Monetizing Indonesia Upstream Revival - 18 May 2026]]): Elnusa is the listed proxy for Indonesia's upstream capex revival (SKK Migas upstream investment realization of US$10.4bn through August 2026 tracking above 2025's full-year US$14.9bn pace, per Warta Ekonomi, 26 Oct 2026) while its cash engine is now the lower-margin but fast-growing downstream distribution book, where 1H26 volumes rose +18% YoY on subsidy-fuel demand. The unresolved swing factors are the **PDSI merger** (still "internal discussion", union opposition voiced September 2026), the **receivables cycle** with the ultimate parent, and the post-period oil-price spike (Brent ~US$116/bbl in late October 2026 on Middle-East escalation, per ANTARA/Kontan reporting), which cuts both ways for Pertamina's payment capacity and ELSA's cost base.

No market data was provided for this run; on external context, Bahana Sekuritas (per Kontan, 6 Sep 2026) rated the stock Buy with a Rp1,000 target at 6.0x 2026E P/E, projecting 18% CAGR in upstream services profit through 2028. On our own prior assessment ([[ELSA Q2 2026 Analysis]], fair value Rp920–950), we maintain a **HOLD/ACCUMULATE** stance: earnings momentum and a fortress balance sheet argue for accumulation, but receivables concentration (80.5% of revenue from related parties), the structural gross-margin mix drag, and the thinning FCF (Rp45,169 in 9M 2026, OCF less capex) cap the multiple. **HOLD/ACCUMULATE, fair value Rp920–950** (per prior vault analysis; not re-derived here given absent market data).

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The filing discloses **no production, volume, or price tables** in the captured detail lists (`production: []`), so the volume/price decomposition rests on segment revenue and external reporting:

- **Distribution (volume-led):** the goods/energy distribution & logistics segment grew external sales +23.5% YoY. Per the 1H26 results call (Stockbit Snips, 31 Jul 2026), fuel-transport throughput was ~14 million kilolitres with volume **+18% YoY**, and subsidised retail prices were unchanged (Pertalite Rp10,000/litre, subsidised diesel Rp6,800/litre per CNBC/ANTARA reporting of early September 2026), while non-subsidised Dex-series prices were adjusted upward in July and September 2026. Growth is volume and mix, not subsidised-price pass-through.
- **Upstream (activity-led):** integrated upstream services external sales +12.7%. Management stated on the 1H26 call that project costs are booked as incurred while revenue is recognised ~2–3 months after project completion — so the visible Q3 revenue surge partially reflects H1/Q3 work being certified and invoiced, and simultaneously builds unbilled receivables (see §4.2).
- **Support services (declining):** oil & gas support services fell −17.5%, continuing the structural decline flagged in [[ELSA Q1 2026 Analysis]] and [[ELSA Q2 2026 Analysis]].
- **FX:** net FX gain of Rp37,246 in other income (vs Rp18,719); Note 37 sensitivity shows 1% rupiah strengthening adds Rp7,570 to pre-tax profit. FX is a modest tailwind to reported results but a cost risk on imported equipment.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Supportive in-period and post-period drivers:

- **Upstream capex upcycle:** SKK Migas unaudited upstream investment of US$10.4bn (Rp179.2tn) to 31 August 2026 against a US$17.9bn full-year target, tracking above 2025's US$14.9bn (Warta Ekonomi, 26 Oct 2026); Pertamina Drilling completed 90 wells in WK Rokan Jan–Sep 2026; 12 SKK Migas high-impact wells (~31% of planned drilling) underway. Pertamina Hulu Energi's US$33.1bn investment plan through 2029 (per Bahana, via Kontan 6 Sep 2026) is the multi-year demand backdrop.
- **Contract book:** 1H26 new contracts of Rp11.6tn (downstream Rp9tn / 77.6%; upstream Rp1.6tn, −13% YoY; support Rp1tn) and backlog of Rp9.5tn (+7% YoY) per Warta Ekonomi, 7 Sep 2026.
- **Post-period oil spike (external, post-period):** Brent ~US$116/bbl and WTI ~US$103/bbl in late October 2026 on Middle-East escalation; SKK Migas projects ICP at US$95–100/bbl by end-2026 (ANTARA, 20 Oct 2026), versus ICP of US$81.68 for July 2026 (Bisnis.com, 10 Aug 2026). Higher oil supports Pertamina/SKK Migas cash and hence the receivables cycle, but rupiah pressure (BI held at 5.75% on 22–23 September 2026 explicitly for rupiah stability, per Bisnis.com, 24 Sep 2026) raises imported cost exposure — Note 37 shows net FX monetary assets of Rp757,008 as a partial natural hedge.

Watch items: subsidy/compensation disbursement of Rp376.8tn to September 2026 (+54.1% YoY, 84.3% of budget, per ANTARA 9 Oct 2026) underwrites Patra Niaga's liquidity as the payer; the Amurang LPG depot option (Pertamina may buy the assets for Rp107,000 or extend five years; deferred income of Rp30,317 remaining) is a discrete 2026–27 revenue hinge in the support segment.

Blended gross margin **contracted** year-on-year — this is the comparator reality flagged in the research brief and consistent with the H1 pattern (9.5% vs 10.4%, [[ELSA Q2 2026 Analysis]]). **Receivables / DSO — the single biggest balance-sheet issue.** Net receivables of Rp5,106,140 comprise gross Rp5,324,242 less impairment allowance of Rp218,102. The related-party detail (Note 5) is stark:

Unbilled balances now represent **~72% of related-party gross receivables**, with Patra Niaga's unbilled alone at **19.0% of total assets** (Rp2,234,033 / Rp11,773,439). This is mechanically consistent with the 2–3-month upstream revenue-recognition lag and with the Q3 activity surge (work delivered, certification pending) — but the **aging is a warning light**: overdue 1–30-day balances jumped to Rp999,666 from Rp125,489, and 31–60 days to Rp190,861 from Rp32,157. That pattern is more consistent with a *slower billing-to-collection cycle* than with pure WIP growth. **Open question (must-explain):** the counterparty-level unbilled split at 30 Sep 2026 is now answered by Note 5 (Patra Niaga Rp2,234,033; Pertamina EP Rp731,195), but whether the overdue build reflects certification lag or genuine payment delay at Pertamina group cannot be resolved from the filing. No public reporting of formal Pertamina payment-term changes was captured; the only adjacent episode was July 2026 media claims of late payment to BBM transporters, which Patra Niaga Sumbagut publicly denied (CNBC Indonesia / Warta Ekonomi, 17 Jul 2026), while state subsidy disbursement of Rp376.8tn (+54.1% YoY) to September argues against a systemic Pertamina cash squeeze (ANTARA, 9 Oct 2026).

Computed **DSO is 115.2 days** (annualized-revenue basis, per the deterministic ratio block), **DIO 11.8 days**, **DPO 33.3 days**, and **cash conversion cycle 93.7 days**. Note the methodology caveat: [[ELSA Q2 2026 Analysis]] reported 49.8 days and an earlier pass of this note 38.4 days, both from annualizing the flow at ×4 (a single-quarter run-rate) rather than the 12/9 cumulative basis used here; on a like-for-like 6M basis H1 2026 DSO was ~99 days, so receivables days have risen, not fallen. The ratio block's 115.2 days (receivables including unbilled over annualized revenue) implies **~Rp3.8tn of revenue-equivalent tied up in receivables**. The collections ratio was 90.2% (collections Rp10,943,780 vs revenue Rp12,131,372).

Other components: inventories +12.9% to Rp521,017 (gross Rp530,478, allowance steady at Rp9,461; inventory expense in COGS Rp2,158,987 vs Rp1,951,631 — scaling with the trading book). Advances surged: current operating advances Rp129,121 vs Rp45,006, non-current advances for fixed assets Rp87,285 vs Rp39,498 (Note 8) — early evidence of Q4 project mobilisation. Prepaid expenses Rp66,362 vs Rp34,518. Prepaid taxes fell to Rp517,562 current (total Rp825,827 vs Rp926,110 at FY25) even after rebounding from Rp342,010 at 30 June — consistent with PPh 25 instalments being credited against a higher year-to-date tax charge; **not fully reconciled from the filing, flagged as open** (see §12/§13).

Haiyanto holds 5.80% (423,692,800 shares), down from 6.23% at December 2025 — a meaningful insider reduction worth noting. ### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary

Concrete in-period actions: (i) the Rp100bn buyback (announced 31 July 2026, window to 2 November 2026; 6,622,600 shares repurchased by 30 September) explicitly funded "from optimising internal cash" (CNBC Indonesia, 3 Aug 2026); (ii) debt paydown of Rp387,765 and sukuk retirement; (iii) capital recycling into distribution infrastructure (depot/warehouse CIP); (iv) public positioning on non-captive expansion (South Sumatra seismic, Iraq market entry) and PDSI as "internal discussion". Management's 1H26 disclosures — the 2–3-month upstream revenue-recognition lag and the intent to grow the non-Pertamina revenue share — are the interpretive keys to this quarter's balance sheet.

### 12.3 Governance Observations

- **Verification flags (unresolved):** the buyback window extending past the reporting date; the internally inconsistent Note 19g wording on the public-company tax-rate discount; the lease-liability classification gap (Rp202,686 liquidity table vs Rp186,997 supplementary flow); several docling table misalignments (intangibles comparative, Note 36 hierarchy, merged Note 36 rows). - Concentration disclosure: management's "no dependence on any particular customer" boilerplate (Note 35) is technically defensible (contract diversification) but strained against 80.5% related-party revenue.
- Employee structure shifted toward permanent staff (1,459 vs 1,397) — quality-of-workforce investment, with termination benefits of Rp2,149 recognised.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

The 9M capital allocation sequence — Rp477.8bn capex > Rp387.8bn debt repayment > Rp323.3bn dividend > Rp100bn buyback — is a coherent "fund growth, then return" framework executed from a net-cash position. The open question is calibration: with committed capex of Rp393.6bn ahead and receivables absorbing operating cash, the simultaneous buyback is only prudent if the unbilled book converts on schedule in Q4. The implicit ROIC test: Rp477.8bn of capex into an 8.25%-gross-margin distribution build-out must clear a much lower hurdle than the upstream assets it accompanies.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 9/10

Net cash, covenant headroom of an order of magnitude, 4.45x DSCR, undrawn facilities, proven tax-refund execution. One point deducted for the cash-ratio slide to 0.37x and the FCF collapse to Rp45,169 — liquidity quality, not quantity, is the issue.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 7/10

Distribution is executing superbly; upstream delivery is credible but margin-dilutive; support services is shrinking; collections are the weak operational link (90.2% collections ratio, aging deterioration).

### 13.3 Growth Score: 7/10

+15.8% revenue and +29.4% profit with Rp9.5tn backlog and a sector tailwind (US$17.9bn SKK Migas target, US$33.1bn PHE plan). Capped by mix quality (growth in the lowest-margin segment), unconfirmed PDSI optionality, and only +3.5% third-party revenue growth.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 6/10

No market data to score precisely; on the prior framework (Rp920–950 fair value, ~5% yield) the risk-reward is balanced — sub-book-value downside protection from Rp776 BVPS and 16.6% ROE, versus multiple compression risk from margin mix and receivables quality.

### 13.5 Risk Profile

**Moderate-to-high, concentrated in a single axis: Pertamina.** The financial risk is minimal; the commercial risk is that the parent's payment cycle, group restructuring, or activity budget turns. Market/external risks (oil, FX, subsidy policy) are secondary. Earnings-quality risk is moderate (FX/tax flattered the 9M margin gain).

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

- Q3 standalone revenue of ~Rp4.5tn (derived) proves the acceleration; Q4 certification of the Rp3.7tn unbilled book converts paper profit into cash, restoring FCF.
- The distribution flywheel (volume +18%, margin +66 bps, depot capacity coming online December 2026) compounds through 2027.
- Upstream revival: SKK Migas investment tracking above 2025, PHE's US$33.1bn plan, Bahana's 18% upstream-profit CAGR — ELSA is the cleanest listed proxy.
- PDSI merger confirmation is a step-change re-rating catalyst; the late-October oil spike (post-period) strengthens Pertamina's cash generation and hence the receivables cycle.
- Net cash + Rp776 BVPS + 16.6% unlevered ROE + ~5% yield = a floor under the stock.

### 14.2 Bear Case

- The receivables build is structural, not timing: Pertamina's internal payment cycle lengthens (overdue 1–30 days ×8), cash conversion keeps sliding, and the buyback/dividend compete with capex for a shrinking cash balance.
- Gross margin keeps compressing as mix shifts; opex and tax offsets exhaust; net margin mean-reverts toward the gross line.
- PDSI merger dilutes or displaces minority holders; Pertamina group restructuring unilaterally re-prices captive contracts.
- Support services and upstream margins signal pricing power loss; fleet renewal (Rp1.98tn fully-depreciated in use) demands capex beyond the committed Rp393.6bn.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **Q4 2026 unbilled-to-cash conversion** (Patra Niaga Rp2.23tn unbilled) — the single most important number in the FY2026 report.
2. **PDSI merger decision** (management: "internal discussion"; union opposition live).
3. Fuel depot completion (December 2026) and warehouse (February 2027) — distribution capacity and margin.
4. Pertamina 2027 work-program/budget announcements and the 12 SKK Migas high-impact wells.
5. Full-year audited confirmation of the tax-rate position (Note 19g inconsistency).
6. Post-period oil price direction (ICP US$95–100 end-2026 per SKK Migas) as a leading indicator of the receivables cycle.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD/ACCUMULATE — fair value Rp920–950** (carried from [[ELSA Q2 2026 Analysis]]; not re-derived given absent market data; external broker reference: Bahana Buy/Rp1,000 at 6.0x 2026E P/E, Kontan 6 Sep 2026). The 29.4% earnings growth, fortress balance sheet, and upstream-revival positioning justify accumulation on weakness, but the 40.6% receivables build, 76.7% cash conversion, and mix-driven gross-margin compression argue against chasing strength until Q4 demonstrates the unbilled book converting to cash. **Review trigger:** the FY2026 audited cash flow statement — if Q4 collections do not visibly draw down the Rp3.7tn unbilled balance (especially Patra Niaga's Rp2,234,033), downgrade to HOLD/SELL on earnings-quality grounds; conversely, conversion plus a PDSI path would support upgrading to the bull-case range above Rp1,000.
