## 1. Executive Summary

PTBA delivered the cleanest earnings print in its post‑2022 history on the income statement and the messiest on the balance sheet. Nine‑month revenue rose **12.36%** to **Rp35,201,385mn** while cost of revenue was essentially frozen at **Rp27,935,167mn (+0.62%)** — a 1,200 bps price‑versus‑cost spread that more than doubled gross profit to **Rp7,266,218mn (+103.77%)** and lifted gross margin from **11.38% to 20.64% (+926 bps)**. Pre‑tax profit tripled to **Rp5,742,035mn (+212.31%)** and net profit rose **215.55%** to **Rp4,418,156mn**, driving basic EPS to **Rp383** from **Rp121** (+216.5%). The gross‑margin re‑rating is a price‑realisation event: revenue grew 12.4% on a realised ASP uplift management attributes to Newcastle and ICI‑3 strength through September 2026, with volumes actually down year on year, and roughly half the book capped by the US$70/t DMO price for PLN generation — which is precisely why PTBA's ASP capture (+18% per management commentary) trails seaborne indices (+25–26%).

What was not frozen was the working‑capital and funding stack. Trade receivables jumped **47.94%** to **Rp5,821,427mn**, taxes payable **+36.52%** to **Rp1,334,146mn**, accrued expenses **+32.73%** to **Rp5,946,953mn**, and current assets **+31.12%**, pushing working capital to **Rp3,702,728mn** from **Rp1,514,066mn** at year‑end (+144.5%). Cash rose only **8.13%** to **Rp4,889,847mn** despite nine‑month operating cash flow of **Rp4,436,479mn**, because the company simultaneously repaid **Rp3,447,187mn** of borrowings, spent **Rp1,568,925mn** on capex and paid the FY2025 dividend of Rp115/share (total **Rp1,318,481mn**, per the dividends note). Gross loans rose to **Rp4,692,423mn** as the Rp3.56tn syndicated Himbara investment credit was drawn, yet interest expense fell **44.94%** to **Rp136,518mn** — the leverage went up while the cost of it went down.

The cash conversion story is coherent at the aggregate level and unproven at the quarterly one. OCF/net profit is **1.004x** and free cash flow is **Rp2,867,554mn** (FCF margin **8.15%** of revenue, down from a derived 5.60% — actually up on the prior‑year absolute of Rp1,754,852mn). But DSO has widened to **45.3 days** (annualised basis), the cash cycle is **53.0 days**, and OCF grew only **5.54%** against a 216% increase in net profit. That divergence — not the margin — is the analytical question the market has to resolve, and it is the reason this note treats earnings quality as a first‑order risk rather than a footnote.

Valuation cannot be assessed on house numbers: **no market data was supplied for this run** — price, market cap, shares outstanding, P/E, P/B, EV, EV/EBITDA and yield are all **not disclosed** and the ratio block returns null for every valuation multiple. On fundamentals, ROE of **23.63%** (annualised, total equity) and **23.71%** (owners' equity) against a book value of **Rp2,354.48/share** describes a business priced for a mid‑cycle coal tape, not this one. For external context only, sell‑side coverage dated 9 September 2026 (Mirae Asset Sekuritas, "Finding the Higher Gear") carried a Rp3,870 target at 5.8x EV/EBITDA — equivalent to 1.64x the fact‑sheet book value and ~7.6x a derived annualised EPS of Rp511. **Rating: BUY, with a commodity‑price and policy‑channel risk qualifier**, contingent on the tax/working‑capital items in §11 being note‑level timing rather than structural.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Item | 9M 2026 (Rp mn) | 9M 2025 (Rp mn) | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue — consolidated | **35,201,385** | 31,330,296 | **+12.36%** |
| — Coal sales (Note 26) | 34,666,721 | 30,753,028 | +12.73% |
| — Other activities (Note 26) | 534,664 | 577,268 | −7.38% |
| Cost of revenue | (27,935,167) | (27,764,355) | +0.62% |
| Gross profit | **7,266,218** | 3,565,941 | **+103.77%** |

Revenue growth of **12.36%** (ratio block: 0.123557) is the headline, but the composition is the point. Coal sales carried essentially the entire increase — up Rp3,913,693mn — while "other activities" (port, power, services, trading) fell **7.38%**. That is a reversal of the pattern of recent years, in which the non‑coal leg grew faster than coal and cushioned the cyclicality. Coal is now **98.5%** of consolidated revenue (34,666,721/35,201,385), versus 98.2% a year earlier.

Note that the segment disclosure (Note 33) is presented **before eliminations**: segment revenue sums to **Rp39,853,104mn** (Coal 34,666,721 + Others 5,186,383) against consolidated Rp35,201,385mn, a **Rp4,651,719mn** inter‑segment gap. The "Others" segment therefore includes material internal sales into the coal operation (haulage, port handling, power, mining services) that the note 26 "other activities" line does not. Any segment margin comparison in §7 must respect that asymmetry — segment gross profit (Coal 6,994,566 + Others 644,182 = 7,638,748) exceeds consolidated gross profit (7,266,218) by the same elimination logic.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

PTBA's own disclosure and management commentary for the period frame the revenue line as **price, not volume**. Per external management commentary contemporaneous with the release (Bisnis.com, 29 Oct 2026; Kompas, 29 Oct 2026), nine‑month ASP rose ~18% year on year against Newcastle +25% YoY and ICI‑3 +26% YoY, while **sales volume fell ~4% to 32.23m t from 33.70m t**. Production over the same period was **34.4m t**. That volume/price inverse is consistent with the 12.4% revenue growth: roughly +18% price on a −4% volume base ≈ +13%, and the residual FX/timing noise.

The volume split matters for the margin debate. Per the same external coverage, **domestic volume fell to 16.62m t from 18.82m t** while **exports rose to 15.61m t from 14.88m t**, taking the export share of volume to **~48%** (from ~44%). Two consequences follow. First, the mix shift *towards* export improves realised ASP, because the domestic leg is capped: the DMO obligation under Kepmen ESDM 399/2023 is **25% of current‑year production** for public/own electricity and industrial raw material, with the coal price for public electricity supply set at **US$70/t FOB vessel (6,322 kcal/kg GAR reference)**, and ESDM reiterated through February and June 2026 that the cap was unchanged. Second, the *absolute decline* in domestic tonnage (−2.2m t) means the DMO‑capped tranche shrank in absolute terms even as total volume fell — so the blended ASP was helped twice: by higher seaborne prices and by a smaller capped book.

That is also the explanation for the ASP/index gap. With roughly half the book sold domestically at capped benchmarks and half priced off seaborne indices, a 25–26% index move translates into an 18% blended ASP move arithmetically. The margin consequence is that **PTBA's capture of a coal rally is structurally diluted** — it is the price of IUP certainty and a 25% captive domestic demand base. The corresponding benefit is revenue stability: the domestic book does not fall with the seaborne curve on the way down either.

FX is a second‑order but real tailwind. The group does not hedge (Note 35) and states that export sales provide only a "limited natural hedge"; a **10% Rupiah move against the US Dollar would change post‑tax profit by Rp408.9bn** (2025: Rp187.26bn) on the risk‑management disclosure — i.e. FX sensitivity to reported earnings has more than doubled year on year, consistent with the larger export book and larger USD cash balances (USD time deposits at related parties rose to USD 101.4m / Rp1,813,506mn from USD 14.95m / Rp249,964mn; net foreign currency assets Rp5,133,103mn vs Rp2,400,811mn).

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Three composition facts deserve the PM's attention.

**(i) Related‑party concentration is high and falling only slowly.** Related‑party sales were **Rp15,655,059mn, or 44% of consolidated revenue** in 9M 2026, versus **Rp15,603,604mn / 50%** in 9M 2025 (Note 30). The absolute level is flat; the ratio fell only because the denominator grew. Within that, the largest disclosed counterparties are **PLN Grup Rp10,803,166mn** (coal and other activities), **MIND ID Trading Pte Ltd Rp2,789,936mn**, **HBAP Rp1,303,804mn**, **Pusri Rp394,420mn** and **BPI Rp204,170mn**. Customers individually exceeding 10% of revenue aggregate **Rp10,803,166mn** vs Rp10,762,469mn a year earlier — effectively unchanged, and effectively the PLN relationship. This is a state‑owned ecosystem revenue base: low credit risk, low price discovery, high policy sensitivity.

**(ii) Receivables concentration mirrors the revenue concentration.** Of the Rp5,821,427mn net trade receivable, related‑party Rupiah balances were **Rp3,689,628mn** (PLN Grup Rp2,680,748mn; HBAP Rp864,902mn; BPI Rp83,083mn; Pusri Rp31,400mn) plus **Rp596,954mn** of USD receivables from MIND ID Trading. PLN alone is ~46% of net receivables. The note also discloses **>90‑day ageing of Rp269,503mn** against an allowance of **Rp150,994mn**, including a **Rp51,899mn** impairment allowance on the HBAP balance that is unchanged period on period.

**(iii) The geographic mix is now export‑led but the counterparty mix is still domestic‑institutional.** A ~48% export volume share with only ~44% related‑party revenue implies roughly a fifth to a quarter of revenue is genuinely third‑party export (India, Vietnam, Cambodia, Bangladesh per the export mix implied by the export coverage) — the price‑exposed, credit‑exposed slice. That slice is the one that grew, and it is the one that the 2027 export‑channel regime targets.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

**Price.** The external evidence supports the view that Q3 realisation sat inside an elevated H2‑26 plateau rather than on a spike: Newcastle broke **US$150/t on 3 September 2026** (highest since June 2026), HBA for 6,322 GAR rose to **US$126.87/t for the first half of September 2026**, and the benchmark was still **US$152.55/t on 28 October 2026** on El Niño‑driven cooling demand, Indian monsoon supply disruption and higher gas prices (CNBC Indonesia, 4 Sep 2026 and 25 Oct 2026; Bloomberg, 10 Sep 2026; detikFinance, 2 Sep 2026). This is post‑period information and is used only as forward context — it cannot explain in‑period results beyond confirming that the Q3 exit rate was not a one‑off spike.

**Volume.** PTBA's 9M base of 34.4m t produced / 32.23m t sold leaves roughly **15.6m t of sales needed in Q4** to reach the guided **~50m t FY26 target** (management was publicly confident of clearing 50m t as of 8 Oct 2026; RKAB at 53m t; FY25 actual 47.2m t). The external read‑across is that volume, not price, is the Q4 swing factor. The constraint is logistics and stripping, not reserves: the Tanjung Enim–Kramasan corridor (TLS VI & VII, ~93% physical progress at the time of the September financing) plus the Coal Handling Facility and Train Loading Stations is the gating asset for the haulage step‑up.

**Policy — the single largest forecast risk.** Two separate regimes bite from here. MoT Regulation No. 15/2026 (effective 1 September 2026) designates coal a strategic natural resource commodity and requires exports to be conducted **exclusively through PT Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia from 1 January 2027**, with PTBA permitted to export under its current ET status only to **31 December 2026** — the pricing mechanism, fees and margin treatment of that channel are not disclosed in the fact sheet and are the largest unquantified item in any FY27 revenue line. Separately, GR No. 21/2026 (effective 1 June 2026) requires mining exporters to retain **≥30% of DHE SDA in a special FX account for at least three months** with conversion to Rupiah capped at **50%**, layered on GR No. 8/2025's 12‑month 100% placement requirement. These are cash‑flow and channel rules rather than revenue rules, but the 2027 channel change is effectively a transfer‑pricing event on the export book.

**Watch items.** (a) Whether the post‑period relaxation of the 2026 RKAB to ~700m t national output (ESDM, 16–20 Oct 2026) proves to be a 2027 price headwind — sell‑side base cases already assume price normalisation in 2027. (b) Whether the DMO percentage stays at 25% and the US$70/t cap holds; any upward revision to the cap is a direct ASP upgrade, any increase in the percentage is a downgrade. (c) Whether the 2027 spread between HBA/HPB benchmarks and realised prices narrows under Kepmen ESDM 268/2025, which requires sales to reference HPM/HPB even when sold below it and keeps that benchmark as the royalty and tax base.

## 3. Profitability Analysis

### 3.1 Gross Margin Analysis

| Item | 9M 2026 (Rp mn) | 9M 2025 (Rp mn) | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 35,201,385 | 31,330,296 | +12.36% |
| Cost of revenue | (27,935,167) | (27,764,355) | +0.62% |
| **Gross profit** | **7,266,218** | **3,565,941** | **+103.77%** |
| Gross margin | **20.64%** | 11.38% | **+926 bps** |
| COGS / revenue | 79.36% | 88.62% | −926 bps |

The +926 bps gross‑margin expansion (ratio: 0.206419 vs prior 0.113816) is the entire earnings event. Decomposing it: revenue +12.36% on COGS +0.62% means the *incremental* gross margin on the revenue increase was **225%** — mathematically only possible because cost per tonne was flat‑to‑down while price per tonne rose. External segment analysis puts derived unit COGS at roughly Rp867k/t versus Rp824k/t (+~5%) against ASP +18%, a ~13 ppt price/cost spread.

The question the margin raises is *why COGS did not move*. Coal COGS is not a fixed cost: royalties are ad valorem and rise with HPM, fuel is the largest cash cost lever, and mining services scale with overburden. The disclosed cost detail gives a partial answer:

| Cost driver (Note 27 commentary) | 9M 2026 | 9M 2025 | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Mining services | 11,170,915 | 9,828,094 | **+13.66%** (headwind) |
| Third‑party coal purchases | nil | 48,219 | −100% (relief) |
| Depreciation allocated to COGS | 981,011 | 1,077,266 | −8.94% (relief) |
| Amortisation of mining properties | 113,312 | not disclosed | all to COGS |
| Royalties | not disclosed separately in captured lines | not disclosed | **open question** |
| Fuel / BBM | not disclosed separately | not disclosed | **open question** |

So the frozen total conceals a **+13.66% increase in mining services** — the single largest third‑party cost line — offset by the elimination of coal purchasing, lower depreciation, and (on external evidence) a lower stripping ratio in H1‑26 versus H1‑25. Management commentary (Indo Premier via Kontan, 3 Sep 2026) put Q2‑26 cash cost at Rp899k/t, +6% QoQ and +8% YoY, with **fuel +59% QoQ / +54% YoY** and **mining services +16% QoQ / +30% YoY** — i.e. the real cost pressure in H1 was severe and was offset elsewhere. The B50 biodiesel mandate from 1 July 2026 (Bisnis.com, 19 Jun 2026) is an additional identified headwind for H2.

**This is the load‑bearing assumption of the note.** If the royalty line did not escalate with HPM, or if costs were capitalised/deferred into payables and accruals (+25.0% and +32.7% respectively), then part of the Rp3.7tn gross‑profit increase is a timing artefact rather than an economic gain. The fact sheet does not break out the royalty line, so we cannot close this within the note — see §11.1 and §14.2.

### 3.2 Operating Margin Analysis

The fact sheet does not provide a reported **operating profit** line; the ratio block returns `operating_margin: null` with formula `None/35201385.0`. The table below uses a **derived proxy** (gross profit less selling and G&A expense) and is labelled as such throughout.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross profit | 7,266,218 | 20.64% | 3,565,941 | 11.38% | +103.77% |
| Selling & marketing | (634,776) | 1.80% | (559,384) | 1.79% | +13.47% |
| General & administrative | (1,702,555) | 4.84% | (1,511,177) | 4.82% | +12.66% |

Opex discipline is good but not heroic. Selling expense grew **13.47%**, marginally ahead of revenue, and G&A **12.66%**, marginally below — so the opex ratio is essentially unchanged (6.64% of revenue vs 6.61%), and the entire operating margin expansion flows from the gross line. That is the correct read: PTBA did not cut overheads to make the quarter, it monetised price.

Two disclosures support that conclusion with real numbers. **Depreciation allocated to G&A** fell to Rp90,895mn from Rp91,004mn and to **selling** to Rp120,726mn from Rp134,623mn — flat to down, as expected. **Employee benefits expense** rose to **Rp511,481mn from Rp475,050mn**, with the pension component up sharply to Rp143,103mn from Rp13,545mn (a figure the notes themselves flag as inconsistent with a separate Rp8,673mn print) — a reminder that the actuarial line is noisy and non‑cash.

Interest coverage is not computable from a reported operating line (ratio null), but on the derived proxy it is **36.1x** (4,928,887/136,518) versus 6.0x a year earlier (1,495,380/247,932) — a mechanically enormous improvement driven as much by the collapse in finance cost as by the profit rise.

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

| Item | 9M 2026 (Rp mn) | 9M 2025 (Rp mn) | Change |

The finance line flipped from a net cost of Rp90,375mn to a net income of Rp46,984mn — a Rp137,359mn swing that is ~3.5% of pre‑tax profit. The mechanism is visible in Note 28: bank borrowing interest fell from **Rp105,908mn to Rp9,436mn**, and lease interest from **Rp48,285mn to Rp36,678mn**. That is astonishing against a gross loan book that rose 21.6% to Rp4,692,423mn. Three candidate explanations, and the note cannot distinguish between them: (i) refinancing of the 7.15% BRI short‑term facility (Rp1,950,000mn at Dec‑25, nil at Sep‑26) into cheaper DHE‑linked floating lines and the INDONIA+margin syndicated credit; (ii) **capitalisation of borrowing costs**, which the fixed‑asset note shows rising to **Rp102,414mn from Rp7,590mn** at capitalisation rates of 2.40% versus 0.21%; (iii) intra‑year timing of the drawdown. Mechanically, (ii) alone explains roughly three‑quarters of the reduction. This matters for forward margin: capitalised interest becomes depreciation, not expense, so the reported finance‑cost saving is partly a reclassification across the P&L and the balance sheet — and it flatters the net margin by roughly 100–300 bps at the pre‑tax level depending on the split.

Related‑party finance costs collapsed to **Rp9,436mn from Rp105,908mn** (7% of total finance costs vs 43%), with BNI falling to Rp7,846mn from Rp73,052mn and BRI to Rp1,201mn from Rp32,850mn — the state‑bank relationship was repriced, not ended (bank borrowings from related parties rose to **Rp4,211,352mn from Rp3,240,478mn**).

**Other income** of Rp184,856mn (+84.02%) is the least trustworthy line in the P&L. The notes attribute the increase to an **FX gain of Rp116,400mn** (vs Rp57,912mn) and **penalty income of Rp63,543mn** (vs Rp9,888mn), partly offset by a **donation expense of Rp179,720mn** (vs Rp125,326mn). Arithmetic on the quarterly path implies **Q3‑26 contributed only ~Rp23bn** of the Rp184,856mn, i.e. the entire uplift was booked in H1 and the Q3 run rate is *below* the prior‑year quarter by ~29%. Treat the line as non‑recurring.

**JV/associate pickup** is a derived residual (PBT less the disclosed lines) of **Rp581,308mn**, up from Rp333,087mn, and it is real: Note 9a shows HBAP contributing a **share of profit of Rp644,961mn** plus a Rp558,786mn translation gain (carrying value Rp7,915,010mn), while BPI contributed a **share of loss of Rp63,653mn** and paid a Rp37,158mn dividend (carrying value Rp2,896,210mn). HBAP's own summarised results are the driver: revenue **Rp4,646,952mn vs Rp1,722,363mn** and profit **Rp1,504,516mn vs Rp698,088mn**, though the note's HBAP finance‑income line (Rp507mn vs Rp2,742,785mn prior) looks anomalous and should be verified. BPI swung to a loss of Rp106,533mn from a Rp31,712mn profit, driven by "other expenses, net" of Rp394,885mn versus Rp22,076mn — a genuine negative surprise in the power leg that is masked at group level by HBAP's strength.

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

**Receivables — the headline working‑capital event.** Gross trade receivables rose to Rp5,821,427mn net of a **Rp150,994mn** allowance; the **>90‑day bucket is Rp269,503mn** (~4.6% of gross). The related‑party Rupiah balance more than doubled to **Rp3,689,628mn from Rp2,018,134mn**, led by **PLN Grup at Rp2,680,748mn** (from Rp1,225,584mn) and **HBAP at Rp864,902mn** (from Rp579,350mn). The USD balance from **MIND ID Trading was Rp596,954mn**. Against this, third‑party receivables appear to have fallen — the H1 note recorded MIND ID Trading USD receivables collapsing to Rp87,872mn from Rp419,146mn, so the year‑end‑to‑September path includes a re‑build of that specific balance.

The economically correct interpretation is **mix and scale**: a 48% export volume share carries longer documentary/LC tenor than PLN domestic billing, and a Q3 revenue run rate of ~Rp13.2tn (derived) means one month of uncollected sales is ~Rp1.6tn larger than a year ago. The alternative interpretation — collection slippage — is not supported by the aggregate cash data (collections 94.5% of revenue; OCF/net profit 1.004x), but it is not excluded either, and it is the reason §11 ranks receivables as a MODERATE risk with an explicit trigger: **any widening of the >90‑day bucket beyond ~7% of gross or a fall in the collections ratio below 90%**.

**Inventories.** Net inventories rose to **Rp5,524,146mn from Rp4,883,306mn (+13.12%)**, of which **coal inventories were Rp5,388,542mn** against a **Rp262,402mn** NRV/obsolescence allowance. DIO of 43.0 days is a stockpile position, not a demand problem: PTBA builds ROM and product stock ahead of the Q4 run rate and the logistics corridor commissioning. The analytical risk is that a high‑cost inventory layer sits in the balance sheet to be expensed in Q4‑26/FY27 if prices normalise — i.e. the mirror image of the low‑cost‑stockpile benefit that helped H1. Note that inventories are carried at the lower of cost and NRV, and a 5% NRV shortfall on the coal stockpile would be ~Rp269mn — immaterial in the context of Rp4.4tn of net profit, but the *cost layer* effect on future COGS is not immaterial even though it is unquantifiable from the disclosed lines.

**Prepaid taxes — nil from Rp264,487mn.** This is a classification/settlement event, not a write‑off: Note 17a shows total prepaid taxes of **Rp4,406,427mn** (vs Rp3,307,502mn) now **entirely non‑current**, with the current portion at nil versus Rp264,487mn, VAT prepaid at Rp4,331,146mn (vs Rp3,196,130mn) and Article 28A at Rp75,281mn (vs Rp111,372mn). The H1 note had already flagged the reclassification. Economically this is a **Rp264mn‑scale nothing** on a Rp4.4tn profit line, but it is a reminder that the group's tax position is a live balance‑sheet item: taxes payable rose to **Rp1,334,146mn** (Article 29 corporate income tax Rp548,785mn; PBB Rp331,351mn; VAT Rp302,340mn) and there is an extensive **PBB dispute** portfolio (2007, 2012, 2019, 2023 and 2024 assessments; the 2012 judicial review was decided against the company in the period; 2019–2024 remain on appeal) plus a **Rp90,386mn VAT STP for fiscal 2022** in litigation.

**Other working‑capital items.** Accrued expenses rose to **Rp5,946,953mn from Rp4,480,404mn (+32.73%)**, dominated by **mining services Rp2,642,803mn** (from Rp1,333,019mn — nearly doubled), **coal transportation Rp1,059,930mn** and **construction in progress Rp718,963mn**. ### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

**BBK (99.39%) — mining temporarily suspended.** Rp43,418mn of assets; no revenue contribution. Optionality on a restart, immaterial in the near term.

**BPI (59.75%) — the power leg that broke.** Summarised results show a **loss of Rp106,533mn for nine months 2026** versus a Rp31,712mn profit, driven by **other expenses, net of Rp394,885mn** versus Rp22,076mn. The group's share of that loss was Rp63,653mn and it still paid a Rp37,158mn dividend, with carrying value rising only via a Rp171,991mn translation gain. **BPI is the clearest negative surprise in the filing and is not explained by any external source captured.** It is a question mark rather than a write‑down candidate at Rp2,896,210mn of carrying value, but it deserves monitoring: the group holds an impairment policy on associates and states management considers the allowance sufficient.

**BATR (10%) — pre‑operative railway.** Assets Rp47,566mn, liabilities Rp259,328mn, loss Rp174mn (2025: Rp65mn). Effectively a shell awaiting the coal rail corridor's second phase. Immaterial.

**BEI/BEST merger (July 2026)** and the ownership‑column inconsistencies flagged in §4.5. Not material to earnings, but governance‑relevant: a mid‑year restructuring with an unexplained ownership roll‑forward.

**Idle/optional assets:** the coal methane subsidiaries (BAMO, BAME) and BAB remain "not yet operating" at negligible asset values — evidence that the CBM/gasification optionality has not advanced. The Tanjung Enim Industrial Estate (Rp124,853mn CIP, 2.71% complete, completion est. Dec 2039) is a very long‑dated capital commitment.

**HBAP shares pledged.** All of the Company's shares in HBAP (existing and future) are pledged to **China Export‑Import Bank** as collateral for the HBAP project loan (SOE Minister approval 17 May 2018). This is a disclosed constraint on disposal that materially limits the group's ability to monetise its most valuable non‑coal asset.

### 7.4 Growth Options

Four are visible in the disclosures, in descending order of materiality:

1. **The Tanjung Enim–Kramasan logistics corridor.** TLS VI & VII plus the Coal Handling Facility and the KSO PP‑WIKA‑KMK EPC contract (Rp4,493,640mn excluding Rp189,958mn of bored piling; 10% advance Rp449,364mn; effective 24 Oct 2024 to 16 Jun 2027, amendment still under discussion at the report date). External sources put physical progress at ~93% and capacity uplift at ~20m t/yr with cost savings on haulage. This is the volume and unit‑cost lever for FY27 and the reason the syndicated Rp3.56tn facility exists. **Watch: the EPC amendment under discussion means the final cost is not fixed.**
2. **The 25‑year Kramasan coal terminal agreement** with PT Kereta Api Logistik (signed 20 Sep 2024, effective 1 Jul 2025 or operational readiness) — a contracted logistics relationship that converts capex into a rail‑linked throughput asset.
3. **The power JV leg (HBAP/BPI).** HBAP's revenue more than doubled to Rp4,646,952mn with profit Rp1,504,516mn; Sumsel‑8 is now in commercial operation (COD 7 Oct 2023) and is the growth engine inside the "Others" segment. The risk is that HBAP's numbers are dominated by finance costs (Rp1,388,552mn) and a very low reported finance income (Rp507mn vs Rp2,742,785mn prior — an anomaly to verify), making its earnings sensitive to its own leverage.
4. **Downstream/adjacent diversification** (coal methane, hospital, palm, industrial estate) — immaterial today, not a second leg.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

- **Zero net debt on Rp4.69tn of gross borrowings** — net cash of **Rp602,419mn**, with **Rp4.89tn** of cash and **Rp405mn** of liquid FVOCI securities. Liquidity is not a constraint on any plausible downside scenario.
- **Interest coverage of ~36x on a derived operating proxy** and **cash interest of only Rp59,267mn for nine months** — the debt service burden is trivial relative to earnings power.
- **ROE of 23.63% annualised on a 52.84% equity ratio** — a structurally under‑levered balance sheet that still earns an equity return above the cost of capital.
- **Free cash flow of Rp2,867,554mn** covering the FY2025 dividend 2.18x and, on a core basis (~Rp3.4tn after adjusting for the DHE lock‑up and non‑cash capex), covering it ~2.6x.
- **Long‑dated, low‑cost funding now in place**: Rp3.56tn Himbara syndicated credit to 2030 with a 24‑month grace period at a negative pledge rather than a secured claim; all short‑term bank borrowings unsecured.
- **Fully funded pension** (plan assets Rp2,062,611mn vs present value of funded obligation Rp1,948,142mn, even after the asset‑ceiling effect of Rp184,769mn) — unusually strong for an Indonesian SOE; the unfunded healthcare obligation of Rp4,140,351mn is the longer‑dated tail.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- **A genuine margin re‑rating**: +926 bps of gross margin and +103.77% gross profit on +12.36% revenue, achieved with operating expenses growing in line with revenue. There was no cost‑cutting sleight of hand in the overhead lines.
- **Contract book depth**: PLTU Sumsel 8 to 2048 (US$31.40/t), PLTU Banjarsari to 2044 (US$39.39/t), the 10,000 MW Phase I programme at Rp811,721–841,416 to 2032 (Rp6,566bn), plus KAI coal‑delivery agreements (Tanjung Enim–Tarahan Rp4,724bn to Dec 2027, Kertapati Rp776bn, others) — a multi‑year volume floor that most coal peers lack.
- **DMO compliance as a moat**: 25% of production allocated to domestic electricity under Kepmen ESDM 399/2023 gives PTBA a captive demand base and a regulatory licence to operate, at the cost of a US$70/t price cap on that tranche.
- **Logistics vertical integration**: the Tanjung Enim–Kramasan corridor (TLS VI & VII transferred out of CIP, ~93% physical progress externally) plus the 25‑year Kramasan terminal agreement with PT Kereta Api Logistik and controlled port subsidiaries (PBP, PIT, BPB) — the cost lever for FY27 unit economics.
- **Workforce stability**: 1,648 permanent employees (from 1,679) — essentially flat headcount against a materially higher output run rate, i.e. positive operating leverage.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- **State ownership and IUP certainty**: 65.93% held by MIND ID, wholly‑owned by the Government of Indonesia; **10‑year IUP extension rights** under GR No. 25/2024 with a minimum 30% non‑dilutable PTBA ownership condition — a reserve runway that is policy‑anchored, not geology‑limited.
- **A power leg that is contracted, not merchant**: HBAP (45%) and BPI (59.75%) hold PPAs with PLN. HBAP is now the second engine — revenue Rp4,646,952mn and profit Rp1,504,516mn in nine months, contributing Rp644,961mn of equity‑accounted profit plus a Rp558,786mn translation gain.
- **Optionality that is paid for**: BBK suspension, coal methane (BAMO/BAME), the Tanjung Enim Industrial Estate and BATR rail are all cheap options on a future beyond thermal coal.
- **Pillar 2 shield**: the Transitional Safe Harbour under PMK 136/2024 means no top‑up tax for the period — a structural advantage over peers with foreign parentage.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — Earnings quality: flat COGS on a 12% revenue increase, with royalties not separately disclosed.** COGS +0.62% against revenue +12.36% and mining‑services cost +13.66% is only reconcilable if the royalty line, fuel and inventory cost layers moved favourably or if cost recognition lagged. Trade payables +25.00% and accrued expenses +32.73% (with mining‑services accruals nearly doubling to Rp2,642,803mn) are the places where a cut‑off effect would hide. **If even 3% of the Rp3.70tn gross‑profit increase is a timing artefact, that is ~Rp111mn of pre‑tax profit — small; if the royalty rate did not step up with HPM as it should have, the effect could be an order of magnitude larger.** The fact sheet does not break out royalties, so this cannot be closed here. **Trigger: the FY2026 audited accounts.**

**HIGH — The 2027 export‑channel regime (MoT Reg. 15/2026).** From **1 January 2027** coal exports must run exclusively through **PT Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia**; PTBA's direct export window ends **31 December 2026**. The pricing mechanism, fees and margin allocation of that channel are not disclosed. Given a ~48% export volume share, this is a direct hit to the most price‑exposed half of the book. This is the largest single unquantified risk to FY27 earnings and is the main reason the growth score is capped.

**MODERATE — Receivables build (+47.94% to Rp5,821,427mn).** DSO 45.3 days, >90‑day bucket Rp269,503mn, PLN Grup Rp2,680,748mn. Related‑party composition mitigates credit risk but concentrates collection timing in a state utility's payment cycle. **Trigger: >90‑day ageing above ~7% of gross, or the collections ratio falling below 90%.**

**MODERATE — The Q3 effective tax rate.** Derived Q3 ETR of ~28.2% versus ~19.2% in Q2‑26 — unexplained in the captured notes. If this is a structural change (loss of a facility, a non‑deductible item, a DMO/royalty adjustment) rather than a timing true‑up, FY26 EPS is ~5–8% lower than the 9M annualisation implies. **Trigger: the tax note in the audited accounts.**

**MODERATE — Stripping ratio trajectory.** The nine‑month stripping ratio of **5.59x** against Q1 5.3x and Q2 5.2x implies a **Q3 ratio of roughly 6.2x** (derived) — overburden removal stepping up to chase the 50m t target. Higher strip ratio is a leading indicator of higher cash cost per tonne in Q4‑26 and FY27. **This is the mechanism by which the "frozen cost base" unfreezes.**

**MODERATE — DHE SDA liquidity lock‑up.** Time deposits >3 months rose to **Rp1,819,506mn** from Rp15,000mn (including USD 1,813,506mn placed under GR 21/2026). Combined with GR 8/2025's 12‑month 100% placement requirement, a structurally larger share of cash is unavailable for dividends, capex or debt service at any given moment. This is why the net cash cushion narrowed despite record profit.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**MODERATE — Thermal coal demand decline in export markets.** The long‑run thesis risk, and the reason a terminal‑value‑sensitive DCF is unkind to any coal producer. PTBA's mitigants (domestic DMO, power JVs, 10‑year IUP) are real but partial.

**MODERATE — Equity‑accounted dependence on two power plants.** 21.0% of assets are JVs, and **BPI swung to a Rp106,533mn loss from a Rp31,712mn profit**, driven by "other expenses, net" of Rp394,885mn. The group's share was a Rp63,653mn loss. HBAP's profit is large but its own finance costs (Rp1,388,552mn) mean it is a leveraged asset whose earnings are sensitive to its own debt terms — and **all PTBA shares in HBAP are pledged to China Export‑Import Bank**, limiting any disposal.

**MODERATE — Intercompany/related‑party intensity.** 44% of revenue, 33% of COGS+opex, 24% of assets and 28% of liabilities are with related parties, with Rp4,651,719mn of inter‑segment revenue eliminated on consolidation and material allocations between consolidated subsidiaries and equity‑accounted JVs. Where the margin lands — coal segment or "others" — is partly a policy choice.

**LOW — Unfunded post‑employment healthcare.** Present value of obligation Rp4,140,351mn (18.79‑year duration), unfunded, with a **medical cost trend of 11.50%** and sensitivity of **+Rp904bn for a 1% trend increase**. This is a long‑dated, slowly compounding liability that is larger than the entire pension obligation. It does not threaten solvency (4.6x covered by cash) but it is a real call on future cash.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

**HIGH — Coal price mean reversion.** The entire earnings step‑up rests on a realised ASP that rose ~18% YoY. Sell‑side base cases already assume 2027 normalisation, and the post‑period relaxation of the 2026 RKAB to ~700m t national output (ESDM, 16–20 Oct 2026) is a supply‑side signal in the wrong direction for price. A 10% fall in realised ASP on an unchanged cost base would reduce revenue by ~Rp3.5tn and, at ~80% flow‑through to gross profit, would remove roughly half the gross‑profit uplift.

**MODERATE — DMO policy.** The 25% obligation at US$70/t is set by Kepmen ESDM 399/2023. A higher percentage, a lower cap, or an extension of the cap to industrial users beyond cement/fertiliser would compress blended ASP directly.

**MODERATE — Royalty and benchmark regime.** Kepmen ESDM 268/2025 (effective 8 Aug 2025) revoked Kepmen ESDM 72/2025 and requires sales to reference HPM/HPB even when sold below it, and that benchmark remains the royalty and tax base. This is a mechanism for royalty expense to rise independently of realised price — precisely the risk embedded in the flat‑COGS puzzle.

**MODERATE — Licensing and mining‑services rules.** GR No. 39/2025 amends PP 96/2021 and PP 25/2024 to prohibit subsidiaries/affiliates from providing mining services without ministerial approval, with sanctions up to permit revocation; MEMR Decree 365.K/MB.01/MEM.B/2026 requires IUP holders to prioritise local mining‑service companies and obtain approval before engaging affiliates. **This directly touches SBS (95%, Rp2,904,498mn of assets) and the Pamapersada relationship.** Not currently a financial event; potentially a contractual one.

**MODERATE — Tax disputes.** The PBB dispute table covers assessment years 2007 through 2024 with amounts including 2019: Rp214,737mn and Rp47,932mn; 2023: Rp187,030mn and Rp37,928mn; 2024: Rp334,603mn and Rp44,504mn; plus a Rp90,386mn VAT STP for 2022 in litigation and a Rp38,600mn PPh 23 assessment (judicial review rejected) and Rp13,882mn PPh badan 2017 on appeal. Individual amounts are manageable against a Rp27.2tn equity base; the aggregate and the direction of travel (the 2012 review was decided against the company in the period) are not trivial.

**LOW — Currency.** A 10% Rupiah move changes post‑tax profit by **Rp408.9bn** (2025: Rp187.26bn). The group does not hedge. Export revenue is a partial natural hedge, but the DHE retention rules force USD balances to be held onshore, increasing translation sensitivity.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

**HIGH — Valuation cannot be assessed.** No market data was supplied. There is no house P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, market cap or yield in this note, and the ratio block returns null for all of them. Any statement about whether PTBA is cheap or expensive must be read as external context (Mirae's Rp3,870 target, 9 Sep 2026, at 5.8x EV/EBITDA) or as a derived cross‑check (1.64x implied P/B on a canonical book value of Rp2,354.48/share). **This is a data limitation, not an analytical choice, and it is the single largest gap in the note.**

**MODERATE — Peak‑earnings multiple risk.** A 23.6% ROE and Rp383 of nine‑month EPS are cycle‑elevated. Applying a mid‑cycle multiple to peak earnings overstates value, and applying a peak multiple to peak earnings doubly so. The correct frame is normalised earnings — which the note cannot compute without a royalty disclosure.

**MODERATE — Dividend policy uncertainty.** FY2025's Rp115/share was a 65.4% cut from Rp332. With 9M cover of 2.18x on FCF and 3.35x on net profit, a restoration is arithmetically available, but the state‑ownership dynamic, the syndicated loan amortisation from FY27 and the 2027 export channel all argue for conservatism. A market pricing in a 12% yield (external) is pricing a policy that has not yet been declared.

## 12. Management and Governance

### 12.1 Leadership

The fact sheet does not capture the names or composition of the Board of Directors or Board of Commissioners; **key management identities are not disclosed in the materials available for this note.** What is disclosed is compensation: the **Board of Directors received Rp25,570mn** (1.30% of employee expense), the **Board of Commissioners Rp12,318mn** (0.63%) and **other key management personnel Rp5,017mn** (0.26%) — total Rp42,905mn, versus Rp39,238mn in the prior year (+9.3%). That is modest relative to a Rp4.4tn profit pool and consistent with SOE remuneration norms.

The controlling shareholder is **PT Mineral Industri Indonesia (Persero) (MIND ID)**, holding **65.93%** of the 11,520,659,250 issued shares — an entity wholly owned, directly and/or indirectly, by the Government of Indonesia. The state‑ownership chain is the defining governance feature: it underwrites the DMO obligation, the domestic contract book and the IUP runway, and it also constrains pricing, dividend and M&A decisions.

### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary

Actions disclosed in the period, in order of financial significance:

1. **Refinancing and new investment credit.** The Rp1,950,000mn 7.15% BRI short‑term facility was fully repaid (nil at 30 Sep 2026) and replaced by **Rp750,000mn each of BRI and Mandiri DHE facilities** plus a drawn **Rp2,681,521mn** syndicated Mandiri/BRI/BNI investment credit. Finance costs fell 44.94% and interest paid fell 61.56%. **This was executed well** — the terming‑out of short‑term debt into a 5‑year facility with a 24‑month grace period at a lower cost is exactly the right move ahead of the corridor commissioning.
2. **Capex discipline.** Cash capex cut 35.93% to Rp1,568,925mn while the corridor was completed — the heavy spend was behind, not deferred, which the CIP transfer of TLS VI & VII confirms.
3. **Dividend payment.** FY2025's Rp115/share (Rp1,318,481mn) paid 10 July 2026 on schedule, with the balance net of tax recorded as dividend payable at 30 Sep 2026.
4. **Group simplification.** The **BEI/BEST merger completed July 2026**; BAM's group ownership rose to 99.04%.
5. **External guidance.** Management publicly guided to ~50m t FY26 production (8 Oct 2026), described 2026 as an operational recovery, and named fuel price, stripping ratio and overburden management as the three cost levers it cannot fully control (14 Sep 2026). It secured a Rp3.56tn Himbara term sheet in early September 2026 explicitly earmarked for strategic projects.

### 12.3 Governance Observations

**Positive.** Clean audit trail on the interim statements (authorised 27 October 2026); a full set of covenant‑compliance and related‑party‑relationship disclosures; the pension is funded; **no Pillar 2 top‑up tax** because Indonesia legislated the Transitional Safe Harbour; a stated intention to adopt **PSAK 118** when effective (periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027) with the impact still being assessed; and a substantially expanded related‑party note (Note 30) that reports sales, purchases, finance income/costs, assets and liabilities against percentages of the consolidated totals — genuinely better disclosure than most IDX mid‑caps.

**Items to monitor.** (i) The note numbering is misaligned in places (a chunk header referencing note 2 "Penerapan Denda dan Kompensasi" maps to Note 29 subsections e–h), and several tables have docling artefacts (the fixed‑assets accumulated‑depreciation "Jumlah" row split across two rows, the healthcare remeasurement block mislabelled, the lease counterparty mapping reversed between PT Andalan Artha Prima and PT Rifansi Dwi Putra). These are **conversion artefacts, not necessarily filing errors**, and none changes a material number — but they mean the reader should verify any single line against the PDF before relying on it. (ii) The **BEI/BEST ownership roll‑forward** does not reconcile between the direct and group columns. (iii) A **negative opening NCI balance** of Rp(4,463)mn for "Others (each below 5%)" should be verified. (iv) The **prior‑year pension expense** appears as Rp8,673mn in one table and Rp13,545mn in another. (v) Related‑party services of **Rp3,685,372mn to Pertamina** appear twice with identical amounts in two different categories.

**Structural governance point.** Related‑party sales of 44% of revenue, purchases of 33% of COGS+opex, and 28% of liabilities sitting with related parties — in a 65.93% state‑owned company with price caps, benchmark royalties and a mandated 2027 export channel — means a substantial share of PTBA's economic outcome is **administered rather than negotiated**. The 2018 SOE Minister approval to pledge all HBAP shares to China Exim is a further reminder that the controlling shareholder's strategic priorities can bind the listed entity's asset‑level flexibility.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

**Retained for growth and dividend, in that order, with a bias to deleveraging avoided.** In nine months of 2026: **Rp4.44tn** of operating cash flow was deployed into **Rp3.45tn** of debt repayment, **Rp1.57tn** of cash capex (plus ~Rp1.14tn of non‑cash fixed‑asset additions) and a **Rp1.32tn** dividend, leaving cash up only Rp238mn. The cash drawdown from 30 June (Rp1.99tn) is fully explained by those three items plus the DHE lock‑up.

Judged on returns, the capital allocation is **adequate but not outstanding**: ROE of 23.63% is above the cost of equity, FCF covers the dividend 2.18x, and the largest new commitment (the Rp3.56tn syndicated loan at INDONIA+margin against a corridor that unlocks ~20m t/yr of haulage) is a sensible use of a low‑cost, long‑dated facility at a moment when the equity is earning peak returns. Judged on disclosure, it is **thin**: the note cannot state the FY25 net profit (so the payout ratio is not computable), cannot state FY26 capex guidance, and cannot state whether a buyback is contemplated — despite the 6,302,000 treasury shares that must be transferred by 31 March 2029.

The one capital‑allocation decision that looks questionable in hindsight is **BPI**, where a Rp394,885mn "other expense, net" turned a Rp31,712mn profit into a Rp106,533mn loss. That is a JV‑level event PTBA does not control, which is the structural cost of keeping the power leg equity‑accounted.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 8/10

**Justification.** Net cash of Rp602,419mn, current ratio 1.247x, equity ratio 52.84%, ROE 23.63%, cash interest of Rp59,267mn and 36x derived interest coverage are the metrics of a balance sheet with essentially no financial distress risk at any point in the coal cycle. Points are deducted, not added, for: net cash halving to Rp602,419mn from Rp1,118,961mn; gross loans +21.65%; a 53‑day cash cycle with receivables +47.94%; a quick ratio of 0.878x (inventory‑dependent liquidity); an unfunded healthcare obligation of Rp4,140,351mn; and the absence of a reported operating‑profit line, which prevents the standard coverage metrics from being computed at all.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 8/10

**Justification.** A 926 bps gross‑margin expansion and 103.77% gross‑profit growth on 12.36% revenue growth, with opex held to revenue growth and headcount flat, is a genuinely strong operating performance. The deduction is for three things: **volume fell ~4%** (price did the work, not throughput); the **stripping ratio rose to 5.59x** with the derived Q3 print near 6.2x, i.e. the fourth quarter will be bought with overburden; and the **flat COGS is not yet explained** — mining services rose 13.66% and fuel rose sharply per external commentary, so the net zero implies offsets that are either favourable (inventory layers, strip ratio in H1) or non‑economic (cost timing, capitalisation). An 8 reflects excellent output with unresolved input questions.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 7/10

**Justification.** EPS +216.53%, net profit +215.55%, gross profit +103.77% — extraordinary headline growth. But it is **cyclical and price‑led**, with volumes down, and the two structural growth levers are constrained: the corridor is a **cost/volume enabler, not a new revenue pool**, and the **2027 export‑channel regime is a growth risk, not a growth option**. On the positive side, HBAP's revenue more than doubled to Rp4,646,952mn and its profit to Rp1,504,516mn, giving a genuine second engine; the external volume path to 50m t FY26 is credible (15.6m t needed in Q4 against an August run rate of ~5m t/month); and IUP certainty to a 10‑year horizon is a growth enabler that pure‑play peers lack. A 7 reflects real forward growth optionality that is only partly under management's control.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 7/10 (provisional — market data not supplied)

**Justification.** The score is necessarily provisional because **no share price, market capitalisation or multiple was provided for this run**. On the only valuation anchors available: a book value of **Rp2,354.48/share** against a 23.63% annualised ROE implies that a 1.0x P/B price would capitalise the equity at ~7.9x a derived annualised EPS — i.e. **the book value itself is a demanding price unless ROE normalises sharply downward.** The external sell‑side marker (Rp3,870, 9 Sep 2026, 5.8x EV/EBITDA) implies **1.64x P/B and ~7.6x annualised EPS**, which is fair‑to‑modestly‑cheap for a 23% ROE net‑cash producer with 2.2x FCF dividend cover. Peer context (pipeline database, latest available periods — not contemporaneous) brackets P/B from 0.53x (ABMM) to 12.83x (BYAN) with AADI at 0.86x, ADRO at 0.62x, BSSR at 2.08x and ADMR at 2.31x, on ROEs of 8.8%–34.9%. A mid‑to‑upper‑range multiple on above‑median ROE and below‑median leverage is defensible: **7/10, provisional, and to be re‑scored once market data is available.**

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

**The bull case is that PTBA has re‑rated from a low‑margin, price‑taker utility proxy into a 20%+ gross‑margin, net‑cash, dividend‑restoring coal producer with a contracted power leg — and that the market has not yet repriced it.**

The evidence: 9M gross margin of **20.64%** versus **11.38%**, gross profit **+103.77%**, net profit **+215.55%**, EPS **Rp383 versus Rp121**. The cost base is not just frozen but structurally improving: mining‑services expense grew 13.66% against a 12.36% revenue increase because the strip ratio in H1 was below the prior year's rain‑disrupted 6.0x, and the Tanjung Enim–Kramasan corridor — now transferred out of CIP — removes haulage bottlenecks and unit cost beginning in Q4‑26. Volume guidance of **~50m t** for FY26 requires 15.6m t in Q4 against an August run rate already at ~5m t/month, i.e. the volume bridge is convergence, not a miracle. Free cash flow of **Rp2,867,554mn** covers the FY2025 dividend **2.18x** despite a 36% capex reduction, so a dividend restoration from Rp115 toward the FY2024 Rp332 is arithmetically available. The balance sheet is **net cash** with 36x derived interest coverage and a pension that is fully funded.

The margin‑of‑safety argument is that the equity is being valued on a cycle it has already left. A **1.64x P/B on a 23.63% ROE** (external target price versus canonical book) is the kind of multiple normally reserved for a mid‑cycle, no‑growth coal producer — not one with HBAP contributing Rp1.5tn of profit inside an equity‑accounted leg, a 10‑year IUP runway, and a contracted domestic book to 2032. External sell‑side coverage responded to the Q3 print by raising FY26F/FY27F EPS by **94%/64%** (Mirae, 9 Sep 2026) — the direction of estimate revisions is the bull case's most powerful argument.

### 14.2 Bear Case

**The bear case is that this was a price event with a tax and working‑capital tail, in a business whose export channel is being nationalised in ten weeks' time.**

The sequencing: revenue +12.36% was **entirely price** — volumes fell ~4% to 32.23m t, and roughly half the book is capped at **US$70/t** by the DMO, which is why ASP captured only ~18% of a 25–26% index move. The coal tape that delivered it is post‑period already at US$152–155/t but sell‑side base cases assume **2027 normalisation**, and the government relaxed the 2026 RKAB to ~700m t national output in mid‑October — a supply response that helps PTBA's tonnage and hurts its price simultaneously. Meanwhile **COGS rose only 0.62%** while mining services rose 13.66%, fuel rose sharply per external commentary, and accruals for mining services nearly doubled to Rp2,642,803mn. Either the royalty line did not escalate with HPM as the ad valorem mechanism implies, or something was deferred. **The note cannot prove it either way, and that is the point: the single largest driver of the earnings is the single least disclosed cost line.**

Then the quality signals. Net profit **+215.55%** against operating cash flow **+5.54%**. Cash conversion of **1.004x** versus **3.002x**. Receivables **+47.94%**, DSO 45.3 days, collections ratio down 459 bps to 94.54%. Net cash **halved** to Rp602,419mn with gross loans **+21.65%**. **Rp1.82tn** of cash immobilised in DHE SDA deposits. A **Q3 ETR of ~28.2%** versus ~19.2% in Q2 that nobody has explained. And on 1 January 2027 the export book — ~48% of volume, and the price‑exposed half — must be routed through **PT Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia**, at terms that are not disclosed.

Valuation conclusion for the bear: at 1.64x book on peak returns, PTBA is priced for the ROE to hold. If ROE reverts to 12–14% (FY2025‑like, which the 9M25 gross margin of 11.38% illustrates is entirely possible), a 1.64x P/B is expensive and the stock de‑rates on both numerator and multiple.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

**Positive (next 3–9 months):**
1. **FY2026 audited accounts** — resolution of the royalty, tax and accrual questions; the single largest information event.
2. **Q4 volume delivery toward 50m t** — the 15.6m t bridge; monthly production disclosures.
3. **Tanjung Enim–Kramasan commissioning confirmation** and any disclosed haulage cost saving / capacity addition (~20m t/yr externally cited).
4. **Dividend announcement for FY2026** — a restoration above Rp115/share is the clearest re‑rating trigger, with 2.18x FCF cover.
5. **Coal price persistence** — Newcastle held US$152–155/t through late October 2026; HBA 6,322 GAR at US$126.87/t for early September.
6. **Estimate revisions** — post‑Q3 consensus resets (external precedent: FY26F/27F EPS +94%/+64% at Mirae, 9 Sep 2026).
7. **Clarity on the Danantara export channel's pricing and fees** — a benign mechanism would remove the largest single overhang.

**Negative:**
1. **2027 export‑channel transition terms** — punitive fees or price pass‑through would directly hit realisation.
2. **Coal price normalisation** — the 2027 base case in most models.
3. **Higher strip ratio flowing into Q4/FY27 unit costs.**
4. **A structural tax rate step‑up** (if the Q3 ETR proves not to be timing).
5. **Receivables/ageing deterioration** or a collections‑ratio break below 90%.
6. **BPI‑style JV losses** repeating or HBAP earnings weakening as its own finance costs bite.
7. **RKAB supply response** translating into price weakness in 2027.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**BUY — risk qualifier: commodity‑price and policy‑channel risk (HIGH).**

The rating rests on four things that are visible in the canonical data: a **20.64%** gross margin delivering **Rp4,418,156mn** of net profit and **Rp383** of nine‑month EPS; a **net cash** balance sheet with 36x derived interest coverage and a fully funded pension; **free cash flow of Rp2,867,554mn** covering the FY2025 dividend 2.18x; and a second engine in HBAP contributing Rp644,961mn of equity‑accounted profit plus translation. Against that, the note carries four unresolved items that could each reduce FY26 EPS by 5–10% and one — the 2027 export channel — that could reduce FY27 more than that.

**Target price range: Rp3,400–3,900 per share — provisional.** This is not a house target derived from our own market data (none was supplied). It is anchored as follows: the upper bound is the external sell‑side target of **Rp3,870** (Mirae Asset Sekuritas, 9 Sep 2026, 5.8x EV/EBITDA, pre‑Q3); the lower bound corresponds to **~1.45x** the canonical book value of **Rp2,354.48/share**, which on a derived annualised EPS of ~Rp510.7 is ~6.7x — i.e. the low end already assumes some ROE normalisation. At the midpoint, the implied multiple is ~1.55x P/B and ~7.2x annualised EPS. **This range must be re‑based against an actual market quote before any position is sized**, and it will be structurally wrong if the 2027 export channel materially changes realised ASP.

**Positioning notes.** (i) The thesis is a **cash‑return and normalisation thesis**, not a coal‑price bull thesis; it works if Q4 volume lands and the dividend is restored. (ii) The appropriate stop‑loss is fundamental, not technical: **a collections ratio below 90%, a >90‑day ageing bucket above ~7% of gross receivables, or an FY26 ETR materially above 25%** would each force a re‑rating of the earnings quality and a move to HOLD. (iii) The 2027 policy calendar is binary — reduce exposure ahead of terms disclosure if the mechanism appears punitive.
