# PT Indo Tambangraya Megah Tbk (ITMG) — Q2 2026
---

**Reporting basis.** This analysis covers the interim (six-month, 6M26) consolidated financial statements for the period ended 30 June 2026, authorised for issue by the Board of Directors on 10 August 2026, and compared against 6M25 for income-statement and cash-flow lines and against the prior fiscal year-end (31 December 2025) for balance-sheet lines. All figures are in **thousands of US Dollars** (the Group's functional and presentation currency) unless otherwise stated. Dividend per-share amounts are full US dollars. The statements are **unaudited interim** statements. Where a "Q2 2026 standalone" figure is quoted, it is derived (6M26 minus Q1 2026) and flagged as such; the "Q2 2026" filing itself is the six-month report. The ultimate controlling entity is Banpu Public Company Limited (Thailand); the direct parent is Banpu Minerals (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

ITMG delivered a strong first half of 2026: net revenue of US$1,000.5m (6M26) versus US$919.4m (6M25), up **+8.8%**, gross profit up **+14.1%** to US$256.5m with gross margin up ~120bps to **25.64%**, and net profit up **+16.5%** to US$109.5m (US$106.0m attributable to owners), an annualized return on equity of **23.0%**. Basic EPS was **US$0.10 versus US$0.08** in 6M25 (+25% on the reported basis; the pipeline's +124,900% "growth" flag is a near-zero/mis-scaled prior-period EPS value in the XLSX parse — an artifact, not an event). The margin expansion was operationally led: a mix shift toward own-mine coal (+10.5% own-coal volumes per management, external sources) and away from thin-margin third-party trading (-18-20%), plus a modest ASP gain (+~4% to ~US$81/t realised, versus HBA benchmarks up 21-36% yoy), with the regulated DMO mix capping price transmission. The effective tax rate was ~31.0% of pretax (the ratio table's -0.31 is a sign artifact), i.e. no tax-led margin boost, and external analysis (Aldiracita) confirms no disposal-gain support.

The balance sheet remains exceptionally conservative — and increasingly *trapped*. Cash fell to US$737.6m from US$807.9m at FYE25, but the decline is not operating decay: it reflects a **US$169m collapse in tax-refund (restitusi) receipts** (per management's 6M26 news release) that drove operating cash flow down **-77.8%** to US$62.4m, alongside US$42.8m of loan repayments, US$64.5m of dividends, and US$26.6m of share buybacks. Meanwhile prepaid taxes rose +97% to US$55.7m (current) and restricted cash rose +77% to US$85.8m, the latter driven by Kepmen ESDM 344/2025's requirement to replace reclamation bank guarantees with time deposits. Add PP 21/2026's DHE SDA export-proceeds retention rules (effective 1 June 2026) and the Group's cash is now subject to a three-way regulatory claim even though it is debt-light: total bank debt is only ~US$18.4m carrying value after US$17.9m of Permata term-loan repayments, loans-to-equity is **0.05x** (including lease-financing lines within the parsed loans fields), and net gearing is reported negative (-37% per management). Current ratio is a fortress **3.46x**.

Valuation: **no market data was provided** in this run, so P/E, P/BV, EV/EBITDA and dividend-yield multiples are **not disclosed** here rather than estimated. As cross-sectional context only, peer Alamtri Resources Indonesia (ADRO) traded at **P/E 5.65x, P/BV 0.62x, ROE 10.9%, D/E 0.28** at its latest period in the pipeline DB (31 Oct 2025, per pipeline DB) — indicative of the deep-discount band in which large Indonesian thermal-coal producers trade; ITMG's higher ROE and negative net gearing would argue for the upper end of that band, but no current ITMG price data is available to quantify it.

Net assessment: high-ROE, near-debt-free, buyback-active coal producer with improving realized margins, carrying a cash-conversion timing problem (restitusi) and a structurally less liquid balance sheet. **Recommendation: BUY (medium-risk qualifier)** — see §14 for the thesis, target framing, catalysts and review triggers.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Line (US$'000) | 6M26 | 6M25 | % Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net revenue | 1,000,491 | 919,416 | **+8.8%** |

Revenue grew +8.8% on a six-month basis. Per the news release and external coverage (Katadata, 2026-08-11), the growth was driven by +5% sales volume (~12.3 Mt total) and +~4% ASP (~US$81/t realized vs ~US$78/t in 6M25), with own-coal sales up +11% (10.5 Mt) and third-party trading volumes down -18-20% (to ~1.8 Mt). Note 24 splits 6M26 revenue into coal sales to third parties US$963.9m, coal sales to related parties US$36.8m, and services US$1.6m. The coal-swap MTM line moved from a US$9.2m gain (6M25) to a US$1.8m loss (6M26), already netted in these figures per the fact sheet's P&L mapping.

The pricing picture is a story of **muted transmission**: benchmarks rose 21-36% yoy, yet ITMG's ASP rose only ~4%. This is a durability point: ITMG's realized price will under-react both to rallies and to slumps, supporting revenue stability at the cost of upside torque.

External sourcing: the volume/ASP attribution is management's own disclosure, corroborated by Katadata (2026-08-11), KB Valbury (2026-08-31) and the ITMG public expose (2026-09-04); treat unit-volume splits as management-attributed (high confidence, but not from the fact sheet).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

External, in-period evidence supports 6M26 revenue quality: benchmarks were genuinely higher (HBA 6,322 kcal US$121.83-123.91/t June 2026 vs US$100.97/t June 2025; Newcastle +36% yoy in Q2-26). Post-period, however, the price tailwind is cooling: Newcastle averaged ~US$131/t in August 2026 (IEA Coal Mid-Year Update, 2026-09) versus ~US$139/t in June — the external read is that **2H26 earnings hinge on volume, not price** (KB Valbury: 3Q26 target 5.9 Mt, FY26 RKAB 23.0 Mt, FY26 output 22.0-22.9 Mt).

Structural runway: the permit estate is long-dated — IMM (PKP2B/CCoW to 31 Mar 2028), TCM (to 27 Feb 2035), JBG (to 3 May 2035), BEK (to 29 Jun 2041), TIS (IUP to 11 Apr 2029), NPR (IUP to 20 May 2033), GPK (IUP to 14 Sep 2029) — with BEK and TCM providing the longest-dated core. No reserve/production tables were extracted in this run, so reserve life metrics are **not disclosed** here.

Watch items: (1) PP 24/2026 progressive coal-export channelling via BUMN Ekspor (transition ending no later than 31 December 2026) — Group believes it is compliant, but the regime could add export friction from 2027; (2) MoEMR 6/2026 (effective 12 June 2026) adds prior approval for coal blending and new quarterly reporting — management assessed no material impact; (3) DMO shortfall accruals of US$3.8m (2025 compensation) and US$0.9m (2026) remain a small, recurring regulatory cost.

Gross margin expanded ~120bps. Treat these Q2-standalone numbers as derived/secondary.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
- Coal-price benchmarks rose 21-36% yoy in the half and ITMG captured even a muted slice; own-coal mix shift (+11%) mechanically lifted margins and can persist as trading volumes stay de-emphasised.
- Near-debt-free balance sheet with ~23% ROE and heavy capital return (dividends + buybacks > capex by 3x); buyback at up to 10% of capital provides per-share accretion and a soft price floor.
- The OCF collapse is a refund-timing artifact (US$169m) that reverses as DJP processes refunds — a recovery catalyst with no earnings impairment attached; US$249.8m of prepaid taxes is a recoverable asset.
- Volume runway into 2H26 (5.9 Mt 3Q target, 22-23 Mt FY26) with permits to 2041 at BEK.

### 14.2 Bear Case
- The price tailwind is fading (Newcastle ~US$131/t in August vs ~US$139/t in June); 2Q26 standalone gross margin already compressed ~2.1pp qoq on fuel (~US$1.15/litre) and a heavier DMO mix — 2H26 EPS could fall qoq.
- The trapped-cash complex deepens: DHE SDA 100%/12-month retention (from 1 Jun 2026), permanent reclamation deposits, US$249.8m prepaid taxes — dividend capacity narrows even as earnings hold.
- TCM royalty audits (FY2018-2023) with no provision; a large assessment would hit the core subsidiary directly.
- Regulatory ratchet: DMO mix rising, PP 24/2026 BUMN Ekspor channelling from 2027, Pillar Two beyond the CbCR safe harbour — the state is extracting more of the cash flow each year.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. **DJP refund normalization** — recovery of the ~US$169m refund gap would restore OCF toward prior-year run-rate and validate the timing thesis (watch 6M26 balance-sheet prepaid-tax line in 2H).
2. **3Q26 volume delivery** — 5.9 Mt target per external guidance; volume is the 2H26 EPS driver as price cools.
3. **Buyback continuation** — 0.9% of shares repurchased post-period; programme capacity to ~10% of capital.
4. **Resolution of TCM royalty audits** — either clean closure or quantified provision removes the largest contingent overhang.
5. **Newcastle price path** — any re-acceleration toward US$140/t restores the price leg given ~67% indexed volumes.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**BUY (medium-risk qualifier).** Fundamentals-for-price: a 23%-ROE, net-cash, permit-secure thermal-coal producer trading, on the peer evidence, in a deep-discount band, with a management actively shrinking the share count and the single worst number in the filing (-77.8% OCF) being a reversible refund-timing item explicitly quantified by management. Target-price range: **not set** — no market data was provided in this run; the frame to apply on pricing is US$1.79 book value per share and ~US$0.20 annualized EPS (US$0.10 half-year) against the ADRO comp band (5.65x P/E, 0.62x P/BV per pipeline DB, 31 Oct 2025), with ITMG deserving a premium on ROE and gearing. **Review trigger:** (a) 3Q26 gross margin below ~22% (would confirm the qoq compression is structural, not fuel-driven); (b) any material provision against the TCM royalty audits; (c) DJP refunds failing to normalize by the FY2026 audit report; (d) a dividend cut or buyback suspension that would signal the DHE SDA regime is binding on distributions.
