# PT Indo Tambangraya Megah Tbk (ITMG) — Q2 2026
---

**Reporting basis.** All figures are in **thousands of US Dollars (US$'000)** unless otherwise stated, per the unaudited interim consolidated financial statements for the six months ended **30 June 2026** (income statement and cash flow vs six months ended 30 June 2025; balance sheet vs 31 December 2025). The statements were authorised for issue by the Board of Directors on 10 August 2026. ITMG's functional and presentation currency is USD. The quarter is reported on a cumulative H1 basis; standalone Q2 figures discussed in this note are derived or external and labelled as such. Period-end FX: Rp17,856/US$ (31 Dec 2025: Rp16,782); average Rp17,196 (H1 2025: Rp16,475). Ultimate controlling entity: Banpu Public Company Limited (Thailand); direct parent: Banpu Minerals (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.

## 1. Executive Summary

ITMG delivered a solid but low-quality half: net revenue of US$1,000,491k (+8.8% YoY), gross profit of US$256,477k (+14.1%), and net profit of US$109,514k (+16.5%), with basic EPS of US$0.10 versus US$0.08 (+25%). The YoY margin expansion — gross margin 25.64% versus 24.45% (+119 bps), net margin 10.95% — is largely a weak-base and mix effect rather than pricing power: external research shows the H1 2026 realised ASP of ~US$81/t (+3.8% YoY) actually benefited from a *falling* domestic revenue share (17% of revenue vs ~22% in H1 2025), while the benchmark rally (HBA 6,322 kcal from ~US$100–103/t in Q1 to US$121–132/t in Jun–Jul) hit mostly *after* the reporting window given a 64% fixed-price book at 1Q26. Sequentially the story was cost-led and adverse: per the September 2026 public expose, cash cost ex-royalty rose from US$57/t (1Q26) to US$61/t (2Q26) on the April 2026 industrial B40 diesel spike (Pertamina +Rp5,100/litre to Rp28,150/litre on 18 April 2026) and a strip ratio stepping from 9.3x to 10.3x.

The most alarming line is cash flow: operating cash flow collapsed −77.8% to US$62,376k against net profit of US$109,514k (cash conversion 0.57x). We treat this as a timing artifact rather than operating deterioration. Collections were flat at 99.0% of revenue (US$990,280k), supplier payments rose 9.9% tracking the higher cost of revenue, and the dominant driver per management's 6M26 news release and Indonesian coverage was the **non-repeat of ~US$169m of H1 2025 tax refund receipts** — the same refunds that flattered 6M25 OCF of US$281,259k. Prepaid taxes +97% to US$55,677k is the mirror image: a PPh instalment/overpayment rebuild off a post-refund low base. Restricted cash +77% to US$85,787k is regulatory, driven by the Kepmen ESDM 344/2025 migration of reclamation/post-mining guarantees from bank guarantees to state-bank time deposits — non-discretionary, recoverable, and set to keep growing as the revised ~23 Mt RKAB executes.

The balance sheet remains a fortress: cash US$737,575k against total bank borrowings of only US$99,339k (loans-to-equity 0.05x; D/E 0.26x including all liabilities; current ratio 3.46x; working capital US$843,582k). Annualised ROE of 22.9% and ROA of 18.2% are exceptional for the asset base. Capital returns are running hard: H1 2026 buyback of 21,186,800 shares for ~US$26,571k, a further 10,690,300 shares (~US$14,341k) repurchased post-period, and US$64,543k of 2025 final dividends paid 19 May 2026 — partially explaining the −US$67,774k net cash change.

The unresolved items are mostly definitional: the "loans_noncurrent +34%" flag is contradicted by management's own news release showing long-term *bank* loans falling 55% to US$14m — the line move is likely lease liabilities or provisions, immaterial at ~1% of equity; G&A +40% is a 2Q25 comparator artifact (implied 2Q25 G&A ~US$6.9m); and the other-income swing (−US$4,277k) is a coal-swap mark-to-market loss of US$1,833k against a ~US$9,232k prior-year gain per pipeline prior work. Post-period catalysts dominate the outlook: the ~23 Mt RKAB approval (Aug 2026) de-risks 2H volume, the HBA rally to US$126–127/t supports 3Q ASP with a lag, diesel has retreated to Rp18,950/litre by September, but a planned 14.0x strip-ratio quarter in 3Q26, Mahakam low-water barge underloading (30–40%), and the PP 24/2026 sole-channel export mandate from January 2027 (83% of H1 revenue is exports) temper the recovery. Market data was not provided for this run, so valuation multiples are stated as not available; on fundamentals we assess this as a **HOLD / selective accumulation on policy clarity**, detailed in §14.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Item | H1 2026 (US$'000) | H1 2025 (US$'000) | % Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue | 1,000,491 | 919,416 | **+8.8%** |

Revenue growth of +8.8% was composed of third-party coal sales of US$963,940k, related-party coal sales of US$36,758k (Banpu group, Note 24), and services revenue of US$1,626k. External context attributes growth to a mix of volume (~12.3 Mt of sales, +5.1%) and ASP (~US$81/t, +3.8% YoY), with Q2 ASP of ~US$82.7/t versus ~US$79.4/t in Q1. The coal swap line produced a loss of US$1,833k within revenue (vs a gain of US$9,232k in H1 2025 per pipeline prior work) — an ~US$11.1m adverse swing inside the topline.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

- **Price.** Realised ASP of ~US$81/t (+3.8% YoY; external, management/broker estimates) sits structurally ~US$25–40/t below HBA benchmarks (HBA 6,322 kcal averaged roughly US$100–103/t in Q1 2026, rising to US$121–132/t Jun–Jul; ICI-3 only broke out above US$84/t in Aug–Sep). The gap is the DMO mechanism: a minimum 25% domestic allocation with a US$70/t power-sector price cap, modelled by analysts at 25–30% of ITMG's output.
- **Volume.** External reporting puts H1 sales at ~12.3 Mt with Q2 production ~5.3 Mt (+13% QoQ) and 1H production of 9.9 Mt slightly below the company's 10.1 Mt internal target.
- **Cost-in-revenue.** Third-party coal purchases within COGS were US$106,456k, and supplier PT Pamapersada Nusantara was paid US$318,115k (vs US$259,748k), consistent with a contractor-led volume push.
- **FX.** The rupiah average moved from Rp16,475 to Rp17,196 (+4.4% depreciation). With a USD functional currency and mostly USD-denominated export sales, translation is modest; Note 33 shows net foreign currency *assets* of US$6,906k at 30 June 2026 versus net liabilities of US$20,335k at 31 December 2025, and Note 29 records a foreign exchange gain of US$6,414k in H1 2026 versus a US$939k loss in H1 2025 — a tailwind to other income, not revenue.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Post-period, the benchmark path supports 2H ASP with a lag: HBA rose to US$126.87/t (Sep H1) and US$126.81/t (Oct H2), though Oct H1 softened −3.4% from Sep — the "2H tailwind" is narrower than the Apr–Oct range suggests. Transmission is capped by the DMO US$70/t obligation (25–30% modelled share) and by the 64%-fixed price book that locks ASP gains out of the near term. Volume is now quota-de-risked: the revised 2026 RKAB of ~23 Mt was approved in August 2026, above the 22.0–22.9 Mt target. The key 2027 structural issue is PP 24/2026 (effective 1 June 2026, Phase II from 1 January 2027): coal exports must route through the designated state export BUMN as sole intermediary, with the BUMN setting selling price and taking a margin — an as-yet-unpriced layer on 83% of revenue. DHE SDA rules (PP 21/2026; per the fact sheet, 100% of export proceeds in special accounts ≥12 months with a 50% monthly IDR conversion cap, with PP 21/2026 exceptions for certain mining trade arrangements) reduce cash fungibility but do not affect revenue recognition. Reserve/runway: mining permits run to 2028–2041 (IMM CCoW to 31 Mar 2028; TCM to 27 Feb 2035; JBG to 3 May 2035; BEK to 29 Jun 2041; TIS/GPK IUPs to 2029; NPR to 20 May 2033). No production or reserve tables were disclosed in this chunk.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — 3Q26 strip ratio of 14.0x**: overburden removal per tonne steps up ~36% vs the 10.3x half-year average; combined with the 5.9 Mt production plan, this is the biggest threat to 2H unit cost just as diesel relief arrives.
- **HIGH — Cash conversion**: OCF of US$62m vs net profit US$110m; the refund-base explanation is credible, but the prepaid-tax build (US$55,677k current + US$194,172k non-current) must actually convert to cash in 2H or the "timing" thesis fails.
- **MODERATE — Mahakam low water**: 30–40% barge underloading at the Melak area raises logistics cost/tonne and caps the volume recovery through the dry season (Jul–Oct), overlapping exactly the 2H earnings bridge.
- **MODERATE — Coal-swap MTM**: further Q3 mark-to-market losses are likely against the US$131.85/t HBA peak; Note 34 sensitivity is ±US$7,828k on a 5% coal price move.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — PP 24/2026 sole-channel exports from 1 Jan 2027**: 83% of revenue must route through the state export BUMN, which sets price and takes a margin; the structure of the Marubeni relationship (13.5% of revenue) and realised ASPs are both exposed. Unpriced by the market.
- **MODERATE — DMO at 25–30% of output capped at US$70/t**: the structural wedge between HBA (US$127/t) and realised ASP (US$81/t); any enforced increase in domestic share directly compresses blended ASP.
- **MODERATE — Reserve/permit cliff asymmetry**: IMM's CCoW expires 31 Mar 2028 — the flagship permit renewal timeline is the single most important 2027–28 structural item, not disclosed in this chunk.
- **LOW — Asset quality flags**: EBP and GPK subsidiary-asset table distortions (docling); unrecognised DTAs on US$117.9m of losses with five-year expiry windows.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **HIGH — Tax/royalty disputes and audits**: 12 open DGT cases (FY2011–2024) plus TCM royalty audits for FY2018–2023 with no provision disclosed — individually small, collectively a persistent drag; royalty payments scale with HBA.
- **MODERATE — DHE SDA encumbrance**: export-proceeds placement rules (per the fact sheet: 100% special-account placement ≥12 months, 50% monthly IDR conversion cap) reduce cash fungibility for buybacks/dividends precisely as distributions accelerate.
- **MODERATE — B50 mandate from 1 Oct 2026**: full biodiesel composition in Q4; industrial pricing market-based at Rp15,000–16,000/litre; the absolute diesel price has fallen, but the discount to crude parity narrows and CPO volatility enters the cost base.
- **LOW — Coal blending approval regime** (MoEMR 6/2026): management assessed no material impact.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- No market data was available for this run; we flag that the stock screens on a 22.9% ROE and net cash, but the 2H consensus step-up (H1 = 43.7% of FY26 estimates per KB Valbury) embeds a volume/cost recovery that the 14.0x strip quarter and river draft constraints put at risk. ## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
- 2H ASP tailwind lands with a lag: HBA US$126–127/t into 4Q with the 64% fixed book rolling over; RKAB at 23 Mt removes the quota constraint; Q2 production already +13% QoQ.
- Diesel relief: industrial B40 down ~Rp9,200/litre from the April peak into September — a direct Q4 margin lever if the 14x strip quarter is executed to plan (management target full-year SR 10.3–10.5x implies 4Q offset).
- Cash-flow normalisation: prepaid taxes (US$250m combined) convert to refunds; OCF rebounds toward net profit; the balance-sheet cash mountain funds continued buybacks at accretive prices.
- Coal swap MTM reverses on settlement if benchmarks hold; FX gain line may persist under the weaker rupiah.

### 14.2 Bear Case
- The "margin expansion" is an optical illusion: YoY comparisons flattered by weak H1 2025; sequentially margins compress on a structural cost step (SR 9.3x → 10.3x → 14.0x plan; fuel; logistics). If HBA fades (Oct H1 already −3.4%) while costs stay, 2H net margin compresses below H1.
- PP 24/2026 sole-channel exports from 2027 compress realised ASP and inject counterparty risk on 83% of revenue — a multiple-de-rating event, not just an EPS event.
- Cash-flow thesis fails: refunds don't repeat on schedule, DHE/reclamation encumbrance keeps growing, and the 0.57x conversion becomes the run-rate; dividend capacity (already trending down per share) compresses further.
- DMO share is forced higher at US$70/t; IMM CCoW renewal (Mar 2028) drags into uncertainty.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. Q3 2026 results (Nov 2026): ASP realisation vs the Jun–Sep HBA rally; execution of the 5.9 Mt / 14.0x strip plan.
2. 2026 interim dividend announcement (management: "still being calculated", 9 Sep 2026) and further buyback tranche disclosure.
3. Implementation regulations for PP 24/2026 Phase II (sole-channel from 1 Jan 2027) — the dominant 2027 variable.
4. Tax refund receipts / prepaid-tax unwind in 2H cash flow.
5. IMM CCoW renewal clarity ahead of 31 March 2028.
6. HBA trajectory and Q4 diesel/B50 pricing.

### 14.4 Recommendation
**HOLD (moderate risk).** Without market data we frame this on fundamentals: a fortress balance sheet (9/10 financial health) and 22.9% annualised ROE argue against selling, but the flagged margin expansion is low-quality, the 2H execution path is genuinely two-sided (quota de-risked vs a 14x strip quarter and river draft), and the 2027 sole-channel export regime is an unpriced structural discount on 83% of revenue. For holders: accumulate on weakness tied to policy headlines rather than earnings prints. Target price range: not set (no market data provided this run); on normalised FY26 owner earnings of ~US$0.20/share annualised EPS and the US$1.79 book value, a peer-anchored range of 4.5–6.5x annualised EPS with net cash support implies ~US$0.90–1.30/share — presented as a framework, not a target, pending a market-data refresh. **Review trigger:** Q3 2026 results — if gross margin holds above ~25% on the 14x strip quarter, upgrade to BUY; if OCF conversion stays below 0.6x for a second consecutive half, downgrade to SELL.
