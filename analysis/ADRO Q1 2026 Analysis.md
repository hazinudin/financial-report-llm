# PT Alamtri Resources Indonesia Tbk (ADRO) — Q1 2026
---

**Reporting basis.** All figures in this note are **thousands of US Dollars (US$k)** unless otherwise stated, drawn from the Company's unaudited interim consolidated financial statements for the three months ended **31 March 2026** (comparative income statement/cash flow: three months ended 31 March 2025; comparative balance sheet: 31 December 2025). The statements were authorized for issue by Directors' resolution dated 29 April 2026. Effective tax rate, margins, and all ratios are as computed in the canonical ratio block; several fact-sheet sign conventions are corrected per the research reconciliation (noted inline). No market-data stub was provided for this run, so market-based valuation multiples are quoted from the pipeline DB as context only and are flagged as such.

## 1. Executive Summary

Alamtri Resources (ADRO) — the renamed Adaro Energy holding entity, parent of 85%-owned listed miner Alamtri Minerals (ADMR) — delivered a strongly operating-driven quarter. Revenue rose **23.4% YoY to US$470.9m**, gross profit surged **61.8% to US$178.6m (37.9% gross margin)**, operating profit rose **85.1% to US$141.6m (30.1% margin)**, and profit attributable to owners jumped **67% to US$128.1m (EPS US$0.00445, +74.5% YoY)**. Critically, and contrary to what the raw fact-sheet signs might suggest, the bottom-line beat was **not tax-driven**: income tax expense rose 97% to US$36.6m and the effective tax rate was a perfectly normal **~20.3% of PBT**. The earnings lift came from (i) a 14% coal sales volume increase to ~1.46 Mt with ASP up 17.9% to ~US$182/t (per UOB Kay Hian via Investor.id, 2026-05-08), (ii) severe operating leverage in the mining-services (SIS) fleet business, and (iii) a US$22.8m swing in joint-venture income as the Batang 2×1,000MW plant stabilized.

The balance sheet tells a transition story. Total assets grew 5.4% to US$7.19bn, but within that, fixed assets in **construction in progress reached US$1.44bn** (~20% of total assets earning zero return) as the KAI 500ktpa aluminium smelter build accelerates. Bank loans rose to US$988.6m (from US$745.0m at FY25 end) on US$250.1m of new drawdowns — identified as KAI smelter facility debt — while the 32% drop in current liabilities is largely the **settlement of the US$250m interim dividend payable in January** plus current→non-current reclassification, not deleveraging (total liabilities were broadly flat). The headline securities-current balance of US$829.1m (+55%) is **mark-to-market on the retained ~15% AADI stake carried at FVOCI**, not cash deployment; the associated US$296.7m revaluation gain ran through OCI, not the P&L, and cannot fund capex or dividends.

Cash flow improved markedly: operating cash flow more than doubled to **US$195.1m (OCF/net profit = 1.36×)**, reflecting collections of 117.8% of revenue and US$60.2m of cash interest received. Free cash flow was **negative US$28.3m** after US$159.1m capex, US$250m dividends paid, and only US$3.2m of loan repayments — meaning Q1 paired US$250m of new borrowings with a US$250m dividend, i.e., **capital returns are partly debt-funded at the trough of the smelter build**. This is the central tension in the thesis.

On valuation, no market-data stub was provided; the pipeline DB (latest ingested period, 2025-10-31) shows ADRO at PER 5.65×, PBV 0.62×, ROE 10.9%, DER 0.28 — the cheapest large-cap multiple set in the ingested coal universe versus subsidiary ADMR at 11.76× PER / 2.31× PBV. Given normalized quarterly earnings power of ~US$128m attributable, the parent trades at a material discount to the sum of its parts even before the smelter option. **Rating: BUY (medium risk)** —详见 §14.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

Per UOB Kay Hian (via Investor.id, 2026-05-08, external), Q1 2026 coal sales volume rose **~14% to ~1.46 Mt** and ASP rose **~17.9% to ~US$182/t**. The external price backdrop turned supportive only late in the quarter: FOB Newcastle 6,000kcal averaged ~US$126/t in March 2026 vs ~US$114/t in February (Wood Mackenzie, 2026-04-15), with Indonesian HBA rising from US$103.30/t to US$106.11/t (Antara, 2026-02-03) as RKAB quota-cut talk tightened supply. Because the price shock hit from late February, the Q1 ASP understates the run-rate benefit visible in Q2 — corroborated post-period by 1H26 gross profit +48.7% YoY (CNBC Indonesia, 2026-08-31, post-period context).

The production/volume disclosures in the fact sheet (`production: []`) are not broken out at the parent level; volume/ASP figures above are external-sourced and labelled as such. No FX tailwind is apparent in revenue — indeed Note 29 shows a net FX **loss** of US$2.2m, implying modest translation headwind rather than tailwind.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

- **Related-party concentration remains very high:** related-party revenue totaled **US$300.0m, or 63.7% of consolidated revenue** (1Q25: 71.4%), dominated by AI (US$169.2m) and AIS (US$95.8m) — the internal trading/marketing chain. This is structurally normal for the Alamtri group but is the single largest earnings-quality optic for outside shareholders.
- **Customer mix (per details block):** AI US$166.2m and AIS US$95.8m, each ~10% flagged of the underlying sales base; the largest third-party exposures sit in the destination table (Singapore US$113.3m).
- **Segment mix:** Mining (coal) US$266.0m external + US$0.4m internal; Mining Services US$194.3m external + US$35.1m internal; Others US$10.5m external + US$3.6m internal; eliminations −US$39.2m. Mining services external revenue of US$194.3m is notable — SIS earns meaningful third-party revenue alongside captive work.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Supporting factors: committed coal sales agreements of ~1.1 Mt for 2026–2027 delivery (MC and LC, per commitments note); mining-services contracts with ADMR subsidiaries running to 2029+; the JV income leg (Batang plant) is recurring rather than one-off. The external price environment moved sharply higher post-quarter (Newcastle to ~US$132–138/t by March/April per Wood Mackenzie and GoodStats, 2026-03-04), implying Q2 2026 realization above Q1's ~US$182/t ASP — post-period evidence, used here only as forward context.

Watch items: RKAB quota policy (supply-side), the DMO compensation regime, and the fact that the "Others" segment (aluminium/power/logistics) posted a **US$7.5m operating loss** per external research (aluna, 2026-05-01) — the second leg of revenue is still a drag, not a driver, until KAI ramps (guided meaningful revenue from Q4 2026, positive net profit from 2027, per NH Korindo Sekuritas, 2026-06-15, post-period). Reserve/runway disclosures are absent from this fact sheet ("not disclosed" at parent level); see [[ADMR Q1 2026 Analysis]] for the CCoW structure (LC 46,620 ha; MC 24,990 ha; SBC 24,993 ha; JC/KC ~24,988 ha each, with a possible source duplication flagged).

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

This section requires the fact-sheet's inverted sign conventions to be corrected (per the research reconciliation of the interim statements, high confidence):

- **Finance income fell 53.1% to US$15,175k** (from 32,404). But **cash receipts of finance income rose 143% to US$60,178k** (from 24,785). The divergence reflects a 1Q25 accrual-heavy base (unwinding interest on loans to related parties) versus cash collection in 1Q26, plus the related-party loan book shrinking: **US$114.3m of related-party loan repayments were received in Q1 2026** (vs 62.5m). This is a base effect, not a yield collapse.
- **Accrued finance costs fell 68.1% to US$1,745k** (from 5,464) — the fact sheet's "interest expense up 68%" is a sign flip. Cash finance charges **paid** rose 65% to US$24,021k, consistent with interest being **capitalized into smelter construction** rather than expensed.
- **Other expenses, net rose ~58% to US$(4,689)k** (from (2,966)) — composition (Note 29): net FX loss (2,167); loss on disposal of fixed assets (176); net FV loss on other investments (2,635); others (289).
- **JV income swung +US$22.8m:** share of JV net profit was US$25,251k vs 2,425k (+941%), dominated by 34%-owned PT Bhimasena Power (Batang 2×1,000MW, 1Q26 net profit US$67.5m per external research), plus TPI +4.1m and KHN −2.1m. Note the income statement has an unlabeled subtotal row of US$38,681k between finance costs and PBT (likely share of JV profit plus other items) — presented as-is from the statements.

Supporting infrastructure: AIA/ABI/ATDI investment vehicles (US$4.9bn combined assets), KIPI industrial estate, and the solar/transmission cluster (Karimun/Batam entities). The smelter is the reason for the US$250m drawdown, the US$1.44bn CIP, the capitalized interest, and the prepaid tax build — every odd line in this quarter traces back to it.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Related-party exposure is the structural feature: revenue 63.7% of consolidated; trade receivables US$246.3m (246,343); loans to related parties US$432.3m (6.0% of assets, down from 8.0%) — mainly the US$289.9m Adaro facility (SOFR-based, due 2028) and US$127.7m BPI loan (no maturity, repayable on demand subject to covenants, fair value 132,934 vs carrying 127,655). Management judges impairment immaterial (no ECL). Items to monitor: the 31–60 day overdue receivable jump (2.0→33.3m), the US$114.3m of loan repayments received (a positive — cash coming back up the chain), and the parent's US$250m dividend paid while lending US$430m to related parties — the intra-group cash plumbing deserves continued scrutiny.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths
- Net cash position of −US$1.09bn net debt (0.32× D/E, 0.18× loans/equity, 75.5% equity ratio) despite the smelter build.
- Current ratio 4.32×, cash ratio 1.97×; US$1.16bn cash plus undrawn uncommitted revolvers (US$50m UOB, US$25m BRI, both signed March 2026).
- OCF/net profit 1.36×; collections 117.8% of revenue — receivables discipline is visible in cash, even if the aging bucket blipped.
- Covenants complied on all facilities; loan maturity profile back-loaded (nil 2026 amortization on KAI).

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
- Severe, demonstrable operating leverage: opex +4.5% on revenue +23.4%.
- 14% volume growth and 17.9% ASP gain simultaneously — price and volume rarely move together for coal.
- Diversified destination mix (domestic 70%, Singapore 24%, Japan/Korea 6%) with committed volumes of ~1.1 Mt for 2026–27.
- JV portfolio turning: BPI (Batang) equity income now a material, recurring earnings leg.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths
- Vertically integrated: captive mining services (SIS) capture the margin between miner and contractor — Q1 proved this absorbs price upside rather than leaking it.
- The aluminium option: KAI commissioning end-2026 offers a genuine second earnings leg and a rerating path off a pure-coal beta (see [[ADMR Q1 2026 Analysis]], [[KAI Analysis]]).
- Active capital returns: ~100% payout plus Rp5trn buyback authorization and in-progress capital reduction.
- Controllers ASI + Garibaldi Thohir at 54.52% provide strategic stability; audit committee (Mohammad Effendi chair, Ignatius Robby Sani, Lindawati Gani) independent-constituted.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Debt-funded dividends at the capex peak:** US$250m borrowed against US$250m paid out with capex running US$158m/quarter. If coal prices normalize, this becomes a forced choice between the smelter, the payout, and the balance sheet.
- **MODERATE — Related-party receivable aging:** the 31–60 day overdue bucket jumped US$2.0m→US$33.3m against the AI/AIS chain; 63.7% of revenue is related-party.
- **MODERATE — Price-shock dependence:** the March price spike (Newcastle ~US$126–138/t) is a geopolitical artifact (Middle East disruption, per Wood Mackenzie); its reversal compresses the same margins that just doubled.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — Smelter execution:** US$1.44bn CIP (20% of assets, zero return), KAI only 56%-owned, aluminium revenue guided only from Q4 2026 and profit from 2027 — a long, leveraged, single-project execution window.
- **MODERATE — Earnings quality:** US$296.7m of the comprehensive income is non-cash AADI mark-up in OCI; headline equity growth overstates deployable value.
- **MODERATE — "Others" segment inefficiency:** 61% of assets for <14% of net profit and an operating loss externally reported.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **HIGH (exogenous) — Coal policy:** RKAB quota restrictions, DMO obligations and the DHE-SDA 100%/12-month retention rule (GR 8/2025) directly constrain cash fungibility and volumes.
- **MODERATE — Pillar Two / tax:** PMK 136/2024 global minimum tax applies; no top-up currently, but rate creep is a live structural drag on the near-100% payout math.
- **LOW/MODERATE — Litigation:** Hariyadi Rp3trn claim rejected on appeal; cassation pending. PSM Rp526.7bn loan fully provisioned.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- The historical 0.62× PBV (pipeline DB, dated) embeds a deep holdco discount; the closing catalyst is KAI execution, and until then the parent re-leverages to fund a payout the market may not fully credit.
- No live market data for this run — P/E, EV/EBITDA, and yield math cannot be responsibly stated; flag as a data gap rather than guessing.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
Coal prices hold above Q1 realizations (Newcastle ~US$126–138/t run-rate vs Q1 ASP ~US$182/t met coal), mining-services leverage persists, BPI JV income compounds, and KAI hits full 500ktpa capacity by end-2026 with aluminium revenue from Q4 2026 — collapsing a 0.6×-book holdco discount toward ADMR's 2.3×. Post-period 1H26 evidence (net profit +76.8%, gross profit +48.7%) shows the margin trend persisted, supporting the run-rate.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Coal prices revert (the March spike was geopolitical); the smelter slips into 2027 or ramps poorly; the ~100% payout forces further borrowing while US$1.44bn of CIP earns nothing; the AADI paper gain reverses with the market; related-party concentration (63.7% of revenue) plus the encumbered DHE cash constrain flexibility exactly when flexibility is needed.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. KAI first commercial aluminium sales (guided from Q4 2026) and commissioning milestones through 2026.
2. Q2 2026 results — coal price realization above Q1's ~US$182/t ASP.
3. Buyback execution under the Rp5trn authorization (12 months from 18 Apr 2026) and completion of the capital reduction (pending ministerial approval).
4. RKAB quota and HBA policy decisions.
5. Resolution of the Hariyadi cassation and any unwinding of the PSM provision.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**BUY (medium risk).** The quarter proved the operating engine (not tax, not one-offs) at a moment when the balance sheet remains net-cash by US$1.09bn and the market — on dated pipeline-DB multiples — values the parent at a deep discount to its listed 85%-owned subsidiary alone. The recommendation is conditioned on accepting smelter execution risk and coal-price cyclicality; the ~100% payout funded partly by debt while capex peaks is the specific discipline risk to monitor. **Target price: not computable for this run** — no market-data stub was provided (price, market cap, shares outstanding in market terms not disclosed). A framework: on pipeline-DB historical multiples (0.62× book vs ADMR 2.31×), a re-rating to even 1.0× the US$0.1700/share book value implies ~60% upside; frame any target after refreshing live market data. **Review trigger:** KAI commissioning slippage beyond mid-2027, coal benchmark retreat below ~US$100/t Newcastle, related-party overdue receivables exceeding ~US$50m, or evidence that dividends require further external borrowing for a second consecutive quarter.
