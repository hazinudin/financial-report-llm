SEGMENT SUMMARY  
  
This month in our Market Prospect segment we covered Tin, which remains one of the more overlooked critical metals, despite increasingly compelling market fundamentals. The global tin market is only around 400,000 tonnes per year, roughly 1–2% of the size of the copper market. That small scale matters: relatively modest changes in supply or demand can have an outsized impact on price.  
  
Inventories are very low, the market appears to be moving into deficit, and some analysts expect those deficits to grow through to 2030. At the same time, very few meaningful new tin mines have entered production over the past four decades.  
  
Supply is also highly concentrated. Myanmar and Indonesia account for up to 40% of global production and a large proportion of recent supply disruptions, including restrictions on illegal and unpermitted mining.  
  
On the demand side, almost half of global tin consumption goes into solder. That provides direct exposure to some of the fastest-growing areas of the global economy notably from demand in:  
  
o Semiconductors and advanced electronics  
o AI data centres  
o Electric vehicles  
o Renewable-energy infrastructure  
o Increasingly complex electrical systems  
  
Our guest for this month’s Drilling Deeper was Rimas Kairaitis, who also spoke in depth about tin’s role within advanced AI semiconductor packages. Tin-silver-copper solder connects the chips, memory stacks and interposers inside AI accelerators. He noted that industry participants are increasingly looking for both conventional tin and specialised low-alpha tin suitable for advanced semiconductor applications.  
  
His view was that the supply-chain response to AI investment is only beginning to reach the tin market and that substantially higher incentive pricing may ultimately be required to support the necessary new supply.  
  
Rimas also discussed Sky Metals’ Tallebung project in New South Wales, where X-ray ore sorting has demonstrated the potential to upgrade approximately 0.17% tin material to more than 2.3% tin at around 95% recovery.  
  
Tin may be a relatively small market but its exposure to AI, electrification and constrained global supply makes it increasingly difficult to ignore.
