# PT Central Omega Resources Tbk (DKFT) — Q1 2026
---
## 1. Executive Summary
PT Central Omega Resources Tbk (DKFT) delivered a record Q1 2026 — the strongest quarter in the company's history — with **revenue up 20.2% year-over-year to Rp 506.01 billion** and **net profit up 74.1% to Rp 237.73 billion**. To put the quarter in perspective: Q1 2026 net profit alone equals **41.5% of the entire FY2025 net profit** (Rp 573.27 billion). The surge was driven by a powerful combination of higher domestic nickel ore prices (underpinned by Indonesia's February 2026 RKAB quota cuts to ~260–270 million wet MT, which created domestic ore scarcity), higher sales volumes (Q1 production of 554,376 MT, ~29% of the full-year plan), and sharply lower unit costs (manufacturing costs fell 24.8% YoY). Gross margin expanded by 13.4 percentage points to an exceptional **65.48%**, while the company **swung from a net debt of Rp 145.43 billion to a NET CASH position of Rp 123.60 billion** after repaying Rp 348.69 billion of loans (including the full Rp 245 billion CCB Indonesia facility). The balance sheet now holds Rp 644.26 billion in cash plus Rp 679.88 billion in securities against only Rp 520.65 billion of (largely non-interest-bearing, restructured) loans. However, two yellow flags temper the record result: **trade receivables ballooned 216% to Rp 299.20 billion** (cash collections covered only 59.6% of revenue), and customer concentration intensified — the top three buyers (PT Sino Indo Nickel 55%, PT Megah Surya Pertiwi 33%, PT Five Star General Resources 10%) now account for **98% of sales**. The stock trades at approximately Rp 650 per share — roughly **5.2x TTM earnings, 3.8x annualized Q1 earnings, 2.4x book value, and ~2.2x EV/EBITDA**, with a ~5.4% dividend yield.
**Investment Rating: BUY (Speculative)** — Record profitability, a newly minted net cash balance sheet, a 47-year reserve life, and deep-value multiples provide a compelling risk-reward, tempered by extreme customer concentration, the unresolved Q1 receivables build, nickel price cyclicality (LME fell from the May peak of US$19,675 to ~US$16,220 by early July), and the stranded-asset overhang of the suspended CORII smelter.
*All figures in Indonesian Rupiah (Rp); "Rp B" denotes billions of Rupiah unless otherwise stated. Financial statements are unaudited.*

## 2. Revenue Analysis
### 2.1 Revenue Performance
| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Rp 506,014,505,076 | Rp 420,967,646,133 | **+20.20%** |
The 20.20% revenue increase marks a sharp acceleration from FY2025's full-year growth of +7.87% (revenue Rp 1.58 trillion) and continues the momentum from FY2025's profit recovery (FY2025 net profit +56.6% to Rp 573.49 billion).
### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price
The revenue surge was driven by **both volume and price**, based on production disclosures and industry data:
**Nickel Ore Segment (97.8% of revenue — primary driver):**
- Nickel ore sales of **Rp 494.78 billion**, up **+17.9% YoY** from Rp 419.64 billion
- Q1 2026 production of **554,376 MT** (BKA Konawe Block 1: 138,553 MT; MPR Morowali Block 2: 116,056 MT; IMN Lambolo: 299,767 MT) — already **28.8% of the full-year 2026 sales plan of 1,926,378 MT**, tracking ahead of schedule
- **Price environment strongly favorable:** Indonesia cut 2026 RKAB mining quotas to ~260–270 million wet MT in February 2026 (a reduction of roughly one-third), creating domestic ore scarcity and strengthening domestic ore prices/premiums. LME nickel traded in the ~US$17,000–17,250/MT range through most of Q1 before surging to a two-year high of US$19,675/MT on May 6, 2026 (post-Q1)
- Implied average selling price of roughly **Rp 0.89 million/MT (~US$52.5/MT)** assuming sales volumes approximated production — an approximation only, as actual shipment volumes were not disclosed
- **Weak Rupiah tailwind:** USD/IDR mid-rate of 16,993 at March 31, 2026 (vs. 16,782 at December 31, 2025) — nickel ore pricing is USD-linked while costs are largely Rupiah-denominated. See [[Rupiah & Ammonia - Meridian Research]] for the weak-Rupiah macro thesis
**Limestone Segment (2.2% of revenue — emerging second leg):**
- Limestone sales of **Rp 11.23 billion**, up **~8.5x YoY** from Rp 1.32 billion, as the ALJ operation (2 crusher lines, 1.5M MT/year capacity, started commercial operations 2024) ramps toward its **2026 target of 1,200,000 MT/year**
**Revenue Quality Assessment — the key caveat:**
- Cash received from customers: **Rp 301.44 billion vs. revenue of Rp 506.01 billion** — collections covered only **59.6% of revenue**, a sharp deterioration from Q1 2025 when collections (Rp 413.64 billion) covered 98.3% of revenue
- The Rp 204.6 billion shortfall is visible on the balance sheet: trade receivables surged 216% to Rp 299.20 billion. The receivables are concentrated in the same top-3 smelter customers (Sino Indo Nickel Rp 134.04 billion; Megah Surya Pertiwi Rp 108.70 billion; Five Star Rp 49.18 billion) and are classified as **current/not-yet-due**, suggesting late-quarter shipment timing rather than distress — but collection in Q2 is the single most important item to verify
- Partially offsetting: **customer advances rose 35.4% to Rp 249.71 billion** (customers pre-paying for ore), a strong demand signal that suggests buyers are competing for scarce quota-constrained ore
### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations
- Revenue is effectively a **single-commodity, single-country business**: 97.8% nickel ore (saprolite) sold domestically to Indonesian smelters; 2.2% limestone
- **Customer concentration intensified dramatically:** top-3 customers = **98% of Q1 2026 sales** (Sino 55%, Megah Surya 33%, Five Star 10%) vs. top-3 = 78% in Q1 2025 (when PT Vantama Arja Sejahtera contributed 26% and bought nothing in Q1 2026). The loss/diversification of Vantama was more than replaced by Sino (+135% YoY)
- Inter-segment (internal) nickel sales of Rp 192.15 billion were eliminated on consolidation — ore mined by subsidiaries (MPR, IMN, BKA) is sold through the parent trading entity, which then sells to external smelters
- All sales are domestic (no exports), consistent with Indonesia's domestic processing mandate (PP No. 25/2024)
### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook
- **Positive — RKAB scarcity:** Quota cuts have created genuine ore tightness; smelters are competing for supply (evidenced by the 35% jump in customer advances). Wood Mackenzie notes NPI/saprolite ore tightness persisting; mines without quotas have closed, benefiting quota-holders like DKFT
- **Positive — Post-Q1 LME surge:** Nickel hit a two-year high of US$19,675/MT on May 6, 2026, which should support Q2 realized prices
- **Negative — June–July price reversal:** LME nickel fell to US$16,400 by June 30 and a year-to-date low of US$16,220 by July 2, 2026, partly on (denied) rumors of Indonesia raising RKAB quotas to 360M MT. If quotas are increased in July–August, domestic ore scarcity premiums would compress
- **Positive — Volume runway:** Residual reserves of **89.85 million MT** vs. a ~1.93M MT/year sales plan implies ~47 years of mine life at planned rates; exploration additions continue (E&E assets +10.1% in Q1)
- **Positive — Limestone ramp:** 1.2M MT/year 2026 target vs. minimal 2025 base provides incremental growth
- **Watch — Receivables conversion:** Q1's reported revenue must convert to cash in Q2/Q3 to be validated

The gross margin of 65.48% is extraordinary for a mining company and was achieved through a powerful pincer movement:
- **Revenue +20.20% while COGS fell 13.47%** — a 33.7-percentage-point jaws ratio
- Total manufacturing cost fell **24.83%** YoY (Rp 169.71 B vs. Rp 225.77 B): direct labor halved (-51.1% to Rp 4.96 B) and manufacturing overhead fell 23.6% (to Rp 164.74 B), indicating lower contractor/mining-service rates and/or better ore grades and strip ratios

- Nickel segment external gross margin: **66.8%** (Rp 330.53 B gross profit on Rp 494.78 B external sales)
- Limestone segment gross margin: **7.3%** (Rp 0.82 B on Rp 11.23 B) — still sub-scale
- Inventory effect: Q1 2025 saw a Rp 23.90 B finished-goods build (production outpacing sales), while Q1 2026 drew down Rp 4.97 B of finished goods — modestly flattering the YoY COGS comparison but not changing the underlying margin story

**Operating Leverage:** A 20.20% revenue increase produced a 71.40% operating profit increase — an operating leverage factor of **~3.5x**, characteristic of mining businesses where costs are largely fixed or volume-independent in the short run.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors
### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
1. **Receivables Conversion (HIGH SEVERITY):** Trade receivables +216% to Rp 299.20 B; collections covered only 59.6% of revenue; core operating cash flow actually **fell 35.7% YoY**. The balances are current and from three large smelters, so this is likely timing — but Q2 2026 collections are the single most important verification point. If receivables age past due, the quarter's earnings quality is impaired.
2. **Nickel Price Reversal (HIGH SEVERITY):** LME nickel fell from US$19,675 (May 6) to US$16,220 (July 2 year-to-date low). Rumors of RKAB quota increases to 360M MT (officially denied) could compress the domestic ore scarcity premium that powered Q1's margins. Wood Mackenzie flags a possible targeted saprolite quota increase in July–August.
3. **Customer Concentration (HIGH SEVERITY):** Top-3 customers = 98% of sales; Sino Indo Nickel alone = 55%. The loss of Vantama (26% of Q1 2025 sales, zero in Q1 2026) demonstrates how quickly individual buyer volumes can disappear.
4. **Tax Cash Outflows (MODERATE):** Taxes payable of Rp 85.82 B (3x QoQ) will be paid in Q2; prepaid taxes of Rp 101.83 B represent cash stranded with the tax office.
### 11.2 Structural Risks
5. **Single-Commodity Concentration (HIGH SEVERITY):** 97.8% of revenue is nickel ore. DKFT is a levered proxy on Indonesian saprolite ore prices with no downstream hedge while the smelter is idled.
6. **CORII Stranded Asset (MODERATE SEVERITY):** The suspended FeNi smelter (Rp 931.66 B gross machinery) may require further impairment beyond the existing Rp 57.22 B allowance if the battery-nickel pivot never materializes; the Cohesion 5% stake sale has been pending since 2018.
7. **Mine Closure Liability (MODERATE SEVERITY):** Rp 443.89 B reclamation/mine-closure provision (27.5% of total liabilities) — nearly tripled in 2025; adequacy is management-estimated, not actuarial.
8. **Legacy Receivable Impairments (LOW-MODERATE):** Rp 61.99 B of impaired receivables (Shanxi Minmetals, Ivoryline) from past trading activities — a reminder of historical credit discipline issues; full recovery unlikely.
9. **License Renewal (LOW-MODERATE):** BKA's Konawe IUP expires December 2027 — renewal is routine but not guaranteed in the current policy environment.
10. **Related-Party History (LOW-MODERATE):** Long-standing Rp 37.89 B land advance to controlling shareholder Jinsheng Mining; past intra-group payable write-offs. Governance is family/insider-dominated (Jinsheng 61.63%).
### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
11. **RKAB Policy Risk (HIGH SEVERITY — two-sided):** The entire margin thesis rests on quota scarcity. A quota increase (rumored 360M MT) would release ore supply and compress premiums; conversely, DKFT's own future quota allocations are not guaranteed to rise with its 1.93M MT sales plan.
12. **Royalty/Fiscal Risk (MODERATE):** Indonesia has been raising mining fiscal take (progressive royalties); accrued royalties already halved to Rp 1.68 B, and further rate increases would hit margins directly.
13. **EV Demand Softening (MODERATE):** LFP batteries are displacing NMC chemistry (~70% of Chinese EV makers), capping long-term battery-nickel demand growth; the offset is resilient NPI/stainless demand for saprolite ore.
14. **US–Iran War / Sulfur Shock (MIXED):** The war (see [[Strait_of_Hormuz_Crisis]]) spiked sulfur prices to ~US$1,100/MT, devastating HPAL/limonite economics — relatively **advantaging** saprolite-NPI routes like DKFT's customers; a peace-driven energy normalization could narrow that advantage.
### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
15. **Micro-Cap Illiquidity (MODERATE):** ~US$211M market cap, 33.4% free float, thin trading — position sizing and exit liquidity are real constraints; the stock's 52-week range (500–1,055) shows high volatility.
16. **Earnings at Cyclical Peak (MODERATE):** Multiples are optically cheap precisely because earnings are at record levels; if margins mean-revert to FY2025 levels (~36% net), the ~5x P/E becomes ~6.5–7x — still inexpensive, but the cushion is thinner than headline numbers suggest.

## 14. Investment Thesis
### 14.1 Bull Case
DKFT is a quota-protected, net-cash Indonesian nickel ore producer generating record margins at the exact moment its product is scarcest. The RKAB quota regime has handed ore sellers extraordinary pricing power — 65% gross margins and a ~70% annualized ROE — while the market prices the stock at just ~3.8x annualized earnings and ~2.2x EV/EBITDA with ~22% of the market cap covered by net financial assets. The Q1 receivables build is timing, not distress (all current, all blue-chip smelter customers, customer advances up 35%), and Q2 collections will confirm it. The Eximbank restructuring means the remaining Rp 524 B debt costs nothing to carry, with a Rp 185.91 B deferred-interest waiver as embedded equity upside by 2031. Reserves last ~47 years, the weak Rupiah (see [[Rupiah & Ammonia - Meridian Research]]) keeps margins inflated, and a ~5.4% dividend yield pays investors to wait. Re-rating to even 6–7x FY2026E earnings implies Rp 850–1,000 per share.
### 14.2 Bear Case
Q1 2026 is the top of the cycle. LME nickel has already fallen ~18% from its May peak, Indonesia could raise RKAB quotas as early as July–August, and the 65% gross margin is unsustainable. Worse, the quarter's profit has not yet turned into cash: collections covered only 59.6% of revenue, receivables tripled, and core operating cash flow fell 36% YoY — if the three smelter customers (98% of sales) stretch payments, earnings quality unravels. The company carries the baggage of past distress: the idled CORII smelter (potential further impairment), Rp 62 B of impaired legacy receivables, Rp 444 B of mine-closure obligations, and an insider-dominated register with a history of related-party dealings. At ~US$211M market cap with a 33% float, the stock is a volatile micro-cap where the 500–1,055 52-week range is the norm. If ore premiums mean-revert, a "cheap" 5x P/E on peak earnings becomes a fair-to-rich 7x on trough earnings with limited downside support above book (Rp 267/share).
### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. **Positive:** Q2 2026 cash collections confirming receivables conversion (the single most important catalyst)
2. **Positive:** RKAB quotas held at 260–270M MT (or targeted saprolite-only adjustments that don't flood DKFT's market)
3. **Positive:** LME nickel recovery on sustained NPI tightness; post-Q1 price surge already partially captured in Q2 realized prices
4. **Positive:** Full Eximbank principal settlement (2025–2028 schedule) triggering the Rp 185.91 B deferred-interest waiver
5. **Positive:** Dividend declarations (May 2026 ex-date already signaled) and potential payout ratio increases
6. **Positive:** Limestone reaching the 1.2M MT run-rate; CORII battery-nickel pivot decision
7. **Negative:** RKAB quota increase toward 360M MT → ore premium compression
8. **Negative:** LME nickel remaining below ~US$17,000 through H2 2026
9. **Negative:** Receivables aging past due or new impairments
10. **Negative:** Loss of one of the top-3 customers (Vantama precedent)
11. **Negative:** Progressive royalty/fiscal increases on miners
### 14.4 Recommendation: BUY (Speculative)
**Target Price:** Rp 850–1,000 per share (implying ~31–54% upside from Rp 650), based on 6–7x base-case FY2026E EPS of ~Rp 127–145.
**Investment Rationale:**
- DKFT offers rare pure-play exposure to Indonesia's quota-driven nickel ore scarcity with a repaired, net-cash balance sheet and ~100% earnings retention for shareholders
- Q1 2026's record results (revenue +20.2%, net profit +74.1%, 65.5% gross margin) demonstrate the operating leverage available when ore is scarce — and the valuation (~3.8x annualized P/E) already discounts heavy mean-reversion
- The Rp 803 B net financial asset position and ~5.4% dividend yield provide downside ballast; even bear-case earnings keep the stock in single-digit P/E territory
- Position sizing should respect micro-cap liquidity and the receivables verification pending in Q2
**Key Risks to Monitor:**
- Q2 2026 collections vs. the Rp 299 B receivables balance (weekly/monthly attention)
- RKAB quota decisions (July–August window per Wood Mackenzie)
- LME nickel price and domestic ore premium trajectory
- Top-3 customer offtake continuity (Sino, Megah Surya, Five Star)
- Eximbank settlement progress toward the deferred-interest waiver
- BKA IUP renewal (December 2027) and CORII impairment disclosures
- USD/IDR (Rupiah strength would compress margins)
**Time Horizon:** 12–18 months, with a hard review after the Q2 2026 cash flow statement confirms (or refutes) the receivables thesis.
