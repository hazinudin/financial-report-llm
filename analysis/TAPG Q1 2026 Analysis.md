## 1. Executive Summary

TAPG’s Q1 2026 interim filing reveals a balance sheet that continues to strengthen while earnings transparency remains incomplete. Total assets expanded to Rp 15,403,824 million, up 4.8% from December 31, 2025, and total equity rose 6.6% to Rp 12,454,507 million, driven by a surge in cash and cash equivalents to Rp 2,797,577 million from Rp 1,651,433 million. The group now holds cash equivalent to 2.6× its total loan principal of Rp 1,088,820 million, producing a gross liquidity surplus even though a formal net debt figure is not disclosed because marketable securities are not quantified in the extracted data. Current and quick ratios improved to 2.73× and 2.14×, respectively, reinforcing a fortress-like liquidity position.

Operational visibility is limited to segment disclosures and footnotes. The palm oil segment reported external sales of Rp 2,486,623 million and segment net profit of Rp 678,083 million, while the rubber segment contributed only Rp 5,574 million of external sales and a segment net loss of Rp 1,633 million. Note 26 indicates that PT Agro Multi Persada (AMP), the 94.93%-owned holding subsidiary, recorded sales of Rp 2,492,197 million (down 4.9% year-over-year) and net profit of Rp 530,449 million (down 8.0% year-over-year). Basic earnings per share fell to Rp 37 in Q1 2026 from Rp 41 in Q1 2025 per Note 36, implying a roughly 9.8% decline in attributable earnings per share despite the stronger balance sheet.

Cash flow generation softened. Operating cash flow declined 15.4% year-over-year to Rp 654,283 million, reflecting lower collections from customers (Rp 2,683,443 million versus Rp 2,841,610 million) that outpaced a reduction in payments to suppliers. Capital expenditure was stable at Rp 137,562 million, yielding core free cash flow of Rp 516,721 million. Investing cash outflows widened to Rp 516,188 million, and financing cash flow beyond loan repayments of Rp 10,590 million and interest paid of Rp 15,488 million is not fully disclosed, leaving a reconciliation gap to the period’s net cash increase of Rp 1,144,393 million.

The investment case rests on balance-sheet optionality versus earnings opacity. Leverage is negligible, undrawn credit facilities total at least Rp 1,000,000 million, and the group carries no hedged commodity or FX exposure, leaving profits fully levered to CPO prices and the rupiah. Tax disputes at PT First Lamandau Timber International and PT Brahma Binabakti remain unresolved, and the absence of consolidated P&L line items prevents margin and tax-rate analysis. Without market data, valuation multiples cannot be computed. We rate TAPG a **HOLD (Qualified)** pending the release of half-year consolidated earnings and a clearing price for the shares.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration data in the filing is fragmented across multiple tables. PT Sinar Alam Permai accounted for 12.48% of sales (Rp 311,094 million) and PT LDC East Indonesia for 7.17% (Rp 178,590 million). PT Multimas Nabati Asahan is identified in the context notes as a new major customer in 2026 with no sales in 2025. Other disclosed customer percentages—including PT Kutai Refinery Nusantara at 17.82% and PT Energi Unggul Persada at 10.02%—attach to smaller absolute amounts, suggesting they relate to a different reporting scope or subsidiary-level disclosure rather than group consolidated revenue. All named customers are Indonesian-domiciled PT entities, indicating purely domestic revenue recognition. Segment composition is overwhelmingly palm oil: the palm oil segment’s external sales of Rp 2,486,623 million dwarf the rubber segment’s Rp 5,574 million, with no internal sales disclosed in the current-period segment table.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The revenue runway is tied to mature estate productivity and CPO pricing, neither of which is disclosed in the forward-looking statements. Biological assets recorded a fair value gain of Rp 20,896 million in Q1 2026 versus a loss of Rp 42,622 million for full-year 2025, removing a prior headwind but not indicating a durable valuation trend. Inventories are fully insured for Rp 572,320 million and no obsolescence allowance is carried, suggesting management expects full realizable value. Post-period commodity price moves are not disclosed. Watch items include the pending Supreme Court judicial review on the PT FLTI tax dispute and the group’s unhedged exposure to a 10% USD/IDR move, which Note 40 estimates could impact income by Rp 63,542 million.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

The crown jewel is the palm oil segment, which holds assets of Rp 14,876,192 million (essentially the group’s operating base) and generated segment net profit of Rp 678,083 million in Q1 2026. AMP, the 94.93% holding vehicle, sits atop this structure with assets of Rp 9.3 trillion and Q1 sales of Rp 2.49 trillion. The operating footprint spans 20+ plantation and mill subsidiaries concentrated in Kalimantan and Sumatra. Planted oil palm area is stable at 82,671 hectares, and the group harvested an estimated 164,124 tons of FFB in the quarter. Key external customers include PT Sinar Alam Permai and PT LDC East Indonesia; no single external customer exceeds 13% of disclosed sales concentration. No licenses, royalty rates, or reserve tonnages are disclosed in the extracted data.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

Tax disputes create contingent overhang: PT FLTI faces a remaining Rp 17,133 million tax assessment under Supreme Court judicial review, and PT BBB has appealed SKPLB and SKPKB assessments for 2021 and 2023. The biocoke associate PT ATP Bio Indonesia had zero sales in Q1 2026 and recorded a loss of Rp 26,423 million, raising questions about the timeline to commercialization. Coldspace Pte. Ltd. was diluted to 22.87% in August 2025 after not participating in a new share issuance, reducing the group’s influence in that logistics platform. Mining subsidiaries PT Persada Membangun Bangsa and PT Bintang Buana Pertiwi hold negligible assets (Rp 7,228 million and Rp 246 million) and appear non-core.

### 7.4 Growth Options

Near-term growth is limited. Planted area was flat quarter-over-quarter, and no new land acquisition or mill expansion is disclosed. The biocoke project and Coldspace stake represent optionality but are currently earnings-dilutive. Financial optionality exists through undrawn facilities: BNI working capital up to Rp 300,000 million (expires August 2026), DBS uncommitted revolving credit up to Rp 500,000 million (expires July 2026), and Mandiri working capital up to Rp 200,000 million (expires December 2026). This Rp 1,000,000 million liquidity backstop, combined with Rp 2.8 trillion of cash, provides dry powder for acquisitions if CPO sector distress emerges.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Related-party receivables from associates and JVs total Rp 1,168 million (ATP Bio Rp 1,094 million; SMG, GCM, HHK, and SLM combined Rp 74 million). Related-party payables to the same JVs total Rp 18,308 million. The group purchases raw materials from these JVs—Rp 31,208 million from SMG, Rp 15,753 million from GCM, and Rp 15,331 million from HHK—indicating a vertically integrated supply chain where JVs feed upstream into consolidated mills. Other payables include Rp 1,014 million to Dana Pensiun Triputra, an entity under common control. An investment in shares of PT Kreasi Pangan Samadhi (same key management) is carried at Rp 26,011 million. Intercompany eliminations are not quantified, but the absence of internal sales in the palm oil segment table suggests they are netted out at the group level.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths
The balance sheet is the clearest bull case. Cash of Rp 2.8 trillion exceeds total loans by Rp 1.7 trillion, producing a gross cash surplus that is rare in the plantation sector. The current ratio of 2.73× and quick ratio of 2.14× provide deep liquidity buffers. Debt-to-equity of 0.24× and loans-to-equity of 0.09× mean the group is effectively unlevered. Free cash flow of Rp 516,721 million in the quarter confirms capital discipline. Undrawn committed and uncommitted facilities from BNI, DBS, and Mandiri total at least Rp 1,000,000 million, providing additional liquidity without collateral encumbrance.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths
Palm oil segment profitability is robust at the disclosed segment level: Rp 678,083 million of segment net profit against external sales of Rp 2,486,623 million implies strong unit economics, though the exact margin is not reported. The estate base is mature and stable at 82,671 hectares, and FFB harvest in Q1 was 164,124 tons. Trade receivables are 100% current, inventory is fully insured, and no fixed assets are pledged as collateral, preserving operational flexibility. The vertically integrated model—upstream JVs supplying raw materials to consolidated mills—should theoretically insulate input costs.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths
TAPG’s controlling shareholder, PT Triputra Investindo Arya, increased its stake slightly during the period, signaling alignment. The group’s cash hoard and undrawn credit lines provide M&A optionality in a cyclical downturn. The BNI facility at 6.00% is competitively priced, and covenants (net debt/equity ≤3.00×, net debt/EBITDA ≤4.00×) are comfortably complied with. The absence of material litigation and the clean receivables aging support governance quality.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **MODERATE**: OCF declined 15.4% year-over-year and collections from customers fell 5.6%, suggesting softer cash conversion or delayed receipts despite the clean aging table.
- **MODERATE**: Trade payables dropped 23.3% while other advances more than doubled, potentially indicating a shift from supplier credit to prepayments that could strain working capital if sustained.
- **LOW**: Rubber segment losses widened to Rp 1,633 million from Rp 253 million in the prior year (per note), with negligible revenue contribution.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH**: The consolidated income statement is not disclosed in the extracted data, preventing calculation of gross margin, operating margin, effective tax rate, and interest coverage. Investors are flying blind on corporate-level cost structure.
- **MODERATE**: Tax disputes at PT FLTI (Rp 17,133 million pending Supreme Court) and PT BBB (objections rejected; appeals filed April 2026) create contingent cash calls and management distraction.
- **MODERATE**: Zero hedging on FX, interest rates, or commodity prices per Notes 39 and 40. A 10% USD/IDR depreciation would hit income by Rp 63,542 million, and a 50bp rate rise would cost Rp 350 million.
- **MODERATE**: Biological asset fair value volatility and absence of deferred tax asset recognition on Rp 61,600 million of tax loss carryforwards due to uncertainty.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **MODERATE**: CPO price and export levy dynamics are not quantified in the notes but represent the dominant earnings driver. Any adverse policy shift on biodiesel mandates or export taxes would flow directly to the unhedged bottom line.
- **LOW**: PSAK 118 and PSAK 119 become effective January 2027; the group is still assessing the impact, creating a minor disclosure overhang.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
- **HIGH**: No market data was provided, making it impossible to assess whether the stock trades at a premium or discount to peers on P/E, P/B, or EV/EBITDA.
- **MODERATE**: Basic EPS declined ~9.8% year-over-year. If this trend persists, annualized earnings will fall short of prior-year consensus unless CPO prices rally sharply in H1.

## 12. Management and Governance

### 12.1 Leadership
Management names and board composition are not disclosed in the extracted filing data. The analysis therefore cannot comment on individual track records or tenure.

### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary
Management approved an additional capital contribution to PT Alam Teduh Sentosa in December 2025 per Notarial Deed No. 5. PT Triputra Investindo Arya increased its shareholding slightly (Note 24), reinforcing the controlling shareholder’s position. Key management compensation rose sharply to Rp 20,707 million from Rp 16,222 million, a 27.6% increase that outpaces both inflation and the disclosed earnings decline. On the financing front, management secured a new BNI facility of Rp 830,000 million at 6.00% in September 2025 and fully repaid OCBC term loans, leaving only CIMB Niaga (Rp 280,000 million) and BNI (Rp 808,820 million per Note 22, though Note 40 cross-checks suggest Rp 808,820 million is the correct long-term principal) as material lenders.

Employee headcount expanded to 17,726 from 17,172, a 3.2% increase that may reflect estate maintenance or seasonal labor but adds to the fixed-cost base. No material litigation is flagged. Related-party transactions are moderate and predominantly with JVs under common control or key management, but the amounts are small relative to the balance sheet.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

The group reduced marketable securities exposure as bonds matured, shifting liquidity into time deposits and cash. New advances for investments of Rp 19,360 million suggest incremental deployment into non-core or early-stage assets.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 8/10
The balance sheet is exceptionally strong: net cash-like, low leverage, deep liquidity, and positive free cash flow. The score is not a 10 because OCF is declining and the financing cash flow reconciliation gap raises minor treasury transparency questions.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 6/10
The palm oil segment generates solid segment profit, but group-level margin and volume/price data are opaque. EPS is down year-over-year, and the rubber segment is loss-making. Receivables and inventory management appear disciplined.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 4/10
Planted area is flat, AMP sales contracted 4.9%, and biocoke/Coldspace options are non-revenue contributing. The only growth vectors are financial (M&A optionality) rather than organic volume or acreage expansion.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 3/10
No market data, no EV, no multiples. The declining EPS trend and lack of price inputs make the stock unassessable on a quantitative valuation basis.

### 13.5 Risk Profile
**MODERATE**. The balance sheet de-risks credit and liquidity concerns, but earnings visibility is poor, commodity/FX exposures are naked, and tax disputes present contingent liabilities. The stock is suitable only for investors willing to underwrite CPO price direction and accept interim-reporting opacity.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
TAPG’s balance sheet is a sector outlier. With Rp 2.8 trillion of cash, minimal debt, and Rp 1 trillion of undrawn facilities, the group can acquire distressed plantation assets in a down-cycle without diluting equity. If CPO prices rebound, the palm oil segment’s implied operating leverage—evidenced by Rp 678 billion of segment net profit on Rp 2.5 trillion of external sales—could drive a sharp EPS recovery from the depressed Q1 base. The captive JV supply chain and clean receivables aging support operational stability.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Without consolidated P&L disclosure, investors cannot verify whether corporate overheads, biological fair value swings, or tax charges are eroding the segment-level profitability that appears healthy. AMP sales and net profit both declined year-over-year, and basic EPS followed suit. Unhedged FX and commodity exposure means any rupiah depreciation or CPO price drop flows directly to the bottom line. Biocoke and associate investments are currently cash traps. If the tax disputes resolve unfavorably, one-off charges could further depress sentiment.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. Resolution of the PT FLTI tax dispute at the Supreme Court.
2. H1 2026 CPO price trajectory and Indonesian export policy announcements.
3. Disclosure of full consolidated semi-annual P&L, which would restore margin transparency.
4. Any announcement on planted area expansion, mill upgrades, or accretive M&A funded by the cash balance.
5. Decision on FY2026 dividend policy.

### 14.4 Recommendation
**HOLD (Qualified)**. Target price range: **Not determinable** — the absence of market data and consolidated earnings prevents a quantitative fair-value estimate. Key catalysts are the H1 2026 commodity price environment, tax court rulings, and the release of full semi-annual financials. Review trigger: a material CPO price move of greater than 15% or publication of H1 2026 audited consolidated statements.
