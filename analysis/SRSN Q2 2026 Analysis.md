# SRSN — Q2 2026
---

*All figures in thousand Indonesian Rupiah (IDR '000). Financials are unaudited interim statements for the six-month period ended 30 June 2026 (H1 2026), with comparative H1 2025. Balance-sheet comparisons are to 31 December 2025 (prior fiscal year-end).*

## 1. Executive Summary

SRSN’s H1 2026 results deliver a sharp earnings inflection masked by a softer top line. Revenue contracted 9.5% year-on-year to IDR 557.6 billion, driven by a double-digit decline in the core ethanol segment. Yet gross profit surged 32.1% to IDR 166.3 billion, lifting the disclosed gross margin to 29.82%. Operating profit more than doubled (+112.5%) to IDR 94.6 billion, and net profit rose 190.8% to IDR 72.1 billion, translating to basic EPS of IDR 11.97 versus IDR 4.12 in H1 2025. The divergence between revenue and profitability points to a severe input-cost relief or mix effect that the interim notes do not fully itemise.

The balance sheet has been transformed by aggressive deleveraging. All short-term bank loans were extinguished by June 2026—dropping to zero from IDR 95.0 billion at December 2025—leaving only IDR 6.2 billion in non-current loans against a cash balance that quintupled to IDR 32.0 billion. Total equity grew 8.1% to IDR 875.3 billion, and the current ratio stands at 5.46x. Inventories were drawn down 44.6% to IDR 171.8 billion, releasing substantial working capital, while prepaid taxes fell to zero.

Cash generation nearly doubled: operating cash flow hit IDR 128.9 billion, with collections running at 100.35% of revenue and cash conversion at 1.79x net profit. Reported free cash flow was negative IDR 95.0 billion only because financing outflows—principally IDR 168.8 billion of loan repayments and IDR 6.6 billion of dividends—absorbed the operating surplus. The cash balance still closed at IDR 32.0 billion, up from IDR 6.3 billion at December 2025.

No market data were provided, so valuation multiples and a target price are indeterminate. Operationally, the margin recovery and balance-sheet clean-up are credit-positive, but the revenue contraction and absence of disclosed production volumes or pricing drivers keep us cautious. We assign a **HOLD (Unqualified)** recommendation pending an observable market-price anchor and confirmation that Q3 2026 revenue stabilises.

The top three named customers—Tanduay Distillers, Inc (IDR 55.8 billion), Everlasting Harvest PTE. A domestic versus external revenue split is not provided.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Raw-material supply visibility exists through molasses purchase contracts for the 2026 harvest with PT Kebon Agung, PT Perkebunan Nusantara IX–XII, and PT PG Rajawali II, disclosed at IDR 152,100 thousand. The absolute amount appears nominal relative to revenue, suggesting the figure may represent a deposit, a unit-tonnage reference, or a partial contract value; the interim notes do not clarify the unit intent. No reserve life, capacity runway, or post-period pricing updates were disclosed. The critical watch item is whether ethanol demand from the disclosed customer base rebounds in Q3 or whether the H1 contraction reflects a structural demand step-down.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

- **Delevered balance sheet:** Short-term bank loans are zero; the only remaining interest-bearing debt is IDR 6.2 billion non-current (likely lease liabilities), against IDR 32.0 billion cash.
- **Extreme liquidity:** Current ratio of 5.46x and quick ratio of 3.88x provide a deep buffer against operational or funding shocks.
- **High equity cushion:** Equity ratio of 86.4% and debt-to-equity of 0.16x leave essentially no refinancing risk.
- **OCF quality:** Cash conversion of 1.79x and collections at 100.3% of revenue indicate that reported profits are being collected.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- **Margin inflection:** Gross margin expanded to 29.8% (disclosed) from an undisclosed prior level, while operating profit more than doubled on lower revenue, demonstrating severe cost leverage.
- **Ethanol profitability:** Segment gross profit for ethanol rose 34.1% despite a 13.5% sales decline, suggesting a favourable cost or price realisation swing.
- **Interest burden eliminated:** Interest coverage is effectively ~48x after the short-term debt repayment, freeing future earnings for reinvestment or dividends.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- **Secured undrawn facilities:** The BCA revolving facility (max IDR 385 billion) and overdraft (max IDR 30 billion) remain in place, secured by land, inventories, and machinery, providing contingent liquidity.
- **Supply contracts:** Molasses offtake agreements for the 2026 harvest are in place with major plantation groups, offering raw-material visibility.
- **No NCI leakage:** 100% of equity and profit accrues to owners.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

- **HIGH — Revenue contraction in core ethanol:** A 13.5% drop in ethanol sales (IDR 69.0 billion absolute) is the dominant top-line risk. Without disclosed volume or price data, it is unclear whether this is a demand destruction event or a deliberate volume sacrifice to maximise margin.
- **HIGH — Customer concentration opacity:** The top three named customers aggregate IDR 167.0 billion of sales. If any one of them reduces offtake, the revenue impact could be material, yet individual concentration percentages are not disclosed.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

- **MODERATE — Limited growth pipeline:** Aside from the IDR 7.5 billion CIP project, there are no disclosed capacity expansions, reserves, or second-leg ventures. The company is essentially a single-product ethanol play with a small acetic acid adjunct.
- **MODERATE — Legacy commercial paper:** The IDR 4.8 billion of 1996 commercial paper at 20.75% remains unresolved. A sudden claim or legal action could create a contingent cash outflow or P&L hit.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

- **MODERATE — Molasses supply chain dependency:** Feedstock is sourced from state-linked plantations (PT Perkebunan Nusantara IX–XII). Any regulatory change, export restriction on molasses, or crop failure would squeeze input availability.
- **LOW — FX exposure:** Net foreign-currency monetary assets are IDR 67.1 billion, and USD payables have risen, but the overall FX position is not large relative to the balance sheet.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

Ethanol margins have structurally inflected: the company is earning IDR 146.4 billion of segment gross profit on 13.5% lower ethanol sales, suggesting a new cost or price regime. With the balance sheet now net cash and undrawn secured facilities of IDR 415 billion available, SRSN has optionality to fund an acetic acid scale-up, acquire distressed feedstock assets, or return capital aggressively. If revenue stabilises in Q3, the operating leverage could drive full-year ROE above 35%.

### 14.2 Bear Case

The revenue decline is not a choice but a demand signal. If Tanduay, Nippon Shokubai, or Everlasting Harvest reduce offtake further, the fixed-cost base will eventually overwhelm margins. There is no disclosed reserve or resource base to guarantee long-term ethanol viability, and the 1996 commercial paper could re-emerge as a liability. Without a second leg, SRSN is a melting ice cube with temporarily fat margins.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **Q3 2026 revenue print:** Does ethanol sales stabilise or accelerate?
2. **CIP completion (Sep 2026):** What capacity or cost benefit does the IDR 7.5 billion project deliver?
3. **Commercial paper resolution:** Any movement on the 1996 Bakrie Sekuritas paper could create a one-off.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Unqualified).** The operational turnaround and balance-sheet repair are genuine, but the revenue contraction and complete absence of market data prevent us from sizing risk versus reward. We decline to assign a target price range until a share price and peer multiples are available. **Review trigger:** Market data disclosure or Q3 2026 revenue volume that confirms ethanol demand has stabilised.
