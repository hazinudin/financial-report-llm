# PT Pulau Subur Tbk (PTPS) — Q1 2026
---

*Reporting currency: Indonesian Rupiah (IDR), full units. Financial statements are unaudited interim statements for the period ended March 31, 2026, authorized for issue on April 30, 2026.*

## 1. Executive Summary

PT Pulau Subur Tbk reported a severe operational contraction in Q1 2026, with revenue collapsing 48.3% year-over-year to Rp10,133,512,105 from Rp19,615,375,460 in Q1 2025. The topline implosion drove a swing from net profit of Rp9,551,922,341 (EPS Rp4.41) to a net loss of Rp431,542,433 (EPS negative Rp0.20). Gross profit fell 76.9% to Rp2,581,275,134 as COGS proved sticky, declining only 10.7% despite the revenue halving. The fact sheet does not disclose an operating profit line, but G&A expense alone consumed Rp1,496,373,647—58% of gross profit—leaving minimal headroom before financing items. Pre-tax profit was just Rp68,410,680, and a tax benefit of Rp499,953,113 produced an effective tax rate of negative 730.8%, further underscoring the loss-making dynamic.

The balance sheet, however, remains a fortress. Cash and cash equivalents stood at Rp63,210,004,564, down only Rp2.0 billion quarter-on-quarter, against total liabilities of merely Rp2,873,493,090. The current ratio is 33.2x, the quick ratio 33.0x, and the equity ratio 98.4%. All bank loans were fully repaid during 2024; the only remaining financial liability is a vehicle lease with PT Dipo Star Finance at Rp712,271,632. Total equity declined a marginal 0.25% QoQ to Rp180,372,226,324. Net debt is not formally disclosed because securities are null, but cash dwarfs total loans by roughly 89x, implying a net-cash structure.

Cash flow turned deeply negative. Operating cash flow was negative Rp1,725,792,388 versus positive Rp8,296,193,813 in Q1 2025, a deterioration of 120.8%. Collections from customers fell to Rp9,872,203,294, broadly tracking the revenue decline, but payments to suppliers more than doubled to Rp7,109,294,585—largely reflecting the settlement of PT Anugerah Mustika Ostindo payables—while salary outflows rose to Rp2,916,515,246. Free cash flow was negative Rp1,730,549,145 after negligible capex of Rp4,756,757. Financing cash outflows were limited to lease repayments of Rp293,338,321 and interest paid of Rp21,733,679; no dividends were paid in the quarter.

Operationally, the company is a single-customer, single-product micro-scale palm oil grower. TBS production was 3,040 MT in the quarter, and planted area is 1,090.64 hectares. PT Gelumbang Agro Sentosa represented approximately 91% of Q1 2026 sales. A Rp42,565,850,430 palm oil mill construction contract with PT Sejahtera Jaya Abadi—scheduled for completion by May 2025—remains the critical capital commitment and strategic optionality, though verification of its current status is required. No market data was provided for the stock, so valuation multiples and a target price range are not disclosed. We assign a **HOLD (HIGH RISK)** rating: the balance sheet provides downside protection, but the income statement and cash flow trajectory are unsustainable without revenue stabilization and mill commissioning.

The company disclosed TBS production of 3,040 MT for the three months ended March 31, 2026. Prior-period production volume is not disclosed, preventing a direct volume growth calculation. ## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

The balance sheet is the primary defensive asset. Cash of Rp63.2 billion exceeds total liabilities of Rp2.9 billion, giving the company a negative enterprise value on an accounting basis. The current ratio of 33.2x and equity ratio of 98.4% are exceptional. All bank debt was extinguished in 2024; the only remaining obligation is a vehicle lease with PT Dipo Star Finance at Rp712.3 million. There is no interest-rate risk from floating-rate bank loans.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

Collections are pristine: the collections ratio is 97.4%, DSO is 18.0 days, and all receivables were settled post-period on April 8, 2026. Inventory turns are rapid at 3.3 days, minimizing carrying risk. The company maintains insurance coverage for bearer plants (Rp94.2 billion) and fixed assets/inventories (Rp5.8 billion).

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

The 30% stake in PT Sumber Enim Alam Lestari provides exposure to a separate palm oil estate with Rp36.7 billion in assets. The construction of a 10-ton-per-hour palm oil mill, if completed and commissioned, offers vertical integration and margin capture that the company currently lacks as a pure FFB seller. The planted area of 1,090.64 hectares provides a tangible biological asset base.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — Revenue Collapse and Customer Concentration:** Revenue fell 48.3% and roughly 91% of sales flow through a single customer, PT Gelumbang Agro Sentosa. Any reduction in offtake or pricing pressure from this counterparty would immediately threaten viability.

**HIGH — Negative Operating Cash Flow:** OCF burned Rp1.7 billion in Q1 2026. If this continues, the Rp63.2 billion cash pile erodes over time, especially if the palm oil mill requires additional capital to complete.

**MODERATE — Tax Dispute:** An overpayment of Article 25 income tax of Rp2.27 billion for FY2025 remains under discussion with the Tax Office. The SKPKB history for FY2022 (settled August 2025) indicates regulatory friction.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**HIGH — Lack of Revenue Diversification:** With only one active customer and no disclosed export sales, the business model lacks resilience. The scale is micro (1,090 hectares, 3,040 MT quarterly production), limiting bargaining power.

**MODERATE — Associate Losses:** SEAL reported a net loss of Rp247.5 million in Q1 2026, and PTPS recognized Rp74.3 million as its share. While the loss narrowed from FY2025 levels, the associate is not contributing to group profitability.

**LOW — Post-Employment Liabilities:** The post-employment benefit liability rose to Rp545,150,072 from Rp494,695,543, with a Q1 expense of Rp100,624,829. The weighted average duration is 18.81 years, creating a long-dated tail risk.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

**MODERATE — Commodity Price Volatility:** FFB prices are not disclosed, but palm oil is a globally traded commodity. A sustained downturn in CPO pricing would compress already-thin realized prices.

**MODERATE — Regulatory and Governance:** The tax amnesty asset of Rp5,123,658,296 is recognized as additional paid-in capital. Any clawback or reassessment could impair equity. The resignation of Mr. Budiman Ong from the Board of Directors in April 2026 adds transition risk.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

No market data was provided, so traditional multiples cannot be assessed. However, negative earnings invalidate P/E-based valuation, and the lack of a share price prevents P/B analysis. The stock is effectively uninvestable on a earnings-based approach until revenue and margins stabilize.

## 12. Management and Governance

### 12.1 Leadership

The Board of Commissioners and Board of Directors are in place, though Mr. Budiman Ong resigned effective April 2026. Total remuneration for key management personnel in Q1 2026 was Rp254,423,550 (Board of Commissioners Rp121,829,100; Board of Directors Rp132,594,450). This is modest relative to the cash balance but represents a fixed cost amid collapsing revenue.

### 12.2 Management Actions and Commentary

Management did not declare a dividend in Q1 2026, a prudent cash-preservation step given the net loss. They continued to amortize the PT Dipo Star Finance lease and advanced the machinery construction in progress to 83.49% completion. No new leases were entered. The financial statements were authorized for issue on April 30, 2026.

### 12.3 Governance Observations

PT Sekawan Kontrindo controls 78.54% of shares, while the public float is 20.76%. Total shares outstanding per the register are 2,167,514,856, though only 2,157,712,165 are used in the EPS calculation. Only 14,856 of 225,000,000 Series I warrants were converted in 2024 at Rp218 per share, indicating minimal dilution but also weak warrant demand. The controlling shareholder also receives office rent of Rp13.5 million per quarter.

### 12.4 Capital Allocation

Capital allocation has shifted to defense. Dividends totaling Rp29.8 billion were paid for FY2023 and FY2024, but no payout was made in Q1 2026. Capex was negligible. Statutory reserves stood at Rp8,670,000,000 as of December 31, 2024. The largest capital commitment remains the Rp42.6 billion mill construction. If management completes and commissions this asset, capital allocation will shift from cash preservation to operational reinvestment.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 6/10

The balance sheet is fortress-like—net cash, zero bank debt, and a 98% equity ratio—but the negative operating cash flow and net loss are unsustainable indefinitely. The score reflects strength in liquidity offset by erosion in earnings power.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 3/10

Revenue halved, customer concentration is absolute, and production scale is minimal. The company is not currently generating value from operations. Collections and inventory management are efficient, but they cannot compensate for a broken cost structure at this revenue level.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 2/10

There is no visible revenue growth. Volume and price headwinds dominate, and the associate is loss-making. The only growth vector is the palm oil mill, which is already delayed past its May 2025 target.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 4/10

Market data is absent, preventing multiple-based assessment. Negative earnings and a tiny float complicate valuation. The theoretical book value of Rp83.59 per share is the only anchor, but without a market price it is meaningless.

### 13.5 Risk Profile

**HIGH.** The combination of single-customer concentration, commodity exposure, negative OCF, and delayed strategic projects creates an elevated risk profile. The balance sheet provides a buffer, but it does not eliminate operational risk.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

The net-cash balance sheet provides a hard floor; at Rp63.2 billion in cash versus Rp2.9 billion in total liabilities, the equity is technically backed by liquid assets. Completion of the palm oil mill could transform the business model from low-margin FFB sales to higher-margin CPO and kernel extraction, while reducing dependence on third-party millers. Post-period collection of all receivables confirms that working capital is not trapped. If revenue recovers to even Rp15–18 billion per quarter, the fixed cost base could yield meaningful operating leverage.

### 14.2 Bear Case

If PT Gelumbang Agro Sentosa reduces offtake further or demands lower prices, revenue could fall below cash breakeven, forcing the company to burn cash quarter after quarter. The palm oil mill may require additional capital to complete, and further delays would leave the company as a pure, sub-scale FFB grower with no processing margin. Associate losses at SEAL, though narrowing, continue to drag equity-accounted returns. Without revenue diversification, the equity is a call option on a single customer relationship.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. **Mill Commissioning:** Confirmation that the PT Sejahtera Jaya Abadi mill is complete and operational.
2. **Q2 Revenue Recovery:** Evidence that Q1 was a seasonal or contractual low, with revenue rebounding above Rp12 billion.
3. **Dividend Policy:** Clarification on whether the board will resume distributions or preserve cash for the mill.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (HIGH RISK).** The balance sheet is strong enough to prevent immediate distress, but the income statement and cash flow are deteriorating. We require evidence of revenue stabilization and mill completion before upgrading. **Target price range: Not determinable — market data not provided.** **Review trigger:** Q2 2026 earnings release or public announcement of palm oil mill operationalization.
