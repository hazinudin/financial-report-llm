## CITA (PT Cita Mineral Investindo Tbk) — Correlation with Aluminium Price Rally

### Company Overview

CITA is an Indonesian **bauxite mining** company listed on the IDX. Its entire revenue derives from bauxite sales. It holds a **39.25% equity stake** in **PT Well Harvest Winning Alumina Refinery (WHWAR)** — an alumina refinery in West Kalimantan — accounted for under the equity method. WHWAR is CITA's single largest asset and the primary driver of consolidated profit.

### Value Chain Position

```
Bauxite (CITA mines) → Alumina (WHWAR refines) → Aluminium (smelters produce)
          ↑                      ↑                        ↑
     CITA's revenue          CITA's equity income       Market price driver
```

CITA sits at the **very start** of the aluminium value chain. The aluminium price rally cascades upstream: higher LME aluminium prices → higher alumina prices → higher bauxite demand/prices.

### Direct Correlation to Aluminium Price Rally

**1. Bauxite → Alumina → Aluminium Price Transmission**
- Virtually 100% of CITA's bauxite sales go to WHWAR (99.93% in FY2025, 100% in FY2024). WHWAR converts bauxite into alumina (aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃), which is the key feedstock for aluminium smelting.
- As aluminium prices rise, alumina refinery margins expand, enabling WHWAR to absorb higher bauxite input costs. This benefits CITA directly through higher bauxite selling prices and volume.

**2. WHWAR — The Crown Jewel**
- CITA's stake in WHWAR (Rp 3.58 trillion) represents **39.25% of total consolidated assets** and is the **single largest value driver**.
- CITA's share of WHWAR's net profit in FY2025 was **Rp 1.83 trillion** — exceeding CITA's own consolidated operating profit and representing the majority of total profit.
- The alumina refinery sits one step closer to the LME aluminium price, making CITA's equity pickup from WHWAR **highly leveraged to alumina spreads**.

**3. Alumina Market Tightening**
- The Strait of Hormuz disruption affects ~1.2–1.4M tons/year of alumina supply to Iranian smelters. With Gulf smelters cutting output, freed-up alumina could be redirected — but the net effect is alumina market tightness.
- Indonesia is a **growing alumina producer**. WHWAR's plant in West Kalimantan is well-positioned to benefit from displaced demand if Gulf alumina flows are disrupted.
- New alumina capacity in Indonesia (Mempawah SGAR, Jinjiang) was ramping in 2025, but Hormuz disruption changes the calculus: alumina that might have gone to the Gulf now needs alternative buyers, **or** Gulf-origin demand shifts to Indonesian sources.

**4. Revenue Growth Already Reflecting Pre-Conflict Upcycle**
- FY2025 net sales grew 12.6% YoY, reflecting the aluminium commodity upcycle that was building through 2025 (LME averaged ~$2,575/t in 2025 per Reuters poll, up from $2,419/t in 2024).
- The FY2025 results **pre-date** the Middle East conflict (which escalated in early 2026), meaning the true price shock benefit has not yet been captured in reported numbers.

**5. Margin Compression Signal**
- COGS grew 16.6% YoY, outpacing the 12.6% revenue growth, compressing gross margin from ~44.5% to ~42.5%.
- The largest COGS component — hauling and overburden (Rp 1.12T) — rose 6.9%, while fuel costs rose 20.5% and clearing costs surged 67%. This suggests cost pressures that could intensify with the conflict-driven energy inflation.
- However, selling expenses fell 36.7% (from Rp 329T to Rp 208T), primarily due to the elimination of a large loading/transportation expense (Rp 148T in FY2024, zero in FY2025), which offset COGS growth.

**6. New Customer Diversification**
- In November 2025, CITA signed a contract with **PT Borneo Alumina Prima (BAP)** to supply bauxite, reducing its near-total dependence on WHWAR. This is strategically important as alumina demand diversifies beyond a single offtaker.

**7. Indonesian Bauxite Export Ban Dynamics**
- Indonesia has banned raw bauxite exports (since June 2023 for washed bauxite with Al₂O₃ ≥42%). This policy forces domestic processing, ensuring WHWAR and other domestic refiners like BAP are captive demand sources for CITA's bauxite.
- The export ban aligns with Indonesia's downstreaming policy and means CITA's bauxite offtake is structurally secured domestically — but the price CITA can charge is linked to alumina (and indirectly aluminium) prices.

**8. Functional Currency = USD**
- CITA's functional currency is USD (though it reports in IDR). This means aluminium price movements in USD translate directly to revenue, with limited FX dilution from the Rupiah.

### Key Risks & Catalysts Specific to CITA

| Factor | Impact Direction | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Aluminium price surge** | Strongly positive | Directly increases alumina and bauxite pricing power |
| **Alumina supply gap from Hormuz** | Positive | Could increase WHWAR alumina margins as displaced Gulf demand seeks alternatives |
| **Alba/Qatalum shutdown** | Mixed | Reduces alumina demand from GCC smelters but tightens overall alumina/ Aluminium balance |
| **China alumina oversupply risk** | Negative | If Iranian smelters stay offline, diverted alumina from India/China could oversupply the market, pressuring alumina prices down |
| **Indonesian mining regulation** | Negative risk | Changes in mining royalties (already 7% of sales value) or export policy could erode margins |
| **Cost inflation** | Negative | Hauling, fuel, and clearing costs are rising; conflict-driven energy inflation could worsen this |
| **WHWAR dividend sustainability** | Monitor | Dividends from WHWAR fell 23.6% YoY (Rp 1.76T → Rp 1.34T) despite higher equity income; watch for cash return vs. reinvestment balance |

### Bottom Line for Equity Analysis

CITA provides **leveraged exposure to the aluminium supply shock** through two channels:

1. **Direct channel**: Higher bauxite selling prices to WHWAR and BAP as alumina prices rise
2. **Equity channel**: The ~39.25% stake in WHWAR captures alumina refinery margin expansion — this is where the bulk of CITA's value lies (investment in associate = 39.25% of total assets, equity income = ~76% of total profit)

FY2025 financials already show the early stage of the commodity upcycle. The **full impact of the 2026 aluminium/alumina price shock is not yet in the numbers**. CITA's FY2026 earnings could see material upside if:
- Alumina prices remain elevated (currently ~$300/t FOB Australia, up from ~$500/t peak in Dec 2024)
- WHWAR captures higher spreads on its alumina output
- Bauxite selling prices reset higher on annual contracts

The key watchpoint is whether alumina prices — the direct driver of CITA's equity income — sustain above WHWAR's cost curve, and whether WHWAR increases production to fill the gap left by disrupted Gulf refiners.
