## 1. Executive Summary

ITMG delivered a middling quarter: revenue of US$497.6 million, up 3.1% YoY, but gross profit of US$132.8 million fell 1.9% and net profit of US$55.7 million dropped 16.1% against a soft but profitable Q1 2025. Net margin of 11.2% and gross margin of 26.7% look "up" only against the depressed FY2025 blend (26.0% and 10.4% respectively, per external context); the company's own quarterly series shows gross margin compressing from 30% in 4Q25 and 28% in 1Q25 to 27% in 1Q26 — the direction is down, not up. The compression is operational: production fell 12% YoY to 4.7Mt on heavy rain and the group strip ratio jumped to 10.4x from 8.8x in 1Q25 (per the company's 1Q26 analyst presentation), while realised ASP slipped ~3% YoY to US$79.4/t as the HBA benchmark stayed flat QoQ (~US$103.6/t average). Cost of revenue rose 5% to US$364.8m on a mix shift toward own coal.

The quarter's tax line is the most misread item in the raw data: the pipeline stores the income tax expense with a negative sign, producing a spurious "-31.9% effective tax rate." Against the primary filing, income tax was a **normal expense** of US$26.2m (vs US$19.3m in 1Q25, +36% YoY), an effective rate of ~31.9% on PBT of US$81.9m — above the 22% statutory rate and essentially in line with FY2025's 32.4% ETR. There is no deferred tax income and no restitusi windfall in P&L; the low 22.5% ETR of 1Q25 is what flattered the prior-year comparison. The real cash tax story is structural: the group carries roughly US$200m of non-current prepaid taxes (CIT US$99.9m, other taxes US$100.0m) that are effectively refund claims awaiting DJP settlement — a working-capital drag, not an earnings item, and now subject to changed refund mechanics (PER-11/PJ/2025 ending book transfers; PMK 28/2026 preliminary refunds effective 1 May 2026, post-period).

The balance sheet remains the thesis: US$747.2m cash, US$192.3m short-term deposits, US$2.4bn total assets against just US$81.6m of loans and lease liabilities, equity of US$1.92bn and net cash of US$857.9m (net debt-to-equity -44.6%). Total liabilities shrank further to US$480.1m. The two striking balance-sheet moves are regulatory-cash dynamics, not performance: restricted cash rose 90% to US$92.2m on a US$43.8m Q1 placement of reclamation/post-mining time deposits under Kepmen ESDM 344/2025 (replacing bank guarantees), and short-term deposits rose 36% to US$192.3m on a deliberate US$51.2m treasury placement of idle cash. Cash conversion was excellent (OCF US$65.3m, 1.17x net profit; FCF US$53.1m on capex of just US$12.2m), collections ran ahead of revenue (1.05x), and net gearing moved from (40)% to (36)% per the company's presentation after an US$11.7m buyback (8.99m shares) and the 17 April 2026 AGM's US$114.6m final dividend declaration.

Valuation cannot be assessed here: no market data (price, market cap, P/E, P/BV) was provided for this run, so all market-relative metrics are stated as "not disclosed." Fundamentally, this is a wide-moat, net-cash coal exporter with an 8%+ annualized ROA, double-digit annualized ROE (~11.6% total, ~11.5% owners), a five-mine core (IMM, TCM, BEK, GPK, TIS) under CCoW/IUP licenses running to 2028–2041, and an Banpu-parented marketing chain — whose earnings quality this quarter was mediocre and whose cost trajectory (April 2026 diesel shock, strip ratio) points to a tougher 2Q26. We rate the company's financial health and cash generation highly, its operational quarter poorly, and its growth optionality (EBP third-party hauling loss-making, JBG idle, power/gas ventures small) as thin. Recommendation follows in §14.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Revenue | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | % Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net revenue (US$'000) | 497,577 | 482,516 | +3.12% |

Revenue grew 3.1% YoY on volume, not price. Per external context (company 1Q26 analyst presentation and 3M26 news release), sales volumes rose ~6% YoY to 6.3Mt — own coal 5.5Mt (vs 4.8Mt) and third-party coal 0.7Mt (vs 1.1Mt) — while realised ASP fell ~3% YoY to US$79.4/t (vs US$82/t in 1Q25). The Q1 2025 comparative also included a materially different segment mix: BEK's external sales were US$236.9m in 1Q25 vs US$57.2m in 1Q26, while EBP (hauling/trading) booked US$336.7m external sales in 1Q26 vs just US$31.6m in 1Q25 — the group's internal reporting structure changed in 2026 (Note 32; comparatives restated), so segment-level YoY revenue comparisons reflect reorganisation as much as economics. Consolidated totals are unaffected: 1Q26 external sales of US$497.6m reconcile to the segment table.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

- **Volume:** production fell 12% YoY to 4.7Mt (own coal 5.5Mt sold; per the 1Q26 analyst presentation rainfall hampered operations), but sales volumes of 6.3Mt were up ~6% YoY on inventory drawdown and third-party purchases (coal purchases fell to US$41.1m from US$72.1m YoY per Note 25 while inventory change swung to +US$26.2m — the trading leg is now largely an internal-hauling story through EBP).
- **Price:** HBA for 6,322 kcal GAR averaged ~US$103.6/t in Q1 2026 — essentially flat vs Q4 2025's ~US$103.6/t average (per ESDM HBA series via Bisnis reporting). Newcastle 6,000 kcal rose ~10% QoQ to ~US$126/t in March 2026 (FRED quarterly: 126.03 vs 114.21), but ITMG's marketing mix (per the presentation, ~64% fixed-price contracts, ~35% indexed) meant index strength passed through only partially and with a lag — the structural ASP discount to Newcastle reflects quality, calorific basis and freight terms.
- **FX:** the Rupiah's translation rate moved to Rp16,993/US$ at 31 March 2026 (Bank Indonesia middle rate, Note 33); the group's net foreign-currency monetary position swung from net liabilities US$20.3m at 31 Dec 2025 to net assets US$17.3m at 31 Mar 2026 — a modest translation tailwind embedded in "Others, net" (FX gain of US$0.3m in 1Q26 vs a US$6.0m loss in 1Q25).

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Geographic mix (external sales, 1Q26 vs 1Q25, US$'000): Japan 159,577 vs 114,044; China 135,742 vs 122,157; Domestic 73,976 vs 125,667; Philippines 36,341 vs 5,320; India 24,622 vs 56,684; Thailand 22,179 vs 7,497; Bangladesh 14,423 vs 19,416; Vietnam 9,649; Hong Kong 7,851 vs 8,242; Korea 6,661 vs 7,901; Malaysia 6,556. Exports totaled ~US$423.6m (~85% of revenue); Europe (US$8.3m) and Taiwan (US$7.3m) dropped out of the 1Q26 mix vs 1Q25. The big shifts — Japan +40%, Philippines +583% (Sual and Ho-Ping-linked cargoes), domestic -41% — reflect both price/volume and the restated structure. Customer concentration: Marubeni and China Bai Gui together represented US$128.5m (>10% of net revenue, Note 24); the disclosed customer list is dominated by Japanese and Chinese utilities/traders (Sual Power US$12.5m, China Bai Gui US$11.2m, Jera US$8.8m, Marubeni US$8.4m, Zhejiang Energy US$6.3m, Shenhua HK US$5.9m) plus PT PLN (Tanjung Jati B) US$31.6m in Rupiah-denominated domestic sales. Related-party coal sales (Banpu/Banpu Minerals) were 3.47% of net revenue (vs 1.55%), with a US$9.2m Banpu trade receivable newly booked.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Runway is not the issue: mining licenses run to 2035–2041 on the largest mines (BEK CCoW to 29 Jun 2041; TCM to 27 Feb 2035; JBG to 3 May 2035), with IMM to 31 Mar 2028 and the TIS/NPR/GPK IUPs to 2029–2033. Price support arrived mostly after the quarter: FOB Newcastle averaged ~US$126/t in March 2026 and ~US$127.6/t in Q2 2026 on Middle East LNG disruption and fuel switching (per Down To Earth reporting); with ~35% of the book indexed, 2Q26 ASPs should reprice upward, partially offsetting cost pressure. Watch items: (a) the 18 April 2026 Pertamina non-subsidised diesel hike (Dexlite Rp14,900→Rp19,400/litre; mining contractors reporting industrial diesel above Rp23,000/litre vs ~Rp16,000 in 2025, with fuel 38–42% of contractor cost — per Tempo/Kontan reporting) — a clear 2Q26 margin headwind via Pamapersada rates; (b) the ~600Mt 2026 RKAB production quota (per Mirae Asset flash), constraining upside volumes; (c) PP 18/2025 & PP 19/2025 royalty schedules remain in force with the higher June revision deferred (external, medium-confidence); (d) DMO shortfall compensation accruals of US$3.8m (2025) + US$1.2m (2026).

### 3.2 Operating Margin Analysis

The filing does not print an operating-profit line, and the ratios block correspondingly reports operating margin as null (not disclosed). The closest reconstruction from printed lines: gross profit 132,753 − selling expenses 43,356 − G&A 13,109 = **US$76,288k implied operating profit, 15.3% margin**, vs 1Q25: 135,350 − 42,624 − 12,064 = US$80,662k, 16.7%. Per the company's presentation, operating margin fell 17%→15% sequentially and YoY. ### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

Finance income fell 20% YoY to US$9.1m (bank interest US$9.1m vs US$11.5m) — BI rate cuts and lower average deployed cash, despite the US$51.2m treasury placement into term deposits. Finance costs were US$1.26m (vs US$1.35m): loan interest fell to US$0.6m from US$1.1m while lease-liability interest rose to US$0.6m from US$0.1m; short-term lease expense dropped to US$2.8m from US$6.4m. "Others, net" improved to a US$2.3m loss from a US$5.1m loss, entirely an FX story (US$0.3m gain vs US$6.0m loss), partially offset by miscellaneous items swinging to a US$2.6m loss from a US$0.9m gain.

### 5.3 Financing Cash Flow

Loan repayments were US$1.3m (the Permata Rp21.6bn installment). No dividend cash outflow is printed in the fact sheet for Q1 2026 (dividends_paid: null — the US$64.5m final-dividend tranche is payable 19 May 2026, and the interim 2025 dividend of US$50.0m was distributed 26 Nov 2025, both outside the quarter). Net cash use in the quarter of US$60.4m is thus explained: ~US$95m of placements/deposits + ~US$43.8m restricted placements against US$65.3m OCF — see §5.5 reconciliation.

### 5.4 Free Cash Flow Analysis

Reported FCF = OCF − capex = 65,268 − 12,186 = **US$53.082m**, a 10.7% FCF margin on revenue. Stripping one-offs: the underlying "core" FCF is essentially the same number — the treasury and restricted-cash placements are investing-section items already excluded from capex; stripping amortisation (US$11.3m) is an operating add-back that a D&A-adjusted FCF would include. On a 4x annualized basis, quarterly FCF of ~US$53m implies ~US$210m+ annual FCF before any working-capital swings — comfortably funding the US$114.6m final dividend and continued buybacks even in a soft-price year. Caveat: Q1 collections (1.05x) and payables unwind flattered the number; the 2Q26 diesel shock and Pama rate resets are cost headwinds.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — April 2026 diesel shock (post-period):** non-subsidised diesel jumped ~Rp5,000–9,400/litre on 18 April 2026 with industrial diesel reported at Rp23,000–28,000/litre vs ~Rp16,000 in 2025; fuel is 38–42% of contractor cost — a direct 2Q26 COGS headwind through Pamapersada rates (external, medium confidence).
- **HIGH — Strip ratio and rain:** 10.4x group strip ratio (vs 8.8x YoY, 9.3x QoQ) and production -12% YoY; deferred stripping additions of US$29.6m in one quarter signal continued cost pressure if ratios persist.
- **MODERATE — ASP lag:** HBA flat QoQ; realised ASP US$79.4/t (-3% YoY). Newcastle's Q2 strength (~US$127/t) passes through only ~35% of the book on indexation; fixed-price legacy contracts cap upside.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **MODERATE — Prepaid-tax refund drag:** ~US$200m of non-current prepaid taxes (CIT US$99.9m, other US$100.0m) are effectively DJP restitusi claims; PER-11/PJ/2025 ended book transfers, and PMK 28/2026 preliminary-refund procedures only took effect 1 May 2026 — timing risk on recovering this cash sits with the tax authority.
- **MODERATE — Reclamation lock-up growth:** restricted cash US$92.2m and rising; every future guarantee conversion under Kepmen 344/2025 moves distributable cash into 12-month-locked state-bank deposits.
- **LOW — Asset consumption:** capex (US$12.2m) below D&A (US$19.4m+); fully-depreciated assets in use of US$629.5m. A harvest-mode posture, unless CIP conversion in 2026 reverses it.
- **LOW — Kitadin post-mining:** closure obligations now funded by deposits; tail cost is bounded but real.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks
- **HIGH — DMO and PP 15/2022/18/2025 tax adjustments:** sales-adjustment add-ons already cost US$6.4m this quarter (vs US$0.2m in 1Q25) — a growing wedge between statutory and effective tax.
- **MODERATE — RKAB quota:** ~600Mt sector-wide 2026 production quota (external) caps volume upside.
- **MODERATE — Tax disputes:** outstanding assessments across TCM/BEK/KTD/TRUST/IMM (individual items US$0.8–2.3m, mostly under appeal/judicial review) plus ongoing audits (BEK FY2021; IMM/ITM/TCM FY2022; TRUST/BEK/IBP/CPI/IMM/EBP FY2024; various VAT).
- **LOW — DHE SDA:** PP 8/2025 requires 100% of export proceeds >US$250k held in special accounts for 12 months; balances US$220.0m special + US$91.0m time deposits — a liquidity lock, not a loss.
- **LOW — FX sensitivity:** a 5% Rupiah move changes post-tax profit by ~US$0.7m (Note 34); net monetary position now net assets US$17.3m.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns
No market data was provided, so no multiple-based concern can be quantified. Structural points: (a) earnings are commodity-cyclical and the current quarter is a local trough in margins — trailing P/E on 1Q26 annualized would overstate cheapness if 2Q26 diesel costs bite; (b) the equity trades partly as a cash proxy, but ~30% of that cash (restricted US$92.2m + DHE SDA US$311.0m) is encumbered or locked and should be haircut from any net-cash valuation; (c) related-party fee creep (marketing fee 14.0% of selling expenses) and the Banpu receivable build are minority-shareholder-type frictions to monitor.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
- Newcastle at ~US$127/t (Q2 2026) reprices the ~35% indexed book into 2H26 while fixed contracts roll — revenue upside without volume growth.
- The US$858m net cash plus ~US$210m+ annualized FCF fund an 80%+ payout and continued buybacks regardless of the earnings cycle; the equity is a cash-heavy, Banpu-anchored yield instrument.
- Q1's cost spike is weather-driven and transitory (strip 10.4x); a normalisation to ~9x with CIP completion restores ~28–30% gross margin, ~US$0.06/qtr EPS.
- Recovery of the ~US$200m prepaid-tax pile under the faster-refund regime (PMK 28/2026, Rp160trn processed in 4M26 per external reporting) would be a one-off working-capital windfall.

### 14.2 Bear Case
- The April 2026 diesel shock (+Rp5,000–9,400/litre) flows straight into Pama rates; with fuel at 38–42% of contractor cost and strip at 10.4x, 2Q26 gross margin could compress below 25% and the FY2026 dividend trajectory into question.
- Rain-disturbed, RKAB-capped volumes mean the price tailwind cannot be volume-leveraged; production -12% YoY repeats if 2H weather is poor.
- Finance income (US$9.1m/qtr) shrinks with BI rate cuts, removing ~2 points of pretax margin over time while the cash sits increasingly in locked DHE SDA/reclamation deposits — the equity's cash optionality is being gradually collateralised to the state.
- Banpu fee creep and related-party receivable build raise transfer-pricing friction; NCI and minority flows are small but the direction is not encouraging.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. **2Q26 results (July 2026):** Newcastle pass-through vs diesel pass-through — the decisive margin print; watch strip ratio and Pama rate resets.
2. **DJP refund settlements:** recovery timing on ~US$200m prepaid taxes (PMK 28/2026 preliminary-refund regime effective 1 May 2026).
3. **CIP completion in 2026** (US$99.4m road/bridge/water infrastructure) — capacity and unit-cost normalisation.
4. **RKAB 2026 quota administration and royalty revisions** (PP 18/19/2025 status; deferred June schedule).
5. **Buyback pace and FY2026 interim dividend** declaration (November 2026 cycle) as the capital-return signal.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (with a BUY bias on weakness), risk qualifier: commodity-cyclical.** The quarter was operationally weak — gross margin -138 bps YoY and falling sequentially, net profit -16%, cost pressure from strip ratio and now diesel — but the negative ETR "anomaly" was a data artifact, the balance sheet is unassailable (net cash US$858m, 3.6x current), and cash generation (OCF +42%, FCF US$53m) is disconnected from the earnings cycle. Without market data we cannot set a price target; the decision-grade framing for a PM: treat ITMG as a net-cash coal yielder whose earnings trough is visible (Q1–Q2 2026) and whose 2H26 repricing (indexed book into a US$127/t Newcastle tape) is the swing factor against a diesel-driven cost squeeze. Target-price range: **not set (market data not provided)** — re-derive against P/BV 0.8–1.0x of US$1.75 BVPS and 4.5–5.5x annualized owners' EPS when price data is available. **Review trigger:** 2Q26 gross margin below 25%, or Newcastle Newcastle 6,000kcal falling back below US$110/t, or a material DJP assessment against any of the outstanding disputes.
