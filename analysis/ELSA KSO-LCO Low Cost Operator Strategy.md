## 1. Definitions

### KSO — *Kerja Sama Operasi* (Joint Operating Agreement)
A contractual arrangement under which ELSA would act as **field operator** for Pertamina-owned concessions jointly with PHE (Pertamina Hulu Energi, ELSA's 51.1% parent), taking operational control and sharing production economics. Unlike a pure service contract, the operator takes operational control and economic upside from production.

### LCO — **Low Cost Operator**
An operating model where ELSA leverages its **existing in-house services ecosystem** (workover, cementing, chemicals, field operations) to operate fields at structurally lower cost than traditional E&P operators. The economic logic is cost compression through vertical integration: 60–70% of field work requirements are services ELSA already performs in-house, capturing margins that would otherwise go to third-party contractors.

Under Indonesia's cost-recovery PSC regime (SKK Migas reimburses contractor Opex), a structurally lower-cost operator delivers the same work cheaper and keeps the spread — the source of the "structurally higher upstream margin profile."

- **Corporate theme 2026:** "Rediscover Technology and Innovation Age"
- **Synergy partners:** PHE and Pertamina TI&I (Technology, Innovation & Infrastructure)

## 6. How KSO/LCO Boosts the Upstream Line (Mechanics)

1. **Direct operator revenue** — new stream recognized in Integrated Upstream O&G Services segment (geophysical/seismic, drilling, oilfield services per FS Note 34)
2. **Recycled service revenue** — 60–70% of KSO capex flows back as in-house service revenue (workover, cementing, chemicals, field ops): Rp1 of KSO capex ≈ Rp0.6–0.7 of upstream segment revenue
3. **Margin uplift** — LCO captures service-provider margins in-house + operator fee overlay; targets 15–25% Opex efficiency per CEO statement
4. **Revenue duration** — field-life contracts (multi-decade) vs 1–5yr service call-outs → higher revenue visibility, lower discount rate

Rough arithmetic (Brights' implicit math): Rp1.8–2.0tr/yr KSO capex × 60–70% recycling = Rp1.1–1.4tr/yr incremental service revenue, plus operator fee share — potentially lifting upstream revenue from Rp4.14T (FY25A) toward Rp6–7T+ by FY28–29F, above Brights' ex-KSO trajectory of Rp5.6–6.0T.

## 7. Key Unknowns / Risks

1. **Which concessions** — 3–4 of ~41 undeveloped PHE blocks not yet publicly named; PHE KSOT tender (62 CAs, 24 partners) suggests broader scope than East Java alone
2. **Capex magnitude** — Rp1.8–2.0tr/yr is Brights' estimate, not company-confirmed
3. **Timeline** — Brights models production FY27F+; but Dual Completion results (Jul 2026) show uplift already materializing — **Brights' timing may be conservative**
4. **Regulatory approval** — SKK Migas must approve KSO structure
5. **Dividend trade-off** — capex surge (~3x current) would temporarily limit dividend upside (Brights: DPS could be capped)
6. **Related-party concentration** — operating parent's fields deepens the 76.7% related-party revenue exposure flagged in [[ELSA Q1 2026 Analysis]]
7. **Execution risk** — Brights flags KSO/LCO, EOR, fracturing as new-initiative execution risks

## 8. Conclusion

**The KSO/LCO catalyst is company-confirmed strategy, not sell-side speculation.** ELSA's President Director has publicly committed to the LCO transformation with specific 15–25% efficiency targets (Apr 2026); PHE's KSOT Batch I 2026 tender is formally underway (May 2026); and the Dual Completion success at Adera Field (Jul 2026) provides a live proof-of-concept delivering 2.6–3.0x production targets on a mature field.

Remaining open questions: concession selection, capex scale, SKK Migas approval, and timing. Notably, Brights' FY27F+ production timeline may prove conservative given July 2026 results — the LCO thesis appears to be executing ahead of the sell-side model.

**Proper valuation treatment:** Base case should follow Brights' ex-KSO forecasts; KSO/LCO is upside optionality with increasing probability weight as confirmations accumulate. The bull case (Brights TP Rp1,110 / Ajaib TP Rp1,100 vs fair value Rp920–950 from [[ELSA Q1 2026 Analysis]] §10) becomes increasingly defensible if: (1) formal KSO concession awards announced; (2) PHR Zona 4-style results replicate across other mature fields; (3) capex plan disclosed in H2 2026 disclosures.
