## 1. Executive Summary

INKP delivered a mixed but marginally stronger Q1 2026, with revenue rising 4.3% year-on-year to USD 816.3m and net profit advancing 11.4% to USD 156.1m. The standout dynamic is not top-line growth but profitability: gross profit surged 20.6% to USD 281.9m, lifting the gross margin to 34.5% per the computed ratio, while operating profit jumped 42.1% to USD 197.0m as selling and administrative costs contracted in absolute terms. Net margin reached 19.1% and basic EPS rose to USD 0.02853 from USD 0.02561. These results suggest effective cost control and operating leverage, even as the revenue footprint remains heavily concentrated through related-party channels.

The balance sheet shifted decisively during the quarter. Cash and cash equivalents collapsed by more than half, falling to USD 592.9m at Mar 31 2026 from USD 1,224.4m at Dec 31 2025, driven by a massive USD 918.1m investing outflow that far exceeded PPE capex of USD 184.1m. The bulk of this deployment landed in short-term securities, which ballooned to USD 2,702.0m from USD 1,941.9m, effectively converting liquid cash into financial assets. Trade receivables continued their upward march to USD 1,569.3m, with related-party receivables—principally PT Cakrawala Mega Indah at USD 1,082.0m—dominating the book. Working capital remains ample at USD 3,953.6m and the current ratio sits at 2.69x, but the quality of liquidity is deteriorating: cash is down, securities are up, and collections are stretched.

Valuation multiples cannot be assessed because no market data stub was provided; P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, and market capitalization are not disclosed. On a per-share basis, book value is USD 1.28 and annualized ROE is 9.0%. The capital structure carries total liabilities of USD 5,653.9m against equity of USD 7,000.0m, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.81x. However, the net debt ratio as defined—bank loans only less cash and securities—reads negative USD 742.8m, a misleadingly flattering figure because it excludes bonds payable of USD 1,496.5m and sukuk mudharabah of USD 527.5m. When the full interest-bearing stack is considered, the firm is deeply levered.

We rate INKP **HOLD (Qualified)**. The earnings profile is resilient and margins are expanding, but the combination of extreme related-party concentration, a cash-to-securities liquidity transformation, and an opaque financing stack warrants caution. A target price range cannot be established without market data. The review trigger is the Q2 2026 release, specifically any normalization of the receivables-to-cash conversion cycle or material change in securities holdings.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is the defining feature. PT Cakrawala Mega Indah (CMI), a distributor under common control, generated USD 372.6m in local net sales—by far the largest single revenue line item disclosed. Branches of APP contributed a further USD 11.6m in export net sales. Combined related-party sales therefore dominate the revenue book. Trade receivables from related parties totaled USD 1,173.5m (per note split), implying that the bulk of receivables are tied to these channels. Third-party trade receivables were USD 395.7m. No geographic split beyond the related-party export note is provided.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The revenue base is underpinned by a 30-year wood purchase agreement with PT Arara Abadi (from 2001), providing long-term raw material supply visibility. However, the concentration to CMI creates a single-point-of-failure risk: any delay or dispute at the distributor level would immediately hit both sales and collections. Post-period-end price moves are not disclosed. The industrial paper segment (USD 249.6m) is exposed to packaging and corrugated demand cycles, while cultural paper faces digital substitution headwinds. Watch items include CMI collection velocity, APP export branch volumes, and any PMK-136 regulatory cost pass-through.

Trade receivables net rose to USD 1,569.3m from USD 1,530.3m. Inventories were effectively flat at USD 418.3m, translating to 46.8 days inventory outstanding. Prepaid taxes fell to USD 35.6m from USD 59.2m. Other advances declined to USD 30.4m from USD 32.6m. Days payable outstanding are 48.1 days, and the cash conversion cycle is 174.1 days—driven almost entirely by the receivables drag.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

- Equity base of USD 7.0bn provides a thick buffer; the equity ratio is 55.3%.
- Current and quick ratios above 2.5x indicate near-term payment capacity.
- Core free cash flow (OCF less capex) is positive at USD 350.3m.
- Tax benefit recorded in Q1, improving net profit.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- Gross margin expansion to 34.5% with simultaneous absolute COGS reduction.
- Operating leverage intact: selling and G&A costs declined year-on-year.
- Integrated supply chain underpinned by a long-term wood purchase agreement with PT Arara Abadi.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- Dominant market position in cultural paper and pulp within the APP group ecosystem.
- Investment subsidiaries (Imperial, IK Investment BVI) provide internal treasury and capital-deployment flexibility.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

- **HIGH — Receivables concentration:** PT Cakrawala Mega Indah alone accounts for USD 1,082.0m in trade receivables. A delay or default would instantly impair both liquidity and earnings.
- **HIGH — Cash drain:** Cash halved QoQ to USD 592.9m. The USD 918.1m investing outflow—mostly securities—raises questions about liquidity prioritization.
- **MODERATE — Tax assessment:** SKPKB No. 00003/206/24/092/25 for the 2024 tax year (Rp 21.5bn) was received Nov 2025; final liability uncertain.
- **MODERATE — Interest burden:** Quarterly interest expense of USD 71.6m is material; annualized cash interest paid is USD 374.9m.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

- **MODERATE — Related-party entanglement:** Sales, purchases, receivables, payables, and management fees all flow through related parties. Governance and transfer-pricing scrutiny risk is elevated.
- **MODERATE — Working capital inefficiency:** DSO of 175.4 days and CCC of 174.1 days are structurally high, locking up capital in intra-group receivables.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

- **MODERATE — PMK-136:** Enacted effective Jan 1 2025; the company states it is still assessing exposure as of the report date.
- **LOW — Environmental compliance:** Indefinite commitments exist, but no quantified provision is mapped.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

- Sustained gross margin expansion and operating leverage could drive double-digit EPS growth even on low-single-digit revenue growth.
- The securities portfolio (USD 2.7bn) represents a liquid asset buffer that could be redeployed to reduce gross debt or fund buybacks if management chooses.
- Pulp and paper cycle recovery, if it materializes, would flow directly to the bottom line given the fixed-cost leverage.

### 14.2 Bear Case

- A collection failure or extended payment delay from CMI would simultaneously crush revenue recognition and cash conversion.
- The tax assessment (Rp 21.5bn) and PMK-136 could impose unexpected cash tax burdens, reversing the recent tax benefit.
- Bonds and sukuk maturities through 2029-2036 create a long-dated but heavy debt service schedule; refinancing risk remains if credit conditions tighten.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

- Q2 2026 trade receivables trend: any reduction in DSO or CMI receivables.
- Securities portfolio liquidation or reallocation to debt reduction.
- Resolution of the 2024 tax assessment and clarity on PMK-136 financial impact.
- Completion and ramp of any post-construction fixed assets moved out of work-in-progress.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Qualified).** INKP is not a buy because the related-party risk and lack of market transparency prevent a margin of safety. It is not a sell because earnings are improving, margins are resilient, and the equity base is substantial. A target price range cannot be established without market data. **Review trigger:** Q2 2026 earnings release; any material change in CMI receivables collection, cash position, or securities holdings.
