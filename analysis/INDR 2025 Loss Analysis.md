# INDR (PT. Indo-Rama Synthetics Tbk) - 2025 Loss Analysis

## Executive Summary

PT. Indo-Rama Synthetics Tbk (INDR) suffered consistent losses throughout 2025, recording a net loss of **US$ 11.6 million** for the full year. The losses were driven by razor-thin gross margins, declining revenue across all markets, high fixed operating costs, and significant debt servicing expenses. Despite generating US$ 778.8 million in revenue, the company's cost structure and market conditions made profitability unattainable.

## Root Causes of Losses

### 1. Extremely Thin Gross Margins

The fundamental problem was that **cost of revenue nearly equaled selling prices** throughout the year:

- Q1 gross margin: 1.1%
- H1 gross margin: 0.3% (critically low)
- Q3 gross margin: 0.8%
- FY gross margin: 1.6%

This indicates INDR was operating with virtually no buffer between production costs and selling prices. The company is heavily exposed to **commodity price risks in global markets** which impact raw material costs (petroleum-based polyester inputs, cotton for spun yarns) and product prices simultaneously.

### 2. Revenue Decline Across All Markets

FY 2025 revenue fell **11.3%** compared to FY 2024, with declines in every geographical market:

The steepest declines occurred in export markets (Rest of World -42.5%, Europe -30.2%, South America -28.6%), suggesting weakening global demand for textile/polyester products.

## Business Profile

INDR operates in four segments:
1. **Spun Yarns** - Manufacturing of spun yarns and sewing thread (plants in Turkey and Uzbekistan)
2. **Polyester** - Manufacturing of polyester filament yarns, polyester staple fibre, textile chips, polyester filament fabrics and PET resin (plants in Purwakarta, West Java)
3. **Minerals** - Gold mining (Cianjur, West Java) and thermal coal mining (Musi Banyuasin, South Sumatra)
4. **Others** - Trading, investment, and other activities

The company employs approximately **6,762 people** and has been in commercial operation since 1976.

### Key Differences Between 2021 and 2025

#### 1. Gross Margin Collapse (-13.1 percentage points)

This is the single most critical factor. In 2021, INDR enjoyed healthy gross margins of 13-15%, generating US$ 129.8M in gross profit. By 2025, margins collapsed to 0.3-1.6%, producing only US$ 12.1M.

| Factor | 2021 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|
| Post-pandemic demand recovery | Strong textile/apparel demand as economies reopened | Weakened global demand, especially in export markets |
| Raw material costs | Manageable; favorable input-output spread | Petroleum/PTA/MEG prices elevated, squeezing margins |
| Pricing power | Able to pass costs to customers | Limited pricing power in oversupplied market |

#### 2. Finance Costs Nearly Tripled (+160%)

| Item | 2021 | 2025 | Change |
|------|------|------|--------|
| Short-term bank loans | 82,374,053 | 36,050,000 | -56.2% |
| Long-term bank loans | 67,109,439 | 89,626,652 | +33.6% |
| **Total Bank Debt** | **149,483,492** | **125,676,652** | **-15.9%** |
| Finance costs | 2,098,332 | 5,453,200 | **+159.9%** |

Despite total debt being **lower** in 2025 than 2021, finance costs are **2.6x higher**. This suggests significantly higher interest rates in 2025 (BI rate hikes, global rate environment) and/or a shift from short-term to more expensive long-term debt.

In 2021, export markets contributed significantly more. By 2025, all export segments shrank dramatically, particularly Rest of World (-42.5% vs 2024), Europe (-30.2%), and South America (-28.6%).

The 11.9% revenue decline meant fixed costs consumed a larger share of a smaller revenue base.

### What Changed Between 2021 and 2025?

1. **End of pandemic recovery boom**: 2021 benefited from post-lockdown demand surge for textiles and apparel. This tailwind dissipated by 2022-2023.

2. **Raw material cost inflation**: Polyester inputs (PTA, MEG, crude oil derivatives) remained elevated while selling prices could not keep pace.

3. **Global economic slowdown**: Reduced demand from key export markets (Europe, Americas, Rest of World).

4. **Higher interest rate environment**: Global and Indonesian rate hikes increased finance costs despite lower total debt.

5. **Structural margin compression**: The industry shifted from a 14-15% gross margin environment to a 1-2% environment, fundamentally changing the profitability equation.

## Conclusion

INDR's losses in 2025 were structural rather than temporary. The company operates in a highly competitive, commodity-driven industry where:

1. **Raw material costs** (petroleum derivatives for polyester, cotton for yarns) are globally determined and volatile
2. **Selling prices** are market-driven with limited pricing power
3. **Fixed costs** (depreciation of US$ 35.4M, G&A of US$ 18.3M, interest of US$ 5.5M) total approximately US$ 59M annually
4. **Revenue decline** of 11.3% further stretched the cost structure

The contrast with 2021 is stark: the company went from generating **US$ 84.6M in net profit on 14.7% gross margins** to **US$ 11.6M in net loss on 1.6% gross margins**. The US$ 96.2 million swing was driven almost entirely by gross margin collapse (-13.1 pp), compounded by higher finance costs (+160%) and declining revenue (-11.9%).

For INDR to return to 2021-level profitability, it would need:
- Gross margins to recover to at least 8-10% (requiring either significantly lower raw material costs or higher selling prices)
- Revenue growth back to US$ 850M+ to better absorb fixed costs
- Finance costs to normalize to pre-2022 levels
- Expansion of the profitable minerals segment

The slight improvement in losses (from US$ 13.8M in 2024 to US$ 11.6M in 2025) is encouraging but insufficient. The polyester segment's deteriorating performance is particularly concerning and warrants close monitoring.
