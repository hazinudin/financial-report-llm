## 1. Executive Summary

PGAS reported Q1 2026 revenue of USD 929.6 million, down 3.8% year-on-year, yet net profit surged 35.1% to USD 121.8 million and basic EPS rose to USD 0.0050 from USD 0.0037 in Q1 2025. The profit expansion is driven by two factors: gross margin widened to 17.0% from an implied prior-year level of roughly 14.6%, and the consolidated effective tax rate turned deeply negative to –10.6%, producing a net tax credit of USD 14.4 million. The tax credit alone accounts for the majority of the net profit growth; without it, year-on-year profit growth would have been roughly single-digit. This raises an immediate question about recurrence, which management has not explicitly resolved in the extracted disclosures.

The balance sheet remains robust. The group carries a net cash position of USD –279.1 million (cash and current securities exceed total loans), a current ratio of 2.01×, and an equity ratio of 60.1%. Equity grew 3.3% versus the prior fiscal year-end to USD 3.73 billion. However, operating cash flow collapsed 64.5% to USD 86.9 million despite the 35% net profit gain, indicating significant working capital absorption—principally a 28.3% reduction in trade payables and a 38% build in taxes payable. The divergence between earnings and cash generation is a critical watch item for dividend capacity and debt service.

Segment data show the crown-jewel Trading and Transmission division generated net profit of USD 144.4 million (up 55.3% year-on-year), while the Exploration & Production segment swung to a loss of USD 5.3 million and Other operations profit fell 68.4% to USD 9.3 million. No market data were provided for the stock; consequently, valuation multiples, market capitalization, and a target price range cannot be established. We assign a **HOLD (Neutral)** recommendation pending clarity on tax normalization and a sustained recovery in operating cash flow.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet contains no production or sales volume disclosures for Q1 2026 (production and reserves arrays are empty). Consequently, a volume-versus-price decomposition is **not disclosed**. Segment-level external sales provide a mixed picture: Trading and transmission rose 0.4% to USD 875.7 million, while Exploration and production fell 11.2% to USD 64.6 million and Other operations dropped 31.8% to USD 77.9 million. The consolidated decline therefore stems from the non-core E&P and Other segments, not from the core gas trading and transmission franchise. External research hypotheses suggest BPH Migas pricing adjustments or HGBT formula changes may have supported realized tariffs, but no post-period-end price moves or confirmed regulatory changes are available in the source notes.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer concentration is acute. PLN and its subsidiaries (related party) accounted for USD 148.0 million, or 15.92% of consolidated revenue. Third-party customer PT Medco Energi Internasional Tbk contributed USD 151.5 million (16.29% of consolidated revenue). The top two counterparties alone represent roughly one-third of sales. Related-party revenue in aggregate reached USD 335.2 million, or 36.1% of consolidated revenue, while related-party purchases totaled USD 184.8 million (23.95% of COGS). All disclosed offtake is domestic; no export revenue is reported.

A data-quality flag is required: the sum of segment-level external sales (USD 1,018.2 million) exceeds consolidated revenue by USD 88.6 million. This suggests unreported intersegment eliminations or classification differences between segment reporting and the consolidated income statement. The consolidated revenue line is the authoritative figure.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

Forward revenue visibility rests on long-term GSPA and transmission contracts. Note 35c discloses committed customer volume of 1,150 BBTUD with an estimated gas price range of USD 7.22–16.33/MMBTU. Key contracts run through the 2030s, including the Grissik-Duri and Grissik-Singapore pipelines via Transgasindo and multiple PLN offtake agreements. However, the LNG trading business is impaired by a Force Majeure notice to Gunvor (November 2023) and an onerous contract provision of USD 72.0 million recognized as of March 31, 2026. An LCIA arbitration claim of USD 130.4 million from Gunvor is scheduled for hearing in May–June 2026; management believes there is no consolidated financial impact, but a negative ruling could alter the revenue outlook for LNG trading. Watch items include the Block Ujung Pangkah PSC expiry in May 2026 and the finalization of the Pangkah POD.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

Trading and transmission is the crown jewel. In Q1 2026 it generated external sales of USD 875.7 million and segment net profit of USD 144.4 million, a 55.3% year-on-year jump. Assets attributed to the segment are USD 7,953.5 million before eliminations. Note 35c indicates a committed customer volume of 1,150 BBTUD with gas prices estimated between USD 7.22 and 16.33/MMBTU.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

Several assets are idled or impaired. Ketapang and West Bangkanai E&E assets were written off in 2025. South Sesulu carried an impairment of USD 99.5 million in 2025 following a reserves audit. The Kepodang-Tambak Lorok (KJG) pipeline holds an impairment provision of USD 107.8 million. ### 7.4 Growth Options

The E&P segment is not currently contributing growth; it posted a Q1 2026 loss of USD 5.3 million versus a small profit of USD 1.6 million in Q1 2025. LNG trading is constrained by the Gunvor Force Majeure and a USD 72.0 million onerous-contract provision. The telecom and property-leasing subsidiaries (PGASKOM, PT Permata Graha) are immaterial. Near-term growth therefore depends on: (i) ramp-up of the LNG Terminal East Java, (ii) successful POD finalization for Pangkah and Muara Bakau, and (iii) resolution of the LNG trading arbitration.

### 7.5 Intercompany and Related-Party Dynamics

Transfer-pricing scrutiny and receivables concentration are elevated. ## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

– **Net cash balance**: Cash and current securities of USD 1,362.3 million exceed total loans of USD 1,083.2 million, yielding negative net debt of USD 279.1 million.
– **Liquidity buffer**: Current ratio above 2.0× and cash ratio above 1.3× provide ample headroom for short-term obligations and facility renewals.
– **Low leverage**: Loans-to-equity of 0.29× and debt-to-equity of 0.66× are conservative for an infrastructure utility.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

– **Regulated monopoly rents**: The trading and transmission segment controls critical national gas pipeline infrastructure with long-term, take-or-pay style contracts to PLN and Pertamina-linked off-takers.
– **USD-denominated cash flows**: Most gas purchase and sale contracts are denominated in USD, providing a natural hedge against currency volatility.
– **Diversified asset base**: Operations span transmission, regasification (Lampung FSRU, Arun LNG), E&P, and telecom, reducing single-asset dependency.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

– **State ownership and implicit support**: Immediate parent Pertamina and ultimate parent the Government of Indonesia provide access to subsidized financing (JBIC two-step loan at 1.1–1.3%) and regulatory advocacy.
– **Tax efficiency**: 19% corporate income tax rate (vs. standard 22%) due to >40% public float; Pillar Two Safe Harbour assessment indicates no top-up tax for Q1 2026.
– **Contracted reserve life**: GSPA and transmission agreements extend to 2033–2034 for major pipelines, underpinning a visible revenue runway.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

– **HIGH — Tax credit recurrence**: The negative effective tax rate (–10.6%) is likely non-recurring. If tax expense normalizes to the statutory rate, net profit would be materially lower, erasing most of the year-on-year growth.
– **HIGH — Operating cash flow collapse**: OCF fell 64.5% to USD 86.9 million. The sharp reduction in trade payables and build in taxes payable suggests working capital is absorbing cash despite lower COGS. If sustained, this threatens dividend coverage and debt service.
– **MODERATE — Receivables quality**: Gross receivables overdue >1 year are USD 179.3 million (30.6% of gross), with a total allowance of USD 199.5 million. A further deterioration would strain cash conversion.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

– **HIGH — Customer/supplier concentration**: The top two counterparties (PLN and Medco) account for ~32% of revenue. The Pertamina group is both the largest supplier and the immediate parent, creating potential conflicts of interest and transfer-pricing risk.
– **MODERATE — E&P segment losses**: The E&P segment swung to a USD 5.3 million loss in Q1 2026. With Indonesian PSC tax rates of 44–48% and Block Ujung Pangkah expiring in May 2026, the upstream portfolio may require further impairment or abandonment.
– **MODERATE — Contingent liabilities**: Material tax disputes (VAT gas sales 2012–2013 underpayment claims totaling hundreds of millions of USD equivalent), the Gunvor arbitration (USD 130.4 million claim), and the Pertagas lawsuit (IDR 11.9 billion material/immaterial damages) represent tail risks.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

– **MODERATE — BPH Migas pricing uncertainty**: Gross margin expansion may reflect a favorable regulatory pass-through. Any reversal of HGBT or domestic obligation pricing formulas could compress margins back toward the mid-teens.
– **MODERATE — Pillar Two / global minimum tax**: While the Group assessed Safe Harbour for Q1 2026, future periods may face top-up tax if the simplified ETR falls below 16%.
– **LOW — FX exposure**: Natural hedge from USD-denominated gas sales and purchases limits transactional FX risk, although JPY-denominated JBIC debt is unhedged.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

Gross margin expansion proves sticky due to BPH Migas pass-through or sales-mix shifts, supporting a 17%+ gross margin through 2026. The core transmission and trading segment continues to generate stable USD cash flows under long-term GSPA contracts. The net cash balance and 2.0× current ratio provide downside protection, while the 19% tax rate and Pillar Two Safe Harbour limit global tax leakage. Completion of the LNG Terminal East Java and Gaskita Mandiri pipeline adds incremental regulated capacity.

### 14.2 Bear Case

The Q1 tax credit is non-recurring; as tax normalizes, net profit growth vanishes and net margin retreats toward 11%. Operating cash flow remains weak due to persistent payables compression and receivables buildup, forcing dividend cuts or increased borrowing. The E&P segment deteriorates further on low oil/gas prices or PSC expiry (Block Ujung Pangkah May 2026). The Gunvor arbitration or VAT tax disputes crystallize into cash outflows. PLN/Pertamina concentration allows counterparty pricing pressure.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts

1. Gunvor LCIA arbitration hearing (May–June 2026).
2. BPH Migas or Ministry of Energy gas pricing regulation updates.
3. Q2 2026 tax rate normalization (or confirmation of further deferred tax benefits).
4. Renewal of short-term credit facilities (BNI, BRI, Mandiri).
5. Final investment decision / POD submission for Pangkah and Muara Bakau blocks.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD (Neutral)**. The 35% net profit growth is largely driven by a non-recurring consolidated tax credit and margin effects against a backdrop of revenue contraction and severe operating cash flow compression. The balance sheet is strong, but earnings quality and cash generation are in question. **Target price range: Not disclosed** (market data not provided). **Risk qualifier**: Pending clarity on tax normalization and operating cash flow recovery. **Review trigger**: Q2 2026 earnings release, any BPH Migas tariff announcement, or resolution of the Gunvor arbitration.
