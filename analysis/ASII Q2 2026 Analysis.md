## 1. Executive Summary

Astra delivered a **sharp headline deterioration in H1 2026 that is only partly operational**. Revenue fell **3.0% to Rp 157,913 bn** (H1 2025: Rp 162,857 bn); gross profit fell **6.8% to Rp 32,452 bn** and gross margin compressed **84 bps to 20.55%** (H1 2025: 21.39%). But the damage to the bottom line was disproportionate to the top line: pre-tax profit fell **30.3% to Rp 16,733 bn**, net profit fell **31.5% to Rp 13,344 bn**, and profit attributable to owners fell **19.2% to Rp 12,533 bn**, with **EPS down 18.3% to Rp 313** (H1 2025: Rp 383). The arithmetic decomposition is unusually clean and unusually concentrated in one line. Of the Rp 7,274 bn fall in pre-tax profit, roughly **Rp 3,459 bn (≈47%) is the swing in "other income — net"**, which moved from **+Rp 944 bn to −Rp 2,515 bn**; a further **Rp 2,381 bn (≈33%)** is the fall in gross profit; and **Rp 2,369 bn of higher combined selling and G&A expense** more than offsets the flat interest line. The company's own H1 2026 press release attributes the bulk of this to **Rp 2,359 bn of non-recurring items** (fair-value adjustments on equity investments plus impairment) versus Rp 526 bn in H1 2025, and reports **underlying net profit of Rp 14,892 bn, down only 7%** ([press release, 30 July 2026](https://pubexlive.idx.co.id/document/download/ASII_Press_Release.pdf)); Maybank Sekuritas, as reported by IDXChannel, sized roughly **Rp 2.76 trn of impairment booked specifically in Q2 2026**, concentrated in geothermal exposure ([IDXChannel, 6 August 2026](https://www.idxchannel.com/market-news/prospek-untr-dibayangi-impairment-analis-pangkas-proyeksi-laba)). The honest read is a **~7% underlying earnings decline wrapped in a ~19% reported decline**.

The quarterly shape matters more than the half-year shape. On derived numbers, **Q2 2026 revenue of Rp 79,245 bn was flat year-on-year (−0.3% vs Rp 79,496 bn)** after a **−5.6% Q1**, while Q2 gross margin of **21.40%** recovered roughly **170 bps off the Q1 2026 trough of 19.70%** — the source of any "margin improved" flag — yet still sits **~96 bps below Q2 2025's 22.36%**. Q2 pre-tax profit of **Rp 8,664 bn** compares with **Rp 13,361 bn** a year earlier. Almost the entire other-income swing is Q2-loaded: other income was **−Rp 19 bn in Q1 2026** and an implied **−Rp 2,496 bn in Q2 2026**, against **+Rp 961 bn in Q2 2025**. This is a **single-quarter, largely non-operating, largely non-cash event**, not a run-rate.

The **balance sheet is carrying two large capital actions simultaneously**. Balance-sheet loans rose **10.1% to Rp 107,923 bn** (31 Dec 2025: Rp 98,048 bn) while cash fell **12.3% to Rp 46,150 bn** (Rp 52,621 bn), lifting fact-sheet net debt from **Rp 28,085 bn to Rp 43,550 bn** and net-debt-to-equity from **9.7% to 15.0%**; on the company's own Note 31 basis (including debt securities, lease liabilities and financial-services funding), net debt is **Rp 73,048 bn vs Rp 57,680 bn** and gearing **25% vs 20%**. Non-financial-services net cash of Rp 7,218 bn at December flipped to **net debt of Rp 6,010 bn**, which the press release attributes to the **PT Arafura Surya Alam gold-mine acquisition, share buyback and working-capital movements** ([press release, 30 July 2026](https://pubexlive.idx.co.id/document/download/ASII_Press_Release.pdf)); management subsequently put H1 capex at **Rp 16.9 trn**, of which **Rp 11.7 trn was Arafura** ([Antara, 10 September 2026](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5735159/astra-catat-capex-capai-rp169-t-mayoritas-buat-akuisisi-tambang-emas)). Liquidity remains adequate but is no longer generous: current ratio **1.19x** (1.24x), quick ratio **0.97x** (1.02x), cash ratio **0.30x** (0.35x), working capital **Rp 29,149 bn** (Rp 36,477 bn, −20.1%).

The **NCI collapse is a mix effect, not a corporate action**. Profit attributable to non-controlling interests fell **79.5% to Rp 811 bn** (Rp 3,966 bn) while NCI equity fell only **3.4% to Rp 59,776 bn** (Rp 61,906 bn) — a divergence that rules out a broad minority buy-out as the primary driver. The mechanism is **United Tractors**: UNTR's attributable profit fell **88% to Rp 956 bn** from Rp 8.13 trn on revenue of Rp 58.3 trn (−15%) ([Bisnis, 30 July 2026](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20260730/192/1992254/united-tractors-untr-cetak-pendapatan-rp583-triliun-semester-i2026); [CNBC Indonesia, 1 September 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20260901104838-17-763923/laba-united-tractors--untr--anjlok-88-ini-penyebabnya)), and with a large listed minority base, that removes roughly Rp 2.9 trn of minority profit — almost exactly the Rp 3,155 bn fall in NCI profit. Offsetting the other way, **Astra Agro Lestari's attributable profit rose 56.5% to ~Rp 1.09–1.10 trn** ([detikFinance, 24 September 2026](https://finance.detik.com/berita-ekonomi-bisnis/d-8677694/laba-bersih-astra-agro-lestari-tembus-rp-1-1-triliun-di-semester-i-2026)), which should have *raised* NCI from agribusiness. Net: reported NCI profit collapse is the arithmetic of a minority-heavy subsidiary's earnings wiping out.

**Valuation cannot be assessed from the inputs provided.** The market-data stub supplied with this note is **empty — no price, market cap, shares outstanding, P/E, P/B or EV is available**, so `pe_annualized`, `pb`, `ev` and `ev_ebitda` are all null in the computed-ratio block and I do not estimate them. §8.2 therefore presents a **valuation-anchor grid** (implied value at alternative P/B and P/E multiples applied to fact-sheet book value and annualised EPS) rather than a market-multiple verdict, and §14's target-price framing is explicitly conditional. On the fundamentals alone the picture is a **quality franchise in a cyclical and partly self-inflicted earnings trough**, with a genuine post-period recovery signal: Q3 2026 group profit reached **Rp 21.60 trn** with the mining and heavy-equipment segment returning to **15% year-on-year profit growth** ([CNBC Indonesia, 28 October 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20261028173355-17-780330/laba-astra-international--asii--rp216-triliun-di-kuartal-iii-2026); [Bisnis, 29 October 2026](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20261029/192/2016627/astra-asii-catat-perbaikan-kinerja-kuartal-iii)). **Recommendation: HOLD, recovery-biased** — see §14.4.

### 2.1 Revenue Performance

| Cost of revenue | (125,461) | (128,024) | +2,563 | −2.00% |

| *Derived Q2 revenue* | *79,245* | *79,496* | *(251)* | *−0.32%* |
| *Derived Q1 revenue* | *78,668* | *83,361* | *(4,693)* | *−5.63%* |

The half-year decline is **entirely front-loaded**. Q1 2026 revenue was down 5.6% year-on-year; derived Q2 2026 revenue of Rp 79,245 bn is flat (−0.3%) against Q2 2025's Rp 79,496 bn. That is the single most important observation in the revenue analysis: the top-line problem was **a Q1-specific collapse that did not persist into Q2**. The Q1 revenue miss was driven by the Martabe gold-mine suspension and the coal RKAB quota shock hitting mining-linked channels; both eased within Q2 (see §2.2). Sequentially, revenue rose **0.7%** (79,245 vs 78,668), which is weak seasonally but positive.

Note that COGS fell faster than revenue (−2.0% vs −3.0%), which is the mechanical reason gross margin compressed: **the revenue mix shifted away from low-COGS, high-margin mining and gold and toward automotive and financial services**, but not enough to preserve the blended rate.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet carries **no production, volume or reserve disclosures** (`production: []`, `reserves: []`), so the volume/price split must be built from segment data and externally reported operating metrics. Those metrics point consistently to a **mining-volume problem against an automotive-price/mix tailwind**:

*(All external operating metrics above are company-reported or press-sourced context; the canonical revenue figure remains Rp 157,913 bn from the fact sheet.)*

**Mining — a policy-driven volume shock.** The dominant negative volume driver is regulatory. The Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources had planned a **2026 RKAB coal production target of roughly 600 mn tonnes against 1.2 bn tonnes in 2025** (actual 2025 output ~790 mn t), which is a direct demand shock for contract miners and heavy-equipment distributors ([Kontan, 9 June 2026](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/intraco-penta-relaksasi-rkab-batu-bara-jadi-angin-segar-bagi-industri-alat-berat)). Komatsu's −27% unit decline, PAMA's −10% overburden and the −10% own-coal volume are all one shock with three faces. Separately, **PT Agincourt's Martabe mine suspended operations early in the year** — its business licence had been listed in a 20 January 2026 revocation announcement — and restarted only in **May 2026** ([Kompas, 7 September 2026](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/09/07/202100926/mulai-produksi-sejak-mei-tambang-emas-martabe-belum-beroperasi-penuh)). Group gold sales of 23,000 oz against 125,000 oz a year earlier is the single largest removal of high-margin revenue in the group.

**Automotive — volume with share at the low end of history.** Group car sales grew 10% in a market that grew ~16%, so **Astra lost share**: 51% is at the lower end of its historical band and is the clearest sign of Chinese-brand pressure in the four-wheel segment. Motorcycles (+1%, 77% share) were flat and stable. Because automotive is the *lower*-gross-margin, higher-revenue segment, its relative growth **inflates revenue and depresses blended margin** — the opposite of the gold effect.

**Price.** External commodity context for Q2 2026 was supportive, not adverse: **CPO averaged US$1,432/t in H1 2026 (Gapki)** with MYR 4,400–4,700/t through Q2 per Panin Sekuritas ([Bisnis, 3 August 2026](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20260803/12/1993039/harga-cpo-2026-diproyeksi-tetap-tinggi-b50-dan-el-nino-jadi-penopang)), and **gold spot held around US$4,000/oz in Q2, down ~9.2% qoq but up 38% YTD** per BRIDS ([Kompas, 12 October 2026](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/10/12/120246126/kinerja-antam-lampaui-proyeksi-begini-prospek-sahamnya)). Both were earnings tailwinds *for the segments that had volume*; gold's price strength was wasted on volumes that did not exist until late in the half. Coal benchmark prices are reported to have firmed through Q2, but benchmark levels are not confirmed in the sourced findings and are treated here qualitatively only.

**FX.** The Rupiah depreciated materially against the USD over the half — **BI mid-rate USD/IDR 17,856 at 30 June 2026 vs 16,782 at 31 December 2025** (fact-sheet `context_notes`, Note 37) — which is a translation tailwind on USD-linked revenue (coal, gold) and a cost headwind on USD-denominated inputs and debt. Note 37 shows **net foreign currency liabilities after hedging of Rp 1,787 bn at 30 June 2026 versus net assets after hedging of Rp 11,989 bn at 31 December 2025** — a material swing that is a P&L risk, not a revenue driver.

Two composition facts dominate. First, **revenue and profit are almost unrelated across segments**: automotive is 41.8% of revenue but only 24.2% of gross profit and 45.8% of net profit (its net-profit weight is flattered by associate and finance income below the gross line), while financial services is 10.9% of revenue but **31.7% of gross profit at a 60.0% gross margin**. Mining is 36.9% of revenue, 32.4% of gross profit, and only **5.7% of net profit** — the last number is the fingerprint of the impairment (§3.3).

Second, **concentration is low and disclosure is robust on customers but absent on geography**. Note 29 states **no individual customer contributed more than 10% of total net revenue**, and **no individual third-party supplier exceeded 10% of net revenue**. Related-party revenue was **Rp 14,451 bn, or 9.15% of total net revenue** (H1 2025: Rp 14,079 bn, 8.65%) — Note 32b — dominated by entities in the Astra ecosystem (Aegis Energy Trading Rp 2,440 bn; PT Astra Honda Motor Rp 2,412 bn; Cipta Commodity Trading Rp 1,493 bn; Astra-KLK Rp 1,380 bn; PT Astra Daihatsu Motor Rp 1,317 bn). That 50 bps increase in related-party revenue share against a 3% revenue decline is worth monitoring but is not yet a governance concern.

The **elimination column is small (−1.6% of revenue, −1.3% of gross profit)**, indicating limited inter-segment trading relative to the group's size — structurally healthy, and it means segment margins are close to economic margins rather than being artefacts of transfer pricing.

Segment **assets do not sum to consolidated total assets**: Note 31 segment assets total **Rp 442,661 bn** against balance-sheet total assets of **Rp 514,517 bn**. The Rp 71,856 bn difference is precisely the **investment in joint ventures and associates** (Note 11: total Rp 71,856 bn, vs Rp 73,441 bn at December), which is carried outside the reporting segments. This is an internal consistency check that passes and is worth stating because it confirms the segment table is internally coherent.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

**The revenue base looks trough-stable, not structurally broken.** Q2 revenue flat year-on-year after −5.6% in Q1 supports the reading that the shortfall was a mining-volume and gold-availability event, and the post-period evidence is that it reversed harder than expected: Q3 2026 group profit of **Rp 21.60 trn** with mining and heavy equipment back to **+15% year-on-year profit growth** ([CNBC Indonesia, 28 October 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20261028173355-17-780330/laba-astra-international--asii--rp216-triliun-di-kuartal-iii-2026)).

**Three forward drivers are identifiable, one is a watch item:**

- **Gold ramp (positive).** Martabe restarted in May 2026 but is **not yet at full capacity**, with UNTR targeting ~**65,000 oz for 2026** and capacity expansion from 2027 ([Kompas, 7 September 2026](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/09/07/202100926/mulai-produksi-sejak-mei-tambang-emas-martabe-belum-beroperasi-penuh)). H1 gold sales of 23,000 oz imply a heavily second-half-weighted year; every ounce added is at ~US$4,000/oz-class prices.
- **RKAB relaxation (positive, policy-dependent).** RKAB coal quotas are reported to have begun easing post-period, which is the direct precondition for Komatsu unit recovery and PAMA overburden recovery. This is a policy variable Astra does not control and it is the largest single swing factor in the 2H 2026 revenue line.
- **Automotive (mixed).** Market growth of ~16% in H1 2026 helped, but Astra's 51% share means it is participating below market. The relevant question for 2H is whether share stabilises at 51% or continues to bleed to Chinese brands — this is the persistent, under-appreciated drag on the consumer leg.
- **Watch item — Arafura contributes nothing yet.** The **PT Arafura Surya Alam** acquisition (100% via PT Danusa Tambang Nusantara, **USD 540 mn ≈ Rp 9.1 trn**, completed 11 February 2026 — fact-sheet Note 3b) is a **non-producing gold asset**. It has consumed capital and balance-sheet capacity and adds no revenue or earnings in this period. Its value is a 2027+ story.

**Post-period price context.** Gold at ~US$4,000/oz, CPO in the MYR 4,400–4,700/t band and a firming coal benchmark are all supportive of the 2H revenue mix — but these are external context from third-party sources, and the note does not forecast commodity prices.

### 3.1 Gross Margin Analysis

| *Derived Q2 gross margin* | *21.40%* | *22.36%* | *—* | *−96 bps* |
| *Derived Q1 gross margin* | *19.70%* | *20.47%* | *—* | *−77 bps* |
| Memo: FY2025 audited gross margin | — | 22.09% | — | — |

The margin story is **directionally dependent on which comparison you choose, and the flag saying "gross margin increased" is a sequential artefact**. Sequentially, gross margin rose ~170 bps (Q1 19.70% → Q2 21.40%); against both the prior-year half (20.55% vs 21.39%) and the prior-year quarter (21.40% vs 22.36%) it **fell by 84–96 bps**. Against FY2025 audited gross margin of 22.09%, H1 2026 is down **154 bps**. The correct characterisation is: **a Q1 trough, a partial Q2 recovery, and a structural year-on-year erosion of roughly 90 bps**.

Two mechanisms explain the recovery and two explain the erosion:

*Recovery (Q1 → Q2):* (i) **Martabe's restart** reinserted the group's highest-margin revenue stream late in the half; (ii) **mix shifted toward financial services (60.0% gross margin) and away from low-margin trading/haulage**, as the mining-linked revenue base shrank.

*Erosion (year-on-year):* (i) **the loss of gold volume** (23,000 oz vs 125,000 oz) removed high-margin ounces outright; (ii) **automotive's revenue weight rose while its 11.9% segment gross margin is the lowest of the four**, mechanically diluting the blend. Without a comparative segment gross-profit split (Note 31 discloses current-period segments only in the fact sheet), the mix-versus-rate decomposition cannot be closed definitively — this is an explicit limitation, not a modelling choice.

**`operating_margin` is null in the computed-ratio block** because the fact sheet carries no operating-profit line; the derived figures above are GP less selling less G&A and are labelled as derived throughout. They should not be treated as a reported subtotal.

The operating story is where the quarter is genuinely ugly. **Revenue fell 3.0% while combined opex rose 15.1%.** Selling expense grew **18.6%** and G&A **13.2%** on a *declining* revenue base, costing **180 bps of opex ratio** and driving derived operating margin down **264 bps**. The drivers are **not disclosed in the fact sheet** and are an explicit open item: candidates are the newly consolidated industrial-warehouse platform (PT Pratista and Mega Manunggal, acquired in 2025 for Rp 1.3 trn and Rp 4.1 trn respectively — Note 3), MSOP accruals, subsidiary expansion, marketing incentives, and Martabe suspension costs. The research brief flags this as an internal-disclosure question only (`internal_must_explain`: "Selling expense +18.6% and G&A +13.2% versus revenue −3%: the drivers ... are internal disclosures not covered by external sources"). Until split, the prudent assumption is that **roughly half is structural (consolidation, headcount) and half is semi-variable** — but that is a judgement, not a disclosed fact.

The one relief is that **interest expense was flat** at Rp 1,856 bn vs Rp 1,878 bn despite a 10.1% increase in gross loans (§4.3) — falling reference rates offset the higher debt stock. This is a temporary benefit: the debt was raised for Arafura, which is not yet earning.

### 3.3 Net Finance Income/Expense and Other Income

This is the decisive section for the half. My reconstruction of the P&L bridge:

| (Rp bn) | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Derived operating profit (GP − selling − G&A) | 14,417 | 19,167 | (4,750) |
| Interest income | 0 (not captured) | 0 (not captured) | — |
| Interest expense | (1,856) | (1,878) | +22 |
| Other income — net | (2,515) | 944 | **(3,459)** |
| *Unallocated income residual (derived)* | *6,687* | *5,774* | *+913* |
| **Pre-tax profit (reported)** | **16,733** | **24,007** | **(7,274)** |

**The other-income swing is the single largest arithmetic contributor to the profit decline — roughly 47% of it — and it is almost entirely a Q2 event.** Other income was **−Rp 19 bn in Q1 2026** and an implied **−Rp 2,496 bn in Q2 2026**, against **+Rp 961 bn in Q2 2025**: a **Rp 3,457 bn swing inside one quarter**.

The *cause* is now identifiable with high confidence. ASII's H1 2026 press release states that **group non-recurring items totalled Rp 2,359 bn** in H1 2026 against Rp 526 bn in H1 2025, described as **fair-value adjustments on equity investments plus impairment**, and that the **Mining Solutions & Heavy Equipment business booked Rp 2.1 trn of non-recurring items in its geothermal and nickel divisions** ([press release, 30 July 2026](https://pubexlive.idx.co.id/document/download/ASII_Press_Release.pdf)). Maybank Sekuritas quantified **~Rp 2.76 trn of impairment recognised specifically in Q2 2026**, "mainly related to the company's geothermal exposure" ([IDXChannel, 6 August 2026](https://www.idxchannel.com/market-news/prospek-untr-dibayangi-impairment-analis-pangkas-proyeksi-laba)). Notably, the **Rp 259 bn mark-to-market loss on equity investments disclosed in the Lain-lain segment was smaller than the Rp 484 bn loss a year earlier** — so fair-value losses are *not* the swing driver. **Impairment is.** The timing match is exact: Q1 other income −Rp 19 bn, Q2 −Rp 2,496 bn.

**A composite residual of Rp 6,687 bn (H1 2026) vs Rp 5,774 bn (H1 2025) sits between the disclosed lines and reported pre-tax profit.** This is the share of results of joint ventures and associates plus any reclassification. CNBC Indonesia, reading the H1 2026 filing, reports the JV/associate contribution up **35.31% to Rp 5.12 trn** (vs Rp 3.79 trn) while PBT fell 30.30% and tax expense fell 25.13% ([CNBC Indonesia, 3 August 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20260803091314-17-755924/semester-i-2026-laba-astra-internasional--asii--rp1253-triliun)). The residual I derive (Rp 6,687 bn) exceeds the reported Rp 5,122 bn associate line, so **either some associate income sits inside the "other income" bucket or another income line is not captured by the schema**. The fact sheet explicitly does not show interest income (carried at 0.0 in both periods despite the group holding Rp 46,150 bn of cash and disclosing Rp 150 bn of related-party interest income alone) — this is a **known parse limitation** and means the ±. The rule here is: **do not quote line-level year-on-year percentages for "other income" as though they were a clean operating signal.** The direction is unambiguous (a large one-off impairment); the allocation between "other income", "impairment" and "share of associates" is not.

**FX is the second-most-likely contributor to the other-income line and is directionally adverse.** The Rupiah moved from USD/IDR 16,782 to 17,856 over the half (a ~6.4% depreciation). The group discloses **net foreign currency liabilities after hedging of Rp 1,787 bn at 30 June 2026 versus net assets after hedging of Rp 11,989 bn at 31 December 2025** (Note 37) and states a **10% USD move would change profit after tax by Rp 126 bn** (vs Rp 948 bn at December) — a *reduced* but now *adverse-signed* exposure. The derivative book is live: **derivative assets Rp 944 bn and liabilities Rp 2,416 bn at 30 June 2026**, with a **Rp 10 bn fair-value loss on derivatives recognised in profit or loss in H1 2026** (Note 8) — small, so derivatives are not the swing driver either.

**Net finance position.** Interest expense of Rp 1,856 bn against a group with Rp 107,923 bn of balance-sheet loans implies a blended cost of roughly 3.4% annualised — consistent with the disclosed loan stack where the largest single facility is a **Rp 26,515 bn long-term syndicated loan book** and Rupiah syndicated tranches price at IndONIA + 1.05%–1.85%, while USD syndicated tranches price at Term SOFR + 0.70%–0.95% (fact-sheet `details.loans`). Post-hedging, the mix is **fixed rate Rp 63,622 bn / floating Rp 55,576 bn** (vs Rp 68,410 bn / Rp 41,891 bn at December) — a **shift toward floating**, which raises the sensitivity: a **100 bps move in floating-rate borrowings changes profit after tax by Rp 125 bn** (Note 37; December: Rp 168 bn).

**The derived "other investing" outflow of Rp 5,045 bn is the analytically opaque line.** Note 3b discloses a **USD 540 mn (≈Rp 9.1 trn) Arafura acquisition completed 11 February 2026**, and management has since said H1 capex of **Rp 16.9 trn included Rp 11.7 trn for Arafura** ([Antara, 10 September 2026](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5735159/astra-catat-capex-capai-rp169-t-mayoritas-buat-akuisisi-tambang-emas)). **Those two statements are not reconcilable with a total investing outflow of Rp 10,175 bn** unless the Arafura consideration flowed through a line not captured in the fact sheet's investing extract (for example netted inside movements in other investments, or partly settled in a non-cash form), or unless the 16.9 trn "capex" definition includes financial-services receivables growth. **This is an explicit reconciliation gap and is flagged, not resolved.** Note 38 partially supports a non-cash element: **acquisition of fixed assets through payables and lease liabilities was Rp 860 bn in H1 2026 vs Rp 566 bn**.

The absence of a visible Arafura outflow in the investing line also means **the "cash fell because of Arafura" narrative cannot be verified from the cash-flow statement as presented** — the Rp 6,471 bn cash decline is arithmetically explained by OCF less ICF less financing, all of which are captured, so the acquisition must be embedded somewhere within the Rp 10,175 bn ICF.

### 7.2 The Core Operating Business

Astra does not have a single crown-jewel segment — it has **three, with completely different economics**, and this is the analytical key to the whole note:

**Financial services — the profit engine (10.9% of revenue, 31.7% of gross profit).** Revenue Rp 17,147 bn, **gross profit Rp 10,281 bn at a 60.0% gross margin**, net profit **Rp 4,733 bn** — i.e. **35.5% of group net profit from 10.9% of revenue**. Assets of Rp 141,112 bn fund **financing receivables of Rp 99,724 bn** (Rp 96,698 bn), of which **consumer financing gross was Rp 134,204 bn with the Group portion Rp 117,706 bn** (Note 7). Credit quality is the single most important thing to monitor in the whole group: **Stage 3 (credit-impaired) receivables rose to Rp 2,651 bn from Rp 1,762 bn (+50.5%)**, with Stage 1 at Rp 74,107 bn (Rp 72,847 bn) and Stage 2 at Rp 28,443 bn (Rp 27,534 bn); total provision **Rp 5,477 bn vs Rp 5,445 bn**. That is a **small provision against a 50% rise in Stage 3** — the coverage ratio fell. Write-offs occur when overdue more than 150 days. Effective lending rates are 7.0%–45.6% for Rupiah consumer finance, so the book is high-yield/high-risk, and funding is largely matched bank lines with some receivables pledged (Rp 159 bn). **This segment is holding the group's profit together and its credit metrics are quietly deteriorating.**

**Automotive — the revenue engine, the margin laggard (41.8% of revenue, 24.2% of gross profit).** Revenue Rp 66,053 bn, **gross profit Rp 7,845 bn at 11.9%** — the lowest gross margin of any segment — but net profit **Rp 6,113 bn (45.8% of group)** because associate income (Toyota-Astra Motor, Astra Daihatsu Motor, Astra Honda Motor) and finance income land below the gross line. **PT Astra Daihatsu Motor**, 31.87% effective, contributed **Rp 656 bn of share-of-result in H1 2026** (Note 32) against DM's own profit of **Rp 2,055 bn vs Rp 1,227 bn a year earlier** (Note 11b) — a strong improvement. **PT Astra Honda Motor**, 50.00% effective, had profit of **Rp 4,905 bn vs Rp 5,035 bn** — roughly flat (Note 11). Automotive's economics are therefore **more resilient than its 11.9% gross margin suggests**, because the profit is earned in associates. The risk is share: **51% of a market that grew 16%** is participation below market, and the segment is where Chinese-brand competition bites.

**Mining solutions and heavy equipment — the cyclical problem child (36.9% of revenue, 32.4% of gross profit, 5.7% of net profit).** Revenue Rp 58,289 bn, **gross profit Rp 10,507 bn at 18.0%** — a respectable gross margin — but **net profit of only Rp 755 bn**. This is the segment where the impairment and the volume shock both land. Derived quarter split: **Q1 2026 gross profit was Rp 4,828 bn with segment profit of Rp 691 bn** ([[ASII Q1 2026 Analysis]]); therefore **Q2 2026 gross profit was ~Rp 5,679 bn (a ~176% sequential increase) while Q2 net profit was only ~Rp 64 bn**. That is the cleanest single proof in this note that **the Q2 problem was below the gross line**: the operating business recovered, and an impairment wiped the segment's profit out. Note 31 confirms **mining segment profit before tax fell to Rp 2,015 bn from Rp 11,020 bn and net debt swung to Rp 9,243 bn from net cash of Rp 7,787 bn**.

**Others (12.0% of revenue, 13.0% of gross profit).** Revenue Rp 18,914 bn, gross profit Rp 4,237 bn at **22.4%**, net profit **Rp 1,743 bn**. This is where **agribusiness (AALI, +56.5% profit), toll roads, property, healthcare and digital** sit. Note that the **Rp 259 bn mark-to-market loss on equity investments in the Lain-lain segment was smaller than the Rp 484 bn loss a year earlier** — the "Others" fair-value drag *improved* year-on-year.

### 7.3 Strategic Question Marks

- **Martabe / PT Agincourt (HIGH attention).** The mine suspended operations early in 2026 after its business licence was listed in a **20 January 2026 revocation announcement**, restarted in **May 2026**, and is **not yet at full capacity**, with UNTR targeting ~**65,000 oz for 2026** ([Kompas, 7 September 2026](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/09/07/202100926/mulai-produksi-sejak-mei-tambang-emas-martabe-belum-beroperasi-penuh)). Note 36h confirms the licence status: the government is **still evaluating the licence** as of 30 June 2026. The regulatory tail risk is therefore **live, not resolved** — this is the highest-severity item in the note.
- **Arafura Surya Alam (MODERATE attention).** Acquired 11 February 2026 for **USD 540 mn ≈ Rp 9.1 trn** (Note 3b) — a **non-producing gold asset** that consumed capital and adds no revenue. Its value is entirely 2027+. With gold at ~US$4,000/oz the strategic logic is sound; the execution risk is that the market is paying for it now through higher net debt.
- **Nickel and geothermal assets (MODERATE attention).** Where the Rp 2.1 trn non-recurring impairment was booked. **Residual mining-property carrying value of Rp 5,293 bn (nickel) plus gold Rp 5,307 bn** is the remaining exposure. Whether further impairment risk exists is disclosed only in the notes/analyst calls — an explicit open item.
- **PT Astra Graphia and PT Acset Indonusa.** Asset bases contracted 6.1% and 6.2%. Small (Rp 2,928 bn and Rp 2,800 bn) but negative-trending.
- **Astra Digital Internasional (−2.5% assets).** The digital second leg is not yet a growth engine.
- **Employee headcount reduction of 2,948 (2.2%)** suggests restructuring is underway; the associated severance/benefit provisioning may explain part of the accrued-liability build (§4.2).

### 7.4 Growth Options

- **Gold, full stop.** Agincourt returning to ~65,000 oz in 2026 and expanding from 2027, plus Arafura from 2027+, is the clearest multi-year earnings torque in the group. Every incremental ounce lands at ~US$4,000/oz-class prices.
- **Components (Astra Otoparts).** Profit rose to **Rp 1,164 bn from Rp 984 bn** (+18.3%) — outperforming the automotive segment, consistent with **press-release disclosure that the components division profit rose 23%**. Moving to ~85% ownership via the October 2026 tender increases ASII's claim on this growth.
- **Agribusiness (AALI).** Profit **+56.5% to ~Rp 1.09–1.10 trn** on high CPO prices. This is a genuine second leg when CPO is strong and a drag when it is not.
- **Financial services.** Asset growth of 4–9% across FIF, Sedaya Multi Investama and SANYF continues, but the Stage 3 deterioration argues for slower, higher-quality growth.
- **Not a growth option: capex.** H1 capex cut 40% is a defensive move, not a strategic one.

**How to use this.** The grids are **not** a valuation opinion; they are the arithmetic that converts a market price into a multiple. The analytical content is in the *spread*: on trough reported earnings the P/E-anchor values are **13%–26% below** the P/B-anchor values at comparable multiple levels, which is precisely the dislocation a non-recurring-heavy half creates. **An investor buying on reported P/E is buying a depressed denominator; an investor buying on P/B is buying a balance sheet that has just been levered up for a non-earning asset.** The honest conclusion is that **Astra needs either a market price (which this analysis does not have) or a normalised earnings estimate (which the fact sheet does not provide)** to be valued; without either, any target multiple is arbitrary.

**A peer-multiple comparison was attempted and abandoned.** The pipeline's `financial_ratios` table was queried for automotive, conglomerate and Astra-adjacent comparators (`sector ILIKE '%auto%'`, `name ILIKE '%astra%'`, `name ILIKE '%tractor%'`) and returned **no rows**; the full ticker universe is **27 codes, all energy/coal** (AADI, ADRO, BYAN, BUMI, etc.). **No peer multiple is quoted because none exists in the available data** — the comp is omitted rather than guessed, per the data-integrity rule.

**EBITDA caveat.** Even with market data, **EV/EBITDA could not be computed** from the fact sheet: there is **no operating-profit, depreciation or amortisation line in the income statement extract**, so EBITDA is not derivable without the filing. Depreciation of Rp 8,083 bn for the half is disclosed in Note 14 (cost of revenue Rp 6,404 bn, selling Rp 169 bn, G&A Rp 967 bn, immature plantations Rp 16 bn, FX translation Rp 527 bn), which on a derived basis gives **EBITDA of roughly Rp 22,500 bn (H1)** — but that is a derived figure built on a derived operating profit and should be treated as indicative only.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

- **The balance sheet remains investment-grade-comfortable.** Liabilities/equity of **0.775x**, equity ratio of **56.3%**, total equity of **Rp 289,844 bn**, and **Rp 94.1 trn of undrawn credit facilities** (Note 36d) plus **Rp 8.4 trn of supplier-financing facilities**. Even after a Rp 15.5 trn net-debt increase, company-basis gearing is **25%**, not 50%.
- **Cash conversion is better than one-for-one.** **OCF/net profit of 1.218x** in a half containing a large non-cash impairment proves the P&L hit is not a cash problem. The **collections ratio rose to 121.2% from 117.9%**.
- **Financial-services earnings are highly cash-generative and high-margin.** A **60.0% gross margin** and **Rp 4,733 bn of net profit from Rp 17,147 bn of revenue** is the most profitable large business in the group and provides a stable, growing earnings floor.
- **Capex is genuinely flexible.** A **40% cut in six months (Rp 8,563 → 5,130 bn)** with no apparent franchise damage demonstrates that a large part of the spend is discretionary — a real downside buffer.
- **Associate dividends are a large, under-appreciated cash source:** **AHM Rp 3,894 bn** and **ADM Rp 607 bn** received in H1 2026, plus **Rp 877 bn of dividend receivables** booked but not yet received.
- **Liquidity coverage of the maturity wall is not in doubt** — Rp 63,251 bn of current loans against Rp 94.1 trn of undrawn facilities. The maturity is a *rollover* issue, not a *solvency* issue.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- **Automotive held 51% four-wheel share in a market that grew ~16%**, and motorcycles held **77%** share with volumes up 1%. Both are structurally strong franchises with high barriers (OEM JVs, dealer networks, financing tie-in).
- **Components outperformed:** **Astra Otoparts profit +18.3% to Rp 1,164 bn** and press-release disclosure of a **23% components-division profit increase** show the parts business is taking share and margin even as vehicle sales lag.
- **Agribusiness delivered: AALI profit +56.5%** to ~Rp 1.09–1.10 trn, evidence that the "Others" segment can produce a genuine earnings contribution.
- **The mining segment's *gross* business recovered strongly in Q2** — derived Q2 gross profit of ~Rp 5,679 bn versus ~Rp 4,828 bn in Q1 — proving the operating problem was not structural.
- **The business acted on costs:** headcount down **2,948 (−2.2%)** to 130,719, capex cut 40%, and credit write-offs of **Rp 361 bn** executed (Note 6) imply active rather than passive management of the downturn.
- **Japan-OEM-and-distribution moat remains intact.** No single customer >10% of revenue and no single third-party supplier >10% of revenue means concentration risk is unusually low for an Indonesian conglomerate.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- **A three-legged structure with genuinely uncorrelated cycles.** Automotive (domestic consumption), financial services (credit spreads and volumes) and mining (commodity prices and policy) do not move together — which is exactly why a mining shock produced a −19% owners' profit outcome rather than a −50% one.
- **The gold pivot is strategically coherent.** Martabe restarting to ~65,000 oz, plus Arafura acquired for **USD 540 mn** as a pipeline asset, positions Astra inside the one commodity with the strongest price trend of the period (gold +38% YTD in Q2 per BRIDS).
- **The NCI structure limits downside capture.** Because minorities bear ~37–40% of UNTR's losses, the **parent's reported profit fell 19.2% versus a 31.5% consolidated decline** — a structural shock absorber.
- **The 17 July 2026 EGMS mandate** — authority for a **buyback of up to Rp 8.0 trn** plus transfer of up to **100,000,000 buyback shares to a management stock ownership programme** (Note 39) — gives management both a valuation tool and a retention tool.
- **Post-period evidence of cyclical recovery is concrete, not speculative:** Q3 2026 group profit of **Rp 21.60 trn** and mining & heavy equipment back to **+15% year-on-year profit growth** ([CNBC Indonesia, 28 October 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20261028173355-17-780330/laba-astra-international--asii--rp216-triliun-di-kuartal-iii-2026)).

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

**HIGH — Martabe's licence is still under government evaluation.** Note 36h states that PTAR's business licence was included in a **20 January 2026 revocation announcement** and that, by 30 June 2026, PTAR had resumed operations **with the government still evaluating the licence**. A re-revocation would remove the group's highest-margin revenue stream for a second time in twelve months, and the impairment already booked (part of the Rp 2.1 trn mining non-recurring charge) would likely need topping up. This is the highest-severity, lowest-probability, highest-impact item in the note.

**HIGH — H2 2026 opex does not reverse.** Selling expense **+18.6%** and G&A **+13.2%** on a **−3.0%** revenue base added **180 bps of opex ratio** and **264 bps of operating-margin erosion**. If these are structural (newly consolidated industrial-warehouse platform, MSOP accruals from July 2026, subsidiary expansion) rather than one-off, the H2 operating margin does not recover even if revenue does. **The drivers are not disclosed and this is the most important thing to learn from the Q3 filing.**

**MODERATE — Financial-services credit deterioration.** **Stage 3 receivables rose 50.5% to Rp 2,651 bn** while total provision rose only 0.6% to **Rp 5,477 bn**. Coverage of credit-impaired assets therefore fell sharply. Write-off policy triggers at 150 days overdue, so a weaker consumer backdrop would convert Stage 2 (Rp 28,443 bn) into Stage 3 quickly. A **Rp 1 trn provisioning increase would cost ~8% of group net profit.**

**MODERATE — Refinancing in a weaker rupiah.** **Rp 63,251 bn of loans are current**; the **USD 648 mn (Rp 11,577 bn)** syndicated facility matures in 2026, the **USD 118 mn (Rp 2,114 bn)** short-term syndicated loan matures **30 September 2026**, and the floating-rate share of borrowings rose to **Rp 55,576 bn from Rp 41,891 bn**. With **net foreign currency liabilities after hedging of Rp 1,787 bn** versus net assets of Rp 11,989 bn at December, refinancing is now a P&L event, not just a liquidity event.

**MODERATE — Prepaid-tax overhang.** **Prepaid taxes +38.4% alongside taxes payable −22.6%** says the group has overpaid PPh 25 instalments against a run-rate that fell. That is a **forward-earnings signal** — management's internal plan at the time of the instalments was more optimistic than what H1 delivered — and it may indicate that **deferred tax assets at loss-making units (Martabe/Agincourt, nickel) are at risk of impairment**.

**LOW — Prepaid taxes under objection.** **Rp 984 bn under objection and appeal** (Rp 1,245 bn at December). Declining, so not a growing dispute, but a cash-timing risk.

### 11.2 Structural Risks

**HIGH — The mining-property carrying value is impaired and partly unverified.** **Mining properties of Rp 15,060 bn, of which nickel Rp 5,293 bn and gold Rp 5,307 bn**, sit behind **concessions expiring between 2026 and 2044** and are depreciated on units-of-production using reserve estimates reviewed prospectively. **A Rp 2.1 trn impairment has just been taken; a second round cannot be ruled out**, and the reserve assumptions are a critical accounting estimate (Note 35) with no quantitative sensitivity disclosed.

**HIGH — Automotive share loss is the persistent, under-priced drag.** **51% share of a market growing 16% means Astra grew 10%** — i.e. it is losing share in an expanding market, which is the most dangerous pattern in autos because it is masked by revenue growth. Chinese-brand competition is a structural, multi-year pressure on both volume and price, and it arrives precisely when **selling expense is already running +18.6%**. This is the single most important long-term risk in the note.

**MODERATE — Related-party intensity is high and rising.** **Purchases from related parties were 30.76% of COGS plus operating expense (28.80% previously)** — principally Toyota-Astra Motor, Astra Honda Motor and Astra Daihatsu Motor. The consolidated gross margin is therefore partly a transfer-pricing outcome, and a 196 bps increase in one half deserves scrutiny.

**MODERATE — Capital allocation is running ahead of cash generation.** In one half the group: paid **Rp 11,693 bn of dividends**, bought **Rp 1,744 bn of treasury shares** (lifting treasury to 563.5 mn shares), spent **USD 540 mn on a non-producing gold mine**, and **cut capex 40%** — while FCF fell to **Rp 11,124 bn** and **net debt rose 55%**. The sequencing is questionable: growth capex was cut while an acquisition was debt-funded.

**MODERATE — Fully depreciated assets.** **Rp 59.5 trn of fully depreciated fixed assets still in use** against **Rp 83.6 bn... (Rp 83,628 bn) of net PPE** means ~71% of the net book sits atop a fully written-down base. Current returns on assets are flattered and the replacement-capex requirement is above the current run-rate.

**LOW — Equity-investment price risk is large and hidden.** A **30% move in other investments would affect other equity by Rp 5.7 trn** (Note 37). That is an OCI volatility source that can move book value by ~2.5% without touching earnings.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

**HIGH — Coal RKAB policy is the dominant exogenous variable.** The 2026 national coal production quota was reportedly targeted at **~600 mn tonnes versus 1.2 bn tonnes in 2025**, which drove Komatsu (−27%), PAMA overburden (−10%) and own coal sales (−10%). Quota relaxation has since been reported, but **the group has no control over the variable that determines a third of its revenue**. Regulatory risk in Indonesian mining is **realised, not hypothetical** — the Martabe licence event proves it.

**MODERATE — Reporting-standard transition.** Revised standards effective **1 January 2026** (PSAK 107, 109, 338 amendments) are stated to have no significant impact; **new standards effective 2027 include PSAK 118, 119, 228 and 413** and the Group is **still evaluating their impact** (fact-sheet `context_notes`). PSAK 118/119 are the revenue/financial-instruments-class standards; for a group with a large financing and insurance arm, the transition could change presentation materially and is an unquantified 2027 risk.

**MODERATE — OECD Pillar Two.** The Group is **within scope and applied the exception**, with management believing no significant impact. Given the group's Indonesian and Singaporean structures this is plausible, but "no significant impact" is a management assertion, not a computed number.

**MODERATE — Commodity price dependence, unhedged in the P&L.** Gold at ~US$4,000/oz, CPO at MYR 4,400–4,700/t and coal benchmarks firmed through Q2 2026. A reversal would hit both the mining segment and the agribusiness leg simultaneously, and the group's hedge book covers **only interest-rate and cross-currency exposure, not commodity price** (Note 34). The gold zero-cost collar referenced in earlier work is a *post-hedge* position, and the fact sheet shows **derivative liabilities of Rp 2,416 bn** at period end.

**LOW — Insurance coverage gaps.** Fixed assets are insured for **Rp 127.2 trn** and inventories for Rp 23.5 trn, but **all immature and mature plantations are not insured** (Note 13) and bearer plants carry **Rp 7,384 bn** of value. Climate-change impacts on plantation useful lives are an explicitly flagged estimation area.

### 11.4 Valuation Concerns

**HIGH — No valuation can be established from these inputs, and that is itself a concern.** The market-data stub is **empty**; `pe_annualized`, `pb`, `ev` and `ev_ebitda` are **null**; and **no peer multiple exists in the pipeline database** (all 27 tickers are energy/coal). An investor therefore has to choose a normalisation: **reported EPS of Rp 313 (P/E-anchored value materially lower) or book value of Rp 5,745.73 (P/B-anchored value materially higher)**. The 13%–26% spread between the two anchor grids in §8.2 is the valuation uncertainty this half has created.

**MODERATE — The dividend and the buyback compete with deleveraging.** A **62.3% payout on annualised H1 EPS**, a stated **"up to 50%" policy ceiling**, an **Rp 8.0 trn buyback mandate**, **25% gearing** and a **non-earning Rp 9 trn acquisition** cannot all be satisfied without further borrowing or a 2H recovery. Something has to give, and the market will price which.

**MODERATE — Earnings quality is weaker than the underlying number because of the other-income ambiguity.** The fact-sheet schema does not cleanly separate **impairment, fair-value losses, FX and share-of-associates**, so a reader cannot independently verify the **Rp 2,359 bn non-recurring** claim from the numbers presented. A valuation built on the "underlying −7%" framing requires trusting a management-defined adjustment.

**LOW — NCI distorts any consolidated multiple.** With **NCI at Rp 59,776 bn** and **NCI absorbing 6.1% of profit but 20.6% of equity** in this half, P/E on consolidated net profit is not the right denominator and EV/EBITDA cannot be computed at all. Any multiple comparison across periods will be distorted by the mix of NCI earnings.

## 13. Overall Assessment

### 13.1 Financial Health Score: 6/10

**Balance sheet: strong.** Equity of **Rp 289,844 bn**, liabilities/equity **0.775x**, company gearing **25%**, **Rp 94.1 trn of undrawn facilities**, covenant compliance, and **OCF conversion of 1.22x** are all solid.

**Cash flow: adequate but deteriorating.** OCF **−29.8%**, FCF **−23.7%**, **FCF after dividends negative (−Rp 569 bn)**, and **net debt +55%** on the computed basis.

**Why not higher than 6.** Three things cost the score: (i) **quick ratio below 1.0x** and working capital down 20.1%; (ii) **net debt rising Rp 15.5 trn in six months** into a non-earning asset and treasury shares; (iii) **derived interest coverage falling from 10.21x to 7.77x** and the floating-rate share of debt rising to Rp 55,576 bn. A 6/10 reflects a balance sheet that can absorb this half comfortably but cannot absorb a second one like it without changing the distribution policy.

### 13.2 Operational Performance Score: 5/10

**What worked:** automotive held 51% share in a market +16%; motorcycles 77% share; components profit +23%; agribusiness profit +56.5%; the mining segment's *gross* business recovered strongly in Q2 (derived gross profit ~Rp 5,679 bn vs ~Rp 4,828 bn).

**What did not:** Komatsu units **−27%**; overburden **−10%**; own coal **−10%**; gold **23,000 oz vs 125,000 oz**; **selling expense +18.6% and G&A +13.2% on a −3% revenue base**; **Stage 3 financing receivables +50.5%** against a 0.6% provision increase; derived operating margin **−264 bps**.

A 5/10 is the correct score because **the revenue franchise held while the cost base and the credit book deteriorated** — and because management's operational response to the revenue decline was largely financial (capex and headcount cuts) rather than commercial. The margin of error is that most of the damage is provably non-recurring.

### 13.3 Growth Score: 4/10

**Revenue −3.0%, gross profit −6.8%, net profit −31.5%, EPS −18.3%, OCF −29.8%.** Nine months of the twelve metrics in §9.4 deteriorated. The **only growing lines are financial services (60.0% gross margin, Rp 4,733 bn profit) and the associates** (ADM profit up 68% to Rp 2,055 bn; share-of-associates up 35.3% per CNBC).

The 4 rather than 3 reflects the **genuinely identifiable forward torque**: Martabe to ~65,000 oz, Arafura from 2027, a re-rated gold price, RKAB relaxation, and the post-period **Q3 2026 profit of Rp 21.60 trn with mining back to +15% year-on-year**. The 4 rather than 5 reflects that **none of that torque is yet in the numbers presented here**, and that **capex was cut 40%**, which mortgages future growth.

### 13.4 Valuation Score: 4/10

**This score is deliberately low and is a statement about the evidence, not about the share price.** There is **no market price, no market cap, no peer multiple in the pipeline database (all 27 tickers are energy/coal), and no EBITDA line in the fact sheet**. `pe_annualized`, `pb`, `ev` and `ev_ebitda` are all null.

What can be said: **annualised reported EPS of Rp 626 versus underlying annualised EPS of ~Rp 744** means the reported multiple overstates the true multiple by ~19%. **Book value of Rp 5,745.73 understates the earning power of the franchise** if the trough is cyclical. **The dividend of Rp 390 against annualised EPS of Rp 626 is a 62.3% payout** — high for a group that needs to delever.

A 4/10 expresses: *insufficient information to establish value, with a bias toward the view that a trough-earnings multiple flatters the stock and a book-value multiple flatters it less.* Any reader with the current price should re-score this section.

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case

1. **The earnings decline is ~two-thirds non-cash and non-recurring.** Reported attributable profit fell 19% but **underlying fell only 7%** (Rp 14,892 bn), and **roughly 47% of the pre-tax decline is a single line** (`other_income_net`, −Rp 3,459 bn) that is **almost entirely an impairment** booked in Q2. Strip it and the operating franchise is broadly intact. If the impairment is genuinely one-off, the H1 2027 base is a low bar.
2. **The revenue problem has already stopped.** Q2 2026 revenue of **Rp 79,245 bn was flat year-on-year (−0.3%)** after −5.6% in Q1, and Q2 gross margin recovered **~170 bps off the Q1 trough**. The trough is behind, not ahead.
3. **The operating recovery is confirmed post-period.** **Q3 2026 group profit of Rp 21.60 trn** with **mining and heavy equipment back to +15% year-on-year profit growth** ([CNBC Indonesia, 28 October 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20261028173355-17-780330/laba-astra-international--asii--rp216-triliun-di-kuartal-iii-2026)) is direct evidence that Q2 was the bottom.
4. **Three uncorrelated earnings legs de-risk the franchise.** Financial services at a **60.0% gross margin delivering Rp 4,733 bn**; automotive at **51%/77% share** with associate income flowing from ADM (profit +68% to Rp 2,055 bn) and AHM (Rp 4,905 bn); and a recovering mining arm. No single customer is >10% of revenue.
5. **The NCI structure means the parent captures a disproportionate share of the recovery.** Owners' profit fell 19.2% against a 31.5% consolidated fall — so the rebound in **UNTR's** earnings, when it comes, likewise returns disproportionately to ASII (effective interest now **63.57%**, up from 62.12%).
6. **Balance sheet capacity to exploit the cycle.** Liabilities/equity **0.775x**, **Rp 94.1 trn of undrawn facilities**, and an **Rp 8.0 trn buyback mandate** (17 July 2026 EGMS) that management can deploy at trough valuations.
7. **Gold is the right commodity at the right time.** Martabe to **~65,000 oz** in 2026 with expansion from 2027, Arafura as a pipeline asset, gold **+38% YTD** through Q2 per BRIDS. This is genuine, multi-year optionality acquired at a cyclical moment.
8. **Agribusiness is a working second leg.** **AALI profit +56.5%** to ~Rp 1.09–1.10 trn, with CPO at MYR 4,400–4,700/t.
9. **Dividend is stable at Rp 390 DPS** through FY2025 (Rp 98 interim, unchanged for two years), with a stated **payout commitment of up to 50%** and a demonstrated willingness to return cash.

### 14.2 Bear Case

1. **The opex problem is unexplained and therefore probably not one-off.** **Selling +18.6% and G&A +13.2% on −3.0% revenue** cost **180 bps of opex ratio**. If even half is structural (newly consolidated warehouse platform, MSOP accruals, subsidiary expansion), the operating margin does not recover to 11.8% in FY2026, and every 100 bps of opex ratio is worth roughly **Rp 1.6 trn of annual pre-tax profit**.
2. **The balance sheet no longer has a cushion.** Net debt **+55%** on the computed basis and **+26.6%** on the company basis; **gearing 25%**; **quick ratio 0.97x**; **working capital −20.1%**; **FCF after dividends −Rp 569 bn**. Worse, the capital went into a **non-producing gold mine** and **treasury shares**, neither of which generates earnings. If 2H 2026 disappoints, the levers are painful: cut the dividend, halt the buyback, or sell assets.
3. **Automotive is losing share in a growing market.** Group sales **+10% versus market +16%** means share fell to **51%**. Chinese-brand encroachment is a multi-year structural threat, and Astra's response is showing up as **higher selling expense**, not higher volume. This is the risk the market under-prices most.
4. **Martabe's licence is not resolved.** Note 36h confirms the government is **still evaluating the licence** as of 30 June 2026, four months after the 20 January 2026 revocation announcement. A second suspension would hit the highest-margin revenue stream for the second time in a year.
5. **Credit quality is quietly weakening.** **Stage 3 receivables +50.5% to Rp 2,651 bn** against total provision up only 0.6%. Coverage of impaired assets fell materially. In a segment contributing **35.5% of group net profit**, a Rp 1 trn provisioning increase costs ~8% of earnings.
6. **The mining impairment may not be finished.** **Gold Rp 5,307 bn + nickel Rp 5,293 bn of mining properties** remain on the books, with reserves as a critical accounting estimate and **no quantitative sensitivity disclosed**. A Rp 2.1 trn charge already taken is a warning, not a conclusion.
7. **Earnings quality cannot be independently verified.** The schema does not cleanly separate **impairment, fair-value losses, FX and share-of-associates**, interest income is carried at **0** despite the group holding Rp 46,150 bn of cash, and the derived residual income line is **Rp 6,687 bn**. An investor relying on the "underlying −7%" framing is relying on a management adjustment they cannot audit from the numbers presented.
8. **No valuation support can be demonstrated.** Without a price, market cap or peer multiple, the analyst cannot assert the stock is cheap. **Reported P/E and P/B give values 13%–26% apart** at the same multiple level, which is the definition of an unanchored valuation.
9. **The dividend is at risk of a cut.** **62.3% payout on annualised H1 EPS** against a stated **"up to 50%"** ceiling. A defended Rp 390 DPS with trough earnings requires either a 2H recovery or additional borrowing.

### 14.4 Recommendation

**HOLD — recovery-biased, with a valuation-data qualifier.**

**The case for HOLD rather than BUY.** The operating trough is real and is behind the company: Q2 revenue was flat year-on-year, Q2 gross margin recovered ~170 bps sequentially, the impairment is a one-off below the gross line, underlying profit fell only 7%, and the post-period Q3 2026 result (Rp 21.60 trn, mining +15%) confirms the turn. The franchise — 51% four-wheel share, 77% motorcycle share, a 60%-gross-margin financial-services arm, and low customer concentration — is intact. On a normalised-earnings view, this is a buying opportunity in a quality compounder.

**The case against BUY.** Three things must be true before the risk/reward is clearly favourable, and none is yet demonstrable: (i) the **opex step-up (+180 bps)** must prove cyclical rather than structural, and management has not explained it; (ii) the **Martabe licence** must move from "still evaluating" to formally resolved, because a second suspension would re-break the highest-margin revenue stream; and (iii) the **capital-allocation sequencing** (Rp 11.7 trn dividend + Rp 8 trn buyback mandate + a Rp 9 trn non-earning acquisition, all against negative post-dividend FCF) must be reconciled with deleveraging. And critically: **I cannot establish value.** `pe_annualized`, `pb`, `ev` and `ev_ebitda` are all null; no market-data stub was supplied; and no peer multiple exists in the pipeline database. **A recommendation without a price is a recommendation about business quality, not about expected return** — and the honest label for that is HOLD.

**Target price range: not set.** This is a deliberate abstention, not an omission. Deriving a target requires a market price (for the multiple) or a normalised EPS estimate (for the level), and neither is in the inputs. What I can state is the **conditional frame**: at annualised reported EPS of **Rp 626** the stock is a trough-earnings story; at annualised underlying EPS of **~Rp 744** it is modestly less so; and at **book value of Rp 5,745.73** it is a balance-sheet story with a levered asset base. **The §8.2 grids give the implied value at 8x–16x P/E (Rp 5,008–10,016) and 0.8x–1.6x P/B (Rp 4,597–9,193)**; a reader with the market price should read the current multiple off those grids and re-score §13.4 before acting. If a normalisation to underlying, post-recovery earnings implies a mid-teens multiple on the trough number, the stock is expensive; if it implies a high-single-digit multiple, it is not.

**Position sizing and stance.** For an existing holder: **hold, do not add aggressively** — the Q3 2026 result validates the recovery, but the opex question and Martabe licence are unresolved and a second disappointment would arrive alongside the leverage increase. For a new investor: **accumulate on evidence, not on hope** — the entry trigger is the Q3 2026 filing showing the **opex ratio back below 11.4%** and **selling expense growth below revenue growth**, at which point the non-recurring bridge converts into a clean earnings recovery.

**Key catalysts** are the Q3 2026 filing (already positive on profit), Martabe's formal licence resolution, RKAB relaxation showing through Komatsu units, the Otoparts tender completing, and any disclosure that explains the opex step-up.

**Review trigger — reassess immediately on any of the following:**
1. **A second Martabe licence action** or a further impairment on the gold or nickel carrying values (Rp 10.6 trn combined).
2. **Q3 2026 opex ratio above 11.4%** or selling-expense growth again exceeding revenue growth — this would make the opex step-up structural and would cut my operational score to 3/10.
3. **Financial-services Stage 3 receivables above Rp 3,500 bn** or a provisioning charge above Rp 2 trn in a half.
4. **A dividend reduction**, a **halt to the Rp 8.0 trn buyback mandate**, or **company-basis gearing above 35%**.
5. **A market price becoming available** — at which point §6.7, §8.2 and §13.4 must be recomputed and this recommendation re-scored.
