# Perusahaan Perseroan (Persero) PT Bukit Asam Tbk (PTBA) — Q1 2026
---

**Reporting basis:** Consolidated interim financial statements for the three months ended 31 March 2026, prepared in millions of Rupiah, **unaudited**, completed and authorized for issue by the Board of Directors on 29 April 2026. P&L and cash-flow comparatives are Q1 2025 (three months ended 31 March 2025); balance-sheet comparatives are 31 December 2025 (prior fiscal year-end). Immediate parent: PT Mineral Industri Indonesia (Persero) (MIND ID); ultimate controller: the Republic of Indonesia. Basic/diluted EPS is stated in full Rupiah (Note 32).

## 1. Executive Summary

PTBA delivered a doubling of earnings on a flat top line. Q1 2026 revenue was Rp9,929,722mn, essentially unchanged from Rp9,958,441mn in Q1 2025 (-0.29%), yet gross profit jumped +47.4% to Rp1,543,782mn and net profit attributable to owners doubled to Rp801,794mn (EPS Rp70 vs Rp34, +105.9%). The arithmetic is unambiguous: COGS fell 5.9% (Rp8,911,252mn → Rp8,385,940mn) on a stable ASP environment, taking gross margin from 10.5% to 15.5% (+505 bps). This is a cost-and-destocking story, not a price story.

The mechanism is now well evidenced. Production fell 22% YoY to 6.62mt on exceptionally high early-2026 rainfall, but sales volume was held roughly flat (-1% at ~10.17mt, per management's Q1 release and press coverage) by drawing down coal stockpiles — visible in inventories falling 31% from Rp4,883,306mn at end-2025 to Rp3,379,850mn. Selling from inventory and lower mining-services volume (PAMA charges fell from Rp1,711,563mn to Rp1,145,638mn per related-party disclosures) compressed the cost line while revenue held. Management framed it as "efficiency discipline and selective mining" (external, [CNBC Indonesia, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20260501060912-17-731440/laba-ptba-naik-105-bos-besar-ungkap-penyebabnya)); ASP rose only ~1% YoY against Newcastle +14% / ICI-3 -2%, so price contributed essentially nothing.

The balance sheet strengthened in form rather than substance. Total liabilities fell 8.2% to Rp19,557,671mn (Rp21,300,262mn at end-2025), driven by repayment of the Rp1.95tn short-term BRI facility (expired 2 January 2026, nil at quarter-end) and a Rp1.54tn reduction in trade payables; equity rose 4.7% to Rp23,670,572mn. Gross loans of Rp2,719,627mn are dwarfed by Rp4,623,727mn of cash and securities — **net cash of Rp1,903,100mn** (net debt/equity -0.08). The headline D/E of 0.83 is lease-, provision- and benefit-liability-driven, not bank-debt-driven; loans-to-equity is just 0.11. Operating cash flow of Rp2,060,004mn (+69.9% YoY) covered capex (Rp915,800mn) and Rp3,103,453mn of loan repayments with room to spare; the quarter ended with cash of Rp4,192,245mn, down only Rp339,430mn sequentially.

Valuation cannot be assessed against market data: **no price, market cap, P/E, P/B, EV or yield was provided for this run — not disclosed.** On fundamentals alone, PTBA exits Q1 with an annualized ROE of ~13.9%, a book value of Rp2,054.5/share (per computed ratio), and a business whose Q1 margin expansion is demonstrably non-recurring in its cost component: post-period data show Q2 2026 cash cost up 6% qoq (fuel +59% qoq) as the Hormuz oil shock passed through, and HBA re-rated from ~US$100/t in Q1 to US$124–127/t by Aug–Sep 2026 — margin pressure and price tailwind arriving together. Our recommendation is a **HOLD (conservative posture pending market data)**; see §14.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

- **Volume:** Production 6.62mt (-22% YoY) on exceptional rainfall; coal transport/rail 8.74mt (-7% YoY); sales volume ~10.17mt (-1% YoY) via "prudent inventory management" (management, external, high confidence, in-period). No stripping-ratio or unit-cost disclosures appear in the interim statements themselves.
- **Price:** HBA 6,322 kcal/kg GAR printed US$102.87/t (period II Feb 2026), US$103.01/t (period II Mar 2026), US$99.87/t (period I Apr 2026) — i.e., a flat ~US$100–103/t backdrop through the quarter (external, Bisnis.com, 18 Feb / 1 Apr 2026). PTBA's disclosed ASP moved only ~+1% YoY.
- **FX:** The Rupiah provides only a "limited natural hedge" via export sales (Note 35); monetary net USD position US$137.9mn equivalent (Rp2,349,394mn) at 31 Mar 2026 vs US$143.3mn (Rp2,400,811mn) at 31 Dec 2025 — a modestly smaller USD asset position, no hedging programme. FX sensitivity: ±10% IDR/USD moves post-tax profit by Rp203.14bn (full amount).

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

The Q1 revenue base is sustainable but its margin is not. Key forward items:

- **DMO regime:** 25% of production at the US$70/t cap (Kepmen ESDM 399/2023); PTBA in fact supplied 53% of Q1 volume domestically. Post-period, management quantified the cost of supplying >54% of volume domestically at ~Rp14.5tn of forgone potential profit (external, [CNBC Indonesia, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20260915181009-4-768181/pasok-dmo-lebih-dari-54-ptba-kehilangan-potensi-laba-rp-145-triliun)) — a policy constraint, not an earnings event.
- **Contract book (Note 29):** long-dated coal sales agreements anchor volume — PLTU 10,000 MW Phase I Rp2,159bn contracted to Dec 2032; PLTU Tarahan Rp137bn to Dec 2031; PLTU Jawa 9-10 (FOT) Rp360bn to Dec 2028; HBAP (Sumsel 8, US$31.40/t) Rp386bn to Oct 2048; BPI (Banjarsari, US$39.39/t) Rp80bn to Jun 2044; KAI rail delivery agreements Rp1,563bn + Rp327bn to Dec 2027. Near-term repricing hinges on HBA-linked domestic contracts (Suralaya Rp724bn and Bukit Asam Rp72bn contracts expire Dec 2026).
- **Post-period prices:** HBA 6,322 GAR rose to US$124.44/t (period I Aug 2026) and US$126.87/t (period I Sep 2026); WTI broke US$100 in early April 2026 (external, ESDM/CNBC, Aug–Sep 2026). This re-rates PTBA's export-linked ASP upward from Q2 onward — but with a fuel-cost offset (see §11).
- **Weather:** Q1's excess rainfall reverses into a possible El Niño "Godzilla" dry season risk on river logistics in Kalimantine trade routes (external, medium confidence, Kompas 28 Apr 2026) — PTBA's Sumatra-based rail/barge logistics are relatively insulated, a structural advantage.
- **License runway:** PP 25/2024 gives PTBA legal certainty for 10-year IUP extensions with non-dilutable ≥30% IUP-holder ownership (Note 29k) — a long reserve/permit runway (producing mines: Muara Tiga Besar, Banko Barat, Air Laya, Banko Tengah, IPC-Palaran, BBK-Bukit Kendi; Peranap under development).

### 3.1 Gross Margin Analysis

| Item (Rp mn) | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | % change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 9,929,722 | 9,958,441 | -0.29% |
| COGS | 8,385,940 | 8,911,252 | **-5.90%** |
| Gross profit | 1,543,782 | 1,047,189 | **+47.42%** |
| Gross margin | **15.55%** | 10.52% | **+503 bps** |

The entire earnings inflection sits in COGS. Drivers: (i) inventory-funded sales (coal inventories fell Rp1,509,739mn in the quarter) with lower mined tonnage and correspondingly lower contractor charges — related-party mining/transport service costs to PAMA fell 33% YoY and to KAI 4% (Rp1,890,039mn vs Rp1,976,099mn); (ii) "selective mining" efficiency framing by management (external); (iii) diesel still cheap — management put the in-quarter fuel impact at only ~+3% YoY (external, high confidence); and (iv) coal purchasing was nil in Q1 2026 vs Rp34,835mn in Q1 2025 (Note 27a). Amortisation of mining properties (Rp24,021mn) and the bulk of depreciation (Rp349,078mn of Rp422,043mn) were allocated to cost of revenue, so the D&A load was roughly flat YoY. Critically, peer ITMG's Q1 2026 profit *fell* 15.8% on higher COGS — confirming this is a PTBA-specific cost/base-period event, not industry cost deflation (external, Kontan, 6 Jul 2026; see [[ITMG Q1 2026 Analysis]]).

### 4.2 Working Capital Components

- **Receivables (DSO 44.5 days, annualized):** Trade receivables rose Rp905,893mn qoq, driven by PLN Group (Rp1,225,584mn → Rp2,038,444mn) and HBAP (Rp704,270mn) — i.e., the receivables build is concentrated in SOE and JV counterparties, low credit risk but slow-paying. Aging shifted oddly: the 1–30 day bucket jumped to Rp1,198,116mn (from Rp225,151mn) while 31–90 days fell to Rp229,699mn (from Rp464,924mn) — flagged in the notes as a classification/timing item to verify. Impairment provision additions were trivial (Rp447mn); the HBAP provision is unchanged at Rp51,899mn.
- **Inventories (DIO 31.1 days):** Coal stock fell from Rp4,675,956mn to Rp3,166,217mn — the destocking engine of the quarter. Inventories are **uninsured** (Note 7) — a standing exposure. An NRV reversal of Rp1,144mn was recorded.
- **Payables (DPO 19.1 days):** Trade payables fell 42.6% to Rp2,078,216mn; related-party payables halved (Rp988,347mn → Rp468,864mn, USD 87,241mn component to nil). Accrued expenses rose to Rp5,289,080mn (from Rp4,480,404mn) with coal transportation accruals jumping to Rp2,032,700mn (from Rp829,380mn) — much of the payable unwind migrated into accruals rather than pure cash settlement. Whether it was cash settlement vs reclassification remains an open item.
- **Cash conversion cycle: 56.4 days** (DSO 44.5 + DIO 31.1 – DPO 19.1), flattered by the payables rundown and inventory release; expect DPO normalization to lengthen CCC in later quarters.

### 4.5 Non-Controlling Interests (NCI)

NCI in equity is just Rp137,403mn (0.6% of equity); NCI in Q1 profit Rp4,316mn (0.5% of net profit). IPC (49%-owned miner) carries the bulk: opening Rp137,550mn, income share Rp3,475mn, closing Rp141,025mn; the residual "others below 5%" is negative (–Rp3,622mn). The structure is a de facto near-wholly-owned consolidation — unlike peers with large listed subsidiaries, PTBA retains essentially all profit for the parent, supporting its historically high payout capacity.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks
- **HIGH — Q1 cost base is the trough, not the trend.** Post-period Q2 2026 data show cash cost +6% qoq (Rp899k/t) with fuel +59% qoq after the Hormuz shock pushed WTI past US$100 in April 2026 (external, high confidence, Kontan 3 Sep 2026). - **MODERATE — Receivables build.** +Rp906mn qoq, concentrated in PLN (Rp2.04tn) and HBAP (Rp704mn); a further DSO drift pressures Q2 OCF.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
- **HIGH — DMO cap strangles ASP.** 25% statutory DMO at US$70/t, but PTBA actually supplied 53% domestically; management quantifies >54% domestic supply as ~Rp14.5tn of forgone potential profit (post-period, external). The 212mt PLN supply mandate for 2026 pushes further in this direction.

- **MODERATE — HBAP structure:** Rp22.5tn liabilities, shares pledged to China EXIM, heavy reliance on PPA-side finance income; impairment provision static at Rp51.9mn on receivables despite the exposure.

### 11.3 Market and Regulatory Risks

## 14. Investment Thesis

### 14.1 Bull Case
PTBA is a net-cash, vertically integrated Sumatra coal-to-power play exiting its weakest cost quarter. From Q2 2026 onward, three forces stack: (i) HBA re-rated to US$124–127/t with lagged pass-through into domestic contracts (Suralaya and Bukit Asam PLN contracts reprice at Dec-2026 expiry); (ii) production normalizes post-rainfall (Q2: +94% qoq to 12.8mt per post-period data), restoring inventory; (iii) the Tanjung Enim–Kramasan rail (June 2026 completion) structurally lowers delivered cost for years. The BPI/HBAP power JV leg (Rp168bn quarterly profit share and growing) diversifies earnings away from pure coal pricing. At ADRO-type multiples (peer reference ~5.7x P/E, 0.62x PBV per pipeline DB, 2025-10-31 — indicative only), an SOE yield of ~65–70% payout on a recovering EPS stream is attractive.

### 14.2 Bear Case
Q1's +105% EPS is the worst possible base for expectation-setting: it was inventory-funded and fuel-cheap, and both reverse — Q2 cash cost already +6% qoq with fuel +59% (post-period, high confidence). Meanwhile, blended ASP is structurally capped by the DMO regime (53% domestic share vs a 25% statutory floor; ~Rp14.5tn of forgone profit per management), and the regulatory pipeline (PP 39/2025 affiliate mining-service restrictions, benchmark-price rules, onshore FX proceeds, one-door export via Danantara) all point toward more state extraction of the margin. PLN receivables of Rp2.0tn are policy credit, not commercial credit. If HBA mean-reverts and fuel stays above US$100 WTI, PTBA's margins compress from both ends.

### 14.3 Key Catalysts
1. **June 2026:** TLS VI & VII rail completion (82.77% at quarter-end) — cost-down proof point.
2. **Q2 2026 results (late July):** ASP +14% qoq / +20% YoY vs cash cost +6% qoq — net margin direction resolved (post-period data suggest net profit +218% YoY in Q2; treat as external, high-confidence).
3. **Dec 2026:** repricing/expiry of the Suralaya (Rp724bn) and PLTU Bukit Asam (Rp72bn) PLN contracts at higher HBA.
4. **FY2025 dividend declaration** (pattern: June declaration, July payment) — payout read on the recovering earnings base.
5. **Policy:** 212mt PLN supply mandate and DMO allocation (17.7mt target) outcomes; RKAB 2026 revision effects on price.

### 14.4 Recommendation
**HOLD (pending market data; risk qualifier: policy/commodity-driven earnings volatility).** The fundamentals case is genuinely good — net cash, integrated logistics, contracted power leg, IUP certainty — but we cannot set a target price without a market quote, and the one quarter of data we have is, by construction, the best cost quarter of the cycle. Directionally, we would accumulate on any weakness that prices PTBA at a discount to its Rp2,054 book value with a double-digit potential yield, and we would avoid chasing the +105% headline. **Indicative value anchor (not a target):** book value per share Rp2,054.5, with earnings power between the Q1 annualized Rp70/quarter cost-trough EPS and a fuel-inflated Q2 run-rate.

**Review triggers:** (i) Q2 2026 results confirming gross margin above ~12% despite fuel inflation; (ii) DMO/policy changes that relax the domestic pricing cap; (iii) rail project commissioning slip beyond June 2026; (iv) any PLN receivables impairment or HBAP provision movement; (v) provision of market data enabling a priced target.
