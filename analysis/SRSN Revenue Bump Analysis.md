# SRSN — Q1 to Q2 2026 Revenue Bump Analysis

*All figures in thousand Indonesian Rupiah (IDR '000), unless otherwise stated. Q2 standalone derived as H1 − Q1.*

---

## 1. The Question

SRSN's Q1 2026 revenue collapsed −23.3% YoY to Rp239.5bn, but Q2 2026 (derived standalone: H1 − Q1) jumped to Rp318.1bn — a +32.9% QoQ recovery and +4.9% YoY growth. What caused this?

Every segment grew QoQ: ethanol +13%, acetic acid +57%, Others +405%. But the profit story is decoupled from revenue — **segment profit (gross margin proxy) grew +32.1% even as total revenue fell −9.5% YoY.**

## 3. Root Cause #1 — Raw Material Cost Collapse (Dominant Driver)

The most important number in SRSN's H1 2026 income statement is not revenue — it's COGS.

From **Note 23 (Cost of Goods Sold)** in the Q2 report:

| COGS Component (Rp bn) | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw Material Usage | 218.3 | 358.7 | **−39.1%** |
| Raw Material Purchases | 91.8 | 215.6 | **−57.4%** |
| Manufacturing Cost | 73.2 | 66.5 | +10.0% |
| Finished Goods Purchased | 80.5 | 77.2 | +4.2% |
| Packaging COGS | 10.8 | 7.5 | +43.2% |
| **Total COGS** | **391.3** | **489.9** | **−20.1%** |

Raw material usage collapsed by **Rp140.4bn** — nearly 40%. Purchases fell even harder (−57%), while advances for raw material purchases surged +120% to Rp146.6bn (Note 9), meaning SRSN pre-paid for massive volumes of cheap molasses and was consuming pre-purchased inventory.

### Why Molasses Costs Collapsed

- **Bumper 2025-26 sugarcane harvest:** USDA forecast 35.0 MMT production, driven by La Niña rains lasting to April 2025 and area expansion under Indonesia's 3 MMT sugar self-sufficiency program.
- **Molasses procurement up 22%:** SRSN increased contracts from 124,000 tons (2025 harvest) to 152,100 tons (2026 harvest) across 6 plantation groups (PT Kebon Agung, PTPN IX–XII, PT PG Rajawali II) — Note 29/30.
- **Regional oversupply:** IMARC/EMR confirmed "lower upstream costs for cassava and molasses continued to erode support for regional distillers' production costs" throughout H1 2026. India's Ex-Kolhapur molasses fell −6.34% MoM in June 2026.

The inventory drawdown reinforces this: inventories fell from Rp310.3bn (Dec 2025) → Rp233.7bn (Mar 2026) → Rp171.8bn (Jun 2026), releasing Rp138.5bn in working capital. SRSN was running production hard, selling finished goods carried at a low-cost inventory basis, and restocking with incoming cheap molasses.

## 4. Root Cause #2 — Acetic Acid Price Rally (+30% in Q2 2026)

Asian acetic acid spot prices:

| Month | NE Asia | SE Asia |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2026 | $440/MT | $440/MT |
| May 2026 | **$570/MT** | **$560/MT** |
| Jul 2026 | $480/MT | $490/MT |

### The Causal Chain: Hormuz → Methanol → Acetic Acid

The Q2 2026 rally was triggered by a methanol supply shock with roots in the [[Strait_of_Hormuz_Crisis]]:

1. **US-Iran war (Feb 28, 2026):** SoH effectively closed. Middle Eastern methanol shipments — which accounted for **40% of SE Asia's 2025 imports** (~1.9mn tonnes) — were halted. ([[Southeast Asia faces methanol supply crunch - Mar 2026]])

2. **Simultaneous regional plant outages:** Both of SE Asia's largest methanol producers went offline:
   - **Sarawak Petchem (Malaysia):** 1.7mn t/yr, shut late Jan → Mar 8 — planned maintenance
   - **Brunei Methanol (BMC):** 850k t/yr, shut early Feb → Mar 8 — technical trip

3. **Methanol hits 4-year high:** Cfr SE Asia methanol reached **$400-410/MT on March 6**, with customers requesting 2× their usual term allocations. Only Petronas Labuan and Kaltim Methanol (Indonesia) remained online.

4. **~2-month pass-through to acetic acid:** Methanol is the primary feedstock for the methanol carbonylation HAc process. The March input-cost shock translated into the May acetic acid price peak ($570/MT) as producers passed through costs and regional HAc inventories depleted from the Q1 supply disruption.

5. **Methanol-to-HAc spread widened:** HAc prices rose faster than methanol, meaning SRSN's acetic acid segment captured both price inflation **and** margin expansion — segment profit surged +74.3% YoY on just +22.4% revenue growth.

The rally peaked in May/June and corrected to ~$480-490/MT by July as Sarawak/BMC resumed production and the June 17 MOU briefly restored Hormuz transit.

## 5. Root Cause #3 — "Others" Segment Seasonal Surge (+405% QoQ)

The "Others" segment swung from Rp9.7bn (Q1) to Rp49.0bn (Q2). Financial report notes reveal what's inside:

- **Packaging COGS +43%:** Rp10.8bn in H1 2026 vs Rp7.5bn in H1 2025 — indicating a separately packaged product line
- **Fertilizer operations:** Rp1.48bn selling expense line item (Note 24) confirms SRSN sells fertilizer-related products
- **CO2 sales:** Rp4.0bn to PT Sama Mandiri (related party), a CO2 buyer
- **Finished goods purchases for resale:** Rp80.5bn (H1 2026), suggesting significant trading/merchanting activity within "Others"

The most likely explanation: **Q2 is both the fertilizer/agro-input application season in Indonesia (dry-season planting) AND the molasses crush campaign**, producing a seasonal spike in by-products — CO2 from fermentation, spent wash, fertilizer, bagasse derivatives. The +43% packaging cost rise confirms real product was shipped, not an accounting reclassification.

Top-3 customer concentration fell from Rp228.8bn → Rp167.0bn (−27%). SRSN replaced the lost Singapore trader Pinnacle Synergy with Everlasting Harvest (a newly formed entity trading ethanol) and diversified into smaller buyers. But the gross revenue bump came from **smaller, more diverse customers and seasonal product lines**, not from the top-3 accounts.

## 7. What Did NOT Cause the Bump

- **Not ethanol price rises:** SE Asia ethanol fell ~$40/MT in H1 2026 (from $900 to $860/MT). The same cheap molasses that boosted SRSN's margins also depressed ethanol selling prices, especially for industrial grades.
- **Not E5 bioethanol mandate:** Started July 2026 (after Q2 closed), limited to non-subsidized fuel segments.
- **Not IDR/USD FX:** IDR weakened only modestly during Q2 (16,993 → 17,856 at June 30). Not a material revenue driver.
- **Not CIP commissioning:** The IDR 7.5bn CIP project was at 70% completion at June 2026; completion expected September 2026.
- **No announced contracts or capacity:** Zero IDX pada periode Q2 2026 regarding new customer wins, product launches, or capacity milestones.

## 9. Synthesis

The Q2 revenue bump is a **three-legged story** with uneven staying power:

| Driver | Magnitude | Sustainability |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap molasses (bumper crop) | Very high (COGS −39%) | **Medium.** Molasses prices could firm if the 2026-27 harvest is smaller or E5 demand pulls ethanol volumes higher. |
| Acetic acid rally (methanol + Hormuz) | High (HAc segment +74% profit) | **Low.** Rally already corrected from $570 → $490/MT by July. Hormuz remains contested but the specific methanol supply shock unwound when Sarawak/BMC restarted. |
| Seasonal Others spike | Moderate (Others +405% QoQ) | **Cyclical.** Q3 should see normalization as the fertilizer season passes, though CO2 and derivatives provide a higher base than Q1. |

The margin windfall — net margin at 12.9% in H1 versus implied ~4% in H1 2025 — is almost entirely a **COGS input-cost windfall** from record-cheap molasses, amplified by a perfectly-timed acetic acid rally. The core ethanol business (79.5% of revenue) actually contracted 13.5% YoY even in H1 2026. If molasses prices rebound in H2 2026 and the acetic acid rally has passed, the margin structure as reported in H1 may not repeat at the same magnitude.

## 10. Key Watchpoints for H2 2026

1. **Q3 molasses pricing** — does the 2026-27 harvest maintain cost relief?
2. **Acetic acid trajectory** — already at $490/MT in July, down from $570 peak
3. **E5 mandate absorption** — does fuel-grade ethanol demand materialize at scale from July 2026?
4. **Everlasting Harvest risk** — counterparty incorporated Dec 2024 with no track record
5. **[[Strait_of_Hormuz_Crisis]] evolution** — renewed closure would re-spike methanol/HAc but is not the base case
6. **CIP commissioning** — expected September 2026; benefits unknown
