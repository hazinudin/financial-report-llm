# MSTI Tbk (MSTI) — Q1 2026
---

All financial figures are presented in Indonesian Rupiah (IDR) full units, sourced from the unaudited interim financial statements for the three months ended 31 March 2026. Comparative income-statement and cash-flow data refer to the three months ended 31 March 2025; balance-sheet comparisons are against the prior fiscal year-end of 31 December 2025.

## 1. Executive Summary

MSTI delivered a weak start to 2026. Revenue contracted 8.03% year-over-year to Rp 1,095,972,132,331, while gross profit fell a steeper 10.09% to Rp 204,079,563,684, compressing the gross margin to 18.62%. Net profit attributable to owners declined 7.54% to Rp 106,613,521,116, translating to basic EPS of Rp 34.0 versus Rp 37.0 in the prior-year quarter. The income statement shows absolute cost discipline—selling and general & administrative expenses both ticked down year-over-year—but the top-line erosion and margin compression drove a 7.54% bottom-line decline.

The balance sheet remains fortress-like. Total equity grew 4.83% since December 2025 to Rp 2,402,046,101,744, and the current ratio stands at 3.59x. The company carries no material net debt; Note 16 explicitly records excess cash over borrowings of Rp 1,407,271,603,556, producing a negative net-debt-to-equity ratio of -58.59%. Liquidity is ample, with cash of Rp 1,499,157,049,830 and a quick ratio of 3.05x. However, asset quality is shifting: trade receivables rose to Rp 893,235,403,323 from Rp 788,637,128,851 at year-end, and the cash-flow statement reveals a sharp deterioration in collections.

Operating cash flow swung deeply negative to Rp -126,369,381,385 from positive Rp 63,570,518,849 in Q1 2025. Collections from customers amounted to only Rp 937,575,743,502 against revenue of Rp 1,095,972,132,331—a collections ratio of 85.5%. Payments to suppliers actually exceeded collections, at Rp 925,136,066,853, while salary outflows remained sticky at Rp 86,141,399,280. Free cash flow was negative Rp 136,136,923,227 after Rp 9,767,541,842 of capex. The cash burn is the most pressing issue; the balance sheet can absorb it for multiple quarters, but a sustained trend would erode the net-cash cushion.

No market data (share price, market capitalization, or enterprise value) was provided, so valuation multiples are not disclosed. The investment case therefore rests entirely on fundamental inflection. We assign a HOLD rating with an earnings-risk qualifier. The stock is uninvestable on a valuation basis until a price is available, and even on fundamentals, management must demonstrate that Q1’s revenue slip and cash-burn were project-timing artifacts rather than structural demand decay. Key review triggers are the Q2 2026 revenue print and operating-cash-flow recovery.

### 2.2 Revenue Drivers — Volume vs. Price

The fact sheet contains no explicit volume, unit-shipment, or pricing disclosures. Management does not break out volume versus price, and no production or reserve data is provided. What is observable is the customer-concentration profile: no single customer exceeded 10% of net sales in Q1 2026, whereas in Q1 2025 PT Telekomunikasi Selular accounted for Rp 138,412,746,036 of revenue. On the procurement side, Cisco International Limited represented Rp 129,393,907,006 of purchases (11.8% of net sales) in Q1 2026, down from Rp 184,744,726,391 in Q1 2025. The vendor roster also includes PT Helios Informatika Nusantara, PT ECS Indo Jaya, and PT Tech Data Advanced Solution Indonesia, pointing to an IT distribution and systems-integration model. The sharp drop in “project in progress” inventory from Rp 150,784,144,695 to Rp 68,250,818,416 suggests a completion of prior-period projects without an equivalent new-project ramp in Q1, likely explaining the revenue gap.

### 2.3 Revenue Composition Observations

Customer mix was volatile. Note 5 flags significant receivables swings that mirror revenue concentration shifts: PT Bank Mandiri Tbk appeared at Rp 61 billion in March 2026 versus nil in December 2025; BPJS Kesehatan collapsed from Rp 21.7 billion to Rp 77 million; PT Bursa Efek Indonesia fell from Rp 17.8 billion to Rp 76 million; and several government-related debtors (Badan Teknologi, Informasi dan Intelijen Keuangan, Direktorat Jendral Pajak, PT Artajasa Pembayaran Elektronis) dropped to nil from above Rp 12 billion each. This lumpy, project-driven customer base implies revenue recognition is tied to discrete delivery milestones rather than recurring subscription flows. Domestic versus external revenue is not disclosed.

### 2.4 Revenue Sustainability and Forward Outlook

With no disclosed order backlog or pipeline, visibility is limited. The flush of project inventory is a near-term headwind unless new contracts are signed. Watch items include: (i) the Cisco relationship, which still supplies over 10% of purchases; (ii) the ability to replace lumpy government and telecom projects with new private-sector wins; and (iii) the IDR/USD exposure—a 10% rupiah weakening against the dollar would hit profit by Rp 13,067,346,726 given the net USD monetary asset position of Rp 130,673,467,264. No post-period-end price or contract announcements are disclosed.

## 10. Strengths

### 10.1 Financial Strengths

- **Net cash position**: Note 16 records excess cash over borrowings of Rp 1.4 trillion; the balance sheet carries no material financial debt.
- **Liquidity buffers**: Current ratio of 3.59x, quick ratio of 3.05x, and cash ratio of 1.72x provide a wide margin of safety against short-term shocks.
- **Covenant compliance**: Debt-to-equity, DSCR, and current-ratio covenants are all satisfied, preserving access to bank facilities including PT Bank Mandiri, BCA, UOB, OCBC, Permata, and Danamon lines.

### 10.2 Operational Strengths

- **Cost discipline**: Absolute selling and G&A expenses both declined year-over-year despite inflationary pressures, indicating management is not allowing the cost base to balloon.
- **Interest income growth**: Rose to Rp 11.6 billion from Rp 10.5 billion, partially offsetting higher lease interest.
- **Diversified customer base**: No customer exceeded 10% of sales in Q1 2026, reducing single-account dependency compared to the prior year.

### 10.3 Strategic Strengths

- **Vendor relationships**: Cisco remains a core partner, and the company maintains multiple credit facilities with major Indonesian banks.
- **Employee alignment**: The MESOP program (phase 1 exercisable through November 2026) and relatively stable headcount (1,024 vs 1,032) suggest controlled labor dynamics.
- **Tax efficiency**: The effective tax rate of 23.25% is moderate, and the company holds significant prepaid tax positions (Rp 238.6 billion) that may smooth future cash tax.

## 11. Concerns and Risk Factors

### 11.1 Near-Term Risks

- **HIGH — Operating cash burn**: OCF swung to negative Rp 126.4 billion from positive Rp 63.6 billion. Collections coverage of only 85.5% signals a widening receivables gap.
- **HIGH — Receivables quality**: DSO of 74.4 days and large individual-account swings (Bank Mandiri new Rp 61 billion, several government debtors dropping to nil) suggest lumpy collection risk.
- **HIGH — Revenue trajectory**: An 8% top-line decline with no disclosed backlog raises the risk that Q1 was not a one-off blip.

### 11.2 Structural Risks
